## Statement

Let `ℓ` be an odd prime and `L` a CM field, unramified over `L⁺` at all finite places and without a primitive `ℓ`-th
root of unity, with further conditions on a finite set of places of `L⁺` split in `L` and on an auxiliary place `v₀`
(`Nv₀ ≢ 1 mod ℓ`). Let `n ∈ {2, 3, 4}`, let `χ̄_1, …, χ̄_n` be pairwise distinct residual characters whose sum is
conjugate self-dual up to `ε̄^(1−n)`, and let `B` be the universal ring of ordinary polarized determinants lifting
`det(⊕ χ̄_i)`. Let `x` be a `ℚ̄_ℓ`-point of `B` with regular weights, and `ρ` a representation with the determinant of
`x`, with coefficients in a finite extension of `ℚ_ℓ` containing the images of `L`. Suppose that `ρ` is attached to a
regular algebraic, conjugate self-dual, cuspidal automorphic representation of `GL_n(𝔸_L)` (with two technical
clauses), that it is upper triangular and generic at the places above `ℓ`, that it is unramified at `v₀` (where the
`χ̄_i` are unramified and coincide), that it is irreducible on `G_{L(μ_{ℓ^∞})}`, and that elements with `n` distinct
eigenvalues are dense in `ρ(G_{L(μ_{ℓ^∞})})`. Then, given a bundle of background theorems, `H¹_f(L⁺, ad ρ) = 0`.

In the Lean source the theorem takes this bundle as its one hypothesis. Its contents are listed under Caveats, and we
did not check how it is discharged. The statement is a case of Theorem A of Thorne (2022), for which irreducibility
suffices, and, by a short deduction of ours, of Theorem A of Newton–Thorne (2023). The formal proof invokes neither.

## Context

The general vanishing theorem for adjoint Bloch–Kato Selmer groups is proved in the literature by Taylor–Wiles
patching. The formal proof establishes the case it needs (ordinary points, ranks two to four, residual sums of distinct
characters) by itself, using the published method in the ordinary setting. The patching limit is replaced by a count of
derivations at one Taylor–Wiles level for each precision.

## Comparison with the literature

The statement is a case of the published theorem, and it is proved by the published method. It is a special case of
Theorem A of Newton–Thorne, and of Thorne's Theorem A, and it is established by their Taylor–Wiles argument at the
characteristic-zero point, in the ordinary residually reducible setting of Thorne and of Allen–Newton–Thorne. The
ingredients are theirs: determinants, Taylor–Wiles places chosen for the characteristic-zero representation, a
comparison of levels up to a power of a discriminant, and Selmer counts modulo powers of the uniformizer with uniform
constants. Two steps vary the published ones. The patching limit becomes a count of derivations at a single level for
each precision, and the dual Selmer group is controlled by an argument with traces and density instead of by enormous
image and purity. We did not find either variation in the literature, but for the first we did not look beyond the
papers listed below.

We have not read §4.4 of Newton–Thorne, where the patching is done, and a reader who counts the removal of patching as
essential could class this as a different argument for a special case. That density and irreducibility give an
enormous image is our own deduction, and no one has confirmed it. Only one check was made: no second, independent
reader has looked at this statement or at the comparison. We would like to know whether a count at a finite level in
place of patching is already known, and whether the deduction of enormous image holds.

## Sketch of the proof

Reduction to the ordinary case. At a place above `ℓ`, with regular weights and genericity, a crystalline cocycle of
`ad ρ` is, up to a coboundary, upper triangular with zero diagonal on inertia. Away from `ℓ` it vanishes on the inertia
elements killed residually. So it suffices that the ordinary adjoint Selmer group vanish, or equivalently that a
derivation of `B` at `x` killing the inertial characters above `ℓ` kill all traces.

From cocycles to derivations. After an integral normalisation of `ρ` and its pairing, an ordinary cocycle `z` gives a
point of `B` over the dual numbers, that is, a derivation `δ` at `x` with `δ(tr g) = tr(z(g)ρ(g))`. If `δ` kills all
traces then `z` is a coboundary, since `ρ` spans `M_n` and derivations of a matrix algebra are inner.

The Hecke side. The big ordinary Hecke algebra of a definite unitary group, localised at the maximal ideal of
`⊕ χ̄_i`, carries a determinant. This gives an ideal `J ⊂ B` and a `B`-module `M`, finite free over the weight algebra
`Λ` (power series in `D = n[L⁺:ℚ]` variables) and supported on `V(J)`. Automorphic arithmetic points of rank
`2 ≤ n ≤ 4` lie on `V(J)`, and this is where the bound on the rank enters. Control and semisimplicity on classical
forms give an element `t` with `x(t) ≠ 0` such that `t·ker(x)` kills `M/𝔭_x M`.

Detection. By irreducibility and density, a cocycle `z` of `ad ρ ⊗ ε` with `tr(z(σ)ρ(σ)^k) = 0` for all `k` and all `σ`
in a suitable finite set of regular elements of `G_{L(μ_{ℓ^∞})}` is a coboundary. Indeed, cocycles form a
finite-dimensional space, vanishing at all regular elements extends by density and continuity, irreducibility makes the
first-order deformation trivial there, and what remains is `H¹` of the cyclotomic quotient in a nontrivial character.

Taylor–Wiles data. For each level `N` one takes `q` split places of norm `≡ 1 mod ℓ^N` whose Frobenius elements
approximate the chosen regular elements (residually the eigenvalues need not be distinct). At level `N` there are the
ordinary determinant ring `R_N` with these places added, and a module of Iwahori-type level, projective over
`Λ[Δ_N]`, where `Δ_N` is the `ℓ`-part of the tori at the `q` places. There are maps both ways between this module and
the module at hyperspecial level, whose composites are multiplication by a product `f` of differences of ordered
Frobenius eigenvalues. Relations between diamond operators and traces of tame inertia hold after multiplication by `f`
and raising to the power `2·n!`.

The count. Write `O` for the ring of integers of the coefficient field, `ϖ` for its uniformizer and `k` for its residue
field. From the above there is `c ≠ 0` in `O`, independent of `N` and of the precision `m`, such that for each `m` and
all sufficiently large `N` each of the `D + nq` directions (a weight variable or a generator of `Δ_N`) carries a
derivation of `R_N` at `x` into `O/ϖ^m` equal to `c` on that variable and `0` on the others. By the Greenberg–Wiles
formula, with the dual Selmer group bounded by the detection step, the trace functions of ordinary Selmer cocycles
modulo `ϖ^m` at level `N` number at most `|k|^(m(D + nq) + C)`, with `C` independent of `m` and `N`. Every derivation
of `R_N` gives a Selmer cocycle with a fixed constant times its trace function. If `δ` did not kill all traces, it would
join the `D + nq` derivations to inject `(O/ϖ^(m − v))^(D + nq + 1)` into the trace functions, where `v` is the
valuation of a fixed nonzero element of `O`, and this is impossible for large `m`. One level `N` is used for each `m`.
No limit over `N` is taken and no patched module is formed. The hypothesis that `L` has no primitive `ℓ`-th root of
unity is not used. It follows from `Nv₀ ≢ 1 mod ℓ`.

## Key steps

- **Reduction to the ordinary case** (`UpperTheory.V.Th.adjointLiftingTheorem_of_locPrints_of_core`, with the local
  input `UpperTheory.V.Th.bkOrd`): Bloch–Kato classes are ordinary at the places above `ℓ`.
- **Detection at regular elements** (`UpperTheory.V.Th.regDet`): finitely many regular elements of `G_{L(μ_{ℓ^∞})}`
  detect cocycles, by density and irreducibility, without purity. This is one of the two steps that differ from the
  published ones.
- **Taylor–Wiles places** (`UpperTheory.V.Pa.arPl`): split places of norm `≡ 1 mod ℓ^N`, chosen for the
  characteristic-zero representation.
- **Derivations dual to the variables** (`UpperTheory.V.Th.split`): one derivation into `O/ϖ^m` for each of the
  `D + nq` variables, with a constant independent of `N` and `m`.
- **Selmer bound** (`UpperTheory.V.Th.b1O`): at most `|k|^(m(D + nq) + C)` trace functions of ordinary Selmer cocycles
  modulo `ϖ^m`.
- **The count** (`UpperTheory.V.Th.count`): `D + nq + 1` independent derivations against that bound. This replaces the
  patching limit and is the other step that differs.

## Caveats

The theorem proved has one hypothesis, `HypothesesH'`, a bundle of background theorems: `p`-adic representation theory,
base change, Galois representations of cuspidal representations and their purity, class field theory, duality, the
Greenberg–Wiles formula and the local Bloch–Kato computation. Judging by their names, none of them is a patching,
`R = T`, automorphy lifting or adjoint vanishing statement, but we read only the lists of fields and did not audit how
the bundle is discharged. A text search of the directories of the argument found no `sorry`, `axiom` or `opaque`.
Nothing was compiled. We read in full the statement and the chain down to the count, the detection step and the
reduction. We did not read the Hecke ideal, the theorem on points where `n ≤ 4` enters, the modules at level `N`, the
Taylor–Wiles places or the Greenberg–Wiles counts. Of the literature we did not read §4.4 and §5 of Newton–Thorne,
§6.7 of Thorne (2022), Thorne (2015), Kisin, Breuil–Hellmann–Schraen or Pan.
