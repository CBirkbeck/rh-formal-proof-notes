## Statement

Let `p` be any prime (2 included), `C` an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0 with a real-valued
non-archimedean absolute value, `|p| < 1`, and `𝒪` its valuation ring, assumed `p`-adically complete (for a real-valued
valuation with `|p| < 1` this amounts to `C` being complete; the remark is not in the source). A `p`-divisible group `G`
of height `h` over `𝒪` is a tower of commutative, cocommutative Hopf algebras `A_n` (functions on `G[p^n]`), finite
projective of rank `p^{nh}`, with surjections `A_{n+1} → A_n` whose kernel is generated by the image of the augmentation
ideal under multiplication by `p^n`. `G^D` has the dual Hopf algebras `A_n^∨`; `T_pG` is the module of systems of points
`x_n : A_n → 𝒪` with `p·x_{n+1} = x_n`; `ω_{G^D}` is the inverse limit of the cotangent modules `I/I²` of the `A_n^∨`
(`I` the augmentation ideal). The Hodge–Tate map `𝒪 ⊗_{ℤ_p} T_pG → ω_{G^D}` sends `1 ⊗ (x_n)` to the classes of
`x_n − 1`, `x_n` read as a group-like element of `A_n^∨`. Theorem: for every `κ ∈ 𝒪` with `|κ|^{p−1} ≤ |p|`, `κ·ω_{G^D}`
lies in the image of this map, so `κ` kills its cokernel; at `p = 2`, `κ = 2` qualifies. Finite-level form
(`smul_dUnit_mem`; no completeness): for a commutative, cocommutative bialgebra `A`, finite projective over `𝒪`, with
finitely many group-like elements, `q ≠ 0` in `𝒪`, and a submodule `M ⊆ I/I²` containing `q·(I/I²)` and the classes of
`u − 1`, `u` group-like: `κ·(I/I²) ⊆ M`.

## Why it might matter

The Hodge–Tate map compares two lattices attached to `G`: the span of the Tate module and the invariant differentials
of the dual. A bound for the cokernel says how far apart they are (in particular the map is onto after inverting `p`);
integral constructions with the Hodge bundle of abelian varieties with level structure at `p` use such a bound. The
statement covers `p = 2` with the same exponent `1/(p−1)`; the argument uses only the Hopf algebras of the levels.

## What the checks found

The first reading compared the statement on this page (with `C` complete), so its verdict bears on it; at finite level
it compared finite flat group schemes, not the Lean's bialgebras with any `q` and `M`. Its verdict: known as a
statement, stated for every prime in Fargues (2011, Théorèmes 2 and 3), high confidence. What it did not find in print
(medium confidence) is a complete proof at `p = 2`: there Fargues's proof rests on a remark extending Faltings's
integral comparison, which Faltings (1999) writes for `p ≥ 3`, leaving `p = 2` to a remark; Fargues (2010, Théorème 7)
and a 2006 preprint of Fargues, which the first reading takes to be a chapter of Fargues–Genestier–Lafforgue, assume
`p ≠ 2`; Pilloni–Stroh (Remarque 1.10) record `p = 2`, with the weaker factor `4`, as unwritten. A forward search (six
keyword searches, incomplete) changed nothing: Boxer–Pilloni (preprint, 2021) still use the factor `4` at `p = 2`;
Howard (2020) gives an integral description of the Hodge–Tate map only for `p > 2`; Kim (2012) proves part of
Fargues's remark over 2-adic discrete valuation rings, not the part his proof uses. The second opinion: known, high
confidence (Fargues's theorem covers `p = 2` and the endpoint). Its corrections: "no complete proof in print" should be
"none located", as Fargues supplies a proof using his all-prime remark; weaker bounds in families do not show a gap over
`𝒪`; Fargues's theorem is not literally the Lean's bialgebra lemma, whose novelty, like the method's, it leaves open.
Both say known; they differ on the gap at `p = 2`. Open: is the comparison Fargues uses written out at `p = 2`? Is the
bialgebra lemma, or its method, in print?

## How the formal proof goes

Reduction to linear functionals (`smul_dUnit_mem`, `mem_of_forall_dvd`). Take `A`, `q`, `M` as in the finite-level
form, with counit `ε`. Let `N ⊆ A` be the `𝒪`-span of the group-like elements, of the products
`(a − ε(a))(b − ε(b))` and of `qA`. It is finitely generated (here the finiteness of the set of group-like elements is
used), and `a ↦ class of a − ε(a)` sends `N` into `M`; so it suffices to prove `κA ⊆ N`. Being finitely generated and
torsion-free over the valuation ring `𝒪`, `N` is free; composing multiplication by `q : A → N` with the coordinates of
a basis shows that `x ∈ N` as soon as `q | φ(x)` for every linear `φ : A → 𝒪` with `φ(N) ⊆ q𝒪`. Such a `φ` is
divisible by `q` on group-like elements and satisfies `q | φ(ab) − ε(a)φ(b) − ε(b)φ(a)`: a derivation at the origin
modulo `q`. What remains is `q | κ·φ(a)` for all `a` (`dvd_mul_of_val`; `dvd_mul_of_norm` once `‖p‖ = 1/p`).

Two convolution algebras. `B₁ = Hom_𝒪(A, C)` and `B₂ = Hom_𝒪(A ⊗ A, C)`, with the convolution products, are
`C`-algebras, commutative because `A` is cocommutative. Three algebra maps `B₁ → B₂` are used: `Δ(f) = f∘(product)`,
`i(f) = f ⊗ ε`, `j(f) = ε ⊗ f`. On each algebra `N₁`, resp. `N₂`, is the supremum of `|f|` over `A`, resp. `A ⊗ A`
(finite, as `A` is finitely generated). These are homogeneous, ultrametric and submultiplicative, `N₂(Δf) ≤ N₁(f)` and
`N₂(i(f)·j(g)) = N₁(f)·N₁(g)`. Put `y = q⁻¹φ ∈ B₁`. The derivation property says `N₂(Δy − i(y) − j(y)) ≤ 1`.

Bounded powers (`isIntegral_inv_smul_of_closed`, `exists_supN_pow_le`). `φ` lies in the finite `𝒪`-algebra `A^∨`,
whose `𝒪`-points are the evaluations at the group-like elements of `A` (`exists_isGroupLikeElem`, by biduality); so
every point takes on `φ` a value divisible by `q`. Over `C` the minimal polynomial of `φ`, as an element of `B₁`,
splits, and each root `α` is the value at `φ` of a `C`-valued character of the subalgebra generated by `A^∨`; that
character sends `A^∨` to elements integral over `𝒪`, hence into `𝒪`, so it is a point and `q | α`. Thus `y` is a root
of `∏(Y − α/q)`, monic over `𝒪`; its powers lie in a finitely generated `𝒪`-module and `N₁(y^k) ≤ K` for all `k`. This
uses that `C` is algebraically closed and `𝒪` integrally closed in it; reducedness of `A^∨ ⊗ C` is not used here.

The estimate (`norm_smul_le_one_of_lt`, stated abstractly for `B₁, B₂, N₁, N₂, Δ, i, j, y`). Let `‖c‖ < p^{−1/(p−1)}`,
`z = cy`, `e = Δy − i(y) − j(y)`, `t(m) = N₁(z^m/m!)`. The formula for the `p`-adic valuation of `m!` gives
`‖1/m!‖ ≤ p^{m/(p−1)}` (`norm_factorial_inv_le`); so, with `ρ = p^{1/(p−1)}·‖c‖ < 1`, `t(m) ≤ K·ρ^m → 0` and
`N₂((ce)^l/l!) ≤ ρ^l < 1` for `l ≥ 1`. Suppose `t(1) > 1`, and let `k` be the last index at which `t` attains its
maximum. Since `Δ(z) = i(z) + j(z) + ce`, the binomial formula for divided powers writes `Δ(z^{2k}/(2k)!)` as the sum
of the terms `i(z^a/a!)·j(z^b/b!)·(ce)^l/l!` with `a + b + l = 2k`. The term `(k, k, 0)` has `N₂` equal to `t(k)²`.
Every other term has `N₂ < t(k)²`: a factor `< 1` if `l ≥ 1`, an index beyond `k` if `l = 0`. And
`N₂(Δ(z^{2k}/(2k)!)) ≤ t(2k) ≤ t(k) < t(k)²`, against the ultrametric inequality; so `N₁(cy) ≤ 1`. The case
`‖c‖ = p^{−1/(p−1)}` (`norm_smul_le_one`) follows by multiplying `c` by an `n`-th root of `p` and letting `n` grow.
Hence `q | κ·φ(a)`. No step treats `p = 2` apart.

The `p`-divisible group (`smul_mem_range_dlogLinear`). For a `p`-divisible group `H`, let `L ⊆ ω_H` be the image of
`𝒪 ⊗ T_p(H^D)`. The finite-level form is applied to `A = A_n(H)`, `q = p^n`, and `M` the image of `L` at level `n`.
Its hypotheses hold: `p^n` kills `I/I²` of `A_n`; every group-like element of `A_n` is an `𝒪`-point of `H^D[p^n]`, and
these points are finite in number and all come from `T_p(H^D)` (`natCard_pts_eq`, `pt_surjective`). The count,
`p^{nh}`, uses that a finite Hopf algebra over a field of characteristic 0 is étale (a classical theorem, proved in
the source as `etale_of_hopfAlgebra`). So `κ`, and also `p`, multiply `I/I²` at every level into the image of `L`.

Passage to the limit (`smul_mem_of_levels`). `L` is finitely generated, `T_p` being finite over `ℤ_p`. For `w ∈ ω_H`,
coefficient vectors `c_n ∈ 𝒪^m` are built that express `κw` through generators of `L` at level `n + 1`, with
`c_{n+1} − c_n ∈ p^n𝒪^m`: a solution at level `n + 2` minus `c_n` gives, at level `n + 2`, a vector restricting to
zero at level `n + 1`, hence `p^{n+1}` times a cotangent vector (`exists_of_cotMap_res_eq_zero`), and `p` times that
vector comes from `L`. The `p`-adic limit of the `c_n` expresses `κw` at every level; this is the only use of
completeness. The main theorem takes `H = G^D` and transports along `G ≅ G^{DD}`. A second proof for `H`,
`coker_dlog_killed` (`DvdMulOfGroupLikeVal.lean`, under `src/Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/AlgebraProofs/`,
line 416), assumes `ω_H` finite free and passes to the limit by elementary divisors over `𝒪`.

## Key steps

- **The divided-power estimate** (`GlobalTheory.NearlyPrimitive.norm_smul_le_one_of_lt`): bounded powers of `y` and
  `N₂(Δy − i(y) − j(y)) ≤ 1` give `N₁(cy) ≤ 1` for `‖c‖ < p^{−1/(p−1)}`, by isolating the diagonal term of
  `Δ(z^{2k}/(2k)!)`. The exponent `1/(p−1)` comes from here. (`NormSmulLeOneOfPrimitiveBoundedPowers.lean`, line 121.)
- **Integrality from the values at points** (`GlobalTheory.IntegralOfPointValues.isIntegral_inv_smul_of_closed`): if
  `x` in an integral `𝒪`-algebra has value divisible by `q` at every `𝒪`-point, its image in any `C`-algebra, divided
  by `q`, is integral over `𝒪`. It bounds the powers of `y`. (`IsIntegralOfDivisibleCharacters.lean`, line 24.)
- **Derivations modulo `q`** (`PositivityTheory.HtPt.Farsharp.dvd_mul_of_norm`): sets up `B₁`, `B₂`, the three maps
  and the norms for a bialgebra, checks the hypotheses of the estimate, and concludes `q | c·φ(a)`. The bialgebra
  axioms and cocommutativity enter here. (`FarsharpA.lean`, line 132.)
- **Reduction of the cotangent statement** (`PositivityTheory.HtPt.Farsharp.smul_dUnit_mem`): turns
  `κ·(I/I²) ⊆ M` into the divisibility `q | κ·φ(a)` for functionals `φ` with `φ(N) ⊆ q𝒪`, using that `N` is free over
  the valuation ring (`mem_of_forall_dvd`). (`Farsharp.lean`, line 18.)
- **From the levels to the limit** (`PositivityTheory.HtPt.Farsharp.smul_mem_of_levels`): if `κ` and `p` multiply
  every level `I/I²` into the image of a finitely generated `L ⊆ ω`, then `κ·ω ⊆ L`, when `𝒪` is `p`-adically
  complete. The definition of the tower (kernel of `A_{n+1} → A_n`) is used here. (`FarsharpB.lean`, line 118.)
