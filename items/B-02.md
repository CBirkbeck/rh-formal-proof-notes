## Statement

Let `E` be a CM field with all finite places unramified over `E⁺`, and `U(n)` the unitary group of the standard
Hermitian form on `E^n`, compact at every real place.

*Weak base change.* For every irreducible automorphic `Π` of `U(n)(𝔸_{E⁺})` there are discrete automorphic
representations `Π_i` of `GL_{N_i}(𝔸_E)`, with `Σ N_i = n`, each inside the forms induced from the Speh segment of a
unitary conjugate self-dual cuspidal representation, and at each finite `w` a constituent of the induced product of the
`Π_{i,w}`, with the following properties. The constituent is spherical where `Π` is unramified, with the base-changed
Satake parameter at unramified non-split places, and it is the component of `Π` at every split place. At each embedding
of `E` the infinitesimal characters of the `Π_i` add up to `λ + ρ`, for each weight `λ` of `Π` there, where `ρ` has the
entries `(n−1)/2 − k`, `k = 0, …, n−1`.

*Descent.* Let `π` be a regular algebraic cuspidal representation of `GL_a(𝔸_E)`, conjugate self-dual for the `a`-th
power of the quadratic character of `E/E⁺`. Suppose that `E` contains an imaginary quadratic field, that `[E⁺:ℚ]` is
even, and that the non-split places are prime to an auxiliary `ℓ` and unramified for `π`. Then `π` has the split
components of a representation of `U(a)` unramified at non-split places.

Both are cases of Labesse's Corollaire 5.3 (any inner form of the quasi-split unitary group, any CM extension with
`[F:ℚ] ≥ 2`, discrete representations with discrete series at infinity and a sign property that is automatic for a
group compact at infinity) and Théorème 5.4 (`E/F` unramified at all finite places, the group quasi-split at the inert
places).

## Context

The formal proof uses these statements to pass between definite unitary groups and `GL_n` in its construction of Galois
representations (B01). Labesse's proof rests on the identity of discrete spectra in the stabilised twisted trace formula
and on the fundamental lemma for the whole spherical Hecke algebra. The formal proof has no spectral expansion of the
twisted trace formula, matches only explicit test functions, and needs the fundamental lemma for the unit element only.

## Comparison with the literature

The geometric half of the argument is Labesse's comparison of elliptic terms, specialised to a group compact at
infinity, with explicit matching functions. The spectral half differs. In place of the identity of discrete parts and
the linear independence of Hecke characters it uses an inequality, the truncated geometric trace on `GL_a × GL_b`
bounded by an operator norm, then a power argument dominating the Hecke character of `Π` by operator norms on `L²`, and
a passage from a point of the Gelfand spectrum to automorphic representations. The descent is handled in the same way.
We did not find this spectral argument in print, but that says little. We did not read the deep lemmas, we read Labesse
in the relevant sections with proofs and the rest of the literature only in abstract or as bibliographic records, and
our search was short and not systematic.

A second, independent check could not decide whether the argument differs in substance from the published ones. Taking
`k`-th roots and separating characters are standard devices of spectral-radius and `C*`-algebra theory, and for
exponential sums in general they give only a limsup. It finds in Flicker (1990) a stronger precedent for restricted
transfer than our comparison acknowledges. What may be substantial is the uniform estimate for the truncated kernel,
kept through matching and convolution, and the limit theorem. The second check also points out that `Σ 1/Nw < ∞` and
the Jacquet–Shalika bound alone do not justify the rigidity over split places in the descent as we summarise it below
(compare Ramakrishnan), and we did not read that step of the proof. We would like to know whether the growth argument,
or the truncated-kernel estimate, is in print, and whether the rigidity step of the descent needs more than our
summary of it describes.

## Sketch of the proof

*Unitary side.* For a weight and a level, forms on `U(n)` make a finite-dimensional space. For a test function with
regular support at one place, the trace of a Hecke operator is a sum over rational conjugacy classes (mass, character
of the weight representation, orbital integrals). Fourier analysis on the obstruction groups regroups it
into a sum, over `a + b = n` and pairs of coprime polynomials, of `κ`-orbital integrals.

*Matching, place by place.* Stable twisted orbital integrals on `GL_a × GL_b` must equal a transfer factor times
`κ`-orbital integrals on `U(n)`, which is endoscopy and base change in one step. Pairs are built only for explicit
functions. At split places one takes twisted constant terms. At unramified non-split places the unit matches the unit,
by the fundamental lemma for unitary groups in the unit case, proved in the source from a lattice-counting identity. At
chosen non-split places where `Π` is unramified, an Iwahori double coset of a strictly dominant element matches an
explicit signed combination of Iwahori double cosets, of regular support. At ramified non-split places one uses a
principal congruence subgroup. At infinity one uses very cuspidal functions whose elliptic orbital integrals are
characters of the transferred weights, with multipliers.

*`GL` side.* Take a function very cuspidal at every infinite place and of regular support. Consider the part of
`GL_n(E)\GL_n(𝔸_E)` where every subspace has degree below a profile `τ`, a truncation of Harder–Narasimhan type that is
compact by Mahler's criterion. The integral of the twisted kernel over this part equals the sum of orbital integrals
over regular classes elliptic at infinity once `τ ≥ C(1 + r)`, `r` the size of the support. No spectral expansion of
this truncated trace is used. So for every word in the Hecke generators and their adjoints, the unitary trace equals a
combination of elliptic sums.

*The estimate.* If a test function acts as `T₁UT₂` on `L²` of `GL_a × GL_b`, its twisted kernel on the diagonal is
`⟨k₁(x), U k₂(x)⟩`, with norms bounded by a lattice-point count, so the truncated integral is at most
`C(1 + τ)^N ‖U‖`. Supports of `k`-th convolution powers grow linearly in `k`, whence
`|tr(b^k | forms on U(n))| ≤ C(1 + k)^N max_s ‖(β_s^m R_s(b))^{k−j}‖`. Here `s` runs over the endoscopic data with a
choice of transferred weight, `R_s(b)` is the operator of `b` on the `L²` space of the corresponding `GL_a × GL_b`,
`β_s` is a fixed operator commuting with it, and `m`, `j`, `N` are fixed integers.

*Growth separates.* A commutative `*`-algebra acts on a finite-dimensional space `V` and, by `*`-representations `R_s`,
on finitely many Hilbert spaces. Let a `*`-character `p` occur in `V` on a vector fixed by an operator `u` commuting
with the action. Suppose that for every `b` in the ideal generated by one element on which `p` does not vanish,
`|tr((u b)^k | V)| ≤ C(1 + k)^N max_s ‖(β_s^m R_s(b))^{k−j}‖` for all large `k`. Then for one `s` the character `p`
extends to a `*`-character, bounded by the operator norm, of the `*`-algebra generated by the `R_s(b)` and
`(β_s^m)* β_s^m`. In particular `|p(b)| ≤ ‖R_s(b)‖` for all `b`. So the Hecke character of `Π` is a point of the
`L²`-spectrum of the Hecke algebra on one `GL_a × GL_b`. It is a limit of characters of generalised eigenfunctions in a
weighted `L²` space, which are automorphic forms with non-positive exponents. Forms with converging eigenvalues and
infinitesimal characters tending to a real regular one have a limit induced from discrete representations. A pigeonhole
argument over finite sets of places and strong multiplicity one for isobaric sums give one datum, its stability under
the involution `θ` of the twisted kernel, and the split components.

*Descent.* On the block `a = n` the elliptic sum minus half the twisted trace on `π` is bounded by `C(1 + k)^N` times
the operator norm on the complement of `π`, plus `Cε^k`. The growth lemma leaves three cases: a representation of
`U(n)` with the eigenvalues of `π`, a character on the complement, or a character on a proper endoscopic block. The
last two give another isobaric datum with the Satake parameters of `π` at almost all split places. As we summarise it,
this is excluded by strong multiplicity one over the split places (the non-split places have `Σ 1/Nw < ∞`) with the
Jacquet–Shalika bound. We did not read this step in the source, and the second check doubts that these two facts alone
justify it.

## Key steps

- **The unitary side as an exact trace** (`UpperTheory.BaseChangeIdentities.uSide`): the trace of a Hecke operator of
  regular support on forms of `U(n)`, regrouped into `κ`-orbital integrals. These are Labesse's stabilised elliptic
  terms, specialised to a group compact at infinity. We read the proof.
- **Truncated elliptic identity on `GL`** (`PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.CV.ellFormula2J`): the truncated integral of the
  twisted kernel equals the regular elliptic sum for large truncation. It is the non-invariant counterpart of Labesse's
  `T = T_e`. We read the assembly, but not the vanishing of the non-elliptic terms.
- **Operator-norm bound** (`PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.E1.sBound_holds`): the truncated integral is at most
  `C(1 + τ)^N ‖U‖`. This stands where the spectral side of the trace formula would be. We did not read the proof.
- **Growth separates characters** (`PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.F.PeakTraceBound`): polynomial domination of exponential
  sums by operator norms of `k`-th powers forces each character into the spectrum of one block. Proved as
  `PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.F.peak_trace_bound_holds`, whose proof we did not read.
- **Limit theorem** (`PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.SpectralAccumulation.eFinal_holds`): limits of automorphic forms with
  converging eigenvalues and real regular infinitesimal character lie in spaces induced from discrete representations.
  We did not read the proof.
- **Descent** (`PositivityTheory.Rg.BcB.Desc.GrowthPiJ`, a statement, proved as
  `UpperTheory.V.Z.Tf.GlobalTraceComparison.Pi.growthPiJ_holds`): the twisted trace on `π` is split off the elliptic sum
  up to a norm on its complement, and a trichotomy and rigidity over split places follow. We did not read the chain in
  detail.

## Caveats

We read the statements of the main and key intermediate declarations and the proofs of the assembly steps (`core_of`,
`baseChangeIsobaricN_of`, `fieldN_of`, `growth_of`, `frameBound_of`, `uSide`, `definiteN_of`, among others). We did not
read the several hundred files below them, the fundamental lemma for the unit, or the descent chain in detail. We
compiled nothing. A text scan of the 904 files of the two base-change directories found no `sorry` or `axiom`, and no
use there of the stated stable twisted trace formula (`GlobalTheory.BCT.SimpleTwistedTraceFormulaStable`) as a
hypothesis. We did not open Harris–Labesse, Clozel–Harris–Labesse, Deligne–Kazhdan or Clozel, and we read Mok,
Kaletha–Minguez–Shin–White and Beuzart-Plessis–Liu–Zhang–Zhu in abstract only. The second check did not reread Labesse,
and Dixmier, Bernstein and L. Lafforgue (Astérisque 243) are cited from memory and were not checked.
