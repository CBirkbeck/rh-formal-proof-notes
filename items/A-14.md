## Statement

Let `K/ℚ_p` be finite unramified of degree `n`, `G = Gal(ℚ̄_p/K)`, and `E ⊂ ℚ̄_p` finite over `ℚ_p` containing the
conjugates of `K(ζ_p)`, with integers `O`, uniformizer `λ`, residue field `k`, and embeddings `τ_i : K → E`
(`i ∈ ℤ/n`) with `τ_{i+1}∘Frob = τ_i`.

The position data are an integer `t ≥ 1` and, for each `i`, integers `h_i ≥ 2`, `η_i ≥ 1` and `w_i`, a type (low or
high), and a multiset `U_i` of `r` integers. Put `B_i = {w_i, …, w_i+t−1}`. The window is `[1, η_i]` at low type and
`[h_i−η_i, h_i−1]` at high type. Put `σ_i = ⌊Σ_{j≥0} h_{i+j+1}/p^{j+1}⌋`. Assume:

- `U_i ∪ B_i ⊂ [1, h_i−1]`, and `B_i` lies in the window;
- `η_i + 4σ_i + 6 < p`;
- `|u − β| > σ_i + 2` for `u ∈ U_i` in the window and `β ∈ B_i`;
- for `u ∈ U_i` outside the window and integers `−2σ_i−2 ≤ y ≤ η_i+2σ_i+3`, `u ≢ y` modulo `p` at low type, and
  `h_i − u ≢ y` modulo `p` at high type.

Let `ρ` be a crystalline `E`-representation of `G` of dimension `r` with Hodge–Tate weights `U_i` at `τ_i`. (The
cyclotomic character `ε` has weight `−1`, which the source proves for its own period rings.) Let `T₀ ⊂ ρ` be a stable
`O`-lattice, `χ₁ : G → O^×` a crystalline character of weight `w_i` at `τ_i`, and `χ_c = χ₁ε^{1−c}`. Call a crystalline
`W` *full* if its weights are `U_i ⊔ B_i` and, for every stable lattice `L ⊂ W`, `L/λL` has the Jordan–Hölder factors
of `T₀/λ ⊕ k(χ̄₁) ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ k(χ̄_t)`. Call a finite `O[G]`-module *admissible* if it is `L₁/L₂` for stable lattices in a
finite sum of full `W`.

**Theorem.** Let `z_1, …, z_s ∈ H¹(G, (T₀/λ)(χ̄₁^{−1}))` be `k`-independent classes whose extensions of `k(χ̄₁)` by
`T₀/λ` are admissible. Then `s ≤ d = Σ_i #{u ∈ U_i : u < w_i}`.

In the Lean source this is proved for abstract period rings and under three named hypotheses, described in the
Caveats. No declaration states the bound without hypotheses.

## Context

The statement concerns which extension classes modulo `p` occur in reductions of lattices in crystalline
representations with prescribed Hodge–Tate weights. The hypotheses on the weights allow weights above `p`, where
Fontaine–Laffaille theory does not apply, and they also allow data inside the Fontaine–Laffaille range. The number `d`
is the value that the Bloch–Kato formula gives for `dim H¹_f(G, ρ ⊗ χ₁^{−1}) − dim H⁰`. The source uses the bound to
identify, for every finite coefficient module, the admissible classes with the Bloch–Kato finite ones (`stmtAt_of_real`,
a theorem under named hypotheses).

## Comparison with the literature

We compared with the literature a stronger statement: the bound without hypotheses, for the usual period rings. The
Lean statement assumes three propositions, which are proved elsewhere in the source and combined only inside larger
theorems, and it is stated over an abstract package of period rings. So what follows bears on it only once those
hypotheses are discharged. The published bounds we found are of two kinds. In the Fontaine–Laffaille range, in any rank,
the theory gives the exact dimension of the space of its extensions, which we compute to be `d + dim Hom`, with no
position hypotheses. There we regard the bound as known, and the statement here as weaker, since it needs `p ≥ 11` and
the position hypotheses. An independent second check points out, however, that the theory gives `d + dim Hom`, and that
obtaining `d` from it needs a reason for dropping the `Hom` term. For weights at most `p` there are Chang–Diamond (rank
two, by the same method), Gee–Liu–Savitt (rank two) and Gao (upper triangular reductions). The last two are not
comparable with the statement here.

We found no bound beyond weight `p`, but our search of the literature was brief, and so we say only that the statement
improves on what we found, and only in the range of weights. We have not searched the later literature systematically.
Among later work we found a preprint of Bhatt, Gee and Kisin, which allows any weights but concerns only the
semisimplified reduction. By our own computation, for `11 ≤ p ≤ 29` and with the Serre weights taken from memory, the
case `K = ℚ_p`, `r = t = 1` follows from the Breuil–Mézard conjecture for `GL_2(ℚ_p)` as quoted there. Computed cases
cannot give that implication for every prime. The second check could not decide: it found no checked result that gives
the full assertion, but holds that this does not establish priority. The two assessments thus differ on whether our
failure to find a bound beyond weight `p` supports an improvement. We would like to know whether work above weight `p`
bounds such classes, and whether Fontaine–Laffaille theory gives `d` or only `d + dim Hom`.

## Sketch of the proof

There are two layers. The first is semilinear algebra over `k((X))`, with no `p`-adic Hodge theory: `AdmBound.main`
proves the bound for an abstract "model theory". The second shows that reductions of Wach lattices form one
(`real_of_presented`). In the statement of `resBound_dsum` this is a hypothesis (`TopSit.Real`), supplied inside
`twistedLatticeCohomology_theoremTOP`, which is itself an implication from a bundle of named statements.

*Setting.* `k((X))` carries commuting `φ`, `γ` with `φ(X) = unit·X^p`, `γ(X) = X(1 − θX)`, `θ(0) = 1`. It is the ring
of invariants of the `(p−1)`-torsion of `ℤ_p^×` in `k((π))`. An exact functor sends finite `k[G]`-modules (a class
closed under submodules, quotients and sums) to families `(D_i)_{i∈ℤ/n}` of étale `(φ,γ)`-modules, with
`φ_i : D_{i−1} → D_i`. Vectors fixed by `φ` and `γ` come from `G`-maps from `k`. For lattices `N_i ⊂ D_i`, the height
condition is `φ_i^*N_{i−1} ⊂ X^{−w_i}N_i` and `X^{h_i−w_i}N_i ⊂ φ_i^*N_{i−1}`, and the `Γ` condition is
`(γ_i−1)N_i ⊂ XN_i`. The number `sen(N_i)(x)` is the multiplicity of `x` as a generalised eigenvalue of
`∇ = X^{−1}(γ−1)` on `N_i/XN_i`. A model theory is a class of base families satisfying both conditions and closed under
sums, such that `sen` equals `μ` times the residues of `(U_i ⊔ B_i) − w_i` for some `μ`, the module has the
Jordan–Hölder factors of `μ` copies of `T̄ ⊕ ω⁰ ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ ω^{1−t}` (where `T̄ = (T₀/λ)(χ̄₁^{−1})` and `ω = ε̄`), and `0` is a
semisimple eigenvalue of `∇` modulo `X^{h_i−w_i+1}` at high type. A model of `A` is the image of a base family of `B`
intersected with `D(A₁)`, for `A₁ ⊂ B` and `A₁ ↠ A`. Standard models `N^{std}` of `T̄` (with `sen` the residues of
`U_i − w_i`) and of each `ω^{−e}` are given.

*From a class to a Herr pair* (`exists_model_repr_inZZ`). Let `A` be an extension of `k` by `T̄` with a model `N_A`.
Its image in `D(k)` is a model of the trivial character, hence equals `k[[X]]·1` by the uniqueness below. Lift the
generator to `ξ_i ∈ N_{A,i}`. Then `b_i = φ_iξ_{i−1} − ξ_i`, `g_i = (γ_i−1)ξ_i` is a 1-cocycle of the Herr complex of
`D(T̄)`, where `N = N_A ∩ D(T̄)` is a model of `T̄`. At low type `g_i ∈ XN_i` and `b_i ∈ X^{−w_i}N_i`. At high type, with
`v_i = h_i − w_i`, we have `b_i ∈ X^{−v_i}φ_i^*N_{i−1}` and `g_i − (γ_i−1)x_i ∈ X^{v_i+2}N_i` for some `x_i ∈ N_i`, and
this uses the semisimplicity. If the pair is a coboundary `d(x)`, then `ξ − x` is fixed by `φ` and `γ`, so it comes from
a `G`-invariant vector splitting `A`. Hence independent classes give independent pairs.

*Uniqueness for block characters* (`model_chr_eq`). Two lattice families with the same height condition satisfy
`X^{σ_i}N_i ⊂ N′_i` (`CloseTw.main`). Indeed, the least `m_i` with `X^{m_i}N_i ⊂ N′_i` obey `p·m_{i−1} ≤ m_i + h_i`,
and going round the cycle of embeddings gives `m_i ≤ σ_i`. So a model of `ω^{−e}`, `0 ≤ e < t`, is `X^{κ_i}` times the
standard one, with `|κ_i| ≤ σ_i` and Sen weight `e − κ_i`, and the sign of `κ_{i−1}` is fixed by the type of `i`. In a
composition series of the base module through the given line, `sen` adds up. By the position hypotheses, the Sen
weights in `[−σ_i, t+σ_i]` come from character pieces only and are `μ` copies of `0, …, t−1`. So `Σ κ = 0` at each `i`,
and since the `κ` have one sign, they all vanish.

*The largest model* (`ModelCount.count`). Sums of models are models, and models of `T̄` lie in `X^{−σ_i}N^{std}_i`, so
there is a largest model `N^max ⊃ N^{std}`. With `Q = N^max/N^{std}` one has
`sen(N^max)(y) + mult_Q(y+1) = sen(N^{std})(y) + mult_Q(y)`, and `mult_Q(y) ≠ 0` only if `y − j` is a Sen weight of
`N^{std}` for some `1 ≤ j ≤ σ_i`. Sum over `y ∈ [−w_i, −1]` at low type, or over `[0, v_i+σ_i+1]` at high type. The
position hypotheses kill the boundary terms. The result is that `N^max_i` has no Sen weight in `{0, …, σ_i+1}`, and,
with `d_i = #{u ∈ U_i : u < w_i}`, that `Σ_{0≤j<w_i} sen(N^max_i)(j − w_i) = d_i` at low type and
`d_i + Σ_{0≤y≤v_i} sen(N^max_i)(y) = r` at high type.

*The count* (`PolarBound.main`), applied to `N = N^max`. As `N_i` has no Sen weight in `{0, …, σ_i+1}`, `∇` is
bijective on `N_i/X^{σ_i+2}N_i`. By the gauge lemma (`Gauge.main`) a pair can be changed by `d(y)`, `y ∈ ∏N_i`, so that
`g_i ∈ X^{σ_i+3}N_i`. A pair with `b ∈ ∏N_i` and `g ∈ ∏X^{σ_i+3}N_i` is a coboundary, as `φ` maps `∏X^{σ_i+2}N_i` into
`X` times itself and `1 − φ` is bijective there. The corrected first components span, modulo `N_i`, a space of
dimension at most `d_i`. At low type they lie in the kernel of `∇` on `X^{−w_i}N_i/N_i`. At high type
(`PolarCount.main`), put `F = X^{−v}φ_i^*N_{i−1}` and `L = X^{v+1}N_i`. Every `x ∈ F` is `X^{−1}φ(ψ(Xx))` plus an
element of `∇F`, so the cokernel of `∇` on `F/L`, hence its kernel, has dimension at most `r`. The kernel contains the
classes in question and the generalised `0`-eigenspace of `N_i/L`, which has dimension `r − d_i`. This uses
`1 ≤ v ≤ p − 7` and the operator `ψ` modulo `p` (`lemmaPsi`, proved in the source): `ψ(X^aφ^*M) = M` for
`2−p ≤ a ≤ 1`, and `γ − 1` maps the `ψ`-zero part of `X^aφ^*M` onto that of `X^{a+1}φ^*M` for `2−p ≤ a ≤ 0`.

*Realisation.* `X` is the reduction of the trace of `π` under the torsion of `Gal(K(μ_{p^∞})/K)`. Base families are the
reductions modulo `λ` of the torsion-invariants of Wach lattices of `L ⊗ χ₁^{−1}`, for `L` a lattice in a sum of full
representations. The proof uses (`WachUse.QN`) an invariant basis, heights of `φ` bounded by the weights, the rank-one
case, saturation, additivity, and the characteristic polynomial `∏_{u∈U_i}(Y − c(−u))`, `c(a) = (1−(1+p)^a)/p`, of
`π₀^{−1}(γ−1)` modulo `π₀ + p`. In the source a Wach lattice is part of a package of data with listed properties. The
package exists (`exists_wach`) for the tied period rings, given two named statements. One of them has its own proof in
the source, and the other follows by two further implications from a statement on prismatic `F`-crystals, also proved
there (`bhattScholzeIntegral`).

## Key steps

- **Uniqueness of the model of a block character** (`GlobalTheory.Models.CharModel.model_chr_eq`): in position, the
  only model of `ω^{−e}`, `0 ≤ e < t`, is the standard one. It puts the lift `ξ` in a known lattice, and it needs a count
  over a whole composition series, not over one line. (`TwistedPhiGammaModelCharacterEqStandard.lean`, line 107.)
- **Comparison of lattice families** (`GlobalTheory.PhiGam.CloseTw.main`): equal height conditions give
  `X^{σ_i}N_i ⊂ N′_i`. For `σ_i = 0` this is uniqueness of the lattice family, and the `σ_i` in the position hypotheses
  come from it. (`PhiGammaLatticeFamiliesShiftInclusion.lean`, line 12.)
- **Sen multiplicities of the largest model** (`GlobalTheory.ModelCount.count`): the telescoping identity and the three
  counts for `N^max`. This is where `d_i` enters. (`TwistedPhiGammaModelSenWeightCount.lean`, line 85.)
- **Polar count at high type** (`GlobalTheory.PhiGam.PolarCount.main`): `dim Z/(Z ∩ N) + Σ_{0≤y≤v} sen(N)(y) ≤ r` for
  `Z ⊂ X^{−v}φ^*N₀` with `(γ−1)Z ⊂ X^{v+2}N`. This is the only use of `ψ`.
  (`PolarPartFinrankAddSenWeightsLeRank.lean`, line 200.)
- **Count of polar Herr pairs** (`GlobalTheory.Models.PolarBound.main`): pairs independent modulo coboundaries number at
  most `Σ d_i`, by the gauge lemma (`GlobalTheory.PhiGam.Gauge.main`, `CoeSmulMem.lean`, line 57) and the two polar
  bounds. (`CardCocyclesIndependentModCoboundariesLe.lean`, line 117.)
- **Realisation by reduced Wach lattices** (`GlobalTheory.BasePairs.real_of_presented`): the hypothesis `TopSit.Real`
  holds given the Wach package, its properties `WachUse.QN` and two further named statements (`RealD`, `CrysCalc`),
  which have their own proofs in the source (`realD`, and `crysCalc` for the tied period rings). This is where Wach
  lattices enter. (`ExistsModThOfPres.lean`, line 72.)

## Caveats

`resBound_dsum` is a theorem with a proof. It assumes three propositions, which are definitions: `LemmaPsi`,
`TopSit.Real` and `TopSit.Lem0`. Each is derived in the source from further statements. `LemmaPsi` is derived by
`lemmaPsi`, with no hypotheses. `TopSit.Real` is derived by `real_of_presented`, for a Wach package, from three named
statements (`RealD`, `WachUse.QN`, `CrysCalc`). `TopSit.Lem0` is derived by
`admissibleLocalSituation_admissible_classes` from two named statements (`Closed`, `StdAdm`). The pieces are combined
in `stmtAt_of_real` and `twistedLatticeCohomology_theoremTOP`. The second of these is itself an implication from a
bundle of named statements (properties of the Wach package, local Tate duality, the local Euler characteristic formula
and others), and it takes `WachUse.QN` from that bundle. No declaration states the bound without hypotheses.
"Crystalline" refers to an abstract package of period rings, and `CrysCalc`, on which the derivations of `Real` and
