## Statement

`K` is an imaginary quadratic field with character `η` and complex conjugation `c`, `ℓ` an odd prime, `O ⊂ ℚ̄_ℓ` the
ring of integers of a finite unramified extension of `ℚ_ℓ`, `N` even, `ψ` a Dirichlet character modulo `N` with values
in `O`, `e ≥ 1` with `ψ(−1) = (−1)^e`, `Ψ = χ_ℓ^e·ψ`. Assume `ℓ ∤ d_K·h_K·ord(ψ)`, `gcd(d_K, N) = 1`, `(ℓ − 1) ∤ 2e`,
the component of `ψ` at `ℓ` has square `1`, and `K` is not in the field cut out by `χ_ℓ mod ℓ` and the part of `ψ` of
conductor prime to `ℓ`. Let `S` represent the lifts `r : G_ℚ → GL₃ ⋊ {±1}` of `diag(Ψ̄, 1, Ψ̄⁻¹)` with sign `η` that are
trivial on the inertia elements at `p ≠ ℓ` killed by `r̄`, lie at `ℓ` on `R_ℓ = 𝔘/P` (`P` the minimal prime, through
`Ψ ⊕ 1 ⊕ Ψ⁻¹`, of the saturated ordinary ideal of the universal local lifting ring `𝔘`, for the inertial characters
`Ψ, 1, Ψ⁻¹`), and have `r(c) = 1` in `GL₃` and `r(g₁) − r(c·g₁⁻¹·c)` diagonal, for an element `g₁ ∈ G_K` with
`Ψ̄(g₁)² ≠ 1` (a marked element). Then for every `ι : ℚ̄_ℓ ≅ ℂ` there is a marked `g₁` such that, for every such `S`,
every `ℚ̄_ℓ`-point `ρ` of the `O`-algebra of entries of the universal lift, irreducible on `G_K`, has `ρ|G_K ⊗ χ_ℓ⁻¹`
attached (at almost all places) to a regular algebraic conjugate self-dual cuspidal `π` on `GL₃(𝔸_K)`. Also: (a) the
algebra of entry monomials fixed by the torus `diag(t, 1, t⁻¹)` is finite over `O`; (c) at a point with `ρ|G_K = W ⊕ 1`,
`W` irreducible, the extensions of `W` to `G_ℚ` of trace `0` at `c`, twisted by `χ_ℓ^{−e}`, are attached to newforms of
weight `2e + 1`; and (w1), (w2) below.

## Why it might matter

It is an automorphy lifting statement for a residual representation that is a sum of three characters, two of them
exchanged by the conjugate duality, with no auxiliary place of Steinberg type in the hypotheses. In the formal proof it
attaches automorphic representations to the points of this deformation ring that are irreducible on `G_K`, and newforms
to those of the form `W ⊕ 1`; these are then counted to bound the length of a Selmer-type module attached to `(ψ, e)`.

## What the checks found

The first reading compared the literature with an earlier, wider description, in which (b) gave `π` the weight `(e−1, 0, 1−e)`,
(c) held at every point `W ⊕ μ`, and which included (d), unlinkedness at `ℓ`, a separate proposition in the formal proof. The
statement here has no weight and no (d), assumes `μ = 1` in (c) without stating `det W = η_K` (used in that reading's deduction
of (c)), and adds (w1), (w2), not compared there: its verdict is on that description. It finds (a), (b), (d) incomparable, medium
confidence: Thorne (2015, Theorem 7.1) and Allen–Newton–Thorne (2020, Theorem 1.1), theorems in every rank, need a place of
Steinberg type and residual constituents each conjugate self-dual, so they exclude `Ψ̄ ⊕ 1 ⊕ Ψ̄⁻¹`; Berger–Klosin (2026) prove
`R = T`, stronger in kind, for this shape with no Steinberg place, but for another ordinary condition, with `ℓ` split in `K`,
under Selmer-group hypotheses and an unproved one on Hecke algebras. It finds (c) known in substance from Skinner–Wiles (1999),
by a deduction of its own that no specialist has confirmed. The second opinion, judging the statement as given here:
incomparable, medium confidence; the closest theorems do not cover it, but exhaustiveness, (w1), (w2) and the formalisation are
unverified. Its corrections: no weight in (b); unlinkedness and rigidity are separate; (c) assumes `W ⊕ 1`; "known" covers the
modularity in (c), not automatically (w1), (w2); Pan's theorem also needs oddness and potential semistability; lower-rank control
and connectedness are established methods. The two readings agree. Open: does the deduction of (c) hold for `W` as stated here,
and are (w1), (w2) in print? Can Berger–Klosin's method reach the ordinary flag `Ψ, 1, Ψ⁻¹`?

## How the formal proof goes

The source states (a)–(c) as one named proposition, with two clauses asserted in (b) when `ρ` is continuous and in (c) for `W`
over every imaginary quadratic field on which it is irreducible. (w1): at a place above `ℓ` with a full flag having no two
characters in ratio `χ_ℓ`, there is one with no two in ratio `χ_ℓ` up to finite order. (w2): for each regular algebraic cuspidal
`π′` to which the representation is attached there is a real `t` such that, at the finite places `v` prime to `ℓ` or as in (w1),
the cuspidal support of `π′_v` consists of characters `ξ` with `(Nv)^{t−1/2} < |ξ(ϖ_v)| < (Nv)^{t+1/2}`. A theorem (second `lean:`
line) proves the proposition from a bundle of local Galois cohomology and from `GdEndAlt`: the same conclusions for lifts over
discrete valuation rings finite over `O`, ordinary at `ℓ`. The link is the chart of `R_ℓ` at the diagonal lift
(`UpperTheory.V.Loc.rtChart`): (a) follows once lifts over complete discrete valuation rings of characteristic `ℓ` have the
characteristic polynomials of `r̄` on a subgroup of finite index (`finA_of_end`); each point of the algebra of entries is then
torus-conjugate to a lift over a discrete valuation ring (`exists_ordPt_of_point`), and irreducibility on `G_K` passes to its open
subgroups (`matIrr_open_gd`). This theorem does not use that `O` is unramified.

`GdEndAlt` is proved over a larger field. `frame_ex` gives an abelian CM field `L ⊇ K`, described, though not checked
here, as made from a real quadratic field, the subfield of degree `ℓ` of `ℚ(μ_w)` for a prime `w`, and a layer `m` of
the cyclotomic `ℤ_ℓ`-extension. A bound `C₀` for the `ℓ`-ranks of class groups along the tower comes first (Ferrero and
Washington's theorem, proved in the source as `UpperTheory.V.FW.ferreroWashington`), then `m = 3C₀ + 7`, giving
`d = [L⁺ : ℚ] > 3C₀ + 7` and local degrees above `ℓ` exceeding `5` (a "big" frame).

Over `L`, for `n = 2` and `n = 3`, two rings are compared: the space of polarised ordinary lifts of the diagonal representation of
`G_L`, with its ring `B_n` of torus invariants, its graded algebra `A_n` of entries and the weight algebra `Λ` of dimension
`1 + nd`; and the universal ring `B_det` of ordinary determinants for a definite unitary group over `L` with one auxiliary place
`v₀`. `hida_unitary_of_printed_hida` gives an ideal `J ⊂ B_det` (described, though not checked here, as the kernel of the map to the big
ordinary Hecke algebra at an Eisenstein maximal ideal) with: `B_det/J` finite and torsion-free over `Λ`; the point property (an
arithmetic point whose representation is automorphic with (w1), (w2), unramified at `v₀`, with a flag at `ℓ` as in (w1), lies in
`V(J)`); classicality (an irreducible arithmetic point of `V(J)` is automorphic, with (w2) off `v₀`); and patching data.

`pFromAut` turns the patching data into two statements on the kernels of suitable points of `B_det` with values in `k′[[T]]`, `k′`
a finite field. (P1) If the representation is absolutely irreducible and `J` lies in the kernel, `J` lies in every prime below it.
(P3) Along a chain of points that are sums of two polarised irreducible blocks, joined by "thick" primes, `J` passes from an
irreducible prime below the first to the irreducible and thick primes below the last. The proof uses Taylor–Wiles places for
finitely many detecting elements, two Selmer counts (`b1Ar`, `b2Ar`) and ultrafilter patching.

`absMain` is purely algebraic. Given, for `n = 2, 3`, that `B_det/J` is finite over `Λ` with minimal primes of dimension
`1 + nd`, (P1), (P3), one irreducible point of `V(J)` (a seed), an unramifiedness condition modulo `J` and a condition
(N4) at every point, it shows that the image of `J` in `B_n` lies in every irreducible prime of `A_n`. In rank two the
nonsplit residual lattices form a connected space; at each closed point the local ring has at least `2d + 1` more
variables than relations, so its components are chained through intersections of that dimension, each lying in a nice
prime (bigness is used), where (P1) moves `J` across. In rank three the rank-two conclusion makes the block locus finite
over `Λ` (`UpperTheory.V.Fr.l53`), and (P3) joins components across. A by-product is the statement in characteristic `ℓ`
behind (a).

The seeds (`seed2_alt`, `unitary_three_automorphy_dense_of_prints`) are automorphic of weights `(m, −m)` and `(m, 0, −m)`; they are described, though not checked here, as coming from a newform congruent to an Eisenstein series and from its adjoint lift. Classicality gives (w2) only off
`v₀`, so the argument is run for two places `v₀` and glued (`twoRuns_alt`). Automorphy descends from `L` to `K` by soluble descent
(rank three) and from `L` to `ℚ` (rank two), where the inertial flag `(χ_ℓ^e, χ_ℓ^{−e})` gives a newform of weight `2e + 1`.

Hypotheses: `(ℓ − 1) ∤ 2e`, the condition at `ℓ` and `ℓ ∤ d_K` are described, though this was not traced here, as giving
the marked element, and `ℓ ∤ ord(ψ)` as making `Ψ` trivial on inertia away from `ℓ` where `Ψ̄` is; `N` even gives
`2ℓ | ℓN|d_K|` for the rank-three seeds (traced); a search by name found `ℓ ∤ h_K` used in one lemma
(`UpperTheory.V.U3.char_eq_one`: a continuous character of `G_K`, congruent to `1` and unramified everywhere, is
trivial) and passed to the hypotheses of the Euler system statement, and a use that does not name the hypothesis would
escape that search; `gcd(d_K, N) = 1` and the condition on `K` were not traced. Published theorems (Galois cohomology,
representations of `GL_n`, base change, Galois representations of cuspidal representations) enter as bundles of named
propositions. They are not axioms: `SmallClassNumbers/CorGKOf.lean` has theorems without hypotheses (`litN`, and the
argument of `corGK_of` in `corGK`) supplying every bundle used. Unlinkedness at `ℓ` (`AdmI_s`) and vanishing of adjoint
Bloch–Kato Selmer groups at these points (`RigidityFirstCase`, `RigiditySecondCase`) are separate propositions, proved
the same way.
