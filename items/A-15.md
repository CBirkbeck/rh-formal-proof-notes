## Statement

Write `u = log y`. Let `g` be a measurable real function with `|g(u)| ≤ C₀e^{2u}` for `u ≥ 0`, and suppose that its
Laplace transform `Φ(s) = ∫₀^∞ g(u)e^{−su} du`, defined for `Re s > 2`, extends holomorphically to `{Re s > 0} ∖ Z`,
where `Z` has no accumulation point in `Re s > 0` and each `ρ ∈ Z` is a simple pole with nonzero residue, real and
positive when `ρ` is real. Let `0 < θ < 1` and `δ > 0`. Suppose that for every `η > 0` there are arbitrarily large `U`
and a function `R ≥ 0` on `[U, (1+δ)U]` such that `R(u)e^{−θu}` is integrable there, `g ≤ R` there, and
`∫_U^{(1+δ)U} R(u)e^{−θu} du ≤ e^{ηU}`. This is a one-sided bound in mean, asked only along a sequence of windows, which
in `y` are `[Y, Y^{1+δ}]`. Then `Re ρ ≤ θ` for every `ρ ∈ Z`.

In the variable `y` the result is proved only for Dirichlet `L`-functions. Take finitely many characters `χ_i`, each of
modulus `1` or non-principal, and weights `a_i > 0`, and let `G = Σ a_i Re G_{χ_i}`, where `G_χ(y)` is
`Σ_{n≤y} Λ(n)χ(n)(y/n − 1)` minus its main term. If `G(y) ≤ Cy^θ` on `[Y, Y^{1+δ}]` for arbitrarily large `Y`, then no
`L(s, χ_i)` has a zero with real part greater than `θ`.

## Context

The statement is not a case of Landau's theorem but takes its place: it assumes meromorphy, concludes that there is no
pole, and asks for the bound only on windows. In the formal proof it turns one-sided bounds on windows `[Y, Y^λ]` for
weighted prime sums twisted by Dirichlet characters into zero-free half-planes for their `L`-functions, where Landau's
theorem in its form for Mellin integrals would need the bound for all large `y`. It is proved directly, from neither
Landau's theorem nor the theorem of Kaczorowski and Pintz.

## Comparison with the literature

We compared the literature with an earlier and more general description of the result: a transform meromorphic on
`Re s > θ` with poles of any order, growth controlled by integrability alone, the conclusion that there is no pole in
`Re s > θ` or that the poles there have a largest real part, attained at a real pole with negative leading
coefficient, and a form in `y` for any sum of coefficients. The statement here is a special case of that description
(simple poles, positive residues at real poles, a pointwise growth bound, and in `y` only Dirichlet characters), so
what we found covers it. The result is known in substance. Corollary 1 of Kaczorowski–Pintz I (1986) gives a sign
change of size `x^{Θ−ε}` in every `[Y^{1−ε}, Y]`, for poles of any order. Read contrapositively, it is the criterion
when `Θ`, the supremum of the real parts of the poles, is not itself a pole. A short reduction adds the bound in mean
and a real rightmost pole. We checked that reduction for simple poles. For poles of higher order the reduction is our
own, and we have not seen it in print. Kátai (1967) has an earlier one-sided window theorem. We think the result is at
most a routine variant of the 1986 theorem. We are less sure that the formulation we compared, with the dichotomy at a
real rightmost pole, is not in print, because we could not open Pintz (1980) or Kaczorowski (1984), read Radziejewski
(2014) only in abstract, and made only a brief search of the literature.

We have not searched the later literature systematically. Following citation lists, we found Kátai's paper and two
lemmas, for a single Dirichlet character, that allow real zeros. By Fiorilli–Martin (2023, Lemma 2.1), every window
`[X^{1−ε}, X]`, `X` large, contains a point where `Re ψ(x, χ)`, less `x` for the principal character, is below
`−x^{Θ_χ−ε}`. Hayani (2025, Lemma 3.1) proves the same for a smoothed sum and non-principal `χ`. From the first we derive
a criterion for the unsmoothed `ψ(x, χ)`, with a bound from below on such windows, and we conclude that the form of the
formal proof for Dirichlet `L`-functions is published in substance, apart from the bound in mean and the weighted
family. That comparison was made with the earlier description, and it does not deal with the smoothed sum `G_χ` of the
statement here, which is bounded from above. A second, independent check examined the argument and not the statement.
It could not confirm independently that Corollary 1 implies the form with a measurable function and a bound in mean.
Its view is given in B14. We would like to know whether the general statement, for a meromorphic transform with a real
rightmost pole or with the bound in mean, is in print, perhaps in Radziejewski (2014), and whether Pintz (1980) or
Kaczorowski (1984) state it.

## Sketch of the proof

The normalised derivatives `a_k(z) = (−1)^kΦ^{(k)}(z)/k!` are integrals of `g` against the Gamma kernels
`u^k e^{−zu}/k!`, which for `k` near `(1+δ/2)σU` concentrate on the window. There positivity bounds
`|a_k(σ+iγ)| + a_k(σ)` by `C′e^{ηU}(σ−θ)^{−k}`. A pole with real part `> θ` nearest to the centre `σ`, or strictly
nearest to a shifted centre `σ + iγ`, would by Cauchy's inequalities make `a_k` grow like `d^{−k−1}` with `d < σ − θ`
(at a real pole, through the sign of its residue), and the bound forbids this for small `η`. Neither Landau's theorem
nor Kaczorowski–Pintz is used. B14 gives the full account.

## Key steps

- **Positivity on a window** (`Deduction.landau_window_ineq`, `Deduction.landau_window_key`): the twisted coefficient
  plus the untwisted one is at most twice the integral of `g⁺` against the kernel, which the window hypothesis bounds.
- **A nearest pole** (`Deduction.landau_dichotomy`, `Deduction.landau_nearest`): either the pole nearest to `σ` is real,
  or a generic centre `σ + iγ` has a strictly nearest pole, alone in a disc.
- **Cauchy's inequalities** (`Deduction.landau_pole_asym`, `Deduction.landau_window_contra`): the principal part makes
  `a_k` grow faster than the window bound allows, at a real pole through `c > 0`.
