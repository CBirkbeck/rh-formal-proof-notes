## Statement

Let `E` be a CM field, abelian over `ℚ` and unramified over `E⁺`, containing `K = ℚ(√−d)` with `d ≡ 7 mod 16` prime,
and let `Φ` be the CM type induced from `K`. Let `Sh` be a unitary Shimura variety of signature `(p, q)` at every
`τ ∈ Φ`, and `N = p + q`. Let `I_c(B, F)` be the regularised arithmetic intersection number, per unit degree, of `F`,
of degree `D + 1` in the lines `x_{τ,i}` with Hodge metrics, where `D = |Φ|N(N−1)/2`. It is taken on a model of a cover
of the flag bundle of the component `c`, with Hodge–Tate lattices at the primes `≤ B` or dividing `2·disc(E)·level`
(the bad primes) and Lie lattices elsewhere. The statement is for every tower of such models tied to a model `Y` of
`Sh` by the source's conditions `GoodOrigin` and `Extra`, for `Y` and a level satisfying its condition `IsGlobal` (in
particular the level is good at every prime), and for every intersection system with the properties described in the
sketch below. `B₁` and the constant in `O(log B/B)` depend on the tower, and the existence of a tower is a separate
statement. Write `deg_Fl` and `ĥ_Fl` for the degree and the arithmetic intersection number on the flag variety of
`∏_τ GL_N` over `ℤ`, so that degrees on `c` are `vol(c)·deg_Fl`. Assume `q ≥ 1`, `3q + 1 ≤ N`, and that there are at
most `4|Φ|N²` bad primes.

(a) For `B ≥ B₁` there are reals `α_τ, β_τ` with
`Σ_c I_c(B, m) = (Σ_c vol(c))·(ĥ_Fl(m) − κ(m)) + O(log B/B)` on monomials `m`, where
`κ = Σ_τ(α_τ − β_τ)·S^p_τ + Σ_τ β_τ·S^N_τ`, `S^p_τ(F) = deg_Fl(Σ_{i<p} ∂F/∂x_{τ,i})`, and `S^N_τ` is the same sum over
all `i`.

(b) Let `u` be real, strictly decreasing in `i`, with `|u_{τ,a} − u_{τ,b}| ≥ g > 0` for `a ≥ p`, `b ≠ a`, and let `B`
be an integer with `B ≥ 128` and `B + 1 > 4|Φ|N²(1 + ‖u‖_∞/g)`. Then the mean over `c` of
`I_c(B, ℓ_u^{D+1})/((D+1)·vol(c)·deg_Fl(ℓ_u^D))` is `≥ −C·(Λ·|u|₁ + q·spread(u))` with `C` absolute. Here
`ℓ_u = −Σ u_{τ,i}x_{τ,i}`, `Λ = log(B·rd_E) + |(L′/L)(1, χ_d)|` with `rd_E` the root discriminant of `E` and `χ_d` the
quadratic character modulo `d`, and `spread(u) = Σ_τ(max_i − min_i)u_{τ,i}`.

(c) Suppose `|Φ| ≥ 2` and `u` integral. At the cut-off `B = 4|Φ|N²(1 + ‖u‖_∞/g)`, the mean over the components of the
volume slopes of integral cusp forms of weight `u` is `≥ −C·((log B + log rd_E + |(L′/L)(1, χ_d)|)·|u|₁ + q·spread(u))`,
the volumes being upper limits along the weights `8k·u`. This part is for a level good at every prime, given complex
uniformisation data for the components with the compatibilities the source asks for (`CompLaws`, `UnifAll`), and when
the coefficient field is algebraically closed in the function field of each component.

In the Lean source (a) and (b) are the conclusion of an implication from ten named statements, and (c) of an
implication from six. No declaration of the source gives (a) and (b) without hypotheses (see the caveats).

## Context

The statement bears on an arithmetic form of Hirzebruch–Mumford proportionality: whether the top-degree arithmetic
numbers of automorphic bundles on a non-compact Shimura variety are those of the compact dual up to explicit
corrections. Here the correction has two real constants for each place and each cut-off, which the statement does not
determine. Bounds for two averaged constants occur only inside the proof, and the numbers are limits on the models
above. The statement also bears on lower bounds, uniform in the field, for volumes of lattices of integral automorphic
forms.

## Comparison with the literature

We made the comparison with a wider description of the result: `p, q ≥ 1`, `N ≥ 3`, bounds for the two constants on the
Shimura variety, and a result we called unconditional. The statement here needs `3q + 1 ≤ N` and few bad primes for
every part, the proportionality included, does not export those bounds, and is an implication from named propositions,
so what we compared was a stronger statement. We regard it as not comparable with published formulas. The published
results are exact formulas in other settings: Bruinier–Howard for `GU(n−1,1)`, Guo for compact `U(n,1)` in preprints,
and Maillot–Rössler (2018) for abelian schemes. The published conjectures predict exact values in the non-compact case:
Maillot–Rössler (2002) for semi-abelian schemes with an action of the integers of a number field, which we take to
include the case here, and Köhler for the Siegel case. In the description we compared, the two constants were only
bounded. No Shimura variety is shared and the numbers here have not been identified with the published ones, so the
comparison is one of shapes. Among later work, in a search through citation lists that was only partial, we found an
arithmetic proportionality asked for by Hörmann (2011) and its function-field analogue proved by Feng, Yun and Zhang
(2026, preprint), and nothing for `q > 1` or uniform in the field. None of this changed our view.

An independent second check, made on the statement here, also finds it not comparable with published formulas, but
disagrees with us at two points. That the result is unconditional does not follow from the statement. Reading it as a
case of the Maillot–Rössler conjecture needs a comparison of intersection theories that has not been proved. Any
comparison must keep `3q + 1 ≤ N`, the averaging, the thresholds that depend on the tower and the auxiliary
hypotheses. Berman and Freixas i Montplet themselves distinguish their lattices from an intrinsic formulation with
integral cusp forms. We would like to know whether the two constants take the predicted values, and whether these
numbers are the standard arithmetic intersection numbers.

## Sketch of the proof

In the abstract layer, models are horizontal multihomogeneous prime ideals over `ℤ`, with metrised lines. An
intersection system is any real function of such data that is symmetric and additive in the lines and obeys the
recursion through the divisor of a section and the integral of its log-norm, a projection formula, and vanishing on
families pulled back from lower dimension (`IsFullIntersectionSystem`). A `RootTower` is a family of models `M_B(c)`
with lines `x_{τ,i}`, smooth metrics on them depending on a parameter `t`, an ample line and a set of bad primes, and
`I_c(B, ·)` is the limit `t → 0`. `chain_tower` derives (a) and (b) from properties of a tower, as follows.

(i) By `I8`, for a compact simple reflection `s`, `I_c(B, ℓ_{sλ}^{D+1}) − vol(c)·ĥ_Fl(ℓ_{sλ}^{D+1})` is minus its value
at `λ`. (ii) By `ThmW`, on a compact part of each non-compact simple wall, `|Σ_c I_c(B, ℓ_λ^{D+1})| ≤ C·log B/B`. In
`thmW_tower` a rational weight on the wall is moved off it by `θ = 5|Φ|/B`, `s = θ²` times an ample class is added, and
the metric is regularised at `t = exp(−1/s²)`. The line is then semipositive and nef on every fibre (dominant weights
are nef at the primes with Hodge–Tate lattices, and at another prime `ℓ` when `4|Φ|·‖μ‖_∞ ≤ (ℓ − 2)·gap`), so both
halves of Zhang's inequality of successive minima apply, with heights of points `≥ −C·log(B/s)` everywhere (`I5`) and
`≤ C·log B` on a dense set (`I6`), against a geometric degree `O(θ)`. Regularising costs `O(s)` (`I2`), and returning
to the wall costs `O(θ·log B)` (`I1`). (iii) For `ThmB`, on `ℓ_x^{D+1}` the difference `Σ_c I_c − vol_tot·ĥ_Fl`, with
`vol_tot = Σ_c vol(c)`, is a polynomial of degree `D + 1`, antisymmetric under the compact simple transpositions by (i)
and small on the walls by (ii), where `ĥ_Fl` vanishes. A quantitative lemma on such polynomials puts it within `C·ε`,
coefficient by coefficient, of the Vandermonde product times a linear form invariant under the compact Weyl group,
which is rewritten through `S^p_τ`, `S^N_τ`. (iv) `PropA`, a transitive group of permutations of `Φ` preserving
`Σ_c I_c`, lets `α_τ, β_τ` be replaced by their means. (v) By `Prop6`, for `B₀ ≤ B` the difference
`I_c(B₀, p_k(τ)·Q) − I_c(B, p_k(τ)·Q)`, with `p_k(τ) = Σ_i x_{τ,i}^k`, is `vol(c)` times an explicit sum over the good
primes in `(B₀, B]`. Letting `B → ∞`, two test classes form a triangular system, so the corrected coefficients
converge, and `Σ_c I_c(B₀, p_k(τ)·Q) = vol_tot·(ĥ_Fl − A₀·Σ_σ S^p_σ − b₀·Σ_σ S^N_σ + rest)` holds exactly at every
cut-off `B₀ ≥ 2` (`exists_identAt`). (vi) Take `Λ` as in (b) at `B₀`. The class `Σ_i x_{τ,i}` is a flat line of height
`O(N·Λ)` (`PropF`), so `|p·A₀ + N·b₀| ≤ C′·N·Λ` for `B₀ ≥ 128` with at most `B₀` bad primes, `C′` being the constant
of `PropF` (4 for the Shimura towers). A lower bound for the number of a power of the smaller Hodge determinant times
the fibre class (`SlopeLower`) and an upper bound for the height of a product of Grassmannians give `A₀ ≥ −C·Λ`.
(vii) In `tower_T2_of_inputs`, `ℓ_u^{D+1}` is decomposed along the `p_k(τ)` and the exact identity is applied term by
term. Then `ĥ_Fl ≥ 0` on dominant weights, the remainder is at most `2(D+1)·deg_Fl(ℓ_u^D)·q·spread(u)` by the
condition on `B`, and `A₀·s + b₀·t = A₀·(s − pt/N) + (p·A₀ + N·b₀)·t/N`. Equal volumes then give the mean.

In the Shimura layer, `Sh` is the moduli scheme of abelian schemes with `𝒪_E`-action for a self-dual Hermitian
`𝒪_E`-lattice and an odd principal level `M ≥ 3` split in `ℚ(√−d)`, good at every prime, and `Y` is a normal
projective flat model of it. `volumeTheoremX_of_inputs` proves the properties for every tower tied to `Y` by a
`GoodOrigin`. Its fields give a normal scheme over `Y` with rational `ℓ⁴`-torsion at the primes `ℓ ≤ B` and the bad
primes, on which the patched lattices are vector bundles, a full flag of them, and the Hodge norms of its graded lines.
The lines `x_{τ,i}`, `0 ≤ i < N`, are graded pieces for `Lie_τ` when `i < p`, and duals of those for `Lie_{cτ}` when
`i ≥ p`. Property (i) comes from a relation on the fibre square of each projective-line fibration that forgets one step
of the flag, used on the model and on the flag variety over `ℤ`, and from proportionality of geometric degrees. That
`E` is abelian over `ℚ` is an argument of almost all the statements about the Shimura towers that we read, and we did
not check whether their proofs use it. In (iv) the symmetry comes from automorphisms of the moduli scheme attached to
those of `E` (`TwistAut`). The condition `3q + 1 ≤ N` is used for `SlopeLower` (also, as `q ≤ p`, in the heights on the
base that enter it) and for the equality of the `vol(c)`, which is part of the conclusion. The bound on the number of
bad primes is used for heights on the base. That such a tower exists is a separate statement (`GoodTowerExistsX`). For
(c), `top_meanAll` takes `B = 4|Φ|N²(1 + ‖u‖_∞/g)` and bounds the volume slope on each component (the growth of the
number of integral cusp forms of weight `k·u` of bounded norm) below by the slope of `I_c(B, ℓ_u^{D+1})` minus
`O(log B + log rd_E)·|u|₁`. Small sections are counted by a volume inequality for semipositive lines nef on the fibres
and carried to integral forms.

All these theorems are implications from named statements. The general inputs are an intersection system, Zhang's two
inequalities, arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel and volume inequalities, Riemann–Roch for nef classes, resolution of
singularities, a Hodge–Tate comparison for `p`-divisible groups (`HT.FarguesHodgeTate`), and Zarhin's trick.
`good_tower_volume_theorem_bis` reduces (a) and (b) to ten of them. For each the source has a declaration without
hypotheses, or a derivation from such, which we did not read.

## Caveats

`GlobalTheory.T.volume_theorem_of_inputs` is a theorem with a proof, of an implication: ten named statements imply
`VolumeTheoremX`. Part (c) is an implication from six. No declaration of the source gives `VolumeTheoremX` without
hypotheses. The named statements are discharged elsewhere, in a chain we did not follow to the end. We read in full
`chain_tower`, `thmW_tower`, `mbarTop_squeeze`, `exists_identAt`, `antisymmetry`, `top_meanAll`, `tower_T2_of_inputs`,
`volumeTheoremX_of_inputs` and `antisymmetry_tower_theorem`, and in part `test_height_bound_slope_flat` and
`slope_sub_cost_le_compVolSlope`. Of the properties of the Shimura models (heights of points, nefness on fibres, change
of cut-off, label symmetry, flat line, base heights, existence of the tower) and of the general inputs we read only the
statements. A text search of all `.lean` files finds `sorry` only in `src/Challenge.lean` (the target statements) and
no `axiom` declaration. We compiled nothing.
