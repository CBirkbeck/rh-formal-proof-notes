## Statement

Let `E/F` be a CM extension with `E` Galois over `ℚ` and `E/F` unramified at all finite places. Assume that each inert
place of `F` has odd residue characteristic and residue cardinality `> 3`, and that the additive character `ψ` of the
adeles of `F` (trivial on `F`, equal to `exp(−2πi·Tr x)` at infinity) has conductor `𝒪_v` at these places.

Let `V`, `V′` be Hermitian spaces of dimensions `a > a′` with `a ≡ a′ (mod 2)` and signatures `(a − p, p)` and
`(q, a′ − q)` at each real place, and let `W` be a skew-Hermitian space of dimension `N = a + a′` and signature
`(N − p − q, p + q)`. Assume `2(p + q) + 1 < min((a − a′)/2, a′)`, that `V` and `V′` have unimodular lattices, and that
`W` has a lattice self-dual for the trace form. Let `α`, `β` be unitary Hecke characters of `E` equal to `η_{E/F}^{a′}`
and `η_{E/F}^a` on the ideles of `F`, with infinity types satisfying `m_α − m_β > N` at the real places, and unramified
outside a finite set `T` of split places at which `ψ` has conductor `𝒪_v` and where `ν = α/β` is ramified. Let `I_V(Φ)`
and `I_{V′}(Φ′)` be the integrals over `[U(V)]` and `[U(V′)]` of the theta functions formed with the splitting
characters `(β, β/α)` and `(α, α/β)`.

Then `I_V(Φ)` converges for every Schwartz function `Φ`, and `I_{V′}(Φ′)` converges for every regular `Φ′`, meaning of
rank `a′` at a fixed place `v₁ ∉ T` lying over a prime of `ℚ` split completely in `E`. Some regular `I_{V′}(Φ′)` is
nonzero. Matching data exist: at each place an irreducible `U(W)(F_v)`-module `Π_v` which is a quotient of both local
Weil representations. Each such datum has a unique constant `c`, which is nonzero, with `I_V(Φ) = c · I_{V′}(Φ′)` for
all matched pairs `(Φ, Φ′)` with `Φ′` regular. Two further clauses give the change of `c` on removing a plane.

In the Lean source the statement is the proposition `ThetaIntegralsAgree`, a definition and not a theorem, and the
existence of matching data is the proposition `LocalMatching`. The theorem `theoremK_head2` proves the statement,
together with `LocalMatching`, from two hypotheses, and these two are proved elsewhere in the source without
hypotheses.

## Context

Under these hypotheses the Witt index of `W` is at most `p + q < N/2`, so `W` is not split and `U(W)` has no Siegel
parabolic subgroup. The two theta lifts of the trivial representation therefore cannot be compared through a Siegel
Eisenstein series on `U(W)`. The statement bears on whether theta lifts from two different Hermitian spaces to a
unitary group that is not quasi-split give the same automorphic forms, and on the constant relating them.

## Comparison with the literature

We made the comparison with a differently phrased and partly stronger form of the statement. That form assumes an
isometry `δ⁻¹W ≅ V ⊕ V′(−1)`, where the Lean only prescribes the dimension, signature and lattice of `W`, and it adds
square-integrability, a unique invariant extension of `I_{V′}` and definite signs in the recursion, none of which the
Lean statement has. The point on which the comparison turns, that `W` is not split, holds for the statement here, so
the comparison with the published first term identities carries over to it. Those identities (Kudla–Rallis, Ichino,
Yamana, Gan–Qiu–Takeda, Lu) all need a completely polarised `W`, which is excluded here, and in that sense the
statement is not comparable with them. We read Kudla–Rallis and Ichino only through restatements. For `p + q ≤ 1` the
bare proportionality would follow from Chen–Zou, on the condition that the endoscopic classification for quasi-split
unitary groups holds and that the packets involved are multiplicity free. This is our own deduction, not a statement
of their paper, and we have not verified the identification it rests on. It would not give the convergence, the
nonvanishing or the constant. In later work we found no identity of this kind without a complete polarisation, and no
multiplicity formula for these parameters beyond `F`-rank one. We did find an example of Yasuda, for an inner form of
`Sp(2)`, where theta lifts from two spaces are not proportional. As we read the paper, its mechanism does not occur
over a field (see the reference below), and it did not change our view.

An independent second check agrees that the statement is not comparable with the direct analogues, but does not
exclude that it could be deduced indirectly from published results, and regards it as a specialised theorem of limited
reach. The normalisations, the signs in the recursion among them, are left unspecified, which weakens any claim to an
explicit constant. Proportionality is asserted only for regular second vectors, with no invariant extension.
Square-integrability is proved inside the argument, not stated. The absence of a Siegel parabolic on `U(W)` does not
by itself exclude the published doubling methods. We would like to know whether doubling or multiplicity arguments
already give the identity for regular second vectors, and whether the packet here is multiplicity free, so that
Chen–Zou settles the case `p + q ≤ 1`.

## References

- S. S. Kudla, S. Rallis, *A regularized Siegel-Weil formula: the first term identity*, Ann. of Math. 140 (1994), 1–80.
  For `Sp(n)` and `O(V)`, with `V₀` the complementary space in the Witt tower of `V` and the symplectic group split,
  the residue of the Siegel Eisenstein series attached to `V` is a nonzero constant times the regularised theta
  integral of `V₀`, and the leading term of the regularised theta integral of `V` factors through the representation
  attached to `V₀`. The proof rests on a uniqueness theorem for automorphic realisations. It is the same type of
  statement for another dual pair, and neither implies the other. (Not consulted in the original: we used Kudla's
  survey *Some extensions of the Siegel-Weil formula*, version of 2002, and the restatement by Gan, Qiu and Takeda.)
- A. Ichino, *A regularized Siegel-Weil formula for unitary groups*, Math. Z. 247 (2004), 241–277,
  <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00209-003-0580-5>. For the quasi-split `U(n, n)`, a residue of a Siegel
  Eisenstein series is a constant times the theta integral of the complementary space in the same Witt tower,
  regularised as its unique invariant extension. This is the unitary first term identity, with `W` split. (Abstract
  only, with the restatements by Wu, whose paper was seen only in summary in one of our two checks, and by Gan, Qiu
  and Takeda.)
- W. T. Gan, Y. Qiu, S. Takeda, *The regularized Siegel-Weil formula (the second term identity) and the Rallis inner
  product formula*, Invent. Math. 198 (2014), 739–831, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.4709>. Theorem 7.3(i), a recall
  credited among others to Kudla–Rallis, Ichino and Yamana, together with Theorem 8.1(i): the leading term of the
  regularised theta integral of the larger space is a constant, a ratio of measures, times the theta integral of the
  complementary one, for the classical dual pairs including the unitary ones, with `W` split by construction. It is
  the split counterpart of the identity here, and the two ranges are disjoint. Its constant-term induction and its use
  of doubling are precedents for the method used here. (Read in arXiv version 3; §§2, 7–8 and 11 were checked a second

- S. Yamana, *The Siegel–Weil formula for unitary groups*, Pacific J. Math. 264 (2013), 235–256,
  <https://msp.org/pjm/2013/264-1/p11.xhtml>. Theorem 2: for a skew-Hermitian space with a complete polarisation over
  a division algebra with an involution of the second kind, the Siegel Eisenstein series equals `2` or `1` times the
  (regularised) theta integral when `m ≤ n` or `m − r > n`. The groups are not quasi-split but have a parabolic of
  Siegel type, so a skew-Hermitian space over `E` of Witt index below half its dimension is not covered.
- H. Lu, *The Siegel-Weil formula for unitary groups: the second term range*, preprint, arXiv:1908.11222 (2019),
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.11222>. In Yamana's setting, with a complete polarisation, the first and second term

- R. Chen, J. Zou, *Arthur's multiplicity formula for even orthogonal and unitary groups*, J. Eur. Math. Soc. 27 (2025),

  inner form, and every elliptic `A`-parameter, the discrete spectrum is described by global theta packets, given the

  for `p + q ≤ 1` the bare proportionality would follow if the packets involved are multiplicity free, but not the

  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11954>. Theorem 0.1: for a cuspidal representation of `U(X)`, not assumed quasi-split,
  the maximal pole of an Eisenstein series for a non-Siegel maximal parabolic gives the lowest occurrence of its theta
  lifts. It treats cuspidal representations only, not the theta integral of the trivial representation on a non-split

- T. Yasuda, *CAP automorphic representations of inner forms of Sp(2)*, RIMS Kôkyûroku 1617 (2008), 42–50. For the

  different spaces of automorphic forms. As we read it, proportionality of theta lifts from two spaces to a
  non-quasi-split group is not automatic. The mechanism, a failure of the Hasse principle over the quaternion algebra,
  is absent over a field `E`, so there is no contradiction with the statement here.

## Sketch of the proof

`PositivityTheory.Sph.Tr.closeSph` assembles the proof from about 150 propositions. Two are hypotheses of
`theoremK_head2`, proved elsewhere in the source: the Siegel–Weil formula for Hermitian spaces definite at the real
places, in every rank (`SiegelWeilStd`), and the continuation with moderate growth of Siegel Eisenstein series on the
doubled group (`EisBL`). The other inputs are all theorems of the source: Siegel sets, Hecke L-functions, a filtration
of geometric-lemma type at split places, and duality for lattice vectors at inert places. No isometry
`W ≅ V ⊕ V′(−1)` is assumed. The three spaces are constrained only by their dimensions, signatures and lattices. The
infinity type `m` is `z ↦ (z/|z|)^m`, and the additive character is `exp(−2πi·Tr x)` at infinity.

For convergence (`PositivityTheory.ThKEst.kDecay`), `|θ|²` is bounded by a sum over the rational points of `V ⊗ W` (of
full rank, for regular vectors) of a Schwartz majorant moved by both groups, and this is estimated on Siegel sets. It
gives the convergence statements (`I_V` is in Weil's range, `N + 2p < 2a`) and the square-integrability on `[U(W)]` of
both integrals for the vectors used (`GlobalTheory.ThK.SqInt`, where `2(p + q) + 1 < a′` is used). The identity is
proved for polynomial Fock vectors at the real places and extended by continuity, which is why matched pairs are
defined as limits. For the factorisation (`PositivityTheory.ThKF.factor_of`), the `L²` pairing is an invariant positive
semidefinite form on the local coinvariants and vanishes on the kernel of the map to `Π_v`, so both integrals factor
through `⊗ Π_v`.

The proof is by induction on `p + q` (`PositivityTheory.ThKG.Leaves.const_of`), together with the nonvanishing of both
integrals. In the base case `p = q = 0` (`polZero_of`) all groups are compact at the real places. On the irreducible
module of matched pairs every invariant Hermitian pairing of combinations of `I_V`, `I_{V′}` is a multiple of
`⟨I_{V′}, I_{V′}⟩` (`hermLine_of`). This gives `c` such that `D = I_V(Φ) − c·I_{V′}(Φ′)` is orthogonal to all
`I_{V′}(Ψ)`, and `ϰ` with `⟨D, D⟩ = ϰ·⟨I_{V′}(Φ′), I_{V′}(Φ′)⟩`. The lift of `D` back to `U(V′)` against
`θ′(·, ·; Ψ)` is invariant under `U(V′)(F_v)` at the places outside `T` above rational primes split completely in `E`,
by local uniqueness, which uses `ν_v(ϖ_v) ≠ 1`, a consequence of `E` being Galois over `ℚ` and of the infinity type.
Hence the back-lift is constant (`constOfGood`, using the nonvanishing of Hecke L-functions at 1), and so equal to
`⟨D, I_{V′}(Ψ)⟩/vol = 0`. The doubling integral of the Siegel Eisenstein series against `φ ⊗ conj φ` unfolds, for large
real part, into matrix coefficients of `φ`. At the point `(a′ − N)/2` it is, by the Siegel–Weil formula for `V′`, a
constant times the squared norm of the back-lift of `φ` (`coefLine_of`). Comparing `φ = D` with `φ = I_{V′}(Φ′)` gives
`0 = ϰ·(nonzero)`, so `D = 0`. For the nonvanishing at the bottom (`nonZeroBottom_of_doubling_integral`), suppose
`I(Φ) = 0`. Then a doubling zeta integral of the matrix coefficient `⟨ω(g)Φ, Φ⟩` vanishes at its Siegel–Weil point.
For suitable `Φ` this integral is a product of Hecke L-values of `ν^{±1}` at integers, nonzero because
`m_α − m_β > N`, times nonzero local factors.

In the induction step a hyperbolic plane adapted to the lattices is removed from `V′` and `W` if `q ≥ 1`, and from `V`
and `W` if `q = 0 < p`, giving a triple `G₁`. The constant term of either integral along the isotropic line of `W` has
two parts, on which the torus acts by `α|t|^{a′/2}` and by `β|t|^{a/2}` (`PositivityTheory.ThKCT.ct_all`). Take
`q ≥ 1`. Up to nonzero constants, the `β`-part of `I_V(Φ)` is the theta integral for `(V, W₁)` of a restriction of
`Φ`, and that of `I_{V′}(Φ′)` is the theta integral for `(V′₁, W₁)` of a "cone" transform of `Φ′`, an integral of Tate
type over the ideles of `E` against `ν|·|^{ε₀+1}`, where `ε₀ = (a − a′)/2`. Matching descends (`locB_of`): if
`(Φ, Φ′)` is matched, then a multiple of the restriction of `Φ` and the cone transform of `Φ′` are matched for `G₁`.
By the induction hypothesis the theta integrals of `G₁` are injective on `⊗ Π_v`, so the two constant-term maps
induce maps `Π_v → Π_{1,v}` equivariant for the Levi subgroup. Local multiplicity-one statements make them
proportional place by place, and test vectors give the scalars (Euler factors outside `T`, Gauss sums at `T`). This
fixes `c` so that the `β`-parts of `I_V(Φ)` and `c·I_{V′}(Φ′)` agree (`keyB_of`). Then
`f = I_V(Φ) − c·I_{V′}(Φ′)` has a constant term of the single law `α|t|^{a′/2}`, and such differences vanish by
induction on `p` (`endB_of`, `sumB`). For `p = 0` the `α`-part vanishes too. A difference with zero constant term along
one line is cuspidal (`cuspOfLine_of_keys`). A cuspidal difference is orthogonal to every `I_V(Ψ)` and `I_{V′}(Ψ′)`,
again through constant back-lifts (`orthIso_fin`, `orthAn_of`), and since it is itself a difference of two such
square-integrable functions, it is zero (`perpZero_of`). For `p ≥ 1` the `α`-part restricts to a difference for a
smaller triple. The case `q = 0 < p` is symmetric, with the `α`-parts and `L^T(ε₀, ν)` (`locA_of`, `keyA_of`,
`endA_of`), and the recursion clauses record these constants.

  main ones being that the pairings form a line, that back-lifts are constant, and an inner product formula for

- **Constancy of back-lifts** (`GlobalTheory.ThK.Dbl.thConst_of`): `src/Development/WeilBound/ThetaLifts/LatticeBasics/ThConst.lean`, line 134.
  The lift to `U(V)` or `U(V′)` of a rapidly decreasing combination of the two integrals does not depend on `g`. This
  takes the place of a multiplicity-one statement for `U(W)`, and it is where `E` Galois over `ℚ` and the L-value at 1

  Analytic continuation of a doubling integral from the range where it unfolds to the point `(a′ − N)/2`, where the
  Siegel–Weil formula for the definite space `V′` applies. The Eisenstein series is on the doubled group, not on
  `U(W)`.
- **The constant from one part of the constant term** (`PositivityTheory.ThKG.keyB_of`): `src/Development/WeilBound/ThetaLifts/ConeHypotheses/KeyAOf.lean`, line 37.
  Combines the two constant-term formulas, the descent of matching and the induction hypothesis. The constant of the
  triple and the recursion factor are defined here (`keyA_of`, line 88 of the same file, does the other case).
- **Descent of matching** (`PositivityTheory.ThKG.Loc.locB_of`): `src/Development/WeilBound/ThetaLifts/LocalTheory/LocBOf.lean`, line 17.
  Matched pairs go to matched pairs under restriction and cone transform, up to the factor
  `C · ν(𝔟)^{±1} ν(𝔠)^{±1} · ∏_{v∈T} ν_v(±1) Nv^{f_v ε₀} ν_v(ϖ_v)^{f_v} G(ψ_v, ν_v) · L^T(ε₀ + 1, ν)`, where `𝔟`, `𝔠`
  are the ideals of the planes, `f_v` the conductor exponents, `G` Gauss sums, and `C ≠ 0` is independent of the
  characters. The signs are not specified.
- **Differences of one law vanish** (`PositivityTheory.ThKEnd.endB_of`): `src/Development/WeilBound/ThetaLifts/EigenvalueEndgame/TowerCodimensionTwo.lean`, line 188.
  With `zero_of_line` (line 73) and `sumB` (line 144) of the same file: cuspidality from one line, orthogonality to
  all theta integrals, then vanishing by square-integrability. This closes each induction step.

## Caveats

`ThetaIntegralsAgree` is the definition of a proposition. The theorem `theoremK_head2` proves it, together with
`LocalMatching`, from two hypotheses, which are themselves proved without hypotheses
(`PositivityTheory.SiegelWeilFormula.Fin.siegelWeilStd`, `PositivityTheory.RgEis.Hd.eisBL`). The one declaration we
found that applies the theorem to these two takes a further argument, three other statements, which it passes on and
never uses. We read the proofs of the assembly, the induction, the compact case, the comparison of constant terms, the
descent of matching and the end of the induction step. Of the convergence estimates, the constant-term formulas, the
local statements and the cuspidality and orthogonality lemmas we read only the statements. Of the existence of
matching data we read only the top statement `LocalMatching`, and we did not read the proofs of the inputs. A text
search found no `axiom` declaration, and `sorry` only in `src/Challenge.lean`. We compiled nothing, and we did not
check that the hypotheses can all be satisfied.
