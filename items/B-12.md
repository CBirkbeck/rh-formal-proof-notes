## Statement

Let `R` be either a complete discrete valuation ring of characteristic zero, or a complete noetherian normal local domain
of characteristic zero with a prime `I` such that `R` is `I`-adically complete and `R/I` is a principal ideal domain. Let
`G/R` be semi-abelian with a cubical symmetric line bundle `L`, such that the generic fibre is abelian, `L` is ample on
it with `K(L)` trivial, and `G mod I` is an extension of an abelian scheme by a split torus. In the first case, assume
that the cubical structure of `L` has an even trivialisation on the torus of the closed fibre. Outside the first case,
assume that some section of `L` vanishes identically on no fibre, and that `G` has endomorphisms `w`, `w′` with
`w + w′ = 1`, `L ≅ (1, w)^*Λ` and `(w × 1)^*Λ ≅ (1 × w′)^*Λ`, where `Λ = m^*L ⊗ p_1^*L^{−1} ⊗ p_2^*L^{−1} ⊗ 0^*L` on
`G × G`. Then `(G, L)` has a degeneration datum (`B`, `M`, a lattice `X`, `c : X → B(R)`, periods `ψ`) and a
uniformisation by it. Moreover, for the generic fibre `A`, every `k ≥ 1` and points in an algebraically closed field
`F ⊇ Frac R`, we have `A[k] ≅ Q_k`, with the Weil pairing, where `Q_k` is the group of classes of pairs `(y, g)`, with
`y ∈ X` and `g` an `F`-point of the Raynaud extension such that `g^k = ι(y)`, modulo `ι(X)`. This is a special case of
Faltings–Chai, Chapter II, with the torsion description of their III.7.3.

Conversely, consider the proper models of unitary Shimura varieties constructed in the source. Let `O′` be of
characteristic zero, either a field or a complete discrete valuation ring, and take arc data over `O′` satisfying the
source's conditions: an action of the ring of integers of the CM field, `ψ₀` even with invertible values, a positive
definite hermitian form `b` with a divisibility condition, torsion labels, and compatibility with signature and level.
Then the monomial periods `ψ₀(y)·X^{b(y,y)/2}` over `O′[[X]]` give a unique arc with these data.

Finally, over every complete noetherian normal local domain of characteristic zero, every degeneration datum comes from
a semi-abelian scheme. This is a case of Mumford's theorem, with principal polarisation and a split torus with constant
character group.

In the Lean source, seven of the declarations linked or named here are implications from named statements. The source
has declarations without hypotheses for all of these.

## Context

The formal proof produces degenerating abelian varieties with prescribed periods without Mumford's relatively complete
model and its quotient, and without a rigid-analytic quotient. According to references in the text, which we did not
confirm by a check of the dependencies, these families feed the boundary charts of the Siegel toroidal
compactification, which the source proves rather than assumes.

## Comparison with the literature

The forward direction is the standard proof, specialised: it is Faltings–Chai, Chapter II, in the principally
polarised, split, characteristic-zero case, where `ψ` can be read off the expansion of the one theta function. The
torsion description is organised differently, without the orthogonality theorem and without the dual abelian variety.
We hold this comparison with some reserve, because we read the Lean only at the level of its skeleton and know
Faltings–Chai only through later accounts and from memory.

The converse and the existence theorem are proved differently from the published proofs we compared. The abelian
variety is found as a point of a moduli space of theta points, starting from theta series, by an identity principle
through `ℂ` and a closure criterion, and its data are recognised afterwards on a proper model. No relatively complete
model and no quotient occurs. We are fairly sure that this is not the argument of Mumford, Faltings–Chai or
Raynaud–Bosch–Lütkebohmert as these are described in the sources we read. We are much less sure that no proof by theta
series is in print: of Chai's appendices we have read only the opening pages, we have not read Mumford's *Equations*,
and the introduction of Mumford (1972) records a theta-function proof of uniformisation over discrete valuation rings.
A second, independent check was undecided. It agrees that the forward method is known, but it regards the converse as
known in spirit through Mumford and Chai, and it calls Appendix III of Chai (1985) a substantial precedent for the
closure criterion, though it did not verify that the exact coset criterion is there. Neither our comparison nor the
second check shows that the argument is absent from the literature. We would like to know whether Chai's appendices or
Mumford's *Equations* III contain this construction.

## Sketch of the proof

Objects. A degeneration datum over `(R, I)` consists of an abelian scheme `B` with a symmetric rigidified ample `M`,
`K(M)` trivial, a free abelian group `X` of finite rank, `c : X → B(R)`, and trivialisations `ψ(χ)` of `c(χ)^*M^{−1}`
over `Frac R`, such that `ψ` is even, `τ(a, b) = ψ(a + b)ψ(a)^{−1}ψ(b)^{−1}` is bimultiplicative, `ψ(χ) ∈ I` for
`χ ≠ 0`, and `ψ(χ) ∈ I^n` for almost all `χ`. A uniformisation presents each `G mod I^{e+1}` as an extension of `B` by
the split torus (the Raynaud extension), and matches sections of `L^k` with families `(σ_α)` satisfying
`σ_{α+kμ} = ψ(μ)^k τ(μ, α) T^*_{c(μ)} σ_α`.

Forward direction over a discrete valuation ring. (1) Lift the torus and the abelian quotient of the closed fibre to
every thickening, the cubical bundle descending to the quotient. (2) Algebraise the quotients to `B` with ample `M`,
`K(M)` trivial. This gives the Raynaud extension and `c`. (3) Expand the theta section `θ` of `L`, which is unique up to
scalars, along the torus: `θ = Σ_α f_α`, with every `f_α ≠ 0`. (4) Each twisted bundle on `B` has a one-dimensional
space of sections, so `f_y` is a multiple of the translate of `f_0` by `c(y)`, and the multiplier is `ψ(y)`. Evenness,
bimultiplicativity of `τ`, finiteness and positivity follow from the symmetry of `θ`, the cubical structure and
convergence. (5) All degrees `k` follow, over the algebraic closure, from weight spaces and the irreducibility of the
theta group. (6) A class in `Q_k` acts on families of Fourier coefficients, and a unique `k`-torsion point induces that
operator. A count against `k^{2g}` gives a bijection, and the Weil pairing is the commutator pairing.

General base. Choose a complete discrete valuation ring `V ⊃ R` with `m_V ∩ R = I`, build the tower over `R`, compare it
with `V`, and descend `ψ` using the section that vanishes on no fibre.

Converse along an arc. (7) From the arc data, write formal theta series (theta constants and theta coordinates of
torsion points), which are integral and algebraic over `O′[[X]]`. (8) A polynomial vanishing on the theta points of
complex abelian varieties with this structure vanishes on the series. To see this, descend to a countable ring, embed it
in `ℂ`, and specialise the parameter to small values, where the series converge to classical theta values. (9) Over an
algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, a point of the closure of the theta points with a non-zero
coordinate in every coset of a suitable subgroup of the index lattice is a theta point. Properness is forced through a
semi-abelian model and the forward theory. (10) This gives a point of the moduli scheme over the fraction field of a
finite normal extension of `O′[[X]]`. (11) Its data on every complete discrete valuation ring are the prescribed ones.
Properness and normality of the model, with extension of homomorphisms, give the arc and its uniqueness.

Existence. Embed the datum as an idempotent factor of a datum of unitary type, by tensoring with a positive definite
unimodular lattice (`E₈`). After a finite extension, the proper models and the arcs give a proper modification of
`Spec R` with a semi-abelian scheme having the right data on every trait. Descend it to `R` and cut out the factor. No
relatively complete model and no quotient, formal or rigid, occurs. The argument uses semistable reduction, proper
models from Zarhin's trick, complex uniformisation and the forward theory.

## Key steps

- **Periods from one theta function** (`GlobalTheory.UU.exists_degData_fe_one`, the resulting degeneration datum with
  the functional equation in degree one): `ψ(y)` is the multiplier comparing the Fourier coefficient `f_y` with the
  translate of `f_0`, using that each twisted bundle has a one-dimensional space of sections. This is where the
  principal case simplifies the published argument.
- **Torsion and Weil pairing** (`GlobalTheory.TorsionIso.torsion_eq`): `A[k] ≅ Q_k` through operators on Fourier
  families and a count, with the pairing as a commutator pairing. No orthogonality theorem and no dual abelian variety
  are used.
- **General base** (`GlobalTheory.PrimeUnif.exists_unif`): reduction to one discrete valuation ring and descent of `ψ`.
  This needs two extra hypotheses, a section of `L` vanishing on no fibre and the endomorphisms `w`, `w′` of the
  Statement, from which the even trivialisation on the closed fibre is obtained.
- **Identity principle through ℂ** (`GlobalTheory.ArcIdentity.aeval_arcTx_eq_zero_of_vanishC`): algebraic relations
  among theta values of complex abelian varieties transfer to the formal theta series by specialising the parameter.
- **Closure criterion** (`GlobalTheory.Ex.ClosurePoint.exists_full_of_closure_of_cosets`): a point of the closure of
  the theta points with a non-zero coordinate in every coset is the theta point of an abelian variety. An independent
  second check points to Chai's 1985 appendices as the place to compare.
- **Existence from data** (`GlobalTheory.Mum.has_of_base`): every degeneration datum over a complete normal local domain
  of characteristic zero comes from a semi-abelian scheme, through a proper modification and descent.

## Caveats

We read the Lean statements and the top two or three levels of each proof, with the statements of the lemmas named
there but not their bodies. So we did not read the proofs of the tower and algebraisation, of the complex
specialisation, of the closure criterion or of the descent from the modification, nor how the complex abelian variety
with degenerate periods is obtained in step (8). A text search of the folders concerned found no `sorry` or `axiom`,
and nothing was compiled. Seven of the declarations linked or named here are implications from named statements. The
forward ones assume bundles of facts on abelian schemes, the general base assumes in addition algebraisation statements
and the discrete valuation ring case, and the three of the converse assume seven statements: two bundles of facts, the
discrete valuation ring case with its torsion description, extension of homomorphisms of semi-abelian schemes, two
statements on theta points, and complex uniformisation. The source has declarations without hypotheses for all of
these (`GlobalTheory.T.all_six_abelian_scheme_laws_hold`, `GlobalTheory.LN.ega_holds`,
`GlobalTheory.uniformisation_dvr_unconditional`, `GlobalTheory.arcFam_closed`). That the source's models satisfy the
converse along arcs is `GlobalTheory.partI_closed`, which rests on semistable reduction over `p`-adic fields, proved in
the source, and we did not read that proof. The source also has a general statement of Mumford's construction, but it
is used nowhere, and what is proved is narrower. We did not read at first hand Faltings–Chai, Mumford (1972) beyond its
introduction, Raynaud (1970), Bosch–Lütkebohmert, the book of Lan cited by Madapusi Pera, or SGA 7 IX, and the web
pages we used were read in summary form.
