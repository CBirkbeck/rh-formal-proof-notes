## Statement

Let `k ≥ 1`, `r ≥ 0`, `n = 2r + k + 1`, and `G = U(V) ≅ U(r + k, r + 1)` for the Hermitian space `V = ℂⁿ` made of `r`
hyperbolic planes and a block `W` of signature `(k, 1)`; fix `z ∈ W` of negative norm. The Bessel subgroup is
`S = N ⋊ U_z`, with `N` the unipotent radical of the stabiliser of an isotropic flag of length `r` and `U_z ≅ U(k)` the
compact pointwise stabiliser of the hyperbolic planes and of `z`; `ψ` is any generic character of `N` fixed by `U_z`.
Let `σ` be a discrete series of `G` whose Harish-Chandra parameter has entries of different sign at distance at least
`h ≥ 120·n³`, and `π` an irreducible representation of `U_z`. A Bessel map is a linear map `B` from the smooth vectors
of `σ` to the space of `π`, continuous for the smooth topology, with `B(σ(hm)v) = ψ(m)·π(h)·B(v)` for `h ∈ U_z`,
`m ∈ N`. The formal proof shows that any two Bessel maps are proportional; no interlacing condition between the
parameters of `σ` and `π` is needed. This is a special case (discrete series with the gap condition) of the case
`p = r + k`, `q = r + 1`, small group `U(k)`, of Theorem A of Jiang–Sun–Zhu on the uniqueness of Bessel models for real
unitary groups; the formal proof does not use that theorem.

## Why it might matter

The published uniqueness theorem for real unitary groups rests on the analysis of invariant generalized functions on
singular sets. For discrete series far from the non-compact walls, paired with representations of the compact `U(k)`,
the formal proof gets uniqueness by a softer argument, which also gives a convergent Bessel form of rank one (not a
consequence of uniqueness alone); the argument does not extend to general representations.

## What the checks found

The first reading finds a narrow special case of the published theorem, reached by an argument that differs from the
published proofs (Jiang–Sun–Zhu with Sun–Zhu) in its essential step: the rank-one property of the Bessel form comes from
the symmetry, under an anti-involution of transpose type, of an absolutely convergent pairing of coefficients on the
open double coset of the Bessel subgroup, in place of the reduction to corank one and the vanishing of invariant
generalized functions on singular sets. It needs coefficients decaying like `‖x‖^(−4n³)` and a compact small group, so
it cannot give the published theorem. Confidence medium: high that it differs, medium to low that it is not in the
literature, since six searches found no instance but the ingredients (Gelfand's trick, van Dijk's criterion, Schur
orthogonality, folding) are classical, and it adds that a specialist may regard the combination as known in spirit. The
second opinion calls it known in spirit, medium confidence, taking Hakim's criterion, where the symmetry need hold only
on a set of full measure, as a direct precedent; it has not found this real, vector-valued Bessel version and certifies
neither its originality nor its correctness. The two disagree in classification; neither establishes novelty. A
specialist could say whether the open-cell argument for integrable discrete series is in the literature on generalized
Gelfand pairs, and how far Hakim's criterion anticipates it.

## How the formal proof goes

Decay. For a discrete series with gap `h ≥ 120n³`, matrix coefficients of smooth vectors satisfy
`|⟨v, σ(x)w⟩| ≤ C·‖x‖^(−4n³)`. This rests on an explicit formula for the coefficients of the lowest `K`-type, proved
elsewhere in the formal proof and not re-examined. Coefficients are therefore integrable on `G`, and along `S` with room
to spare.

The Bessel form. `be(u, u′) = ∫ ⟨u′, σ(mh)u⟩·conj ψ(m)·π(h)^(−1) dh dm` over `U_z × N`, an endomorphism of the space of
`π`, converges absolutely, is equivariant in each variable, Hermitian and nonnegative, and satisfies a reproducing
formula coming from Schur orthogonality on `G`.

A pairing on the open cell. For functions `F, F′` on `G` with five decaying derivatives, the two-sided Bessel integral
`𝒥F` over `S × S` is defined at almost every point of the big cell of `S\G/S` (coordinates `(c, 𝔷) ∈ ℂ^r × ℂ`), and
`β(F, F′) = ∫ wt·⟨𝒥F, 𝒥F′⟩` is finite. For coefficient functions, unfolding and Schur orthogonality express `β` as a
constant times the Hilbert–Schmidt pairing of `be(b₁, b₂)^*·be(a₁, a₂)` with a test operator.

The anti-involution. `θ(g) = (ḡ)^(−1)`, for the real structure of a frame adapted to `z`, is an anti-automorphism of
order two of transpose type. It preserves `N`, `U_z`, the Haar measures and `ψ`, fixes a representative of each big-cell
double coset, and sends each `y ∈ U_z` to a conjugate of `y` by an element of the stabiliser in `U_z` of one more vector.
Hence `𝒥(f∘θ) = 𝒥(f)` almost everywhere on the big cell and `β(f∘θ, F′) = β(f, F′)`; the lower-dimensional double
cosets form a null set and are discarded.

Dichotomy and rank one. Either the coefficients of `σ∘θ` are orthogonal to those of `σ`, and then `be = 0`; or an
antiunitary `C` intertwines `σ∘θ` with `σ`, and likewise for `π` on `U_z`. In the second case the previous steps give
the crossing identity `be(b₁, b₂)^*·π(h)·be(a₁, a₂) = be(Ca₁, b₂)^*·π(θh)·be(Cb₁, a₂)`. Schur orthogonality on the
compact `U_z` turns it into a scalar identity `κ(A, B)·conj κ(E, F) = α(A, F)·γ(B, E)` for the matrix entries `κ` of
`be`, so all `2×2` minors of `κ` vanish, and a nonnegative Hermitian form with vanishing minors is `l ⊗ conj l`. Thus
`be(u, u′)` is the operator `ξ ↦ ⟨l(u′), ξ⟩·l(u)` for an `S`-equivariant linear map `l`, possibly `0`, defined on the
vectors whose coefficient against a fixed unit vector `w` decays like `‖x‖^(−4n³)`.

A priori bound. Any Bessel map `B` of any unitary representation is bounded on the orbit `G·w`: write
`g = h·m·(Γ·diag(d)·R)` by the Iwasawa, Levi and Cartan decompositions; if a ratio `M` of consecutive torus entries
exceeds `1`, a bounded root vector `Y` gives `B(σ(g·exp(τY))u) = exp(iMτ)·B(σ(g)u)`, and differentiating in `τ` trades
powers of `M` for derivatives of `u`.

Folding and conclusion. Apply a bounded `B` to the reproducing formula `w = d·∫ conj⟨w, σ(y)w⟩·σ(y)w dy`, passing `B`
under the integral by truncation, and fold the integral over `G` along `S` with a cut-off function. The inner integral
is `be = l ⊗ conj l`, so `B(σ(x)w) = c·l(σ(x)w)`. A general generic character is conjugated to the standard one by a
torus element commuting with `U_z`; `w` is cyclic for the smooth topology and `B` is continuous, so two Bessel maps that
are proportional on `G·w` are proportional.

## Key steps

- **Decay from the gap** (`CompanionTheory.FamilyDerivativeExpansion.DecS.decayM`): coefficients of smooth vectors
  decay like `‖x‖^(−m)` when `thr*(ε) + m < h`, with `thr*(ε) ≤ 40n³`; it is used with `m = 4n³`. This is where the gap
  hypothesis enters.
- **Symmetry on the open cell** (`ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.DS.Mult1.stSymBeta`, with `stSym`): the pairing
  `β` is invariant under the anti-involution `θ`, as an almost-everywhere identity over the big cell. This is the step
  in which the argument departs from the published proofs.
- **Vanishing minors** (`ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.DS.Mult1.TopH.sep_minors`, with `minors_DD`,
  `minorsOn_of_DD`, `multOne_of_minors`): the crossing identity and Schur orthogonality on `U(k)` force all `2×2` minors
  of the Bessel form to vanish; the rank-one statement `ThmM` is assembled in `thmM`.
- **A priori bound** (`CompanionTheory.Bmu.bounded_of`): a Bessel map of any unitary representation is bounded on the
  orbit of a smooth vector, by differentiating along root vectors.
- **Folding** (`ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.DS.Mult1.stMFix`, `CompanionTheory.Bmu.orbit_mult`): the reproducing
  formula folded along `S` shows that a bounded Bessel map is a multiple of `l` on `G·w`.
- **Conclusion** (`CompanionTheory.Bmu.uniq_of`): conjugation of the character to the standard one, cyclicity of `w`
  and continuity give proportionality of any two Bessel maps.
