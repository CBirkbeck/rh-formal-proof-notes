## Statement

These are four narrower forms of published theorems.

(1) Let `K` be imaginary quadratic of discriminant `−d` with `d > 4`, class number `h` and Kronecker character `χ`, and
for each ideal class `C` let `τ_C` be such that `ℤτ_C + ℤ` is homothetic to an ideal of the class `C`. Then
`(1/h)·Σ_C log(4π·Im τ_C·|η(τ_C)|⁴) = γ + log(2π/√d) − L′(1, χ)/L(1, χ)`, together with `ζ_K(s) = ζ(s)·L(s, χ)` for
`Re s > 1` and `L(1, χ) = πh/√d`. This is the Chowla–Selberg formula without Lerch's evaluation by values of `Γ`.

(2) The main theorem of complex multiplication for elliptic curves holds in idelic form, for ideals of the maximal
order. It is proved in the source, not assumed.

(3) Let `E ⊃ K` be a CM field, unramified over its maximal real subfield `F` at finite places, with CM type induced from
`K`. Then the normalised Hecke lattice sums of `E`, for the lattices and weights of the source, are algebraic. Moreover,
for a finite idele `t` of `K`, an automorphism of `ℂ` fixing `K` and acting on `K^ab` as `art_K(t)⁻¹` carries the sum
for a lattice to `|t|^{−|κ|}` times the sum for that lattice moved by `t⁻¹`, with the weights `κ` permuted as the
automorphism permutes the CM type, and with a second period. This stands where an instance of Shimura's reciprocity law
would be cited.

(4) Let `F` be totally real, let the conductor be `(ℓⁿ)`, let `ε` be rational-valued and `ℓ`-integral on narrow ray
classes, and let `𝔠` be prime to `ℓ`. Then `L(ε, 1−k) − N(𝔠)^k·L(ε_𝔠, 1−k)` is `ℓ`-integral for `k ≥ 1`. This is a
case of the Deligne–Ribet theorem. With it comes the Siegel–Klingen rationality of `L(ε, 1−k)`.

In the Lean source the conjugation theorems of (3) are implications from the statements of four published theorems,
and, for the ratios, from the statement for twisted sums. Each of these published statements has a declaration without
hypotheses elsewhere in the source, but we found no declaration that states the algebraicity or the conjugation rules
without these hypotheses.

## Context

In the formal proof these results supply the period, and the denominators and Galois conjugates of values at CM points,
that enter the slopes of lattices of integral forms. The main theorem of complex multiplication for elliptic curves is
proved in the source, not assumed. The one arrangement we did not find in the published work we were able to open is
the passage from lattice sums of `E ⊋ K` to elliptic modular forms by restricting a Hilbert–Eisenstein series to the
diagonal.

## Comparison with the literature

All four are proved by the classical arguments, and each gives a statement narrower than the published one. For the
main theorem the only narrowing is that it is for the maximal order. The class mean comes from Kronecker's limit
formula. The main theorem is proved by reduction at primes of degree one and Frobenius. The conjugation of lattice
sums follows the chain of Damerell, Weil and Goldstein–Schappacher: the main theorem, Weierstrass data at division
points, and an isogeny formula for the non-holomorphic constant. Along the way it reproves the instance of Shimura's
reciprocity law that it needs, by the mechanism from which that law is classically derived. The `ℓ`-integrality comes
from Shintani's cone method, the known alternative, due to Cassou-Noguès and Barsky, to the proof of Deligne and
Ribet. Overall we hold this comparison with some reserve. We are surest of it for the Chowla–Selberg and Deligne–Ribet
parts, where we read the Lean at top level and the routes are confirmed by Gross and by Charollois–Dasgupta. That the
main theorem is proved we say after reading its top-level proof and the statements of the lemmas it calls, and after a
search of its 98 files that found no `sorry` or `axiom`. That its proof is the textbook one we say only from memory.
We are less sure for the conjugation, because we could not open its original sources.

The diagonal restriction from `E` to `K` differs from the two published reductions to `K` that we found, those of Colmez
and of Bergeron–Charollois–García. We see it as a specialisation of the Shimura–Katz method, not a new proof, but we have
not established whether it is in print. Only one check was made on this item. We would like to know whether Goldstein–Schappacher's proof is the chain above, and whether the diagonal reduction is in
Katz (1978) or Colmez (1989).

## Sketch of the proof

Class mean. For `s > 1`, with `d > 4` so that the units are `±1`, sorting ideals by class writes `ζ_K(s)` as
`½·(2/√d)^s` times the sum over the classes of the real-analytic Eisenstein series `Σ′ (Im τ_C)^s |mτ_C + n|^{−2s}`.
Kronecker's first limit formula is proved row by row. For each `m ≠ 0` the sum over `n` is compared with
`∫(t² + m²y²)^{−s} dt`, with error at most the value at `0`. The integrals add up to a multiple of `ζ(2s − 1)`, which
carries the pole and `γ`. At `s = 1` the row errors are evaluated by the partial fractions of the cotangent and sum to
`log|∏(1 − qⁿ)|`, and dominated convergence passes to the limit. With `ζ_K = ζ·L(χ)`, comparing the two expansions at
`s = 1` to first order gives `L(1, χ)` and the class mean. The form actually used is a mean over the embeddings of a
number field. One takes one Weierstrass curve of unit discriminant per class. Isogenies between these curves have
multipliers integral in both directions, so the product formula makes the mean over embeddings the same for each curve,
and a double count gives the class mean. We read this last lemma by its statement only.

Main theorem. For each level `m`, take a finite Galois `L/K` containing the `j`-values, the `m`-torsion of a model, the
ray class field modulo `m` and a twelfth root of unity. At almost all primes `𝔭` of degree one, the natural isogeny from
the curve of `𝔞` to that of `𝔭⁻¹𝔞` reduces to Frobenius. One chooses a prime whose Frobenius is `σ` on `L`, and writes
the idele as a principal idele times a unit congruent to `1` modulo `m` times the idele of `𝔭`. The levels
`m = (n+1)!` are glued by a pigeonhole argument over the finite unit group.

Conjugation. (i) The main theorem applied to the Weierstrass model of `ℂ/𝒪_K` transports `g₂`, `g₃` and `℘`, `℘′` at
torsion points, each divided by the matching power of the period, to the same data for `t⁻¹𝒪_K` at `t⁻¹x`. (ii) An
isogeny formula for multiplication by `√−d` expresses `G₂^* = G₂ − π/Im τ` through values of `℘` on its kernel, so
`G₂^*` is transported too. (iii) Writing elements of `E` in coordinates over `F` turns a lattice of `E` into a lattice
in `F²` of some level `N`, and the lattice sum into an antiholomorphic derivative of a Hilbert–Eisenstein series at the
point all of whose coordinates equal the CM point of `K`. This point is on the diagonal because the CM type is induced
from `K`. (iv) The antiholomorphic direction is traded for a holomorphic one built from `G₂ + ℘(t_u)` at a torsion point
`t_u`. The derivative becomes a finite sum, with integer constants, of powers of `(G₂^* + ℘(t_u))/ω²` times values of
elliptic modular forms `h_μ`. The twists of each `h_μ`, indexed by `GL₂(ℤ/N)`, are permuted by `SL₂(ℤ)`, and their
expansions are read off from the Fourier expansion of the Hilbert–Eisenstein series, whose constant term is rational by
Siegel–Klingen. (v) Symmetric functions of such a family are level-one forms, so the values divided by powers of `ω` are
algebraic. Mixed power sums are transported, which gives a permutation of the family, and `℘`, `℘′` at two basis points
of the `N`-torsion separate `GL₂(ℤ/N)` and fix that permutation. (vi) Summing over classes and reindexing gives the rule
for twisted sums. The periods cancel in the ratio, and the base sum is a Hecke `L`-value, which does not vanish.

Integrality. Narrow ray classes are parametrised by lattice points of finitely many simplicial cones. Weights `ℓ′ − 1`
on ideals divisible by a prime `𝔠` of prime norm `ℓ′` and `−1` on the others are periodic with zero sum on each cone,
so summation by parts continues the cone series and bounds the denominators at `1 − k`. A general `𝔠` prime to `ℓ` is
replaced by a degree-one prime in its ray class modulo a high power of `ℓ`. Siegel–Klingen rationality is proved by the
same method. This is the case of the Deligne–Ribet theorem with one weight: there are no congruences between different
`k` and no `p`-adic measure.

## Key steps

- **Kronecker's limit formula** (`GlobalTheory.tendsto_realEisenstein_sub_pole`): proved as a limit `s → 1⁺` by
  comparing each row with an integral, the cotangent at `s = 1` and dominated convergence. It is combined with
  `ζ_K = ζ·L(χ)` in `GlobalTheory.chowla_selberg`.
- **Main theorem of complex multiplication** (`GroundTheory.Global.Elliptic.main_theorem_complexMultiplication`): at
  each level, the isogeny attached to a prime of degree one reduces to Frobenius, and the levels `(n+1)!` are glued.
- **Transport of Weierstrass data** (`GlobalTheory.LatVal.periodEx_of_mainTheorem`): the main theorem gives the action
  of `g` on `g₂`, `g₃`, `℘` and `℘′` at torsion points, normalised by the period.
- **Diagonal restriction** (`GlobalTheory.T.hilbertEisenstein_value_derivatives_via_jets`,
  `GlobalTheory.T.weierstrass_eisenstein_nonholomorphic_identity`): the lattice sum of `E` becomes a finite integral
  combination of holomorphic derivatives of a Hilbert–Eisenstein series at the diagonal CM point, with
  `G₂^* + ℘(t_u)`.
- **Galois action on level-`N` families** (`GlobalTheory.T.transport_modularFamily_GL2ZMod_weierstrass`,
  `GlobalTheory.QFam.fam_value_isAlgebraic`): level-one symmetric functions give algebraicity and the transport of
  power sums, and division values of `℘`, `℘′` fix the permutation. This is the reproved instance of the reciprocity
  law.
- **Smoothed zeta values** (`PositivityTheory.PfDRL.deligneRibet`, `GlobalTheory.T.ray_class_partial_zeta_integrality`):
  Shintani cones, smoothing at a prime of prime norm, and summation by parts.
