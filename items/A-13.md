## Statement

The Lean statement is about linear data, not about an abelian scheme. Let `p` be an odd prime, `C` an algebraically
closed field of characteristic `0` with a real-valued valuation, `0 < |p| < 1`, `R` its valuation ring (a `ℤ_p`-algebra),
`c_p ∈ R` with `|c_p|^{p−1} = |p|`. Data: a commutative `ℤ_p`-algebra `O` with an involution `σ`, acting on `T = ℤ_p^{2g}`
and on `Lie = R^g`, hence on `ω = Hom(Lie, R)`; a unimodular alternating form `E` on `T` with `E(ax, y) = E(x, σ(a)y)`;
`R`-linear maps `𝔥 : T ⊗ R → ω`, `𝔟 : T ⊗ R → Lie` with `𝔥(ax) = σ(a)𝔥(x)` and `⟨𝔟x, 𝔥y⟩ − ⟨𝔟y, 𝔥x⟩ = c_p·E(x, y)`; a Hopf
order `𝒜`: an `R`-lattice of `R`-valued functions on `T/pT` containing `1`, stable under products, `f ↦ f(−x)` and `O`,
with `f(x + y) ∈ 𝒜 ⊗ 𝒜`, with an `O`-equivariant isomorphism `I/I² ≅ ω/pω` (`I`: the functions of `𝒜` vanishing at `0`),
containing functions congruent modulo `p` to each coordinate of `𝔥` and `𝔟`; and two projectors, taken to be zero. Assume
that idempotents split `T ⊗ R` and `Lie` into parts where `O` acts through ring maps `τ_1, …, τ_m : O → R`, pairwise
distinct modulo the maximal ideal `𝔪` and permuted by `τ ↦ τ∘σ` and by a `φ` with `φτ ≡ τ^p` modulo `𝔪`, with `Lie_τ` free
of rank `r(τ)`. Put `ω_τ = Hom(Lie_τ, R)`, and `|p|^{x(τ)} = ∏_i |c_i|` if `ω_τ/𝔥((T ⊗ R)_{τ∘σ}) ≅ ⊕_i R/(c_i)`. Then:

`x(τ) − x(τ∘σ) = r(τ)/(p − 1) − Σ_{n≥0} p^{−(n+1)}·r(φ^{n+1}τ)` for every `τ`.

## Why it might matter

The data are modelled on a principally polarised abelian scheme over `R` with an action of the integers `𝒪` of a
number field unramified at `p`, an involution of `𝒪` being adjoint for the polarisation: `T` the Tate module,
`O = 𝒪 ⊗ ℤ_p`, `𝔥` the Hodge–Tate map composed with the polarisation, `𝒜` the Hopf algebra of the `p`-torsion. No
construction of the data from such a scheme was found in the source, so the relation is proved for the linear data
only. In the geometric case it would say that the degrees of the Hodge–Tate cokernel at `τ` and at `τ∘σ` differ by a
quantity depending only on the ranks `r(τ)`.

## How the formal proof goes

The proof is linear algebra over `R` and algebra in `𝒜`; no geometry enters. Let `v` be the valuation with `v(p) = 1`
and `e_τ` the idempotents. Degrees of finitely presented torsion `R`-modules rest on a Smith normal form over the
valuation ring, proved in the source (`Smith.smith_gen`), with existence (`lenEx`), independence of the presentation
(`hasLen_unique`) and additivity in exact sequences (`hasLen_ext`).

(1) An equivariant second row (`u1`). As the `τ` are distinct modulo `𝔪`, `E` pairs `(T ⊗ R)_τ` with `(T ⊗ R)_{τ∘σ}`
only and `𝔥` maps `(T ⊗ R)_τ` into `ω_{τ∘σ}` (`εT_E`, `ht_εT`). Replace `𝔟` by `𝔟′ = Σ_τ e_τ𝔟e_τ` (`bt2`): the relation
with `c_p·E` persists, `𝔟′` commutes with the idempotents, and the matrix `Π′` with rows `𝔥`, `𝔟′` has
`(det Π′)² = c_p^{2g}·det E`, so `v(det Π′) = g/(p − 1)`.

(2) Auxiliary colengths. `Π′` maps `(T ⊗ R)_τ` into `ω_{τ∘σ} ⊕ Lie_τ`; let `ξ(τ)` be the degree of the cokernel
(`xModE`). As `τ` varies these blocks decompose the source and the target of `Π′`, so `Σ_τ ξ(τ) = v(det Π′) = g/(p − 1)`
(`eigenLengthProductStatement_holds`).

(3) Duality (`lenId`). Projection to `ω_{τ∘σ}` presents that cokernel as an extension of `ω_{τ∘σ}/𝔥((T ⊗ R)_τ)`, of
degree `x(τ∘σ)`, by `Lie_τ/N_τ`, where `N_τ` is the set of `y` with `(0, y)` in the image. By the relation with `c_p·E`,
`y ∈ N_τ` exactly when `c_p` divides `𝔥ᵀy`, and `c_p` kills `ω_τ/𝔥((T ⊗ R)_{τ∘σ})` (`exists_fromRows_mulVec_iff`,
`cok_kill`). For a matrix `A` with `d` rows whose image contains `c_p·R^d`, the lattice of `y` with `c_p` dividing `Aᵀy`
has colength `d·v(c_p)` minus that of the image of `A` (`dualLen`). Hence `ξ(τ) = r(τ)/(p − 1) − x(τ) + x(τ∘σ)`, and
the theorem becomes `ξ(τ) = Σ_{n≥0} p^{−(n+1)}·r(φ^{n+1}τ)`.

(4) Lifts in the Hopf order. A Smith basis adapted to the blocks (`elementary_divisor_statement`) gives a unimodular
matrix `Λ` of linear forms on `T ⊗ R`, the row `Λ_j` living on one block `κ(j)`, and `ε_j ∈ R` dividing `c_p`, such that
the `ε_jΛ_j` with `κ(j) = τ` span the rows of `Π′e_τ` and `Σ_{κ(j)=τ} v(ε_j) = ξ(τ)`. Since the coordinates of `𝔥` and
`𝔟` are congruent modulo `p` to elements of `𝒜`, so is each `ε_jΛ_j`. The source builds a finite abelian group `G` of
order prime to `p`, acting on `T` through elements of `O`, on which the `τ` are nontrivial characters, pairwise distinct
modulo `𝔪`, with `φτ = τ^p` exactly (`embeddingSeparatingGroup`). Averaging over `G` against `κ(j)` gives
`x_j = ε_ju_j ∈ I` on which `G` acts through `κ(j)`, with `u_j ≡ Λ_j` modulo `𝔪` (`lift_one`). Here `p ≠ 2` is used
(`v(ε_j) ≤ 1/(p − 1) < 1`, so `p/ε_j ∈ 𝔪`), as it is in the construction of `G`.

(5) Monomial basis (`monomials_span_of_prod_eq`). The `p^{2g}` monomials `x^α`, `0 ≤ α_j < p`, lie in `𝒜`, and the
determinant of their values has valuation `(p^{2g}(p − 1)/2)·Σ_j v(ε_j) = g·p^{2g}/2` (`u5`: modulo `𝔪` the `u^α` are
the monomials in a basis of linear forms on `𝔽_p^{2g}`, a basis of all functions). For a Hopf order on an elementary
abelian `p`-group `N`, twice its colength in the `R`-valued functions is at least `|N|` times the degree of `I/I²`
(`u4`, by induction on `|N|` from the case of order `p`, `u3`); here this is `g·p^{2g}`. So the monomials are an
`R`-basis of `𝒜`, and the `x_j` generate `I` modulo `I²`.

(6) Frobenius inequality (`frob_of`). Expand `x_j^p`, `κ(j) = τ`, in the monomial basis. `G` acts on it through `φτ`, so
the coefficient of `x_l` vanishes unless `κ(l) = φτ` (`bR_off`); then it is `b_{jl}·ε_j^p/ε_l`, where `b_{jl} ∈ R`
reduces modulo `𝔪` to the matrix writing the `p`-th powers of the residual forms `Λ̄_j` in the `Λ̄_l`, invertible block
by block (`block_det_ne`; the blocks `τ` and `φτ` have equal size, `rank_eq_rank_of_perm_and_zero`). So this square
matrix `B_τ` has `v(det B_τ) = p·ξ(τ) − ξ(φτ)` (`frobeniusBlock`). Its rows are linear parts of elements of `I²`, as
`x_j^p ∈ I²`. The map sending coefficients `c` on the block `φτ` to the class of `Σ c_lx_l` in `I/I² ≅ ω/pω`, projected
to `ω_{φτ}/p ≅ (R/p)^{r(φτ)}`, vanishes on such rows and is onto, because the `x_l` with `κ(l) ≠ φτ` have zero component
there (`phi_x_grade`, from the equivariance of the isomorphism). So `p·ξ(τ) − ξ(φτ) ≥ r(φτ)` (`u7F3`).

(7) Equality (`unramifiedCokernelLength_of_length_eigenLengthProduct`). Summing over `τ` gives
`(p − 1)·Σ_τ ξ(τ) ≥ Σ_τ r(τ) = g`, and the left side equals `g` by (2). So every inequality in (6) is an equality,
`ξ(τ) = (ξ(φτ) + r(φτ))/p`, and iteration gives the series.

No published theorem enters as an unproved input: the Smith normal form, the facts `u3`, `u4` on Hopf orders and the
determinant `u5` are proved in the same directory, on top of Mathlib. That `C` is algebraically closed is used in `u3`
(a primitive `p`-th root of unity).
