## Statement

Let `M/K` be a Galois extension of number fields of prime degree `l`, `K` totally complex, `n ≥ 1`; a norm character is
a non-trivial Hecke character `η` of `K` with `η^l = 1`, trivial on norms at almost all places. Every cuspidal `π` of
`GL_n(𝔸_K)` either satisfies `π ⊗ η ≅ π` for a norm character or lifts to a cuspidal `P` of `GL_n(𝔸_M)`: Satake
parameters the `f`-th powers of those of `π` at almost all places (`f` the residue degree), the same infinitesimal
characters, `P_w ≅ π_v` at every split place. Every Galois-invariant cuspidal `P` is such a lift; the other `π` with the
same lift are the twists by norm characters. This is a special case of Arthur–Clozel's theorem (totally complex fields
only), weaker at non-split places: there only, for the components `τ = π_v`, `τ' = P_w`, any intertwining operator `I`
and matching `(φ, f)` with `f` supported where the determinant is a local norm (the first reading's reading of the
condition), `tr(τ'(φ)∘I) = c·tr τ(f)` with a constant `c ≠ 0` that does not depend on `(φ, f)`. Also proved: for a
regular algebraic cuspidal `π` of `GL_2` over a totally real field `F` and a totally complex quadratic extension `E`,
either `π ≅ π ⊗ η_{E/F}` or `π` has a cuspidal base change to `E`; and, under conditions, induction from `GL_2` to
`GL_4` for a quadratic extension of totally complex fields.

## Why it might matter

The formal proof uses cyclic base change of prime degree, with patching, to reach every CM field in its construction of
Galois representations (B01); descent and the description of the fibres are proved with it. Arthur–Clozel compare full
trace formulas, with the fundamental lemma for the spherical Hecke algebra and local base change for tempered
representations; the formal proof compares regular elliptic terms only, matches the unit and restricted spherical
functions, and proves no local base change map.

## What the checks found

The first reading: a route that is not Arthur–Clozel's, for a narrower statement. It differs from the published proofs
it could consult on the spectral side: Arthur–Clozel and Labesse compare full trace formulas, Flicker's simple proof
assumes a supercuspidal component; here all cuspidal representations are kept, the non-cuspidal spectrum is removed
approximately by multipliers, and exact identities return through exponential growth in the power of an Iwahori
operator. The geometric side and the substitute for the fundamental lemma are a known alternative (Flicker–Kazhdan,
Flicker); multipliers killing the continuous spectrum are published, but not, as found, for base change; `GL_2` by theta
lifting is a known alternative (thin evidence). Medium confidence: high that the route is not Arthur–Clozel's, low to
medium that it is absent from print (abstracts, later accounts, one search). The second opinion cannot tell (medium
confidence): Labesse (1995) already combines regular functions with multipliers, Flicker (1988) proves full `GL_2` base
change by an asymptotic trace formula, Beuzart-Plessis–Liu–Zhang–Zhu obtain weak base change for unitary groups,
Cognet's theorem is local; what may be substantial is uniform error control as supports and norms grow, not examined.
The opinions disagree. Open: is the argument in Labesse (1995) or Flicker (1988)? Do its uniform estimates go beyond
routine?

## How the formal proof goes

Test functions. On `GL_n(𝔸_M)¹` and `GL_n(𝔸_K)¹` take products: at infinity (all places complex and split, so matching
is convolution) a smooth compactly supported function and its translates by the centre of the enveloping algebra; at
one auxiliary split finite place `q` the indicator of the Iwahori double coset of `ϖ^{jν}`, with `ν` strictly
decreasing, of sum `0` and no proper sub-sum `0`, and `j → ∞`; spherical functions at finitely many unramified places;
a fixed matching pair at the bad places.

Geometric side. By the product formula, for `j` large every rational `δ` contributing to the twisted kernel has norm
with irreducible characteristic polynomial; the kernel on the diagonal vanishes above a height `e^{C₀j}`, and for
matching functions the integrals of the two kernels agree up to the product of local constants. No weighted orbital
integral, no unipotent term, no truncation operator.

Local matching. At unramified inert places: the unit element (Kottwitz's lemma, proved by descent of `σ`-stable lattices
and surjectivity of norms on units); for other spherical functions, not the fundamental lemma for the Hecke algebra but,
for each Satake parameter `t` and each degree bound, a polynomial `γ` with `γ(t) ≠ 0` such that, for every polynomial
`p` within the bound, the spherical functions with transforms `γ·p` upstairs and `b(γ·p)` downstairs match (`b` the base
change of Satake transforms), via an explicit matching of Iwahori functions of strictly dominant `a` and `l·a`. At split
places, products against convolutions. At bad non-split places, only functions supported near the identity, where the
norm is `γ ↦ γ^l`.

Spectral side. No Eisenstein series and no fine spectral expansion. For `R(f) = A∘M∘R(g)` with kernel vanishing above
height `Z`, the kernel integral differs from the twisted trace on the cuspidal spectrum by at most
`C·Z^N·‖M(1 − P_cusp)‖ + C·Z^{−N'}‖M‖`. `M` combines Hecke operators, the centre of the enveloping algebra and a
positive amplifier `A`: truncations of an entire series vanishing at the parameters induced from finitely many cuspidal
data of proper Levi subgroups (norm `≤ C^{d+1}/d!` on the non-cuspidal part, through constant terms), a polynomial
separating one eigensystem from finitely many others, and Chebyshev polynomials in `A`. On each remaining
finite-dimensional space the trace of `U_q^j∘R(g)∘θ` (`U_q` the Iwahori operator at `q`, `θ` the twist) is an
exponential polynomial in `j`, so the asymptotic identities become exact for large `j`: for each non-induced eigensystem
over `M`, the twisted traces equal `c` times the sum of the traces over the eigensystems over `K` lifting to it.

Lift, descent, fibres. With indicators of open subgroups downstairs every term is a positive multiple of a power sum, so
the `K`-side is non-zero for infinitely many `j`: a `σ`-stable cuspidal summand exists upstairs. If the lift is induced,
rank induction and a count of pole orders of Rankin–Selberg type give `π ≅ π ⊗ η`. Upstairs the `σ`-stable summand with
a given eigensystem is unique with non-zero trace, which gives descent; the fibres follow from the same pole count, and
`P_w ≅ π_v` at split places from varying the function there.

The trace identity applies the comparison twice with the same components away from `v`, once with `(φ, f)` and once
with a reference pair, and divides. `GL_2` from real to CM is a theta lift for `SL_2` and the orthogonal group of the
Hermitian `2 × 2` matrices: if a constant term of the lift vanishes it is cuspidal, otherwise `π ≅ π ⊗ η_{E/F}`. The
induction to `GL_4` runs the same machinery on the `σ`-stable non-cuspidal eigensystem of `π ⊞ π^σ`.

## Key steps

- **Elliptic geometric side** (`PositivityTheory.Rg.Tf.Geo.EllipticityFromProductFormula`, a statement, proved as
  `UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.G.g1St`): a high power of a regular Iwahori operator at a split place leaves only
  elliptic regular classes, as in the simple trace formulas of Flicker–Kazhdan and Flicker.
- **Spherical matching without the full fundamental lemma** (`UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.H.locHeckeInert`):
  spherical functions divisible by a polynomial chosen for the Satake parameter, matched through Iwahori functions; the
  unit is matched separately (`PositivityTheory.Rg.Tf.Loc.bcflUnit`). Statement and top-level proof read.
- **Approximate removal of the non-cuspidal spectrum** (`UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.Kill`): truncated entire series
  with norm `≤ C^{d+1}/d!` on the non-cuspidal part, proved through constant terms as
  `UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.kill`. Statement read, proof not.
- **Exact identities from growth in `j`** (`UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.ci`): the asymptotic comparison becomes an
  identity of cuspidal twisted traces because traces of powers of the Iwahori operator are exponential polynomials. Proof
  read in part.
- **Lift** (`UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.T.posSide`): positivity of the `K`-side for infinitely many `j` produces a
  `σ`-stable cuspidal summand upstairs (`T.coreLift`, an implication from the spectral and geometric identities,
  themselves proved as `UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.ci` and `UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.GM.gm`). Top-level proofs
  read.
- **Twisted trace identity** (`UpperTheory.Gal.InertialTypesAlt.G.core`): the comparison at a non-split place, divided by
  a reference identity; the fibre `{π ⊗ η}` gives one trace because `f` lives where the determinant is a local norm.
  Top-level proof read.
