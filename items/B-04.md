## Statement

For a finite place `w` of a number field `E` and `F = E_w`, the formal proof builds a local Artin map `a` and a map `rc`
from the irreducible smooth representations of all `GL_n(F)` to Frobenius-semisimple Weil–Deligne representations, with
dimension `n`, `rc(χ) = χ∘a` on characters, the segment rule, irreducible values on supercuspidals, twists by `|det|^s`
(`s` real) and `|det rc(π)| = |ω_π∘a|`; twists by all characters, `det rc(π) = ω_π∘a`, contragredients and injectivity
only on "good" representations (supercuspidal support of characters and, if `F ≅ ℚ_p`, of character twists of depth-zero
supercuspidals). It stands in for the local Langlands correspondence for `GL_n` and is not it: off the good class every
supercuspidal of rank `n ≥ 2` goes to an unramified twist of one fixed parameter of that rank. In place of Caraiani's
local–global compatibility, for `π` regular algebraic, polarized and cuspidal over a CM field and `w ∤ ℓ`, it proves
`WD(r_π|W_w) = rc(π_w ⊗ |det|^{(1−n)/2})` when the right side is trivial on commutators of the Weil group with `N = 0`,
and, at certain tame places with `E_w ≅ ℚ_p` and `n = 1` or `p > 16n²`, `N = 0` and equal characteristic polynomials on
inertia (the form without the bound on `p` is obtained in the source from two statements proved separately).

## Why it might matter

As the first reading understands the literature, from abstracts and later accounts, the local Langlands correspondence
for `GL_n` and local–global compatibility at every finite place are proved by global methods (Shimura varieties and
their bad reduction; for the correspondence also automorphic induction). On the path that was read, the formal proof
uses neither: it works with a map that has only the properties it needs, and proves compatibility only at principal
series of characters and at certain depth-zero places over `ℚ_p`.

## What the checks found

The first reading finds a narrower statement, not a new proof: high confidence for the local map, medium for the
local–global part, resting on twenty Lean files read in full, with no published proof read in full. The local map is a
definition by cases; its laws follow from the definition and standard theory (Langlands and Zelevinsky classification,
level-zero types, Green's formula at regular elliptic elements, Clifford theory of tame parameters, class field theory),
and nothing is proved about the correspondence, not even its depth-zero formula. The local–global statements are special
cases, relative to this map, reached by solvable base change from unramified places: for principal series of characters
by the base-change technique of Barnet-Lamb, Gee, Geraghty and Taylor, reduced to the unramified case; at depth zero over
`ℚ_p` together with a computation of how depth-zero types move under unramified cyclic base change (Shintani lifting),
from a twisted trace identity. That conclusion is in the literature; six searches found the statement but not this
derivation. There is no second opinion for this item; the first reading found no new proof here. Open: is this
derivation of Shintani lifting published for `GL_n`, and does the map's depth-zero value agree with Bushnell–Henniart's
formula in the normalisations used (geometric Frobenius, `θ` or `θ⁻¹`, the twist by `|det|^{(1−n)/2}`)?

## How the formal proof goes

The local map. (1) A local Artin map `a : W → F^×`, `W` the Weil group at `w`, is obtained, and shown unique, from
global Artin reciprocity and a theorem on units. (2) On supercuspidals a relation is defined: a character `χ` goes to
`χ∘a`; if `F ≅ ℚ_p`, a twist `y₀ ⊗ χ` of a depth-zero supercuspidal `y₀` goes to `V₀ ⊗ χ∘a`, where `V₀` is irreducible
of the right dimension, tame, with determinant the central character and inertial characteristic polynomials given by
the type. The map is a choice function for this relation, and the choice is shown to be forced. (3) `V₀` is constructed:
the invariants under the first congruence subgroup `K₁` contain a cuspidal representation `τ` of `GL_n(𝔽_p)`, unique up
to isomorphism; Green's formula gives a regular character `θ` of `𝔽_{p^n}^×`, unique up to Frobenius (a Parseval count
over the elements of degree `n`); the Artin map of an unramified extension gives a tame character of inertia with values
in `𝔽_{p^n}^×`; an induced representation has as inertial characters the Frobenius orbit of `θ` composed with it, and
the prescribed determinant. By Clifford theory for the tame quotient, an irreducible tame parameter is determined by its
inertial type and its determinant. (4) Every other supercuspidal of `GL_n`, `n ≥ 2`, goes to `J_n ⊗ |·|^e`, with `J_n`
one fixed irreducible `n`-dimensional parameter that is not a twist of a tame one (as far as the first reading read it,
built from a wildly ramified character of the unramified extension of degree `n`) and `e` chosen so that
`|det rc(π)| = |ω_π∘a|`. Here the map leaves the correspondence. (5) The map is extended to all irreducibles by the
segment rule, using existence and uniqueness of Langlands data, proved elsewhere in the source. (6) The laws are checked
segment by segment. That a tame supercuspidal over `ℚ_p` has depth zero is a count of depth-zero types against
irreducible tame parameters with fixed determinant at Frobenius, plus injectivity; it holds because `J_n` was chosen
non-tame.

Local–global compatibility. (7) The input, not examined here, is the existence of the Galois representation `r` and
compatibility where `π_w` is unramified. (8) Principal series of characters: for each Weil element `g` of nonzero degree
there are a solvable CM extension `M/E`, reached by cyclic steps of prime degree along which `π` stays cuspidal, and a
place `u ∣ w` with `g` in the Weil group of `M_u` and the base change unramified at `u` with Satake parameters
`ξ_j(a(g))`; compatibility at `u` gives the characteristic polynomial of `r(g)`; elements of degree zero follow, `N = 0`
descends, and the two Frobenius-semisimple classes agree. (9) Depth zero over `ℚ_p`: by the laws of the map the
hypothesis says that `π_w` has depth zero and the members of rank `≥ 2` of its support are pairwise not inertially
equivalent (the first reading's reading of the condition, not checked against its definition) (the form the main theorem
uses also needs `n = 1` or `p > 16n²`). Cyclic base changes of prime degree, all cuspidal, lead to a place `u ∣ w`
unramified of degree `n!` where the component has supercuspidal support of tamely ramified characters reproducing the
inertial polynomial of `rc(π_w)`, pairwise unlinked; step (8) applies at `u`, and inertia at `w` and at `u` coincide.
(10) That support statement follows a combinatorial label of the `K₁`-invariants. At an inert step the label is replaced
by its Shintani lift, through a twisted trace identity, up to a nonzero constant, between `π_v` and its base change, for
a class function on `GL_n(𝔽_q)` inflated to `GL_n(𝒪)` and its Shintani lift; the identity comes from a global twisted
trace identity and a matching of inflated functions. At a split step the label is unchanged. (11) At `w ∣ ℓ` the same
reductions give de Rham and potentially semistable statements, by descent along finite extensions.

## Key steps

- **Depth-zero values** (`UpperTheory.Zero.LLC.parEx`): builds the tame parameter attached to a depth-zero
  supercuspidal of `GL_n(ℚ_p)` from its `K₁`-type, Green's regular character and the central character. The published
  explicit formula is not used, and agreement with it in the formal proof's normalisations was not checked.
- **The fixed value elsewhere** (`UpperTheory.Zero.LLC.junkEx`, used in `UpperTheory.Zero.LLC.cuspEx`): one irreducible
  non-tame parameter per rank, twisted by an unramified character to fix `|det|`. This is why the map is not the
  correspondence and is unique only on good representations.
- **Segment rule** (`GlobalTheory.LocLanglands.exists_isLExt`): extends a map on supercuspidals to all irreducibles
  through Langlands data, whose existence and uniqueness enter as two statements proved elsewhere in the source
  (`UpperTheory.Zero.LLC.Pr.exh`, `UpperTheory.Zero.LLC.Pr.multi`).
- **Principal series of characters** (`GalTw.GlobalTheory.RoadU.away_eq_of_plainTor`, an implication from two named
  statements, one proved as `UpperTheory.Gal.ToralGalois.inq_holds`, the other obtained from the input of step (7)):
  `N = 0` and equality with the map's value, from traces of Weil elements of nonzero degree after solvable base change
  to an unramified place.
- **Depth zero over `ℚ_p`** (`UpperTheory.Gal.ToralGalois.R2.toTor`, an implication from seven named statements; with
  all of them supplied, `UpperTheory.Gal.ToralGalois.R2.toTor_holds`): the unramified cyclic tower to degree `n!` that
  turns the depth-zero case into the principal-series case at a place above `w` with the same inertia.
- **Shintani lifting** (`UpperTheory.Gal.InertialTypesAlt.L.finTrace`, an implication from five named statements,
  discharged in `finTrace_holds`, `finTrace_of_matching` and `UpperTheory.Gal.InertialTypesAlt.suppBC_outright`): the
  twisted trace identity for inflated class functions and their Shintani lifts that carries the `K₁`-label up an inert
  step; its global input was not read.

## What was checked, and what was not

Twenty Lean files of the local map and of the local–global assembly were read in full, eight more in part; the
construction of the Galois representations, compatibility at unramified places, the global twisted trace identity, the
matching of inflated functions, the Langlands classification and the level-zero type lemmas only as statements. The
dependency closure of the main theorem was not recomputed. No `sorry` or `axiom` occurs in the 18,431 files of
`src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic`; lemmas imported from other directories were not scanned, and that the main
theorem avoids the stated correspondence was checked only on the path read. Not opened: Harris–Taylor's book, Henniart
(2000; Math. Nachr. 158, 1992), Bushnell–Henniart's "essentially tame" series, Silberger–Zink, Shin, Chenevier–Harris;
Haines (arXiv:1012.4968) and Dhar–Nadimpalli (arXiv:2507.09773) were seen only as a search snippet and an abstract. No
published proof was read in full.
