## Statement

Let `X` be an integral closed subscheme of a product of projective spaces `∏ ℙ^{n_k}_ℤ`, flat over `ℤ`, of absolute
dimension `d ≥ 1`, not assumed regular or normal, with the bundles `O(a)` and metrics smooth on the ambient product.
There is a unique real function `I(X; L̄_1, …, L̄_d)`, additive in the last factor, such that for every bundle `Ā` of
multidegree `a ≥ 0` with a smooth metric and every form `s` of multidegree `a` not vanishing on `X`,
`I(X; L̄_1, …, L̄_{d−1}, Ā)` equals `Σ_{W horizontal} m_W·I(W; L̄_1, …, L̄_{d−1})` plus
`Σ_{W vertical} m_W·deg_W(L_1, …, L_{d−1})·log p_W` minus `∫_{X(ℂ)} log‖s‖·c_1(L̄_1)⋯c_1(L̄_{d−1})`; it is symmetric and
multilinear. This is the height of Faltings and Bost–Gillet–Soulé, with several bundles, for these subschemes: a
published theorem (for one bundle, Soulé's 2017 notes, Theorem 1), narrower than Gillet–Soulé's arithmetic Chow rings,
whose role it plays. The source also states an arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem of its own: for `X ⊂ ℙ^N_ℤ` of absolute
dimension `D + 1 ≥ 2`, with coordinates linearly independent over `ℤ`, and a smooth semipositive metric on `O(1)`
invariant under complex conjugation, `−log covol` of the lattice of restrictions to `X` of forms of degree `n`, in the
`L²` norm on a resolution, is `Ō(1)^{D+1}·n^{D+1}/(D+1)! + o(n^{D+1})`. In this form it is a proposition with a
derivation from thirteen further named statements, and no declaration proves it without hypotheses. A search found each
of the thirteen obtained elsewhere in the source, inside the proof of the corresponding statement for a product of
projective spaces, which the source derives from two named statements that it proves separately. One-sided counts of
small sections are closed theorems.

## Why it might matter

The formal proof uses these numbers in place of arithmetic Chow rings, and their construction needs no regularity of the
model. As far as its statements and assembly show, the source's Hilbert–Samuel argument uses neither arithmetic
Riemann–Roch nor analytic torsion, and its derivation of Moriwaki's inequality does not use the continuity of the
volume; they serve two one-sided implications of Zhang's theorem on successive minima.

## What the checks found

For the intersection numbers the first reading finds a narrower statement than Gillet–Soulé's, proved by the standard
elementary argument, specialised: the steps of Soulé's notes (uniqueness by induction, dimension one by the product
formula for an order, an auxiliary section, commutativity of two divisors through lengths, archimedean symmetry by
Poincaré–Lelong and Stokes on a resolution), with the analytic step justified, where the notes argue "at least
formally", by the method of Chambert-Loir–Thuillier. Confidence high: Soulé's notes and the main Lean proofs read in
full. For Hilbert–Samuel the route differs from Gillet–Soulé's; the change of metric is Berman–Boucksom's argument, a
known alternative. For the arithmetic half (projection to `ℙ^D` with traces, the index of a change of lattice, doubling
by the second Veronese embedding) the first reading could not determine how it relates to published proofs (confidence
low; Lean read at statement level, Abbes–Bouche and Zhang not read); this is not a finding of novelty. Zhang's upper
implication: standard (medium); the lower one and Moriwaki's inequality: could not determine (low). There is no second
opinion. Open: is this arithmetic half in Abbes–Bouche, Zhang or elsewhere? Is Moriwaki's inequality by perturbation
and counting, without continuity of the volume, in print?

## How the formal proof goes

Objects. `X` is given by a multihomogeneous prime `P` of `ℤ[x_{k,i}]` with `P ∩ ℤ = 0`; a bundle is a multidegree `a`
with a positive function `h` homogeneous of degree `a`, `‖s‖ = |s|/h`. The archimedean term is defined through a linear
projection finite on `X(ℂ)` onto `ℙ^{d−1}`, integrating over the chart the sum over the fibre of the integrand (in the
recursion, `log‖s‖`) times the mixed Monge–Ampère density of the `log h_j` along local sheets; independence of the
projection comes from a resolution of singularities. Components of `div s` are the minimal primes of `P + (s)` with
lengths as multiplicities; a vertical one over `p` enters with its geometric degree in the fibre, defined by its own
recursion, times `log p`.

Construction and proof. (1) `I(X; L̄, Ā)` is defined by recursion on the number of factors, as the right side of the
recursion for `Ā ⊗ H̄^m` along a form `t` minus that for `H̄^m` along a form `c`, with `H̄ = O(1, …, 1)` and Fubini–Study
metrics. (2) In each dimension three facts are proved together: the right side does not depend on the section, the
recursion holds for every section, and additivity in the last factor. (3) In dimension one `X` is the spectrum of an
order in a number field and the claim is the product formula `log #(O/fO) = Σ_σ log|σ(f)|`. (4) In higher dimension,
prime avoidance gives auxiliary forms meeting `div s` and `div t` properly, and independence follows from (5). (5) For
sections `s`, `s′` whose divisors share no component, the recursion along `s` with `Ā′` among the other factors equals
that along `s′` with `Ā`. Algebraically, `div(s)·div(s′) = div(s′)·div(s)`: in a two-dimensional noetherian local domain
both sides are `ℓ(A/(a, b))` minus the length of the `b`-torsion of `A/a`, which is symmetric. Analytically, the
archimedean expression is symmetric. (6) For the latter, the other factors are made semipositive by additivity and a
twist; `log‖s′‖` is replaced by `½·log(‖s′‖² + ε)`; Poincaré–Lelong and Stokes for smooth functions apply; the curvature
of the regularisation is bounded below uniformly in `ε`, so the integrals are monotone; `ε → 0` by dominated
convergence. This gives `≤`, and exchanging the sections gives equality. (7) Symmetry follows from (5) with sections in
general position, multilinearity from symmetry and additivity, uniqueness by induction with coordinate monomials.

Hilbert–Samuel, read as statements and assembly. (i) A finite linear projection `X → ℙ^D` with trace maps compares the
lattices of `X` with those of `ℙ^D`, and the intersection numbers by the projection formula. (ii) A change of coordinates
over `ℚ` changes the lattice by an index growing like a difference of intersection numbers. (iii) A change of metric
changes `log covol` by the Monge–Ampère energy (derivative against the Bergman function, upper bound by the sub-mean
inequality on polydiscs of radius `R/√n`, total mass the rank, `L¹` convergence). (iv) For `ℙ^D`, (i)–(iii) applied to
the second Veronese embedding give `δ(2n) − 2^D·δ(n) = o(n^{D+1})` for the defect `δ`, and, with bounds on the
increments, `δ(n) = o(n^{D+1})`. Moriwaki's inequality comes from this count for an ample perturbation and a fixed small
section, without the continuity of the volume; Zhang's two implications use it, the lower one through an induction
on the dimension of Kleiman's type.

## Key steps

- **Construction** (`GroundTheory.Heights₂.Heights.Inter.ThmIExists.fam`): the numbers are defined by recursion on the
  number of factors, as a difference of two right-hand sides along arbitrary forms of nonnegative multidegree; all the
  work is in showing this is independent of the choices.
- **Independence of the section** (`GroundTheory.Heights₂.Heights.Inter.WellDefined.proof`): prime avoidance for
  multihomogeneous forms and the exchange of two sections, applied twice. Read in full.
- **Exchange of two sections** (`GroundTheory.Heights₂.Heights.Inter.Swap.of_cycles`): an implication. It expands both
  sides once more and reduces the exchange to three named statements: the cut formula for the degrees of vertical
  components, the commutativity of divisors (`GroundTheory.Heights₂.Heights.Inter.SwapCycles.full`, which rests on a
  length identity in a two-dimensional local domain, read as a statement) and the archimedean symmetry.
- **Archimedean inequality** (`GroundTheory.Heights₂.Heights.Inter.DeLeSemiPos.proof`): an implication from seven named
  statements, among them the Poincaré–Lelong formula, Stokes' formula for smooth functions, the uniform curvature bound
  and the limit `ε → 0`; its own argument is the regularisation of `log‖s′‖` and the passage to the limit; equality then
  by antisymmetry. Read in full.
- **Change of metric** (`ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.G.Geo.metricChange`): tensor with the trivial bundle and
  apply the recursion to the section `1`.
- **Projective space by doubling**
  (`ReciprocityTheory.GeometryOfNumbersTheory.hilbertSamuelDefect_projectiveSpace_isLittleO`): an implication. If the
  Hilbert–Samuel defect `δ` of `ℙ^D`, formed with any real number in the place of the height of `ℙ^D`, satisfies
  `δ(2n) − 2^D·δ(n) = o(n^{D+1})`, then `δ(n) = o(n^{D+1})`. The doubling relation is a hypothesis here; in the assembly
  it comes from the comparison with the second Veronese embedding, and the height of `ℙ^D` is not computed. Read as a
  statement.
