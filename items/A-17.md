## Statement

Let `O` be a complete discrete valuation ring of characteristic 0 with fraction field `E`, and `K` an algebraically
closed field containing `O`, with a real-valued valuation `v` extending the normalised valuation of `O`. Let
`A = ⊕_{j∈ℤ} A_j` be a graded `O`-algebra, generated over `A_0` by finitely many elements of weights `±1, ±2`, with
`A_0` complete Noetherian local with the residue field of `O`, and `x : A → O` an `O`-algebra map killing `A_j` for
`j ≠ 0`. Let `I` be generated by `F_0, …, F_r ∈ A_{−1}` (`r ≥ 1`) and by `s, r′, s′, m` elements `G, P, P′, Z` of
`A_{−2}, A_1, A_2, A_0 ∩ ker x`, and let `a ∈ A_0`. `cot_j(x)` is the image of `A_j ∩ ker x` in `A/sat(x)`, where
`sat(x) = {f : cf ∈ (ker x)² for some c ≠ 0 in O}`. `K^×` acts on `K`-points by `t^j` on `A_j`; a point is two-signed if
it kills neither all negative nor all positive weights. Assume: (M1) there is an `O`-algebra map `φ : A → E[[t]]` with
constant term `x`, in `r, s, m, r′+1, s′+1` variables of weights `−1, −2, 0, 1, 2`, sending `A_j` to series with
monomials of weight `j`, whose linear part induces `(ker x/sat(x)) ⊗_O E ≅ ⊕ E·t_i`; and `F, G, Z` span
`cot_{−1}(x), cot_{−2}(x), cot_0(x)` over `E`; (M2) the image of `A_0` in `A/I` is finite over `O`; (M3) no `K`-point
`x₁ ≠ x` of `A/I` killing all nonzero weights has, among the points of `A/I` equal to it on `A_0`, both one killing all
negative but not all positive weights and one killing all positive but not all negative weights; (M4) at two-signed
`K`-points of `A/I` any two point derivations of `A/I` are proportional; (M5) `x(a) = 0` and `a` vanishes at no
two-signed `K`-point of `A/I`. Then the two-signed `K`-points of `A/I` lie in finitely many orbits,
`ℓ := length_O(cot_{−1}(x)/span_O(F_i))` is finite, and `ℓ ≤ 2·Σ_{ξ∈T} v(ξ(a))` for every finite set `T` of such points
meeting every orbit.

## Why it might matter

The left side measures how far the weight `−1` equations are from generating the weight `−1` cotangent lattice at a torus-fixed
integral point; the right side involves only the values of one weight-zero element on the orbits where both signs of weight survive.
`A/I` is not assumed finite or flat over `O`, nor a complete intersection: only the image of `A_0` is assumed finite. In the source
the bound is applied to graded models of deformation problems at a diagonal representation, with `a` a difference of traces.

## What the checks found

The first reading compared this statement in substance (the Lean form is slightly more general in the field `K`, the set
`T` and the chart clause), so its verdict applies; its remarks on Selmer groups of characters concern an application not
stated here. Its verdict: incomparable (medium confidence). For finite flat complete intersections the Wiles–Lenstra
equality is stronger (constant 1, the congruence module on the right); outside them Iyengar–Khare–Manning bound the
cotangent module from below, and Böckle–Khare–Manning give exact defect formulas for residually irreducible Hecke
algebras; the right side here is at least the congruence module (Berger–Klosin–Kramer). Its web searches ran out twice.
The forward search (eight queries) found nothing stating or implying the bound, but upper bounds of other shapes in
preprints: Khare–Patrikis (adjoint Selmer groups at irreducible points) and Lee (Selmer groups of characters, an
application not stated here, through Euler systems); the multiplicity theory behind the method was seen in abstracts
only. The second opinion agrees (medium confidence), excluding neither priority nor a deduction from general
intersection theory; it finds a close precedent for the method in Viet–Thanh (2015). Its corrections: the first
reading's wording on priority exceeds its evidence; the comparison with the congruence module needs an identification
not supplied here; Böckle–Khare–Manning's exact formulas are exceptions to "the other way"; two-signed points need not
be irreducible ones. Open: can the constant 2 be replaced by 1? Does it follow from positivity of intersection numbers
on Rees algebras, or on schemes with a `𝔾_m`-action?

## How the formal proof goes

The statement is the proposition `LemmaM`; `main_holds` proves it by four reductions and one inequality, `core_holds`. (i) `rk_holds`: the linear
parts of the chart embed `cot_{−1}(x)` in `E^r` with image of full rank, so it is free of rank `r`. (ii) `sm_holds`: by Smith normal form, `F` is
replaced by a family with the same `O`-span such that `F_i ≡ π^{c_i}·y_i` modulo `sat(x)` for `i < r`, the `y_i ∈ A_{−1}` giving a basis of
`cot_{−1}(x)`, and `F_r ∈ sat(x)`; then `ℓ = Σ c_i`, which is the finiteness. (iii) `cut_holds`: a formal implicit function theorem with weights
(`ift_holds`; here `Z` spanning `cot_0(x)` is used) gives a chart `φ` in the variables of nonzero weight only, with `φ(Z_l) = 0`. (iv) `nz_holds`: `a`
is replaced by `a + g·F_k` with `g ∈ A_1`, which has the same values on `A/I` and satisfies `φ(a) ≠ 0`. The finiteness of orbits is `fin_holds`, from
`slice_holds` below.

Let `R ⊆ A[T_0, T_1, T_2]` be the `ℕ³`-graded `A_0`-algebra whose part of degree `μ` is
`N_0^{μ_0}·N_1^{μ_1}·N_2^{μ_2}·T^μ`, with `N_0 = A_{−2}`, `N_1 = A_2`, `N_2 = A_0·F_r² + A_{−2}·A_{−2}·A_2`. The
equations give a list `ts` of sections: the `F_i²` (`i < r`) and `G` in degree `e_0`, the `P_i²` and `P′` in degree
`e_1`, `F_r²` in degree `e_2`. Let `J` be the ideal of elements whose coefficients all lie in `ker φ`. For a finite
graded `R`-module `M`, `χ` is defined by `χ(∅; M)(d) = length M_d` and
`χ((δ, s) :: ts; M) = χ(ts; M/sM) − χ(ts; (0 :_M s)(−δ))` (`chi`), for large `d` and admissible lists: a power of
`𝔪_{A_0}·R_+` lies in `Ann M + (ts)`, `R_+` being generated by the products of elements of degrees `e_0`, `e_1`, `e_2`.
The source proves its calculus (directory `NoetherianQuotientCalc`): additivity, independence of the order, the rules
for a nonzerodivisor and for a product with a factor of degree zero, and positivity (`rulePos`: dévissage to quotients
by homogeneous primes, a section inside a relevant prime being exchanged for one of the same degree outside it);
monotonicity under graded quotients and `χ(ts ++ us; M) ≤ χ(us; M/(ts)M)` (`ruleUB`) follow.

Admissibility of `ts` with `a` (`admissibility_holds`): a prime of `R` containing the sections, `a` and the `Z_l` but not `R_+` contracts to a prime
of `A_0` containing `I ∩ A_0`, which by (M2) is the maximal ideal or the kernel of a `K`-point `σ`. If `σ` kills `A_{−2}·A_2`, points of `A/I` over
`σ` are built that kill only the positive weights, only the negative ones, and both (`zm_holds`, `zp_holds`, `fix_holds`); this contradicts (M3), or,
when the last point is `x`, the spanning of `cot_{−1}(x)`, `cot_{−2}(x)` by `F`, `G` (`ct_holds`, `zii_holds`). Otherwise a point of `A/I` over `σ` is
two-signed and `a` vanishes there, against (M5).

Lower bound (`low_holds`). Let `𝔮 ⊆ R` consist of the elements whose coefficient of `T^μ` maps under `φ` to a series all of whose monomials have order
at least `2μ_0 + 4μ_2 + 1` in the variables of negative weight (`lk_holds`). It is homogeneous, contains `J` and every `b ∈ A_0` with `x(b) = 0` (so
`a`; no variable has weight zero), `π` is a nonzerodivisor on `R/𝔮`, and modulo `𝔮` the section `F_i²` is `π^{2c_i}·y_i²`. Monotonicity gives
`χ(ts; (R/J)/a) ≥ χ(ts; R/𝔮)`; peeling the powers of `π` one section at a time and then positivity (`la_holds`) give `χ(ts; R/𝔮) ≥ (Σ 2c_i)·ΔH`,
where `H(d) = length((R/𝔮)/π)_d` and `Δ` is the mixed difference in the degrees of the other sections. `hf_holds` identifies `H(d)` with the number
of monomials of negative order `2d_0 + 4d_2` and positive order `2d_1 + 2d_2`, and `num_holds` gives `ΔH = 2^{r−1+r′}` for large `d`. Hence
`χ(ts; (R/J)/a) ≥ (Σ c_i)·2^{r+r′}`. Middle step (`chain`, in `AlgMemCoef.lean`): as `φ(a) ≠ 0`, `a` is a nonzerodivisor on `R/J`, so
`χ(ts; (R/J)/a) = χ(a :: ts; R/J) = χ(ts ++ [a]; R/J) ≤ χ([a]; R/(J + (ts)))`.

Upper bound (`up_holds`). For one section of degree 0, `χ([a]; M)(d) = length(M_d/aM_d) − length(M_d[a])`. For
`M = R/(J + (ts))` and large `d`, `A_0/Ann(M_d)` is finite over `O`, and `M_d` vanishes at a `K`-point `σ` of `A_0`
unless `σ` is the restriction of a two-signed point of `A/I` (`eventually_mQ_gr_finite_annihilator`, by the analysis of
primes above). `th_holds` then bounds this difference by `c·Σ_σ v(σ(a))` if the lengths of `M_d` localised at the `σ`
are at most `c`: a prime filtration reduces to a domain `D` finite over `O`, where
`length_O(D/aD) = v(N_{D/O}(a)) = Σ_τ v(τ(a))` over the embeddings `τ : D → K` (characteristic 0 gives separability).
The local lengths are at most those of a weight piece of `A/I_2`, `I_2` generated by the `F_i²`, `G`, `P_i²`, `P′`, `Z`
(`locLen_mQ_gr_le_locLen_aPiece`); these are at most `2^{r+1+r′}` times those of `A/I`, each squared generator at most
doubling a length (`locLen_aPiece_ideal2_le_two_pow`); and those are at most 1 (`locLen_quotientWeightPiece_le_one`).
The last rests on `slice_holds`: for `f ∈ A_{−2}`, `g ∈ A_2`, the ring `(A/(I, f − 1))[1/πg]` has no nonzero point
derivation at a `K`-point, by (M4) applied to a given derivation and the one multiplying `A_j` by `j`, so it is finite
and reduced over `E` (`et_holds`). With `v(ξ(a)) ≥ 0` (integrality, from (M2)) this gives
`χ([a]; M)(d) ≤ 2^{r+1+r′}·Σ_{ξ∈T} v(ξ(a))`, and the three bounds give the claim.

Application (`model_upper`): `l` an odd prime, `O` finite over `ℤ_l`, `r₀ = (diag(Ψ, 1, Ψ^{−1}), η)` with values in
`GL_3 ⋊ {±1}`, and a graded model of a universal ring of normalised lifts with conditions; `a` is the trace of the lift
at `σ ∈ ker η` minus `tr r₀(σ)`. Its hypotheses include `LemmaM` itself, finiteness over `O` of the algebra of
weight-zero monomials in the matrix entries (which gives (M2)), rigidity conditions from which the spanning part of
(M1), (M3) and (M4) are derived (the chart of (M1) is part of the model), and, for (M5), that the trace difference is
nonzero at every point neither upper nor lower triangular on `ker η`. It gives a finite set `T` of points neither upper
nor lower triangular on `ker η`, one per class under conjugation by `diag(t, 1, t^{−1})`, with
`ℓ/e ≤ 2·Σ_{f∈T} v_l(tr ρ_f(σ) − tr r₀(σ))`, `e` the ramification index. Smith normal form, norms as products over
embeddings, prime filtrations, and finiteness and reducedness of formally unramified algebras of finite type over a
field come from the Mathlib library; the rest is proved in the source.

## Key steps

- **The squeeze** (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.core_holds`): file `GradedModulesLength/AlgMemCoef.lean`, line 92. Forms the Rees algebra and
  the sections and combines `(Σ c_i)·2^{r+r′} ≤ χ(ts; (R/J)/a) ≤ χ([a]; R/(J + (ts))) ≤ 2^{r+1+r′}·Σ v(ξ(a))` at one large degree.
  The constant 2 is the ratio of the two powers of 2.
- **Leading forms at the fixed point** (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.low_holds`): file `GradedModulesLength/AdmissibleMonoIdeal.lean`,
  line 191. Passes to `R/𝔮`, where the first `r` sections become `π^{2c_i}·y_i²`, and extracts `Σ 2c_i` times a mixed difference of a
  Hilbert function. The properties of `𝔮` are in `lk_holds` (`LkHolds.lean`, line 8).
- **The monomial count** (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.num_holds`): file `GradedModulesLength/FrobeniusInclusionXEqMulQ.lean`, line 206.
  The mixed difference of the number of monomials of given negative and positive order equals `2^{r−1+r′}` for large degrees; the
  identification of the Hilbert function with this number is `hf_holds` (`ReesGradedPieceSurjection.lean`, line 199).
- **The order of `a` on graded pieces** (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.th_holds`): file `GradedModulesLength/HerbrandFinLen.lean`, line 189.
  Bounds `length(M/aM) − length(M[a])` by `c·Σ_σ v(σ(a))` when the local lengths at the `K`-points `σ` are at most `c`; inside, it is
  shown equal to `Σ_σ length(M_σ)·v(σ(a))`. Used in `up_holds` (`TwoSigned.lean`, line 17).
- **Support of the graded pieces** (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.eventually_mQ_gr_finite_annihilator`): file
  `GradedModulesLength/EventuallyMQGrFiniteAnnihilator.lean`, line 77. In large degrees the pieces are finite over `O` and supported
  at restrictions of two-signed points; this, with `admissibility_holds` (`AdmissibilityHolds.lean`, line 30), is where (M3) enters.
- **Reducedness at two-signed points** (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.slice_holds`): file `GradedModulesLength/SliceHolds.lean`, line 48.
  From (M2) and (M4): finitely many points on a slice `f = 1`, `g ≠ 0`, and a function vanishing at all of them is zero there up to
  a power of `g` and a nonzero constant. It gives the finiteness of orbits and the local length 1.

## What was checked, and what was not

`UpperTheory.V.Alg.LemmaM` is a definition of a proposition; `UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.main_holds` is a theorem with a proof
of it and no hypotheses beyond those of the statement. Later theorems, `model_upper` among them, take `LemmaM` as an
explicit hypothesis, which is supplied by `main_holds` at the uses that were looked at. The 87 source files that
`MainCotSpan.lean` imports directly or indirectly (about 13,000 lines) and the 59 for `ModelUpper.lean` (about 9,200
lines) were searched for `sorry`, `axiom`, `admit`, `native_decide`, `opaque`, `unsafe` and `implemented_by`: none
occurs. The proofs of `main_holds`, `core_holds`, `low_holds`, `up_holds`, `la_holds`, `lk_holds`, `th_holds`,
`slice_holds`, `admissibility_holds`, `rulePos` and `model_upper` were read. Statements only: the constructions of
points, `ct_holds`, the leading-monomial lemma behind `hf_holds`, `ift_holds`, most of the calculus of `χ`, and, in the
application, the lemmas giving (M1)–(M4), the structure of a model, and the statement of its existence (`exists_model`,
an implication from two named statements, which were not traced). Nothing was compiled; Mathlib was not examined.
