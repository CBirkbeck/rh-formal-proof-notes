## Statement

In the variable `u = log y`, let `g` be a measurable real function with `|g(u)| ≤ C₀e^{2u}` for `u ≥ 0`, and suppose
that its Laplace transform `Φ(s) = ∫₀^∞ g(u)e^{−su} du`, defined for `Re s > 2`, extends holomorphically to
`{Re s > 0} ∖ Z`, where `Z` has no accumulation point in `Re s > 0` and each `ρ ∈ Z` is a simple pole with nonzero
residue, real and positive when `ρ` is real. Let `0 < θ < 1` and `δ > 0`, and suppose that for every `η > 0` there are
arbitrarily large `U` and a function `R ≥ 0` on `[U, (1+δ)U]`, with `R(u)e^{−θu}` integrable there, with `g ≤ R` there
and `∫_U^{(1+δ)U} R(u)e^{−θu} du ≤ e^{ηU}`: a one-sided bound in mean, asked only along a sequence of windows, which are
`[Y, Y^{1+δ}]` in `y`. Then `Re ρ ≤ θ` for every `ρ ∈ Z`. In `y` the result is proved only for Dirichlet `L`-functions:
for finitely many characters `χ_i`, each of modulus `1` or non-principal, and weights `a_i > 0`, let
`G = Σ a_i Re G_{χ_i}`, where `G_χ(y)` is `Σ_{n≤y} Λ(n)χ(n)(y/n − 1)` minus its main term; if `G(y) ≤ Cy^θ` on
`[Y, Y^{1+δ}]` for arbitrarily large `Y`, no `L(s, χ_i)` has a zero with real part greater than `θ`. It is not a case of
Landau's theorem but takes its place: it assumes meromorphy, concludes that there is no pole, and asks the bound only on
windows.

## Why it might matter

In the formal proof it turns one-sided bounds on windows `[Y, Y^λ]` for weighted prime sums twisted by Dirichlet characters into
zero-free half-planes for their `L`-functions, where Landau's theorem in its form for Mellin integrals would need the bound for
all large `y`. It is proved directly, from neither Landau's theorem nor the theorem of Kaczorowski and Pintz.

## What the checks found

The first reading compared the literature with an earlier, more general description: a transform meromorphic on
`Re s > θ` with poles of any order, growth by integrability alone, the conclusion that there is no pole in `Re s > θ` or
that the poles there have a largest real part, attained at a real pole with negative leading coefficient, and a form in
`y` for any sum of coefficients. The statement here is a special case (simple poles, positive residues at real poles, a
pointwise growth bound, in `y` only Dirichlet characters), so the verdict, known in substance, covers it. Corollary 1 of
Kaczorowski–Pintz I (1986) gives a sign change of size `x^{Θ−ε}` in every `[Y^{1−ε}, Y]`, for poles of any order; read
contrapositively it is the criterion when `Θ`, the supremum of the real parts of the poles, is not itself a pole; a
short reduction adds the bound in mean and a real rightmost pole (checked by the first reading for simple poles; for
higher order its own, unprinted). Kátai (1967) has an earlier one-sided window theorem. Confidence high that the result
is at most a routine variant of the 1986 theorem, medium that the formulation it compared, with the dichotomy at a real
rightmost pole, is unprinted. The forward search, which could make no web search, used citation lists; it found Kátai's
paper and two lemmas for a single Dirichlet character that allow real zeros: Fiorilli–Martin (2023, Lemma 2.1), by which
every window `[X^{1−ε}, X]`, `X` large, contains a point where `Re ψ(x, χ)`, less `x` for the principal character, is
below `−x^{Θ_χ−ε}`, and Hayani (2025, Lemma 3.1), the same for a smoothed sum and non-principal `χ`. From the first it
derives a criterion for the unsmoothed `ψ(x, χ)`, with a bound from below on such windows, and concludes that the form
of the formal proof for Dirichlet `L`-functions is published in substance, the bound in mean and the weighted family
apart. It compared the earlier description and does not discuss the smoothed sum `G_χ` of the statement here, which is
bounded from above. The second opinion looked at the argument, not at the statement, but says that it could not
independently confirm that Corollary 1 implies the form with a measurable function and a bound in mean; its view is on
the page B14. Open: is the general statement, for a meromorphic transform with a real rightmost pole or with the bound
in mean, in print, perhaps in Radziejewski (2014)? Do Pintz (1980) or Kaczorowski (1984) state it?

## How the formal proof goes

The normalised derivatives `a_k(z) = (−1)^kΦ^{(k)}(z)/k!` are integrals of `g` against the Gamma kernels `u^k e^{−zu}/k!`, which
for `k` near `(1+δ/2)σU` concentrate on the window; there positivity bounds `|a_k(σ+iγ)| + a_k(σ)` by `C′e^{ηU}(σ−θ)^{−k}`. A
pole with real part `> θ` nearest to the centre `σ`, or strictly nearest to a shifted centre `σ + iγ`, would by Cauchy's
inequalities make `a_k` grow like `d^{−k−1}` with `d < σ − θ` (at a real pole, through the sign of its residue), which the bound
forbids for small `η`. Neither Landau's theorem nor Kaczorowski–Pintz is used. The page B14 gives the full account.

## Key steps

- **Positivity on a window** (`Deduction.landau_window_ineq`, `Deduction.landau_window_key`): the twisted coefficient plus the
  untwisted one is at most twice the integral of `g⁺` against the kernel, which the window hypothesis bounds.
- **A nearest pole** (`Deduction.landau_dichotomy`, `Deduction.landau_nearest`): the pole nearest to `σ` is real, or a generic
  centre `σ + iγ` has a strictly nearest pole, alone in a disc.
- **Cauchy's inequalities** (`Deduction.landau_pole_asym`, `Deduction.landau_window_contra`): the principal part makes `a_k` grow
  faster than the window bound allows; at a real pole, through `c > 0`.
