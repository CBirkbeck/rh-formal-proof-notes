## Statement

Assume the absolute Siegel lemma with constant `½·log n`: for every Hermitian lattice of rank `n` over the integers of a
number field and every `ε > 0`, there is, over a finite extension, a line of normalised degree at least
`μ̂ − ½·log n − ε`. Then there is a constant `C ≥ 0` such that

`μ̂_max(Lie Ḡ) ≤ C·log(2 + g + max(h, 0))`

for every semi-abelian scheme `G` over the integers `O_L` of a number field `L`, with an action of the integers `O_E` of
a CM field `E` and a line bundle `ℒ`, satisfying the following conditions.

- `O_E` is unramified over the integers of the maximal real subfield of `E` and contains a square root `r` of `−d`,
  where `d` is a prime with `d ≡ 7 (mod 16)`. Put `w₀ = (1 + r)/2`.
- `ℒ` is a symmetric rigidified line bundle on `G` with a cubical structure. Write
  `Λ(ℒ) = m^*ℒ ⊗ p₁^*ℒ⁻¹ ⊗ p₂^*ℒ⁻¹ ⊗ 0^*ℒ` on `G × G`. We require that `(a × 1)^*Λ(ℒ) ≅ (1 × ā)^*Λ(ℒ)` for every
  `a ∈ O_E`, with `ā` its complex conjugate, and that `ℒ ≅ (1, w₀)^*Λ(ℒ)`.
- The generic fibre `A` is proper, `ℒ` is ample on `A`, and `K(ℒ)` is trivial on `A`, so that `ℒ` is a principal
  polarisation.

Here `Lie Ḡ` is the `O_L`-module of tangent vectors of `G` along the unit section, with the inner product `π·H_σ` at
each embedding `σ : L → ℂ`, where `H_σ` is the Hermitian Riemann form of `ℒ` on `A_σ(ℂ)`. We write `g` for its rank,
`h = −deĝ(Lie Ḡ)/[L:ℚ]`, and `μ̂_max` for the supremum of `deĝ(F̄)/([L:ℚ]·rk F)` over non-zero submodules `F`.

In the Lean source this is an implication from the absolute Siegel lemma. The bound itself (`SlopeBoundSt`) is a
proposition, and we did not find a declaration that proves it without the Siegel hypothesis. The source proves no form
of the bound without the action of `O_E` and the conditions above.

## Context

The maximal slope of the tangent lattice of a semi-abelian model enters slope arguments for abelian varieties over
number fields, in work on periods, on isogenies and on heights of points of moduli spaces. Here, assuming the Siegel
lemma, it is bounded by the logarithm of the height and of the dimension, with one constant for the whole class above.
With this metric on `Lie Ḡ`, `h` should be the stable Faltings height of `A` for the metric
`‖α‖² = (2π)^(−g)·|∫ α ∧ ᾱ|` on `det ω_A`. That identification is not in the source, whose statement does not name the
Faltings height.

## Comparison with the literature

We compared with the literature only a stronger form of the statement: an unconditional bound, with one absolute
constant, for every principally polarised abelian variety. The Lean statement is an implication from the absolute
Siegel lemma, and only for the class above with its action of `O_E`. So our comparison does not bear on it, and we
have made none for this class under the Siegel hypothesis. For the stronger form, the closest results we found are
Bost (1996, Proposition 5.3 (ii)) and Gaudron (2019, Proposition 4.2 and Corollary 4.5). Their bounds are linear in
the Faltings height, and they hold for any polarisation, so for the class above too. A logarithmic bound improves on
them in its growth, but it does not supersede them numerically for given `(g, h)`, since `C` is not explicit. We have
not made a systematic search of the works citing these papers, so a later logarithmic bound may have escaped us. The
later papers we looked at still use linear bounds for the cotangent space, but that does not show that no logarithmic
bound exists.

A second, independent check compared the statement as proved, for this class, and found it not comparable with the
literature. It has better growth in the height and the dimension, but on a restricted class, while the published
bounds cover substantially more varieties. That check found no exact precedent, but its coverage of work on special
endomorphisms is incomplete. It held that an improvement claimed without restriction needs qualification. It also
reported that filtered jets of higher order, the method of the proof, are established machinery (Bost 2001), and that
the Siegel lemma taken as hypothesis is proved in print (Gaudron–Rémond 2013). We consulted neither paper ourselves.
The two assessments disagree, but they are not about the same statement. We would like to know whether this slope has
been bounded in work on abelian varieties with an action of a CM field, and which abelian varieties carry the data of
the class above.

## Sketch of the proof

Write `h⁺ = max(h, 0)`, `‖x‖_σ` for the length of a tangent vector at `σ`, and `mean_σ` for the mean over the `[L:ℚ]`
embeddings. The proof combines `TopSlope.slopeBound`, `line_of_key`, `keyDeg_of_keyBad` and `key_bad_reduction_holds`.

*Reduction to one vector.* Let `F` be a non-zero submodule of `Lie(G)`. The Siegel hypothesis with `ε = 1` gives a
finite extension `L′` and a non-zero `x` in `Lie(G) ⊗ O_L′` with `μ̂(F) − ½·log g − 1 ≤ −mean_σ log‖x‖_σ`. The data
extend to `O_L′` with the same `g` and `h` (`ext_baseChange`). So it suffices to prove
`−mean_σ log‖x‖_σ ≤ C·log(2 + g + h⁺)` for all data and every non-zero integral tangent vector `x` (`LineSt`). The
constant of the theorem is then `C + 2`.

*A theta section of bounded L²-norm.* After a finite extension, `Lie(G)` has a basis `e` and `ℒ` has a section `θ` over
`G` that vanishes identically on no fibre. The source proves a key formula (`keyFormula_holds`). For a principally
polarised abelian variety in characteristic 0 with a rigidified bundle, a non-zero section `θ` and a basis `v` of the
Lie algebra, it gives a non-zero scalar `κ(θ, v)` with `a⁸·κ(aθ, Mv) = det(M)⁴·κ(θ, v)`. This scalar is compatible with
base change, is a unit under good reduction, and over `ℂ` satisfies `|κ|·‖θ‖₂⁸·‖v₁ ∧ … ∧ v_g‖⁻⁴ = (2π)^(2g)`, where
`‖θ‖₂` is the L²-norm for the Haar probability measure. In the class of the statement, `κ(θ, e)` is integral at the
places of bad reduction too (`Kftwo.kp`). This is the first use of the action and of the conditions on `ℒ`. It goes
through `G × G`, on which the integers of a second CM field act by 2 × 2 matrices over `O_E`. By the product formula,
`mean_σ log‖θ‖₂ ≤ (g/4)·log 2π + h/2 ≤ h⁺/2 + g`.

*From the L²-norm to the sup norm.* At each `σ`, sections of `ℒ^⊗n` are theta functions on `ℂ^g` (`frames_main`). An
archimedean lemma (`nform`) gives `ρ_σ` in `(0, √(2g)]` with `sup‖θ‖_σ ≤ ‖θ‖₂·exp(E_σ)`, where
`E_σ = g·log(1 + 1/ρ_σ) + C₀·g·log 2g`, such that for large `m` the mean of `−log(‖θ‖_σ/‖θ‖₂)` over the `m`-torsion
points where `θ ≠ 0` is at least `π/(96ρ_σ²) − E_σ`. Take the value of `θ` at an `N`-torsion point `y`, raise it to the
power `N²`, and read it in the rigidification through `[N]^*ℒ ≅ ℒ^⊗N²`. The result is an algebraic integer `T` with
`|σ(T)| = ‖θ‖_σ(y)^(N²)` (`integral_torsVal`). This is the second use of the action. The proof uniformises `G` over
complete discrete valuation rings (`uDvrSt_holds`), which the source does given endomorphisms `w`, `w′` with sum 1 such
that `ℒ ≅ (1, w)^*Λ(ℒ)` and `(w × 1)^*Λ(ℒ) ≅ (1 × w′)^*Λ(ℒ)`. Here `w = w₀`. The product formula for the `T` bounds
`mean_σ ρ_σ⁻²` by a constant times `Φ + g²·log 2g`, where `Φ = h⁺/2 + g`, and gives numbers `M_σ ≥ sup‖θ‖_σ` with
`mean_σ log M_σ ≤ Φ + C·g·log(2 + g + Φ)` (`mean_log_sup`).

*Integral sections of a power.* Put `K = ⌈2 + g² + h⁺⌉`, `m = 3K`, `n = m²` and `n′ = n/3`. After a further extension
there are `r = m^(2g)` sections `s_j` of `ℒ^⊗n` over `G`, one for each `m`-torsion point `r_j`, with
`‖s_j‖(w) = ‖θ‖(r_j + m·w)`. They are integral, again through the integrality of torsion values. Their sup norms are at
most `M_σ`, and at each `σ` they span the theta functions of level `n` (`Basis.main`).

*Jets along the vector.* `JetMain.jets` defines `J_{i,j}` in `L` as the value at the origin of the `i`-th iterate of
the derivation of `x` applied to `s_j`, divided by the `n`-th power of the rigidification and not by `i!`. If `c` has
coordinates in `O_L` and `Σ_j c_j·J_{k,j} = 0` for all `k < i`, then `Σ_j c_j·J_{i,j}` is an algebraic integer. Under
the same vanishing it is, at each `σ`, the `i`-th derivative at `0` of `τ ↦ Σ_j c_j·f_j(τ·v)`, where `f_j` is the theta
function of `s_j` and `v` is the coordinate vector of `x`. For each `i ≤ n′` some combination vanishes to order exactly
`i`. Indeed, `θ` gives theta functions `s₀`, `s₁` of level 3 (from products of three translates) with `s₀(0) ≠ 0`,
`s₁(0) = 0` and non-zero derivative of `s₁` along `v`, and `s₀^(n′−i)·s₁^i` has level `n` (`lefschetz_direction`). The
Cauchy estimate on the circle of radius `√(i/n)/‖x‖_σ` bounds that derivative by `A_{i,σ}·|c|`, where
`A_{i,σ} = (e·n·n′)^(i/2)·‖x‖_σ^i·√r·M_σ`.

*Product formula.* `flag_slope` takes a non-zero maximal minor of the matrix `(J_{i,j})`, `i ≤ n′`. It is integral at
every finite place, by an elimination that uses only the filtered integrality above, and at each `σ` it is at most
`∏_i A_{i,σ}`, by Gram–Schmidt. Hence `0 ≤ Σ_{i ≤ n′} Σ_σ log A_{i,σ}`, that is,
`−mean_σ log‖x‖_σ ≤ ½·log(e·n·n′) + (2/n′)·(g·log m + mean_σ log M_σ)`. Since `n′ = 3K²` and `K ≥ 2 + g² + h⁺`, the
last term is at most a constant times `log(2 + g + h⁺)`, and so is the first (`SlopeParameterChoice.full`). The height
enters through the one section `θ` and is divided by the number `n′` of vanishing orders.

*Inputs.* The Siegel lemma is the only hypothesis. The source derives that proposition from four named statements: an
arithmetic intersection system, two statements of Hilbert–Samuel type, and resolution of singularities
(`lineOfLargeDegree_of_use`). For each of the four the source has a declaration without hypotheses, or a derivation from
such a declaration. We saw these by their signatures and did not read them. The bound is used in theorems that take the
Siegel lemma, or these four statements, as hypotheses. The key formula, the two uniformisations and the facts on theta
functions are theorems of the source without hypotheses.

## Key steps

- **Reduction to one tangent vector** (`GlobalTheory.T.slope_bound_of_line_large_degree`): the Siegel hypothesis turns
  a sub-lattice of large slope into a short integral vector over a finite extension. This is carried out in
  `TopSlope.slopeBound` (`src/Development/…/GeometryProofs/SlopeBoundOfLargeDegreeLine.lean`, line 94).
- **Theta section of bounded L²-norm** (`GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.Sl.keyDeg_of_keyBad`): the key formula and the
  integrality of its scalar give `mean_σ log‖θ‖₂ ≤ h⁺/2 + g`. Integrality at bad reduction, which needs the action, is
  `PositivityTheory.HtPt.Kftwo.kp` (`src/Development/WeilBound/…/HodgeTatePoints/LieMultComp.lean`, line 159).
- **Sup norm from torsion values** (`GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.Sl.Sup.mean_log_sup`): the values of `θ` at
  torsion points are integral, which also needs the action (`IntTors.integral_torsVal`,
  `src/Development/…/GeometryProofs/TorsionValueIntegral.lean`, line 727). Inside the proof this bounds
  `mean_σ ρ_σ⁻²`.
- **Every vanishing order up to n/3** (`GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.Sl.Jump.lefschetz_direction`): for each
  `i ≤ n/3`, a theta function of level `n` vanishing to order exactly `i` along a given direction. This makes the rows
  of the jet matrix independent.
- **Integral jets and the filtered product formula** (`GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.FlagSlope.flag_slope`): only the
  first non-vanishing undivided derivative is integral (`JetMain.jets`,
  `src/Development/…/GeometryProofs/ExistsIntegralJetBasis.lean`, line 239), and the criterion needs no more.
- **Choice of the power and assembly** (`GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.Sl.LineProof.line_of_key`): the power is
  `n = 9·⌈2 + g² + h⁺⌉²`. The logarithm comes from paying the norm of `θ` once for `n/3 + 1` vanishing orders.

## Caveats

The main declaration is a theorem, with a proof, of the implication above. The bound itself (`SlopeBoundSt`) is the
definition of a proposition, and we found no declaration proving it without the Siegel hypothesis. We read in full the
reduction, `line_of_key`, `keyDeg_of_keyBad`, `mean_log_sup`, `flag_slope`, `lefschetz_direction` and the Cauchy
estimate, and the main parts of `jets`, `Basis.main` and `integral_torsVal`. Of the key formula and its integrality at
bad reduction, the two uniformisations, `nform`, the base-change lemmas and the derivation of the Siegel lemma we read
only the statements. A text search of the whole source found `sorry` only in `src/Challenge.lean`, and no `axiom`
declaration. Nothing was compiled. The form of the bound for every principally polarised abelian variety is not proved:
`GlobalTheory.LieHt.LieSlope`, with a constant `C(g)`, is a proposition derived from three statements taken as
hypotheses. We have not checked that the class of the statement is non-empty.
