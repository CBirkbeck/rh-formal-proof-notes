## Statement

Let `G` be a unimodular second-countable locally compact Hausdorff group with Haar measure `μ`, and `σ` a strongly
continuous unitary representation on a Hilbert space `H` with `∫_G ⟨b, σ(x)a⟩·conj⟨b′, σ(x)a′⟩ dμ = ⟨a′, a⟩⟨b, b′⟩/d`,
absolutely convergent, for some `d > 0` and all vectors (`⟨·,·⟩` antilinear on the left); `w ∈ H` a unit vector. Let
`S ⊆ G` be a closed subgroup with a Haar measure `μ_S` (written `ds`; extended by zero to `G`; invariant on both sides
and under inversion) such that for compact `K ⊆ S` and `ε > 0` there is a measurable `Ω ⊆ S` with `0 < μ_S(Ω) < ∞` and
`μ_S(Ω) ≤ (1 + ε)·μ_S{t ∈ Ω : Kt ⊆ Ω}`; `ν` an s-finite measure on `G` with `∫_G f dμ = ∫∫ f(sx) ds dν(x)` for
measurable `f ≥ 0` (in place of the measure of `S\G`); `τ` a unitary representation of `S` on a finite-dimensional `V`;
`W : G → V` continuous with `W(sx) = τ(s)W(x)`, `∫‖W‖ dν < ∞`, `∫‖W‖² dν = 1` and
`∫_G d·conj⟨w, σ(y)w⟩·W(xy) dμ(y) = W(x)`, absolutely convergent, for every `x`. Let `S′, μ_{S′}, ν′, τ′, W′ : G → V′`
be a second such system, `(C, m)` a measure space, and `h : C → G` measurable with
`∫_G φ dμ = ∫_C∫∫ φ(s·h(c)·s′⁻¹) ds′ ds dm(c)` for measurable `φ ≥ 0`. Put
`O(γ) = ∫_G W(x) ⊗ conj W′(γ⁻¹x) dμ(x) : V′ → V` and `κ(g) = κ_{z,z′}(g) = |⟨σ(z)w, σ(g)σ(z′)w⟩|`. Assume for all
`γ = h(c)`: (i) `O(γ)` converges absolutely; (ii) `∫∫∫∫ κ(sγs′⁻¹)·‖W(z)‖·‖W′(z′)‖ ds′ ds dν′(z′) dν(z) < ∞`; and assume
(iii) `∫∫∫ κ(s)·‖W(z)‖·‖W(z′)‖ ds dν(z′) dν(z) < ∞`, likewise for `(S′, W′, ν′)`. Then the lower integral
`∫_C ‖O(h(c))‖²_HS dm(c)` is at most `1/d` (measurability in `c` is not asserted).

## Why it might matter

The left side is the total `L²` mass, over a parameter space for the double cosets `S·γ·S′` that disintegrates Haar
measure, of the orbital integrals of two functions that are equivariant under `S` and `S′` and reproduced by a
coefficient of one representation satisfying the orthogonality relations. In the source it is applied to Bessel
functions on real unitary groups; how the bound is used further on was not read. The statement uses no structure theory
of `G` and allows `S ≠ S′`. For `S = S′ = {1}` and integrable coefficients of `σ` the hypotheses hold and the orthogonality relations give equality (a direct computation; the source has no declaration for it).

## What the checks found

The first reading compared a differently phrased statement: `σ` irreducible and square-integrable, amenable subgroups,
quotient measures on `S\G`. Here the Schur relations are assumed (a separate theorem derives them from those
properties), a Følner condition replaces amenability and an unfolding identity on `G` replaces quotients; the
conclusion, with `1/d`, is the same, so the verdict stands. Its verdict, incomparable with medium confidence: the
abstract inequality was not found, but where the literature treats such settings it holds with equality, as the
discrete-series term of a local relative trace formula and without amenability (Beuzart-Plessis, covering, by the first
reading's deduction, the proof's instance for `S = S′` and one type, in iterated form; Feigon; Zhang). Its search was
cut short. A forward search added a local Kuznetsov formula of Beuzart-Plessis (equality for opposite unipotents of
quasi-split groups over local fields, real ones too) and found no abstract statement; one paper that might bear on it
could not be opened. The second opinion agrees, incomparable with medium confidence: more general groups, but
substantial convergence and disintegration hypotheses and only an inequality. Corrections: "literal instance" and
blanket equality claims overreach, since matching normalised `W`, `W′`, vector-valued types, measures and convergence
needs further argument; opposite unipotents already give two different amenable subgroups. The readings differ on how
far the published equalities reach, the proof's instance included. Open: does equality hold whenever the integrals
converge absolutely, for two Bessel data and vector-valued types?

## How the formal proof goes

The functions `W`, `W′` behave like `x ↦ Λ(σ(x)w)` for functionals `Λ` that need not be given by vectors. The proof does not
introduce such functionals; it works with vectors obtained by truncation along subsets of `S` and `S′`.

Truncations. For a measurable `A ⊆ S` of finite measure and `e ∈ V` put `y_A(e) = ∫∫_A ⟨W(s⁻¹z), e⟩·σ(s⁻¹z)w dμ_S(s) dν(z)`,
a vector of `H` (`yv`); the integral converges because `∫‖W‖ dν < ∞` and `μ_S(A) < ∞`. Let `Y_A : V → H` be the linear map
`e ↦ y_A(e)` (`Yop`), `Y′_{A′}` the same for the second system, and `X_{A,A′}(γ) = d·Y_A^*·σ(γ)·Y′_{A′} : V′ → V` (`Xop`).

(1) Energy (`lintegral_hsA_sand`, `lintegral_hsA_Xop`). The orthogonality relations give
`∫_G ‖X_{A,A′}(g)‖²_HS dμ(g) = d·‖Y_A‖²_HS·‖Y′_{A′}‖²_HS`. This is the only use of the relations.

(2) Norms along Følner sets (`folner_seq`, `sumsq_eq`, `Fk_value`, `folner_limit`). From the Følner condition and a compact
exhaustion of `G` cut down to the closed set `S`, a sequence `Ω_n ⊆ S` is extracted with
`μ_S{t ∈ Ω_n : Kt ⊆ Ω_n}/μ_S(Ω_n) → 1` for every compact `K ⊆ S`. Expanding the square, with `W(sx) = τ(s)W(x)` and unitarity,
`‖Y_Ω‖²_HS = ∫∫∫ μ_S{t ∈ Ω : qt ∈ Ω}·F(z, z′, q) dμ_S(q) dν(z′) dν(z)`, `F(z, z′, q) = ⟨W(qz′), W(z)⟩·⟨σ(z)w, σ(qz′)w⟩`
(the image of `μ_S|Ω × μ_S|Ω` under `(s, t) ↦ st⁻¹` is computed in `conv_map`, by right invariance of `μ_S`). After division
by `μ_S(Ω_n)` the weight lies in `[0, 1]` and tends to `1` for each `q ∈ S`, and `|F|` is integrable by (iii): dominated
convergence applies. The limit `∫∫∫ F` is computed by folding `(z′, q) ↦ qz′` back into `G`: the inner integral over `G` is
`‖W(z)‖²/d` by the reproducing identity for `W`, and `∫‖W‖² dν = 1`. Hence `‖Y_{Ω_n}‖²_HS/μ_S(Ω_n) → 1/d`.

(3) Approximation (`integral_kG`, `integrable_kG`, `Xop_eq_integral`, `approx_OO`). The kernel
`k_γ(x, x′) = d·⟨σ(x)w, σ(γ)σ(x′)w⟩·W(x) ⊗ conj W′(x′)` on `G × G` has integral `O(γ)`: integrating in `x` first, the
reproducing identity gives `W(γx′)`. With each factor `G` unfolded along `S` or `S′` by the identity for `ν` or `ν′`, its
absolute integrability is hypothesis (ii). `X_{A,A′}(γ)` is the integral of `k_γ` over the part where the coordinates in
`S`, `S′` lie in `A`, `A′`. So `O(γ) − X_{A,A′}(γ)` is a tail: for `ε > 0` there are compact pieces `K ⊆ S`, `K′ ⊆ S′` with
`‖O(γ) − X_{A,A′}(γ)‖_HS ≤ ε` for all `A ⊇ K`, `A′ ⊇ K′`.

Pointwise lower bound (`sigma_Yop`, `hsA_Xop_translate`, `liminf_lower`, `point_lower`). Equivariance gives
`σ(s⁻¹)Y_Ω = Y_{Ωs}·τ(s)^*` for `s ∈ S`, so `‖X_{Ω,Ω′}(sγs′⁻¹)‖_HS = ‖X_{Ωs,Ω′s′}(γ)‖_HS`; and `Ωs ⊇ K` when `s⁻¹` lies in
`{t ∈ Ω : Kt ⊆ Ω}`. By (3), `‖X_{Ω_n,Ω′_n}(sγs′⁻¹)‖_HS ≥ ‖O(γ)‖_HS − ε` on a set of pairs `(s, s′)` whose measure is asymptotic
to `μ_S(Ω_n)·μ_{S′}(Ω′_n)` (Følner condition; invariance of the measures under inversion). Therefore
`‖O(γ)‖²_HS ≤ liminf_n (μ_S(Ω_n)·μ_{S′}(Ω′_n))⁻¹ ∫∫ ‖X_{Ω_n,Ω′_n}(sγs′⁻¹)‖²_HS dμ_{S′}(s′) dμ_S(s)`.

Conclusion (`energyAbs_holds`). Take `γ = h(c)` and integrate over `C`. By Fatou's lemma the left side is at most the lower
limit of the integrals of the right side; by the disintegration hypothesis these are
`(μ_S(Ω_n)·μ_{S′}(Ω′_n))⁻¹ ∫_G ‖X_{Ω_n,Ω′_n}‖²_HS dμ`, which by (1) and (2) tend to `d·(1/d)·(1/d) = 1/d`. The loss to an
inequality comes from Fatou's lemma and the lower bound; no equality statement is proved in this generality.

Inputs. The proof uses Mathlib only. The orthogonality relations and the Følner condition are hypotheses. A separate
theorem of the source derives the relations, for some `d > 0`, from irreducibility and square-integrability of all
coefficients (`Formalism.MainNotions.BridgeCSchur.schur_orth`, `src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/CuspSpanNormBounds/BridgeCSchur.lean`,
line 313). Hypothesis (i) is named when the hypotheses are introduced and is not referred to again in the proof.

The instance in the source. `realObj_inInfinityX_ofG` applies the theorem to a product over real places of groups
`U(J_ε)`, a tensor product of discrete series, subgroups attached to two words `u, u′` whose measures are images of
product measures `μ_N × μ_H` (by their names the Bessel subgroups `N ⋊ U(k)`; not checked), the Bessel functions
`W_u, W_{u′}`, and a parametrisation of the big cell by the diagonal of a Gram matrix of two frames, with an explicit
density. It asserts that the disintegration holds with a positive constant, that the orbital integrals converge on the
parameter set, and the bound `1/∏_v d_v`. This theorem has the numerical gap condition `GapHyp` as a hypothesis
(`r ≥ 1801` and, at each place, consecutive parameters `b_j` of the data at least `16·r¹⁹` apart), and conditions
(i)–(iii) as two more explicit hypotheses (`StepIntG`, `StepConvG`, each stated for all data with that gap); other files
derive these two from a uniqueness statement for Bessel maps of discrete series with a gap, itself a theorem of the
source (`uniqGap_holds`). The equality for coefficients (the weighted `L²` norm over the big cell of the Bessel integral
of a coefficient `F` is `c·|s(F)|²/d`) is a separate theorem, `bessel_setting_uniform_constant`, under a gap condition
and a bundled hypothesis `HypsG`, which `hypsG_of_zuck` derives from a further statement that is itself a theorem of the
source (`zuckerman_gap`).

## Key steps

- **Energy of the truncated operators** (`lintegral_hsA_sand`): `∫_G ‖Y^*σ(g)Y′‖²_HS dμ = ‖Y‖²_HS·‖Y′‖²_HS/d` for bounded
  `Y : V → H`, `Y′ : V′ → H`. The only point where the orthogonality relations enter, and the origin of the constant `1/d`.
- **Truncated vectors along Følner sets** (`folner_limit`): `‖Y_{Ω_n}‖²_HS/μ_S(Ω_n) → 1/d`. It uses (iii) for dominated
  convergence, the Følner condition for one-point sets, and the reproducing identity with `∫‖W‖² dν = 1` for the limit.
- **Truncations approximate the orbital integral** (`approx_OO`): `‖O(γ) − X_{A,A′}(γ)‖_HS ≤ ε` once `A`, `A′` contain large
  compact pieces of `S`, `S′`. It rests on writing `O(γ)` as the integral of a kernel on `G × G`, absolutely convergent by (ii).
- **Pointwise lower bound** (`point_lower`): `‖O(γ)‖²_HS` is at most the lower limit of the normalised averages of
  `‖X_{Ω_n,Ω′_n}(sγs′⁻¹)‖²_HS` over `S × S′`. Translating `γ` amounts to translating the truncation sets; the invariance of
  `μ_S`, `μ_{S′}` on both sides and under inversion is used here.
- **Fatou's lemma and disintegration** (`energyAbs_holds`): the three facts are combined with Fatou's lemma and the
  disintegration of Haar measure along `h`. This is where an equality, if there is one, is lost.
- **Instance for real unitary groups** (`realObj_inInfinityX_ofG`): supplies the hypotheses for discrete series and
  Bessel subgroups; the gap condition and the convergence conditions (i)–(iii) remain explicit hypotheses of this
  declaration.

## What was checked, and what was not

`energyAbs_holds` is a theorem with a complete tactic proof. Its type `EnergyAbs` is a definition that unfolds to the statement
above, every hypothesis being an antecedent and none carried by a structure. The proof and the files it rests on (eighteen
files, about 2,150 lines, importing only Mathlib and one another) were read in full. A text search found no `sorry` and no
`axiom` in them, nor in the 2,085 Lean files of `src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/SiegelLatticeTheory`. Nothing was compiled and no
axiom report was produced. For the instance, the statement and the instantiation (`stepEnergyAt_of_inst`, `inInfinityXAt`)
were read; the suppliers were taken as statements only: orthogonality relations for discrete series, the Følner condition for
Bessel subgroups, the disintegration on the big cell, conditions (i)–(iii), uniqueness of Bessel maps, and the equality for
coefficients. The instance is stated for a structure whose fields include the properties of `W_u`; its construction was not read.
