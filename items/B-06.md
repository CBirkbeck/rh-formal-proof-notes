## Statement

Over a CM extension `E/F`, with `G = U(n, n)`, a Hermitian space of dimension `m` gives a Siegel–Weil section at
`s₀ = (m − n)/2`. The formal statements of B and of the first two cases of A are for the CM fields of the proof's
standing data only (`E` Galois over `ℚ` and embedded in a cyclotomic field, `F` its maximal real subfield, with further
arithmetic conditions); the definite case of A, and C, are for any CM extension satisfying the stated conditions on the
base. A: the Siegel–Weil formula where the theta integral converges: `m > 2n`, all Schwartz data; `n < m ≤ 2n` under
Weil's convergence criterion (isotropic spaces included), Fock data at the real places; spaces definite at the real
places, all `m, n ≥ 1`, `K`-finite data, under conditions on the base, constant `2` when `m ≤ n`. B: for Hermitian `W`
of rank `r ≥ 1`, `t > r` and `n = r + t`, given a continuation to a neighbourhood of `t/2` of the Siegel Eisenstein
series of a Siegel–Weil section of `W ⊥ ℍ^t` (a hypothesis of the statement; the continuation is proved separately),
that series has at most a simple pole at `t/2`, and its residue is annihilated by every finite measure carried by a
relatively compact part of `G(𝔸)` that annihilates all theta functions of `W`. C: for Hermitian `V`, `V′` of dimensions
`a > a′` of equal parity and a non-split skew-Hermitian `W ≅ V ⊕ V′(−1)`, under conditions on signatures, characters and
the base, the theta integral of `V` is a unique nonzero constant times that of `V′` on matched test vectors. A gives
cases of theorems of Weil, Kudla–Rallis, Ichino and Yamana; B is weaker than the first term identity of the regularised
formula; C is not a case of it.

## Why it might matter

In the published proofs as the first reading knew them from later accounts, the regularised formula and its first term
identity rest on regularised theta integrals, and the simple pole on intertwining operators and degenerate principal
series; the second opinion points to a published proof of simple poles by Fourier–Jacobi descent (Ikeda 1996,
Proposition 7.2). B is what the formal proof uses where the published route would quote the first term identity: with a
doubling zeta integral that has a pole at `t/2`, it shows that some theta lift to `U(W)` of a form on a definite unitary
group is nonzero. C concerns a unitary group with no Siegel parabolic.

## What the checks found

The first reading gives three verdicts, resting more on statements and assemblies than on leaf proofs, and on later
accounts of the original papers. A, standard proof, specialised (medium-low confidence): Weil's range by fibre measures
and their uniqueness, the constant fixed fibre by fibre by a dilation argument in rank one (the shape of Siegel's proof,
by recollection); `n < m ≤ 2n` by the Kudla–Rallis comparison of Fourier coefficients, nonsingularity done at a real
place; definite `m ≤ n` by induction on `n`, not Yamana's route. B, narrower, not a new proof of the first term identity
(medium-low): Fourier–Jacobi coefficients as in Ikeda and Wu, shortened by `t > r`, with a rank cut and measure
uniqueness; the simple pole without intertwining operators it did not find in what it read, and could not settle without
Ikeda (1996). C (medium): not a case of a published identity, and an earlier search found no such statement; published
devices plus a base case and closing step like nothing it found in what it read. The second opinion: A known in spirit
(medium confidence) and B known in spirit (B with high confidence on its architecture, lower on the exact lemmas:
Ikeda's Proposition 7.2 proves simple poles by Fourier–Jacobi descent, though not by this unitary cut); C cannot tell
(medium confidence; its overall verdict is also cannot tell), as lacking a Siegel parabolic does not exclude a
derivation by doubling, seesaws or theta-lifting injectivity. They disagree on B's pole and on C. Open: does Ikeda's
descent give B's pole, and can C be derived by doubling, seesaws or theta-lifting injectivity?

## How the formal proof goes

A, Weil's range. Both sides are cut by rank and, by Poisson summation over the Hermitian matrices, the formula becomes
Siegel's formula for each Gram matrix `β`. For nondegenerate `β` the theta side is a `U(V)(𝔸)`-invariant locally finite
measure on the fibre `{Gram = β}`, hence a constant times the product of local fibre measures. The constant is `1` by
induction on the rank, splitting off a rank-one block; in rank one (`m ≥ 3`), for the lattices `a⁻¹L`, the theta measure
of the fibre differs from the product of local densities by at most `C·a^{[F:ℚ]m}`, while that product is at least
`κ·a^{[F:ℚ](2m − 2)}` for infinitely many `a`. Degenerate `β` reduce to smaller rank (not read further).

A, `n < m ≤ 2n`. The nondegenerate Fourier coefficients of the continued Eisenstein series at `s₀` are computed by
unfolding and equal those of the theta integral. The singular ones are removed at a real place: for the vacuum vector
of a suitable `K`-type, a compactly supported `ψ` on Hermitian matrices whose Fourier transform vanishes at singular
matrices, and two group elements, give an operator that multiplies the vacuum section by a nonzero constant, so a
function of that type with only singular coefficients vanishes; the first fundamental theorem for the unitary group
passes from the vacuum to all polynomial Fock vectors. A, definite spaces: `n < m` as above; `m = n` from the
nondegenerate coefficients (factor `2`); `m < n` by induction on `n`. No functional equation and no regularised formula
are used.

B. (1) The Fourier–Jacobi coefficient for a nondegenerate `b₀` of size `ρ`, paired with a theta function, is a Siegel
Eisenstein series on `U(n − ρ, n − ρ)`. (2) For `ρ > r` it converges at `t/2`, so polar coefficients have Fourier
coefficients of rank `≤ r` only; for `ρ = r` the group is `U(t, t)` at its edge of convergence, where a direct estimate
bounds the spherical series by a constant times `(σ − t/2)⁻¹`. (3) At a split place a finite combination `C` of
unipotent translations has Fourier transform vanishing in rank `< r` and, averaged over `K_v`, acts on `K_v`-invariant
sections by an entire scalar `c(z)` with `c(t/2) ≠ 0` (a positivity computation). (4) `C` kills functions whose
coefficients all have rank `< r`, so descending induction removes all orders `≥ 2`. (5) The rank-`r` coefficients of the
residue give a positive invariant measure on an orbit whose singular points are null and on whose rest the group acts
transitively, so it is unique up to a scalar; the theta side gives one of the same kind, and the coefficient is `κ`
times an orbital integral over `U(W)`, `κ` not shown nonzero. (6) Comparison of Fourier coefficients and weak
approximation make the residue a multiple of a `K_v`-average of theta integrals of `W`; Fubini gives the annihilation.

C, as far as the names and dependencies of its assembly show (the leaf proofs were not read, and the steps below were
not checked against them): local matching of the two theta quotients of the trivial representation at every place
(matched pairs share an irreducible quotient of the two local Weil representations); convergence from test vectors of
full rank at one split place, replaced in the form used later by an element of the group algebra at an auxiliary place,
with no auxiliary Eisenstein series; the `L²` pairing factors through an irreducible quotient; nonvanishing from the
Rallis inner product formula (which uses A for definite spaces) and up the tower by constant terms; a base case with all
groups compact at infinity, where the back-lift of a difference is constant, hence zero; an induction removing
hyperbolic planes. No multiplicity one for automorphic representations of `U(W)` is used.

## Key steps

- **Rank-one constant** (`CompanionTheory.PlacePermOperatorBicommutantSpan.sw_one`): in Weil's range, the constant `1`
  for a rank-one Gram matrix and `m ≥ 3`, by the dilation asymptotic; the induction on the rank starts here.
- **Vacuum test at a real place** (statement `PositivityTheory.Rg.Sw.LV`, proved as `PositivityTheory.Rg.Sw.lv`):
  smearing the vacuum section by `ψ` along the unipotent radical and projecting to its `K`-type gives a nonzero multiple
  of it; for `n < m ≤ 2n` this takes the place of the published use of an irreducible local module.
- **Polar part** (statement `PositivityTheory.Rg.Rsw.Polar`, proved as `PositivityTheory.Rg.Rsw.polar`): bounds, through
  Fourier–Jacobi coefficients, the rank of the Fourier coefficients of each polar coefficient at `t/2`, and describes
  the rank-`r` part of the residue; the source of B's pole bound.
- **The rank cut** (statement `PositivityTheory.Rg.Rsw.L.JPos`, proved by
  `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Jp.jPos_of_idemEx`): the split-place operator with Fourier transform vanishing in low
  rank and nonzero scalar at `t/2`; it gives the simple pole without intertwining operators.
- **Uniqueness on the orbit** (statement `PositivityTheory.Rg.Rsw.L.Uniq`, proved by
  `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.OrbUniq.uniq_of_trans`): an invariant measure of the right homogeneity on the
  nonsingular orbit is unique up to a scalar; it matches the rank-`r` part of the residue with theta integrals of `W`.
- **Assembly of C** (`PositivityTheory.Sph.Tr.closeSph`, with `PositivityTheory.ThKG.Leaves`): combines matching,
  convergence, nonvanishing, the base case and the tower steps; read only at this level.

## What was checked, and what was not

A was read at the level of statements and assemblies, with the rank-one dilation lemma, the vacuum test and the abstract
theorem behind it read in full; B's statement and all its leaf statements were read, their proofs in outline; C only at
the level of its assembly. Nothing was compiled. No `sorry` or `axiom` occurs in the directories read (over 2,600
files); the first term identity appears in the source only as unproved statements and hypotheses of conditional lemmas,
but whether the main theorems depend on any of these declarations was not verified. A statement for every anisotropic
space exists only as a definition and as a hypothesis (a field of hypothesis bundles, an argument of conditional
theorems); no proof of it was found. Not opened by the first reading: Weil (1965), Kudla–Rallis (1988 I, II; 1994),
Ichino (2004, 2007), Ikeda (1994, 1996), Yamana (2014), Tan's paper on the poles for `U(n, n)`; its comparison for A and
B rests on later papers' descriptions of them. The second opinion checked Ikeda (1996).
