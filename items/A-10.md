## Statement

Let `K` be a totally complex number field with `r` infinite places, and `I` a nonempty finite set. A *lattice* is a
finite-dimensional `K`-vector space with a finitely generated `O_K`-submodule spanning it and a Hermitian norm at each
infinite place (in the source, a `K`-basis and, at each place, an invertible complex matrix whose columns are
orthonormal). Let `N_i ⊂ V_i` (`i ∈ I`, `d_i = dim V_i`, `R = Σ d_i > 0`) and `C ⊂ W` (`n = dim W`) be lattices, and
`φ_i : V_i → W` `K`-linear maps with `φ_i(N_i) ⊂ C`. Write `μ`, `μ_max`, `μ_min` for twice the normalised Arakelov
slope, maximal slope and minimal slope, and `h(φ) = (1/r)·Σ_w log ‖φ‖_w²` (operator norms). Let `h_i` be reals with
`h(φ_i) ≤ h_i` whenever `φ_i ≠ 0`.

At one infinite place `v`, let `Y_i`, `Y′` be invertible complex matrices, `D ≥ n` an integer, and `A_i` complex
`D × d_i` matrices whose first `n` rows are `Y′⁻¹·[φ_i]_v·Y_i`, where `[φ_i]_v` is the matrix of `φ_i` under the
embedding of `v`. Let `γ_i > 0`, and let `0 ≤ δ < 1`, `0 ≤ e ≤ 1/4`, `η ≥ 0` satisfy `(1 − δ)·D ≤ n`,
`Σ_i ‖γ_i A_i^*A_i − 1‖²_HS ≤ e·R` and `tr(Q²) ≤ (1 + η)·(tr Q)²/D`, where `Q = Σ_i γ_i A_i A_i^*`. Let `s` be real,
and let `u ≥ 0`, `H ≥ 0` satisfy `R·s ≤ Σ_i d_i·(μ(N_i) − h_i)`, `μ_max(N_i) − h_i ≤ s + u` for all `i`, and
`s − μ_min(C) ≤ H`. Then `μ(C) ≥ s − (1 − δ)⁻¹·(4u·log(1 + H/u) + (η + 4√e)·H)`, where the term in `u` is `0` if
`u = 0`.

In the Lean source this is the theorem `frame_theorem_sharp`, with a complete proof. Its statement is the proposition
`TheoremFSharp`.

## Context

The inequality bounds the mean slope of a Hermitian lattice from below by the mean slope of several lattices mapped
into it, less the heights of the maps and explicit error terms. No map need be injective or surjective. Instead a
spanning condition is imposed at a single archimedean place, with defects `e` and `η` that need not be small against
the ranks. The bound is not always better than the trivial inequality `μ(C) ≥ μ_min(C)`. Two later files of the source
use it through `joint_a_ramified_holds`, a form with `log|I|` subtracted.

## Comparison with the literature

We made the comparison with a wider form: any number field, arbitrary linear maps, heights at all places, `e < 1`, the
constant `2/(1 − √e)`, which is 2 for exact frames where the Lean has 4, and a codimension bound. So our finding that
nothing published implies it concerned a stronger statement than the one here. An independent second check, made on
the statement here, found that its extra restrictions do not bring it within reach of any published result. We regard
it as not comparable with published results. We know of no published result that takes an approximate tight frame at
one place as a hypothesis. Where both apply, that is for exact frames, Chen (2009, Theorem 4.2) and the filtration
route give more, the latter with no error term by an argument of our own. The frame step is a variant of Gurvits's
lemma (Theorem 2.12 of Garg, Gurvits, Oliveira, Wigderson). By our own hand computation, with the constant 4 the bound
beats `μ(C) ≥ μ_min(C)` only when `H/u > 9.35`. Working from citation lists, without a search of the wider literature,
we found the exact case of the frame lemma with constant 1 (Bennett–Carbery–Christ–Tao, Chindris–Derksen), but no
slope inequality under a frame hypothesis. This did not change our view.

The second check also agrees that the results for exact frames are stronger on their domain, while the approximate
hypotheses reach beyond it. We did not find this precise combination in print, and we took it to be absent. The
second check finds that the evidence does not support this and regards the question as unsettled. That is our only
disagreement. The right comparison with Bost–Chen is their slope inequality, not a degree inequality from that paper.
We would like to know whether the coefficient of `ε` in the frame lemma can tend to 1 as the defects vanish, and
whether a slope inequality under approximate frames is in print.

## Sketch of the proof

`frame_theorem_sharp` is `theoremFSharp_of` applied to the frame lemma `frame_linear_algebra_first`. The statement is
homogeneous in `(μ, h, s, u, H)`, so the factor 2 plays no role. A lattice is made Euclidean by taking real and
imaginary parts of the coordinates of its vectors at all infinite places. `place_lattice_structure` shows that a
finitely generated `O_K`-submodule becomes discrete, of `ℤ`-rank `[K:ℚ]` times its `K`-rank `rk`. Degrees are
`−log covol`. The step `L_{≥θ}` (`hnStep`) is the largest `O_K`-submodule of minimal slope `≥ θ`, and `hnStep_of` shows,
from supermodularity of the degree, that it is saturated and contains every nonzero submodule of minimal slope `≥ θ`.
The source also proves that the Harder–Narasimhan slopes, listed with multiplicity, sum to the degree, that the number
of them `≥ θ` is the rank of `L_{≥θ}`, and that they lie between `μ_min` and `μ_max`.

Step 1 is a count (`thmF_share`). Fix `τ ≥ 0` and let `N_i(τ)` be the step of `N_i` at level `s − τ + h_i`. The numbers
`μ_j(N_i) − h_i`, over all `i` and all slopes `j`, have mean `≥ s` and are `≤ s + u`, so at most the proportion
`u/(u + τ)` of them is `< s − τ`. That is, `Σ_i (d_i − rk N_i(τ)) ≤ R·u/(u + τ)`.

Step 2 concerns images of steps (`slope_inequality_mean`). For a `K`-linear `φ` and a lattice `A` with `φ(A) ≠ 0`,
`μ_min(A) − h(φ) ≤ μ_min(φ(A))`. The source proves this first for a map of Euclidean lattices that multiplies norms by
at most a constant (`slope_inequality_general`, where saturated sublattices of the image are pulled back and the
covolumes of the quotients are compared through Gram determinants). It then makes the operator norms at all places
equal by rescaling the norms of the target by factors of product one, which changes no slope (`twist_of`). With
`h(φ_i) ≤ h_i` and `φ_i(N_i) ⊂ C`, each `φ_i(N_i(τ))` has minimal slope `≥ s − τ`, hence lies in `C_{≥ s−τ}`.

Step 3 bounds a rank at one place (`thmF_place`). If `O_K`-submodules `N′_i ⊂ V_i` have
`Σ_i (d_i − rk N′_i) ≤ ε·R`, then `n − rk Σ_i φ_i(N′_i) ≤ (η + 4ε + 4√e)·D`. The `ℂ`-span `U_i` of the vectors
`Y_i⁻¹·x_v`, `x ∈ N′_i`, where `x_v` is the coordinate vector of `x` under the embedding of `v`, has dimension
`rk N′_i` (`finrank_span_complex`). The matrix `Y′⁻¹[φ_i]_v Y_i` maps it into the like span for `Σ_i φ_i(N′_i)`, and
this matrix is `A_i` followed by the coordinate projection `P : ℂ^D → ℂ^n`. With `S = Σ_i A_i(U_i)`,
`dim P(S) ≥ dim S − (D − n) = n − dim S^⊥`, and the frame lemma bounds `dim S^⊥`.

The frame lemma is as follows. Let `B_i : V_i → ℋ` be linear maps of finite-dimensional complex inner-product spaces,
`D = dim ℋ`, satisfying the two conditions of the statement (with `B_i` for `A_i`), and let `W_i ⊂ V_i` be subspaces of
total codimension `≤ ε·R`. Then `m ≤ (η + 4ε + 4√e)·D`, where `m = dim 𝒲` and `𝒲 = (Σ_i B_i(W_i))^⊥`. Put `t = tr Q`
and `T = Σ_l ⟨g_l, Q g_l⟩` for an orthonormal basis `(g_l)` of `𝒲`. From below, `T ≥ (t/D)·m − √m·‖Q − t/D‖_HS`, and
the trace condition says `‖Q − t/D‖²_HS ≤ η·t²/D`. From above, `B_i^* g_l ⊥ W_i`, so `T` is at most the sum of the
traces of `γ_i B_i^*B_i` on the spaces `W_i^⊥`, which is `≤ R·(ε + √(ε·e))` by the first condition and
Cauchy–Schwarz. That condition also gives `t ≥ R·(1 − √e) ≥ R/2`. `final_real1` combines these, absorbing the
Hilbert–Schmidt term by `√(m·ηD) ≤ (m + ηD)/2`.

Step 4 is an integration. By Steps 1 to 3 with `ε = u/(u + τ)` and `D ≤ n/(1 − δ)`, the proportion of slopes of `C`
below `s − τ` is at most `c(τ) = (1 − δ)⁻¹·(η + 4u/(u + τ) + 4√e)`, and all of them are `≥ μ_min(C) ≥ s − H`.
`layer_cake_formula` gives `μ(C) ≥ s − ∫_0^H min(1, c(τ)) dτ`, and `∫_0^H 4u/(u + τ) dτ = 4u·log(1 + H/u)`.

Two companion results, `compressTraceSquareBound` and `compressDiagDefectBound`, derive the two conditions from bounds
on pairs. Suppose `‖√(γ_iγ_j)·B_i^*B_j‖²_HS ≤ (1 + η₀)·d_i d_j/D*` for `i ≠ j`, `‖γ_i B_i^*B_i − 1‖²_HS ≤ e₀·d_i`,
`D ≤ (1 + η₁)·D*` and `R ≥ D*/ϖ`, with `D* > 0`, `0 < ϖ ≤ 1/20`, `−1 ≤ η₀ ≤ ϖ/2`, `η₁ ≤ ϖ/2`, `√e₀ ≤ ϖ/2`. Then the
restrictions of the `B_i` to subspaces of total codimension `≤ ϑ·R`, `0 ≤ ϑ ≤ 1/4`, satisfy the two conditions with
`η = 5ϖ + 3√ϑ` and `e = 2e₀`. Here `tr Q²` is the sum of the squared norms of the blocks `√(γ_iγ_j)·B_i^*B_j`, which
do not grow under restriction. `ramification_condition_holds` inserts this into the main theorem.

## Caveats

`frame_theorem_sharp` is a theorem with a complete proof, and `TheoremFSharp` is the definition of its statement (the
source defines each statement as a proposition and proves it in another file). We read in full the proofs of the
theorems listed in the header and of `slopeIneqMean_of`, `thmF_share`, `final_real1`, `layer_cake_formula`,
`slopeIneq_of`, `hnStep_of`, `twist_of`, `finrank_span_complex` and `place_lattice_structure`, and the lemmas they call
in part. We read only the statements of the slope list, covolumes, Gram determinants, saturation, supermodularity, and
of `height_gram_determinant` and `size_maximal_slope`, on which reading `μ` as twice the normalised slope rests. A text
search of the 55 files imported by the main file (81 for the combined form) found no `sorry` or `axiom`. We compiled
nothing. The variants `CorLin`, `JointA`, `FrameHerm` and `FrameHermSharp` are defined as propositions, and we found no
proof of them.
