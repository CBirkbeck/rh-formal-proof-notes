## Statement

Let `ℓ` be an odd prime and `L` a CM field, unramified over `L⁺` at all finite places, without a primitive `ℓ`-th root
of unity, with further conditions on a finite set of places of `L⁺` split in `L` and on an auxiliary place `v₀`
(`Nv₀ ≢ 1 mod ℓ`). Let `n ∈ {2, 3, 4}`, `χ̄_1, …, χ̄_n` pairwise distinct residual characters whose sum is conjugate
self-dual up to `ε̄^(1−n)`, and `B` the universal ring of ordinary polarized determinants lifting `det(⊕ χ̄_i)`. Let `x`
be a `ℚ̄_ℓ`-point of `B` with regular weights, and `ρ` a representation with the determinant of `x`, attached to a
regular algebraic, conjugate self-dual, cuspidal automorphic representation of `GL_n(𝔸_L)` (with two technical clauses),
upper triangular and generic at the places above `ℓ`, unramified at `v₀` (where the `χ̄_i` are unramified and coincide),
with coefficients in a finite extension of `ℚ_ℓ` containing the images of `L`, irreducible on `G_{L(μ_{ℓ^∞})}`, and such
that elements with `n` distinct eigenvalues are dense in `ρ(G_{L(μ_{ℓ^∞})})`. Given a bundle of background theorems,
which the linked theorem takes as its one hypothesis (see the last section), the formal proof shows
`H¹_f(L⁺, ad ρ) = 0`. This is a case of Thorne's theorem (2022, Theorem A, for which irreducibility suffices) and, by a
short deduction of the first reading, of Newton–Thorne (2023, Theorem A); the formal proof invokes neither.

## Why it might matter

The general vanishing theorem for adjoint Bloch–Kato Selmer groups is proved in the literature by Taylor–Wiles patching.
The formal proof establishes the case it needs (ordinary points, ranks two to four, residual sums of distinct
characters) itself, by the published method in the ordinary setting, with the patching limit replaced by a count of
derivations at one Taylor–Wiles level for each precision.

## What the checks found

The first reading found the statement to be a case of the published theorem, proved by the published method. It is a
special case of Theorem A of Newton–Thorne (and of Thorne's), established by their Taylor–Wiles argument at the
characteristic-zero point (determinants, Taylor–Wiles places chosen for the characteristic-zero representation,
comparison of levels up to a power of a discriminant, Selmer counts modulo powers of the uniformizer with uniform
constants), in the ordinary residually reducible setting of Thorne and of Allen–Newton–Thorne. Two steps vary the
published ones: the patching limit becomes a count of derivations at a single level for each precision, and the dual
Selmer group is controlled by a trace-and-density argument instead of enormous image and purity. Neither variation was
found in the literature; the first was not searched for beyond the papers listed. Confidence medium: §4.4 of
Newton–Thorne (the patching) was not read, and a reader who counts the removal of patching as essential could class
this as a different argument for a special case. That density and irreducibility give an enormous image is the first
reading's own deduction, unconfirmed. There is no second opinion. Open questions: is a finite-level count in place of
patching already known, and does the deduction of enormous image hold?

## How the formal proof goes

Reduction to the ordinary case. At a place above `ℓ`, with regular weights and genericity, a crystalline cocycle of
`ad ρ` is, up to a coboundary, upper triangular with zero diagonal on inertia; away from `ℓ` it vanishes on the inertia
elements killed residually. So it suffices that the ordinary adjoint Selmer group vanishes, equivalently that a
derivation of `B` at `x` killing the inertial characters above `ℓ` kills all traces.

From cocycles to derivations. After an integral normalisation of `ρ` and its pairing, an ordinary cocycle `z` gives a
point of `B` over the dual numbers, a derivation `δ` at `x` with `δ(tr g) = tr(z(g)ρ(g))`. If `δ` kills all traces, `z`
is a coboundary, since `ρ` spans `M_n` and derivations of a matrix algebra are inner.

The Hecke side. The big ordinary Hecke algebra of a definite unitary group, localised at the maximal ideal of
`⊕ χ̄_i`, carries a determinant; this gives an ideal `J ⊂ B` and a `B`-module `M`, finite free over the weight algebra
`Λ` (power series in `D = n[L⁺:ℚ]` variables), supported on `V(J)`. Automorphic arithmetic points of rank `2 ≤ n ≤ 4`
lie on `V(J)`; this is where the bound on the rank enters. Control and semisimplicity on classical forms give an
element `t` with `x(t) ≠ 0` such that `t·ker(x)` kills `M/𝔭_x M`.

Detection. By irreducibility and density, a cocycle `z` of `ad ρ ⊗ ε` with `tr(z(σ)ρ(σ)^k) = 0` for all `k` and all `σ`
in a suitable finite set of regular elements of `G_{L(μ_{ℓ^∞})}` is a coboundary: cocycles form a finite-dimensional
space, vanishing at all regular elements extends by density and continuity, irreducibility makes the first-order
deformation trivial there, and the rest is `H¹` of the cyclotomic quotient in a nontrivial character.

Taylor–Wiles data. For each level `N`, `q` split places of norm `≡ 1 mod ℓ^N` with Frobenius approximating the chosen
regular elements (residually the eigenvalues need not be distinct). At level `N`: the ordinary determinant ring `R_N`
with these places added; a module of Iwahori-type level, projective over `Λ[Δ_N]`, `Δ_N` the `ℓ`-part of the tori at
the `q` places; maps both ways to the module at hyperspecial level whose composites are multiplication by a product `f`
of differences of ordered Frobenius eigenvalues; and relations between diamond operators and traces of tame inertia
holding after multiplication by `f` and raising to the power `2·n!`.

The count. Hence there is `c ≠ 0` in `O` (the ring of integers of the coefficient field, with uniformizer `ϖ` and
residue field `k`), independent of `N` and of the precision `m`, such that for each `m` and all sufficiently large `N`
each of the `D + nq` directions (a weight variable or a generator of `Δ_N`) carries a derivation of `R_N` at `x` into
`O/ϖ^m` equal to `c` on that variable and `0` on the others. By the Greenberg–Wiles formula, with the dual Selmer group
bounded by the detection step, the trace functions of ordinary Selmer cocycles modulo `ϖ^m` at level `N` number at most
`|k|^(m(D + nq) + C)`, with `C` independent of `m` and `N`. Every derivation of `R_N` gives a Selmer cocycle with a
fixed constant times its trace function. If `δ` did not kill all traces, it would join the `D + nq` derivations to
inject `(O/ϖ^(m − v))^(D + nq + 1)`, `v` the valuation of a fixed nonzero element of `O`, into the trace functions,
which is impossible for large `m`. One level `N` is used for each `m`; no limit over `N` and no patched module is
formed. The hypothesis that `L` has no primitive `ℓ`-th root of unity is not used; it follows from `Nv₀ ≢ 1 mod ℓ`.

## Key steps

- **Reduction to the ordinary case** (`UpperTheory.V.Th.adjointLiftingTheorem_of_locPrints_of_core`, with the local
  input `UpperTheory.V.Th.bkOrd`): Bloch–Kato classes are ordinary at the places above `ℓ`.
- **Detection at regular elements** (`UpperTheory.V.Th.regDet`): finitely many regular elements of `G_{L(μ_{ℓ^∞})}`
  detect cocycles, by density and irreducibility, without purity. One of the two steps that differ from the published
  ones.
- **Taylor–Wiles places** (`UpperTheory.V.Pa.arPl`): split places of norm `≡ 1 mod ℓ^N`, chosen for the
  characteristic-zero representation.
- **Derivations dual to the variables** (`UpperTheory.V.Th.split`): one derivation into `O/ϖ^m` for each of the
  `D + nq` variables, with a constant independent of `N` and `m`.
- **Selmer bound** (`UpperTheory.V.Th.b1O`): at most `|k|^(m(D + nq) + C)` trace functions of ordinary Selmer cocycles
  modulo `ϖ^m`.
- **The count** (`UpperTheory.V.Th.count`): `D + nq + 1` independent derivations against that bound; this replaces the
  patching limit and is the other step that differs.

## What was checked, and what was not

The proved theorem has one hypothesis, `HypothesesH'`, a bundle of background theorems (`p`-adic representation theory,
base change, Galois representations of cuspidal representations and their purity, class field theory, duality, the
Greenberg–Wiles formula, the local Bloch–Kato computation); by name none is a patching, `R = T`, automorphy lifting or
adjoint vanishing statement, but only the field lists were read and the bundle's discharge was not audited. No `sorry`,
`axiom` or `opaque` was found by text search in the argument's directories; nothing was compiled. Read in full: the
statement and the chain down to the count, detection and the reduction; not read: the Hecke ideal, the point theorem
where `n ≤ 4` enters, the level-`N` modules, the Taylor–Wiles places, the Greenberg–Wiles counts. Not read: §4.4 and §5
of Newton–Thorne, §6.7 of Thorne (2022), Thorne (2015), Kisin, Breuil–Hellmann–Schraen, Pan.
