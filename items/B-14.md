## Statement

In the Laplace variable `u = log y`: let `g` be measurable with `|g(u)| ≤ C₀e^{2u}` for `u ≥ 0`, let `Z ⊂ {Re s > 0}`
have no accumulation point there, and let `Φ` be holomorphic on `{Re s > 0} ∖ Z`, equal to `∫₀^∞ g(u)e^{−su} du` for
`Re s > 2`, with a simple pole of residue `c ≠ 0` at each `ρ ∈ Z`, `c > 0` when `ρ` is real. Let `0 < θ < 1`, `δ > 0`.
If for every `η > 0` there are arbitrarily large `U` and `R ≥ 0` with `g ≤ R` on `[U, (1+δ)U]` and
`∫_U^{(1+δ)U} R(u)e^{−θu} du ≤ e^{ηU}`, then `Re ρ ≤ θ` for every `ρ ∈ Z`. It is applied to finitely many Dirichlet
characters `χ_i`, each of modulus 1 or non-principal, weights `a_i > 0`: if `G = Σ a_i Re G_{χ_i}`, with
`G_χ(y) = Σ_{n≤y} Λ(n)χ(n)(y/n − 1)` less its main term, has `G(y) ≤ Cy^θ` on `[Y, Y^{1+δ}]` for arbitrarily large `Y`,
no `L(s, χ_i)` vanishes in `Re s > θ`. This is not Landau's theorem nor a case of it, but plays its role (meromorphy
assumed, absence of poles concluded, bound only on windows); as a statement it is a special case (simple poles,
pointwise growth bound) of the window criterion that follows from Kaczorowski–Pintz (1986), Corollary 1; the first
reading takes this from a separate comparison of the statements and did not remake it. Separately, Landau's theorem for
generalised Dirichlet series `Σ c_i e^{−l_i s}` with `c_i, l_i ≥ 0`, and its form for the exponential of such a series,
are proved.

## Why it might matter

In the formal proof this criterion turns one-sided bounds for weighted prime sums on windows `[Y, Y^{1+δ}]` into
zero-free half-planes for Dirichlet `L`-functions, where Landau's theorem in its form for Mellin integrals would be
applied with a bound for all large `y`. It is proved directly, from neither Landau's theorem nor Kaczorowski–Pintz.

## What the checks found

The first reading finds that the windowed criterion is proved by an argument that differs from each published proof it
compared (Landau 1905, Kátai 1967, Kaczorowski–Pintz 1986/87): Kátai's scheme (twisted ≤ untwisted + window + tails) with
Gamma kernels `u^k e^{−σu}/k!` in place of a Gaussian, so that the smoothed integrals are single Taylor coefficients at `σ`
and `σ + iγ`, bounded by Cauchy's inequalities around a nearest pole; no contour shift, no power sums. It calls this a variant
of a known method, not a new method, and not a proof of Landau's theorem; in its own identification the nearest classical
relative is Pringsheim's proof as given by Landau. Confidence medium: high that the Lean is as described and differs from
the works read, medium to low that the variant is not in print (Pintz 1980 and Kaczorowski 1984 unread, three searches).
Landau's theorem for Dirichlet series is proved by Landau's own argument (high). The second opinion: known in spirit, medium
(known, high, for Landau's theorem). Its main point: reading high Laplace derivatives as localised Gamma averages is
classical (Post, Widder), and principal-part subtraction, Cauchy estimates, exponential tilting and perturbing the centre are
standard; it found no printed duplicate, but novelty is not established. They disagree on the windowed criterion. Open: do
Pintz (1980) or Kaczorowski (1984) contain this argument, and is there a printed proof through single Taylor coefficients?

## How the formal proof goes

Write `a_k(z) = (−1)^k Φ^{(k)}(z)/k!` and `w_k^a(u) = u^k e^{−au}/k!`.

1. For `Re z > 2`, `a_k(z) = ∫₀^∞ g(u) u^k e^{−zu}/k! du`, by differentiating under the integral sign.
2. The kernel `w_k^a` has mass `a^{−k−1}` and supremum at most `a^{−k}`. For the given `δ` there are `σ > 2` and a constant `C`
   such that for every `U ≥ 1` the index `k = ⌈(1+δ/2)σU⌉ ≥ U` has `∫ w_k^{σ−2} ≤ Cσ^{−k}` outside `[U, (1+δ)U]`, by an
   exponential tilt on each side.
3. Positivity: `|a_k(σ+iγ)| ≤ ∫|g| w_k^σ` and `a_k(σ) = ∫ g w_k^σ`, so `|a_k(σ+iγ)| + a_k(σ) ≤ ∫(|g| + g) w_k^σ`. On the window
   `|g| + g ≤ 2R` and `w_k^σ ≤ (σ−θ)^{−k} e^{−θu}`; off it `|g| + g ≤ 2C₀e^{2u}` leaves the tail mass of step 2. Hence for every
   `η`, arbitrarily large `U` and some `k ≥ U`: `|a_k(σ+iγ)| + a_k(σ) ≤ C′e^{ηU}(σ−θ)^{−k}`, uniformly in real `γ`.
4. No pole lies to the right of `Re s = 2`, so all poles lie to the left of `σ`.
5. Suppose a pole has real part `> θ`. Closed discs in `Re s > 0` contain finitely many poles. Either (B) the pole nearest
   to `σ` is a real `β > θ`, alone in a disc `B(σ, r)` with `σ − β < r ≤ σ − θ`; or (A) there are `D ≤ σ − θ` with no pole in
   `B(σ, D)` and a pole `ρ₁` with `σ − Re ρ₁ < D`; then for `γ` near `Im ρ₁`, avoiding the finitely many values at which two
   poles are equidistant from `σ + iγ`, one pole `ρ` is strictly nearest to `z = σ + iγ`, at distance `d < r ≤ D`, alone in
   `B(z, r)`.
6. Cauchy's inequalities, with `d < r′ < r`: `|a_k(σ)| ≤ M′r′^{−k}` in case (A), and `|a_k(z) − c(z−ρ)^{−k−1}| ≤ Mr′^{−k}` after
   subtracting the principal part.
7. In case (A), `|c|d^{−k−1} ≤ (M + M′)r′^{−k} + C′e^{ηU}(σ−θ)^{−k}` with `d < r′ ≤ σ − θ` and `k ≥ U`, false for
   `η = ½ log(r′/d)` and `U` large. In case (B), `c > 0` gives `a_k(σ) ≥ c(σ−β)^{−k−1} − Mr′^{−k}`, while step 3 at `γ = 0`
   gives `a_k(σ) ≤ C′e^{ηU}(σ−θ)^{−k}`: the same contradiction.
8. Mellin form: with `y = e^u`, a pointwise bound `Cy^θ` on `[Y, Y^{1+δ}]` gives the bound in mean, since
   `∫ dy/y = δ log Y ≤ Y^η`. The pole data for the character families (zeros and their conjugates in `Re s > 0`) are a
   separate theorem.

No Taylor series is summed, no contour is moved, no bound for `Φ` on vertical lines is used, and there are no power sums.
The result is ineffective and loses a factor `e^{ηU}`, that is `y^η`.

Landau's theorem for Dirichlet series follows Landau: let `β > σ₀` be the infimum of the real points of summability; `F`
is analytic in a disc about `β`; expand at `β + ε` and evaluate at `β − ε`. The `n`-th Taylor term is
`Σ_i c_i e^{−l_i(β+ε)}(2εl_i)^n/n! ≥ 0` (termwise differentiation, identity principle along the real axis), so every
partial sum of `c_i e^{−l_i(β−ε)}` is at most `F(β − ε)`. The form for the exponential writes it as a series of the same
kind over finite tuples. The Mellin-integral form of Landau's theorem is also in the source (`Formalism.landauEq`),
proved by the same argument.

## Key steps

- **Taylor coefficients as kernel integrals** (`Deduction.landau_coeff`): for `Re z > 2` the normalised `k`-th derivative of
  the transform is the integral of `g` against `u^k e^{−zu}/k!`.
- **Kernel tails** (`Deduction.landau_tails`): for `k = ⌈(1+δ/2)σU⌉`, the kernel at `σ − 2` has mass at most `Cσ^{−k}`
  outside the window `[U, (1+δ)U]`, by an exponential tilt on each side.
- **Positivity on a window** (`Deduction.landau_window_ineq`, `Deduction.landau_window_key`): the twisted coefficient plus
  the untwisted one is at most twice the integral of `g⁺` against the kernel, bounded by the window hypothesis.
- **A nearest pole** (`Deduction.landau_dichotomy`, `Deduction.landau_nearest`): either the pole nearest to `σ` is real, or a
  generic centre `σ + iγ` has a strictly nearest pole alone in a disc.
- **Cauchy's inequalities and the contradiction** (`Deduction.landau_pole_asym`, `Deduction.landau_window_contra`): the
  principal part makes `a_k` grow like `d^{−k−1}`, faster than the window bound allows; at a real pole, through `c > 0`.
- **Landau's theorem for Dirichlet series** (`PositivityTheory.RgJS.LandauNonnegativeDirichletSeries.linL`): expansion
  at a real point with nonnegative Taylor terms, evaluated to the left of the abscissa. The form for the exponential of
  such a series is `PositivityTheory.RgJS.landauL`.

coefficients.
