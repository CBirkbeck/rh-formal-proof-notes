## Statement

Four narrower forms of published theorems. (1) `K` imaginary quadratic of discriminant `−d`, `d > 4`, class number `h`,
Kronecker character `χ`, and `τ_C` with `ℤτ_C + ℤ` homothetic to an ideal of the class `C`; then
`(1/h)·Σ_C log(4π·Im τ_C·|η(τ_C)|⁴) = γ + log(2π/√d) − L′(1, χ)/L(1, χ)`, with `ζ_K(s) = ζ(s)·L(s, χ)` for `Re s > 1`
and `L(1, χ) = πh/√d`: the Chowla–Selberg formula without Lerch's evaluation by values of `Γ`. (2) The main theorem of
complex multiplication for elliptic curves, in idelic form, for ideals of the maximal order; proved, not assumed. (3)
For a CM field `E ⊃ K`, unramified over its maximal real subfield `F` at finite places, with CM type induced from `K`:
normalised Hecke lattice sums of `E`, for the lattices and weights of the source, are algebraic, and for a finite idele
`t` of `K` an automorphism of `ℂ` fixing `K` and acting on `K^ab` as `art_K(t)⁻¹` carries the sum for a lattice to
`|t|^{−|κ|}` times the sum for that lattice moved by `t⁻¹`, with the weights `κ` permuted as the automorphism permutes
the CM type and with a second period; this stands where an instance of Shimura's reciprocity law would be cited. (4) `F`
totally real, conductor `(ℓⁿ)`, `ε` rational-valued and `ℓ`-integral on narrow ray classes, `𝔠` prime to `ℓ`:
`L(ε, 1−k) − N(𝔠)^k·L(ε_𝔠, 1−k)` is `ℓ`-integral for `k ≥ 1`, a case of the Deligne–Ribet theorem; with it,
Siegel–Klingen rationality of `L(ε, 1−k)`.

## Why it might matter

In the formal proof these results supply the period and the denominators and Galois conjugates of values at CM points
that enter the slopes of lattices of integral forms. The main theorem of complex multiplication for elliptic curves is
proved in the source, not assumed. The one arrangement not found in the published work that could be opened is the
passage from lattice sums of `E ⊋ K` to elliptic modular forms by restricting a Hilbert–Eisenstein series to the diagonal.

## What the checks found

The first reading found these to be the classical arguments, each giving a narrower statement than the published one
(for the main theorem, only in that it is for the maximal order): the class mean by Kronecker's limit formula; the main
theorem by reduction at primes of degree one and Frobenius; the conjugation of lattice sums by the chain of Damerell,
Weil and Goldstein–Schappacher (main theorem, Weierstrass data at division points, an isogeny formula for the
non-holomorphic constant), reproving the needed instance of Shimura's reciprocity law by the mechanism from which that
law is classically derived; the `ℓ`-integrality by Shintani's cone method, the known alternative (Cassou-Noguès, Barsky)
to the proof of Deligne and Ribet. Confidence medium overall: high for the Chowla–Selberg and Deligne–Ribet clauses
(Lean read at top level, routes confirmed by Gross and by Charollois–Dasgupta), high that the main theorem is proved but
medium that its proof is the textbook one (recollection), medium for the conjugation, whose original sources could not
be opened. The diagonal restriction from `E` to `K` differs from the two published reductions to `K` found (Colmez;
Bergeron–Charollois–García); the first reading sees it as a specialisation of the Shimura–Katz method, not a new proof;
whether it is in print is not established. There is no second opinion for this item. Open: is Goldstein–Schappacher's
proof the chain above, and is the diagonal reduction in Katz (1978) or Colmez (1989)?

## How the formal proof goes

Class mean. For `s > 1` (with `d > 4`, so the units are `±1`), sorting ideals by class writes `ζ_K(s)` as `½·(2/√d)^s`
times the sum over the classes of the real-analytic Eisenstein series `Σ′ (Im τ_C)^s |mτ_C + n|^{−2s}`. Kronecker's first
limit formula is proved row by row: for each `m ≠ 0` the sum over `n` is compared with `∫(t² + m²y²)^{−s} dt`, with error
at most the value at `0`; the integrals add up to a multiple of `ζ(2s − 1)`, which carries the pole and `γ`; at `s = 1` the
row errors are evaluated by the partial fractions of the cotangent and sum to `log|∏(1 − qⁿ)|`; dominated convergence
passes to the limit. With `ζ_K = ζ·L(χ)`, comparing the two expansions at `s = 1` to first order gives `L(1, χ)` and the
class mean. The form actually used is a mean over the embeddings of a number field: one Weierstrass curve of unit
discriminant per class; isogenies between them have multipliers integral in both directions, so the product formula makes
the mean over embeddings the same for each curve, and a double count gives the class mean (that lemma read by statement).

Main theorem. For each level `m`: a finite Galois `L/K` containing the `j`-values, the `m`-torsion of a model, the ray class
field modulo `m` and a twelfth root of unity; at almost all primes `𝔭` of degree one the natural isogeny from the curve of
`𝔞` to that of `𝔭⁻¹𝔞` reduces to Frobenius; a prime is chosen whose Frobenius is `σ` on `L`, and the idele is written as
principal times a unit congruent to `1` modulo `m` times the idele of `𝔭`. The levels `m = (n+1)!` are glued by a
pigeonhole over the finite unit group.

Conjugation. (i) The main theorem applied to the Weierstrass model of `ℂ/𝒪_K` transports `g₂`, `g₃` and `℘`, `℘′` at
torsion points, each divided by the matching power of the period, to the same data for `t⁻¹𝒪_K` at `t⁻¹x`. (ii) An
isogeny formula for multiplication by `√−d` expresses `G₂^* = G₂ − π/Im τ` through values of `℘` on its kernel, so
`G₂^*` is transported too. (iii) Writing elements of `E` in coordinates over `F` turns a lattice of `E` into a lattice
in `F²` of some level `N`, and the lattice sum into an antiholomorphic derivative of a Hilbert–Eisenstein series at the
point all of whose coordinates equal the CM point of `K`; this point is on the diagonal because the CM type is induced
from `K`. (iv) The antiholomorphic direction is traded for a holomorphic one built from `G₂ + ℘(t_u)` at a torsion point
`t_u`; the derivative becomes a finite sum, with integer constants, of powers of `(G₂^* + ℘(t_u))/ω²` times values of
elliptic modular forms `h_μ`; the twists of each `h_μ`, indexed by `GL₂(ℤ/N)`, are permuted by `SL₂(ℤ)`, with expansions
read off from the Fourier expansion of the Hilbert–Eisenstein series (rational constant term by Siegel–Klingen). (v)
Symmetric functions of such a family are level-one forms, so the values divided by powers of `ω` are algebraic; mixed
power sums are transported, which gives a permutation of the family, and `℘`, `℘′` at two basis points of the
`N`-torsion separate `GL₂(ℤ/N)` and fix it. (vi) Summing over classes and reindexing gives the rule for twisted sums;
the periods cancel in the ratio; the base sum is a Hecke `L`-value and does not vanish.

Integrality. Narrow ray classes are parametrised by lattice points of finitely many simplicial cones. Weights `ℓ′ − 1` on
ideals divisible by a prime `𝔠` of prime norm `ℓ′` and `−1` on the others are periodic with zero sum on each cone, so
summation by parts continues the cone series and bounds the denominators at `1 − k`; a general `𝔠` prime to `ℓ` is replaced
by a degree-one prime in its ray class modulo a high power of `ℓ`. Siegel–Klingen rationality is proved by the same method.
This is the case of the Deligne–Ribet theorem with one weight: no congruences between different `k`, no `p`-adic measure.

## Key steps

- **Kronecker's limit formula** (`GlobalTheory.tendsto_realEisenstein_sub_pole`): proved as a limit `s → 1⁺` by comparing
  each row with an integral, the cotangent at `s = 1` and dominated convergence; combined with `ζ_K = ζ·L(χ)` in
  `GlobalTheory.chowla_selberg`.
- **Main theorem of complex multiplication** (`GroundTheory.Global.Elliptic.main_theorem_complexMultiplication`): at each
  level, the isogeny attached to a prime of degree one reduces to Frobenius; the levels `(n+1)!` are glued.
- **Transport of Weierstrass data** (`GlobalTheory.LatVal.periodEx_of_mainTheorem`): the main theorem gives the action of
  `g` on `g₂`, `g₃`, `℘` and `℘′` at torsion points, normalised by the period.
- **Diagonal restriction** (`GlobalTheory.T.hilbertEisenstein_value_derivatives_via_jets`,
  `GlobalTheory.T.weierstrass_eisenstein_nonholomorphic_identity`): the lattice sum of `E` becomes a finite integral
  combination of holomorphic derivatives of a Hilbert–Eisenstein series at the diagonal CM point, with `G₂^* + ℘(t_u)`.
- **Galois action on level-`N` families** (`GlobalTheory.T.transport_modularFamily_GL2ZMod_weierstrass`,
  `GlobalTheory.QFam.fam_value_isAlgebraic`): level-one symmetric functions give algebraicity and the transport of power
  sums; division values of `℘`, `℘′` fix the permutation. This is the reproved instance of the reciprocity law.
- **Smoothed zeta values** (`PositivityTheory.PfDRL.deligneRibet`, `GlobalTheory.T.ray_class_partial_zeta_integrality`):
  Shintani cones, smoothing at a prime of prime norm, summation by parts.
