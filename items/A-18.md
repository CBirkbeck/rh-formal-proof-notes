## Statement

Let `K` be an imaginary quadratic field with character `η` and complex conjugation `c`, `ℓ` an odd prime, `O ⊂ ℚ̄_ℓ`
the ring of integers of a finite unramified extension of `ℚ_ℓ`, `N` even, `ψ` a Dirichlet character modulo `N` with
values in `O`, `e ≥ 1` with `ψ(−1) = (−1)^e`, and `Ψ = χ_ℓ^e·ψ`. Assume `ℓ ∤ d_K·h_K·ord(ψ)`, `gcd(d_K, N) = 1` and
`(ℓ − 1) ∤ 2e`, that the component of `ψ` at `ℓ` has square `1`, and that `K` is not in the field cut out by
`χ_ℓ mod ℓ` and the part of `ψ` of conductor prime to `ℓ`. Let `𝔘` be the universal local lifting ring and `P` the
minimal prime, through `Ψ ⊕ 1 ⊕ Ψ⁻¹`, of its saturated ordinary ideal for the inertial characters `Ψ, 1, Ψ⁻¹`.
Let `S` represent the lifts `r : G_ℚ → GL₃ ⋊ {±1}` of `diag(Ψ̄, 1, Ψ̄⁻¹)` with sign `η` that are trivial on the inertia
elements at `p ≠ ℓ` killed by `r̄`, lie at `ℓ` on `R_ℓ = 𝔘/P`, and have `r(c) = 1` in `GL₃` and `r(g₁) − r(c·g₁⁻¹·c)`
diagonal, for an element `g₁ ∈ G_K` with `Ψ̄(g₁)² ≠ 1` (a marked element).

Then (b) for every `ι : ℚ̄_ℓ ≅ ℂ` there is a marked `g₁` such that, for every such `S`, every `ℚ̄_ℓ`-point `ρ` of the
`O`-algebra of entries of the universal lift, irreducible on `G_K`, has `ρ|G_K ⊗ χ_ℓ⁻¹` attached (at almost all places)
to a regular algebraic conjugate self-dual cuspidal `π` on `GL₃(𝔸_K)`. Also (a) the algebra of entry monomials fixed by
the torus `diag(t, 1, t⁻¹)` is finite over `O`, (c) at a point with `ρ|G_K = W ⊕ 1`, `W` irreducible, the extensions of
`W` to `G_ℚ` of trace `0` at `c`, twisted by `χ_ℓ^{−e}`, are attached to newforms of weight `2e + 1`, and the conditions
(w1), (w2) stated in the sketch below hold.

In the Lean source the statement is a definition of a proposition. A theorem proves it from two named propositions, and
theorems without hypotheses in `SmallClassNumbers/CorGKOf.lean` supply both. No declaration states (a)–(c) without
hypotheses.

## Context

This is an automorphy lifting statement for a residual representation that is a sum of three characters, two of them
exchanged by the conjugate duality, with no auxiliary place of Steinberg type in the hypotheses. In the formal proof it
attaches automorphic representations to the points of this deformation ring that are irreducible on `G_K`, and newforms
to those of the form `W ⊕ 1`. These are then counted to bound the length of a Selmer-type module attached to `(ψ, e)`.

## Comparison with the literature

We first compared the literature with an earlier and wider description of the result. In it, (b) gave `π` the weight
`(e−1, 0, 1−e)`, (c) held at every point `W ⊕ μ`, and there was a part (d), unlinkedness at `ℓ`. The statement here
has no weight in (b) and no (d). Unlinkedness at `ℓ` and rigidity are separate propositions in the formal proof. The
statement assumes `μ = 1` in (c) without stating `det W = η_K`, which our deduction of (c) below used, and it adds
(w1) and (w2), which that comparison did not consider. A second, independent check judged the statement as given here
and agrees with what follows.

The closest published theorems do not cover (a) and (b), and (a) and (b) do not imply those theorems. Thorne (2015,
Theorem 7.1) and Allen–Newton–Thorne (2020, Theorem 1.1) are theorems in every rank, but they need a place of
Steinberg type and residual constituents that are each conjugate self-dual, so they exclude `Ψ̄ ⊕ 1 ⊕ Ψ̄⁻¹`.
Berger–Klosin (2026, a preprint) prove an `R = T` theorem, stronger in kind, for this residual shape with no Steinberg
place. But their ordinary condition is a different one, `ℓ` must split in `K`, and they assume Selmer-group hypotheses
and a hypothesis on Hecke algebras that they say they cannot prove. The modularity in (c) is known in substance. It
follows from Skinner–Wiles (1999) applied to `W ⊗ Ψ`, after the ordinary twist and a reduction to continuous points
over discrete valuation rings, and Pan's theorem also covers it. That deduction is our own, and no specialist has
confirmed it. Being known in this sense concerns the modularity in (c), and not automatically (w1) and (w2). The
control of lower rank and the connectedness arguments in the proof are established methods, and the patching and
propagation of Allen–Newton–Thorne are close antecedents of the method.

Our search was not exhaustive, we have not compared (w1) and (w2) with the literature, and we have not verified the
formalisation as a whole, so a closer published result is not excluded. We would like to know whether the deduction of
(c) from Skinner–Wiles holds for `W` as stated here, and whether (w1) and (w2) are in print. We would also like to know
whether Berger–Klosin's method can reach the ordinary flag `Ψ, 1, Ψ⁻¹`.

## Sketch of the proof

The source states (a)–(c) as one named proposition. In it, (b) also asserts two clauses when `ρ` is continuous, and
(c) asserts them for `W` over every imaginary quadratic field on which `W` is irreducible. Clause (w1) says that at a
place above `ℓ` with a full flag having no two characters in ratio `χ_ℓ`, there is one with no two in ratio `χ_ℓ` up
to finite order. Clause (w2) says that for each regular algebraic cuspidal `π′` to which the representation is attached
there is a real `t` such that, at the finite places `v` prime to `ℓ` or as in (w1), the cuspidal support of `π′_v`
consists of characters `ξ` with `(Nv)^{t−1/2} < |ξ(ϖ_v)| < (Nv)^{t+1/2}`.

A theorem (second `lean:` line) proves the proposition from a bundle of local Galois cohomology and from `GdEndAlt`,
which asserts the same conclusions for lifts over discrete valuation rings finite over `O`, ordinary at `ℓ`. The link
is the chart of `R_ℓ` at the diagonal lift (`UpperTheory.V.Loc.rtChart`). Part (a) follows once lifts over complete
discrete valuation rings of characteristic `ℓ` have the characteristic polynomials of `r̄` on a subgroup of finite
index (`finA_of_end`). Each point of the algebra of entries is then torus-conjugate to a lift over a discrete valuation
ring (`exists_ordPt_of_point`), and irreducibility on `G_K` passes to its open subgroups (`matIrr_open_gd`). This
theorem does not use that `O` is unramified.

`GdEndAlt` is proved over a larger field. `frame_ex` gives an abelian CM field `L ⊇ K`. It is described, though we
have not checked this, as made from a real quadratic field, the subfield of degree `ℓ` of `ℚ(μ_w)` for a prime `w`,
and a layer `m` of the cyclotomic `ℤ_ℓ`-extension. A bound `C₀` for the `ℓ`-ranks of class groups along the tower
comes first (Ferrero and Washington's theorem, proved in the source as `UpperTheory.V.FW.ferreroWashington`). Then
`m = 3C₀ + 7`, which gives `d = [L⁺ : ℚ] > 3C₀ + 7` and local degrees above `ℓ` exceeding `5` (a "big" frame).

Over `L`, for `n = 2` and `n = 3`, two rings are compared. One is the space of polarised ordinary lifts of the
diagonal representation of `G_L`, with its ring `B_n` of torus invariants, its graded algebra `A_n` of entries and the
weight algebra `Λ` of dimension `1 + nd`. The other is the universal ring `B_det` of ordinary determinants for a
definite unitary group over `L` with one auxiliary place `v₀`. `hida_unitary_of_printed_hida` gives an ideal
`J ⊂ B_det`, which is described, though we have not checked this, as the kernel of the map to the big ordinary Hecke
algebra at an Eisenstein maximal ideal. The quotient `B_det/J` is finite and torsion-free over `Λ`. The ideal has the
point property: an arithmetic point whose representation is automorphic with (w1), (w2), unramified at `v₀`, with a
flag at `ℓ` as in (w1), lies in `V(J)`. It has classicality: an irreducible arithmetic point of `V(J)` is automorphic,
with (w2) off `v₀`. And it comes with patching data.

`pFromAut` turns the patching data into two statements on the kernels of suitable points of `B_det` with values in
`k′[[T]]`, `k′` a finite field. (P1) If the representation is absolutely irreducible and `J` lies in the kernel, then
`J` lies in every prime below it. (P3) Along a chain of points that are sums of two polarised irreducible blocks, joined
by "thick" primes, `J` passes from an irreducible prime below the first to the irreducible and thick primes below the
last. The proof uses Taylor–Wiles places for finitely many detecting elements, two Selmer counts (`b1Ar`, `b2Ar`) and
ultrafilter patching.

`absMain` is purely algebraic. Suppose, for `n = 2, 3`, that `B_det/J` is finite over `Λ` with minimal primes of
dimension `1 + nd`, that (P1) and (P3) hold, that there is one irreducible point of `V(J)` (a seed), and that an
unramifiedness condition modulo `J` and a condition (N4) at every point hold. Then the image of `J` in `B_n` lies in
every irreducible prime of `A_n`. In rank two the nonsplit residual lattices form a connected space. At each closed
point the local ring has at least `2d + 1` more variables than relations, so its components are chained through
intersections of that dimension, each lying in a nice prime (this is where bigness is used), and (P1) moves `J` across.
In rank three the rank-two conclusion makes the block locus finite over `Λ` (`UpperTheory.V.Fr.l53`), and (P3) joins
components across. A by-product is the statement in characteristic `ℓ` behind (a).

The seeds (`seed2_alt`, `unitary_three_automorphy_dense_of_prints`) are automorphic of weights `(m, −m)` and
`(m, 0, −m)`. They are described, though we have not checked this, as coming from a newform congruent to an Eisenstein
series and from its adjoint lift. Classicality gives (w2) only off `v₀`, so the argument is run for two places `v₀`
and the results are glued (`twoRuns_alt`). Automorphy descends from `L` to `K` by soluble descent in rank three, and
from `L` to `ℚ` in rank two, where the inertial flag `(χ_ℓ^e, χ_ℓ^{−e})` gives a newform of weight `2e + 1`.

On the hypotheses, `(ℓ − 1) ∤ 2e`, the condition at `ℓ` and `ℓ ∤ d_K` are described as giving the marked element, and
`ℓ ∤ ord(ψ)` as making `Ψ` trivial on inertia away from `ℓ` where `Ψ̄` is, but we have not traced these uses. We did
trace that `N` even gives `2ℓ | ℓN|d_K|` for the rank-three seeds. A search by name found `ℓ ∤ h_K` used in one lemma,
`UpperTheory.V.U3.char_eq_one`, which says that a continuous character of `G_K`, congruent to `1` and unramified
everywhere, is trivial, and passed to the hypotheses of the Euler system statement. A use that does not name the
hypothesis would escape that search. We did not trace `gcd(d_K, N) = 1` or the condition on `K`. Published theorems
(Galois cohomology, representations of `GL_n`, base change, Galois representations of cuspidal representations) enter
as bundles of named propositions. They are not axioms: `SmallClassNumbers/CorGKOf.lean` has theorems without
hypotheses (`litN`, and the argument of `corGK_of` in `corGK`) supplying every bundle used. Unlinkedness at `ℓ`
(`AdmI_s`) and the vanishing of adjoint Bloch–Kato Selmer groups at these points (`RigidityFirstCase`,
`RigiditySecondCase`) are separate propositions, proved the same way.

## Caveats

The main declaration is a definition of a proposition. The theorem of the second `lean:` line proves it from two named
propositions, and `SmallClassNumbers/CorGKOf.lean` has theorems without hypotheses whose composition (`corGK`, line 25,
through `corGK_of`) supplies both. No declaration states (a)–(c) without hypotheses, and we checked this chain only on
the signatures of its declarations. We read the statements, and the proofs of the assembling theorems down to
`absMain`, `components_good_rank_two`, `twoRuns_alt` and `frame_ex`. We read only the statements of the Hecke ideal,
the patching data, the Selmer counts, the ultrafilter patching, the seeds, descent, the local chart, unlinkedness,
rigidity, and every bundle of published theorems, and we did not examine whether each bundle states its theorem
faithfully. In the whole source tree `sorry` occurs only in `src/Challenge.lean`, which no file imports, and we found no
`axiom`, `native_decide`, `opaque` or `unsafe` declaration. We compiled nothing.
