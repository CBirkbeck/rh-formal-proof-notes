## Statement

Work in the Laplace variable `u = log y`. Let `g` be measurable with `|g(u)| ≤ C₀e^{2u}` for `u ≥ 0`, let
`Z ⊂ {Re s > 0}` have no accumulation point in that half-plane, and let `Φ` be holomorphic on `{Re s > 0} ∖ Z`, equal
to `∫₀^∞ g(u)e^{−su} du` for `Re s > 2`, with a simple pole of residue `c ≠ 0` at each `ρ ∈ Z`, and `c > 0` when `ρ` is
real. Let `0 < θ < 1` and `δ > 0`. Suppose that for every `η > 0` there are arbitrarily large `U` and a function
`R ≥ 0` with `g ≤ R` on `[U, (1+δ)U]` and `∫_U^{(1+δ)U} R(u)e^{−θu} du ≤ e^{ηU}`. Then `Re ρ ≤ θ` for every `ρ ∈ Z`.

This is applied to finitely many Dirichlet characters `χ_i`, each of modulus 1 or non-principal, with weights
`a_i > 0`. Let `G_χ(y) = Σ_{n≤y} Λ(n)χ(n)(y/n − 1)` less its main term, and `G = Σ a_i Re G_{χ_i}`. If
`G(y) ≤ Cy^θ` on `[Y, Y^{1+δ}]` for arbitrarily large `Y`, then no `L(s, χ_i)` vanishes in `Re s > θ`.

This is not Landau's theorem, nor a case of it, but it plays its role: meromorphy is assumed, the absence of poles is
concluded, and the bound is needed only on windows. As a statement it is a special case, with simple poles and a
pointwise growth bound, of the window criterion that follows from Kaczorowski–Pintz (1986), Corollary 1. We take this
last point from a separate comparison of the statements, which we did not redo.

Separately, Landau's theorem for generalised Dirichlet series `Σ c_i e^{−l_i s}` with `c_i, l_i ≥ 0` is proved, and so
is its form for the exponential of such a series.

## Context

In the formal proof the criterion above turns one-sided bounds for weighted prime sums on windows `[Y, Y^{1+δ}]` into
zero-free half-planes for Dirichlet `L`-functions, where Landau's theorem in its form for Mellin integrals would be
applied with a bound for all large `y`. It is proved directly, from neither Landau's theorem nor Kaczorowski–Pintz.

## Comparison with the literature

The windowed criterion is proved by an argument that differs from each of the published proofs we compared, those of
Landau (1905), Kátai (1967) and Kaczorowski–Pintz (1986/87). It follows Kátai's scheme, in which the twisted integral
is bounded by the untwisted one plus the window and the tails, but with Gamma kernels `u^k e^{−σu}/k!` in place of a
Gaussian. The smoothed integrals are then single Taylor coefficients at `σ` and `σ + iγ`, and these are bounded by
Cauchy's inequalities around a nearest pole. There is no contour shift and there are no power sums. We regard this as
a variant of a known method, not a new method, and not a proof of Landau's theorem. The nearest classical relative we
can identify is Pringsheim's proof as given by Landau. We are fairly sure that the Lean is as described, having read
in full the seventeen files of the criterion and of its Mellin form, and that it differs from the works we read. We
are much less sure that the variant is not in print, since we have not read Pintz (1980) or Kaczorowski (1984) and
searched only briefly for a printed proof with Gamma kernels. Landau's theorem for Dirichlet series is proved by
Landau's own argument, and an independent second check agrees that it is known.

On the windowed criterion a second, independent check differs from us: it thinks the argument is known in spirit. In
its view, treating high derivatives of a Laplace transform as localised Gamma averages is classical (Post, Widder), and
the subtraction of principal parts, Cauchy estimates, exponential tilting and perturbing the centre are all standard. It
found no printed duplicate, but did not establish that there is none. It saw only the bibliographic data of Kátai and of
Kaczorowski–Pintz, and could not confirm independently our account of the proofs of Kaczorowski–Pintz, or that their
Corollary 1 implies the form with measurable `g` and a bound in mean. We would like to know whether Pintz (1980) or
Kaczorowski (1984) contains this argument, and whether there is a printed proof through single Taylor coefficients.

## Sketch of the proof

Write `a_k(z) = (−1)^k Φ^{(k)}(z)/k!` and `w_k^a(u) = u^k e^{−au}/k!`.

1. For `Re z > 2`, `a_k(z) = ∫₀^∞ g(u) u^k e^{−zu}/k! du`, by differentiating under the integral sign.
2. The kernel `w_k^a` has mass `a^{−k−1}` and supremum at most `a^{−k}`. For the given `δ` there are `σ > 2` and a
   constant `C` such that for every `U ≥ 1` the index `k = ⌈(1+δ/2)σU⌉ ≥ U` has `∫ w_k^{σ−2} ≤ Cσ^{−k}` outside
   `[U, (1+δ)U]`. This is proved by an exponential tilt on each side.
3. Positivity. Since `|a_k(σ+iγ)| ≤ ∫|g| w_k^σ` and `a_k(σ) = ∫ g w_k^σ`, we get
   `|a_k(σ+iγ)| + a_k(σ) ≤ ∫(|g| + g) w_k^σ`. On the window `|g| + g ≤ 2R` and `w_k^σ ≤ (σ−θ)^{−k} e^{−θu}`. Off it,
   `|g| + g ≤ 2C₀e^{2u}` leaves the tail mass of step 2. Hence for every `η`, arbitrarily large `U` and some `k ≥ U`,
   `|a_k(σ+iγ)| + a_k(σ) ≤ C′e^{ηU}(σ−θ)^{−k}`, uniformly in real `γ`.
4. No pole lies to the right of `Re s = 2`, so all poles lie to the left of `σ`.
5. Suppose a pole has real part `> θ`. Closed discs in `Re s > 0` contain finitely many poles. Either (B) the pole
   nearest to `σ` is a real `β > θ`, alone in a disc `B(σ, r)` with `σ − β < r ≤ σ − θ`, or (A) there are `D ≤ σ − θ`
   with no pole in `B(σ, D)` and a pole `ρ₁` with `σ − Re ρ₁ < D`. In case (A), take `γ` near `Im ρ₁`, avoiding the
   finitely many values at which two poles are equidistant from `σ + iγ`. Then one pole `ρ` is strictly nearest to
   `z = σ + iγ`, at distance `d < r ≤ D`, alone in `B(z, r)`.
6. Cauchy's inequalities, with `d < r′ < r`, give `|a_k(σ)| ≤ M′r′^{−k}` in case (A), and
   `|a_k(z) − c(z−ρ)^{−k−1}| ≤ Mr′^{−k}` after subtracting the principal part.
7. In case (A), `|c|d^{−k−1} ≤ (M + M′)r′^{−k} + C′e^{ηU}(σ−θ)^{−k}` with `d < r′ ≤ σ − θ` and `k ≥ U`, which is false
   for `η = ½ log(r′/d)` and `U` large. In case (B), `c > 0` gives `a_k(σ) ≥ c(σ−β)^{−k−1} − Mr′^{−k}`, while step 3 at
   `γ = 0` gives `a_k(σ) ≤ C′e^{ηU}(σ−θ)^{−k}`, the same contradiction.
8. Mellin form. With `y = e^u`, a pointwise bound `Cy^θ` on `[Y, Y^{1+δ}]` gives the bound in mean, since
   `∫ dy/y = δ log Y ≤ Y^η`. The pole data for the character families (zeros and their conjugates in `Re s > 0`) are a
   separate theorem.

No Taylor series is summed, no contour is moved, no bound for `Φ` on vertical lines is used, and there are no power
sums. The result is ineffective and loses a factor `e^{ηU}`, that is `y^η`.

Landau's theorem for Dirichlet series follows Landau. Let `β > σ₀` be the infimum of the real points of summability.
Then `F` is analytic in a disc about `β`. Expand at `β + ε` and evaluate at `β − ε`. The `n`-th Taylor term is
`Σ_i c_i e^{−l_i(β+ε)}(2εl_i)^n/n! ≥ 0`, by termwise differentiation and the identity principle along the real axis, so
every partial sum of `c_i e^{−l_i(β−ε)}` is at most `F(β − ε)`. The form for the exponential writes it as a series of
the same kind over finite tuples. The Mellin-integral form of Landau's theorem is also in the source
(`Formalism.landauEq`), proved by the same argument.

## Key steps

- **Taylor coefficients as kernel integrals** (`Deduction.landau_coeff`): for `Re z > 2` the normalised `k`-th
  derivative of the transform is the integral of `g` against `u^k e^{−zu}/k!`.
- **Kernel tails** (`Deduction.landau_tails`): for `k = ⌈(1+δ/2)σU⌉`, the kernel at `σ − 2` has mass at most `Cσ^{−k}`
  outside the window `[U, (1+δ)U]`, by an exponential tilt on each side.
- **Positivity on a window** (`Deduction.landau_window_ineq`, `Deduction.landau_window_key`): the twisted coefficient
  plus the untwisted one is at most twice the integral of `g⁺` against the kernel, which is bounded by the window
  hypothesis.
- **A nearest pole** (`Deduction.landau_dichotomy`, `Deduction.landau_nearest`): either the pole nearest to `σ` is real,
  or a generic centre `σ + iγ` has a strictly nearest pole alone in a disc.
- **Cauchy's inequalities and the contradiction** (`Deduction.landau_pole_asym`, `Deduction.landau_window_contra`): the
  principal part makes `a_k` grow like `d^{−k−1}`, faster than the window bound allows. At a real pole the contradiction
  goes through `c > 0`.
- **Landau's theorem for Dirichlet series** (`PositivityTheory.RgJS.LandauNonnegativeDirichletSeries.linL`): expansion
  at a real point with nonnegative Taylor terms, evaluated to the left of the abscissa. The form for the exponential of
  such a series is `PositivityTheory.RgJS.landauL`.
