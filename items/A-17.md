## Statement

Let `O` be a complete discrete valuation ring of characteristic 0 with fraction field `E`, and `K` an algebraically
closed field containing `O`, with a real-valued valuation `v` extending the normalised valuation of `O`. Let
`A = ⊕_{j∈ℤ} A_j` be a graded `O`-algebra, generated over `A_0` by finitely many elements of weights `±1, ±2`, with
`A_0` complete Noetherian local with the residue field of `O`, and let `x : A → O` be an `O`-algebra map killing `A_j`
for `j ≠ 0`. Let `I` be generated by `F_0, …, F_r ∈ A_{−1}` (`r ≥ 1`) and by `s, r′, s′, m` elements `G, P, P′, Z` of
`A_{−2}, A_1, A_2, A_0 ∩ ker x`, and let `a ∈ A_0`. Write `cot_j(x)` for the image of `A_j ∩ ker x` in `A/sat(x)`, where
`sat(x) = {f : cf ∈ (ker x)² for some c ≠ 0 in O}`. Let `K^×` act on `K`-points by `t^j` on `A_j`, and call a point
two-signed if it kills neither all negative nor all positive weights. Assume the following.

- (M1) There is an `O`-algebra map `φ : A → E[[t]]` with constant term `x`, in `r, s, m, r′+1, s′+1` variables of
  weights `−1, −2, 0, 1, 2`, sending `A_j` to series with monomials of weight `j`, whose linear part induces
  `(ker x/sat(x)) ⊗_O E ≅ ⊕ E·t_i`. Moreover `F, G, Z` span `cot_{−1}(x), cot_{−2}(x), cot_0(x)` over `E`.
- (M2) The image of `A_0` in `A/I` is finite over `O`.
- (M3) No `K`-point `x₁ ≠ x` of `A/I` killing all nonzero weights has, among the points of `A/I` equal to it on `A_0`,
  both one killing all negative but not all positive weights and one killing all positive but not all negative weights.
- (M4) At two-signed `K`-points of `A/I` any two point derivations of `A/I` are proportional.
- (M5) `x(a) = 0`, and `a` vanishes at no two-signed `K`-point of `A/I`.

Then the two-signed `K`-points of `A/I` lie in finitely many orbits, `ℓ := length_O(cot_{−1}(x)/span_O(F_i))` is
finite, and `ℓ ≤ 2·Σ_{ξ∈T} v(ξ(a))` for every finite set `T` of such points meeting every orbit.

## Context

The left side measures how far the equations of weight `−1` are from generating the cotangent lattice of weight `−1` at
a torus-fixed integral point. The right side involves only the values of one element of weight zero on the orbits where
both signs of weight survive. `A/I` is not assumed finite or flat over `O`, nor a complete intersection: only the image
of `A_0` is assumed finite. In the source the bound is applied to graded models of deformation problems at a diagonal
representation, with `a` a difference of traces.

## Comparison with the literature

We compared the statement in substance. The Lean form is slightly more general in the field `K`, the set `T` and the
chart clause, so the comparison applies to it. We regard the bound as not comparable with the published results we
know. For finite flat complete intersections the Wiles–Lenstra criterion gives an equality, with constant 1 and the
congruence module on the right, which looks stronger than our bound there. A second, independent check doubts that it
is: the modules and hypotheses differ, and comparing the right side here with the congruence module needs an
identification that the statement does not supply. The same identification would be needed to conclude from
Berger–Klosin–Kramer that the right side is at least the congruence module, in general strictly. Outside complete
intersections Iyengar–Khare–Manning bound the cotangent module from below, which is the other direction. That direction
is not the only one in print. Böckle–Khare–Manning give exact defect formulas for Hecke algebras of Shimura curves at a
residually irreducible `ρ̄`, which in particular bound the cotangent module from above, and Khare–Patrikis (a preprint)
bound adjoint Selmer groups from above at irreducible points. Both are bounds of another shape, under different
hypotheses, and two-signed points need not be irreducible ones.

We have not made a systematic search of the later literature, and our searches were cut short twice. They found nothing
stating or implying the bound, but we cannot exclude that it is in print, or that it can be deduced from general
intersection theory. Besides Khare–Patrikis they turned up one more upper bound of another shape, Lee's preprint on
Selmer groups of characters through Euler systems. That work, like Wake–Wang-Erickson below, bears only on an
application to Selmer groups that is not stated here. For the method there is a close
precedent in Viet–Thanh (2015), whose recursion is of the kind that defines `χ` in the proof below. The multiplicity
theory behind the method (Kirby–Rees) we have seen in abstracts only. We would like to know whether the constant 2 can
be replaced by 1, and whether the bound follows from positivity of intersection numbers on Rees algebras, or on schemes
with a `𝔾_m`-action.

## Sketch of the proof

The statement is the proposition `LemmaM`, and `main_holds` proves it by four reductions and one inequality,
`core_holds`. (i) The linear parts of the chart embed `cot_{−1}(x)` in `E^r` with image of full rank, so it is free of
rank `r` (`rk_holds`). (ii) By Smith normal form, `F` is replaced by a family with the same `O`-span such that
`F_i ≡ π^{c_i}·y_i` modulo `sat(x)` for `i < r`, with the `y_i ∈ A_{−1}` giving a basis of `cot_{−1}(x)`, and
`F_r ∈ sat(x)` (`sm_holds`). Then `ℓ = Σ c_i`, which gives the finiteness. (iii) A formal implicit function theorem
with weights (`ift_holds`), which uses that `Z` spans `cot_0(x)`, gives a chart `φ` in the variables of nonzero weight
only, with `φ(Z_l) = 0` (`cut_holds`). (iv) `a` is replaced by `a + g·F_k` with `g ∈ A_1`, which has the same values
on `A/I` and satisfies `φ(a) ≠ 0` (`nz_holds`). The finiteness of orbits is `fin_holds`, which comes from `slice_holds`
below.

Let `R ⊆ A[T_0, T_1, T_2]` be the `ℕ³`-graded `A_0`-algebra whose part of degree `μ` is
`N_0^{μ_0}·N_1^{μ_1}·N_2^{μ_2}·T^μ`, with `N_0 = A_{−2}`, `N_1 = A_2`, `N_2 = A_0·F_r² + A_{−2}·A_{−2}·A_2`. The
equations give a list `ts` of sections: the `F_i²` (`i < r`) and `G` in degree `e_0`, the `P_i²` and `P′` in degree
`e_1`, and `F_r²` in degree `e_2`. Let `J` be the ideal of elements whose coefficients all lie in `ker φ`. For a finite
graded `R`-module `M`, `χ` is defined, for large `d` and admissible lists, by `χ(∅; M)(d) = length M_d` and
`χ((δ, s) :: ts; M) = χ(ts; M/sM) − χ(ts; (0 :_M s)(−δ))` (`chi`). A list is admissible if a power of `𝔪_{A_0}·R_+`
lies in `Ann M + (ts)`, where `R_+` is generated by the products of elements of degrees `e_0`, `e_1`, `e_2`. The source
proves the calculus of `χ` (directory `NoetherianQuotientCalc`): additivity, independence of the order, the rules for
a nonzerodivisor and for a product with a factor of degree zero, and positivity (`rulePos`). Positivity is proved by
dévissage to quotients by homogeneous primes, a section inside a relevant prime being exchanged for one of the same
degree outside it. Monotonicity under graded quotients and `χ(ts ++ us; M) ≤ χ(us; M/(ts)M)` (`ruleUB`) follow.

*Admissibility of `ts` with `a`* (`admissibility_holds`). A prime of `R` containing the sections, `a` and the `Z_l` but
not `R_+` contracts to a prime of `A_0` containing `I ∩ A_0`, which by (M2) is the maximal ideal or the kernel of a
`K`-point `σ`. If `σ` kills `A_{−2}·A_2`, one builds points of `A/I` over `σ` that kill only the positive weights, only
the negative ones, and both (`zm_holds`, `zp_holds`, `fix_holds`). This contradicts (M3), or, when the last point is
`x`, the spanning of `cot_{−1}(x)` and `cot_{−2}(x)` by `F` and `G` (`ct_holds`, `zii_holds`). Otherwise a point of
`A/I` over `σ` is two-signed and `a` vanishes there, against (M5).

*Lower bound* (`low_holds`). Let `𝔮 ⊆ R` consist of the elements whose coefficient of `T^μ` maps under `φ` to a series
all of whose monomials have order at least `2μ_0 + 4μ_2 + 1` in the variables of negative weight (`lk_holds`). It is
homogeneous and contains `J` and every `b ∈ A_0` with `x(b) = 0`, hence `a`, since no variable has weight zero. The
element `π` is a nonzerodivisor on `R/𝔮`, and modulo `𝔮` the section `F_i²` is `π^{2c_i}·y_i²`. Monotonicity gives
`χ(ts; (R/J)/a) ≥ χ(ts; R/𝔮)`. Peeling off the powers of `π` one section at a time, and then positivity (`la_holds`),
give `χ(ts; R/𝔮) ≥ (Σ 2c_i)·ΔH`, where `H(d) = length((R/𝔮)/π)_d` and `Δ` is the mixed difference in the degrees of the
other sections. `hf_holds` identifies `H(d)` with the number of monomials of negative order `2d_0 + 4d_2` and positive
order `2d_1 + 2d_2`, and `num_holds` gives `ΔH = 2^{r−1+r′}` for large `d`. Hence
`χ(ts; (R/J)/a) ≥ (Σ c_i)·2^{r+r′}`.

*Middle step* (`chain`, in `AlgMemCoef.lean`). As `φ(a) ≠ 0`, `a` is a nonzerodivisor on `R/J`, so
`χ(ts; (R/J)/a) = χ(a :: ts; R/J) = χ(ts ++ [a]; R/J) ≤ χ([a]; R/(J + (ts)))`.

*Upper bound* (`up_holds`). For one section of degree 0, `χ([a]; M)(d) = length(M_d/aM_d) − length(M_d[a])`. For
`M = R/(J + (ts))` and large `d`, `A_0/Ann(M_d)` is finite over `O`, and `M_d` vanishes at a `K`-point `σ` of `A_0`
unless `σ` is the restriction of a two-signed point of `A/I` (`eventually_mQ_gr_finite_annihilator`, by the analysis
of primes above). `th_holds` then bounds this difference by `c·Σ_σ v(σ(a))` if the lengths of `M_d` localised at the
`σ` are at most `c`. Its proof reduces by a prime filtration to a domain `D` finite over `O`, where
`length_O(D/aD) = v(N_{D/O}(a)) = Σ_τ v(τ(a))` over the embeddings `τ : D → K`, characteristic 0 giving separability.
The local lengths are at most those of a weight piece of `A/I_2`, where `I_2` is generated by the `F_i²`, `G`, `P_i²`,
`P′`, `Z` (`locLen_mQ_gr_le_locLen_aPiece`). These are at most `2^{r+1+r′}` times those of `A/I`, since each squared
generator at most doubles a length (`locLen_aPiece_ideal2_le_two_pow`), and those are at most 1
(`locLen_quotientWeightPiece_le_one`). The last rests on `slice_holds`: for `f ∈ A_{−2}` and `g ∈ A_2`, the ring
`(A/(I, f − 1))[1/πg]` has no nonzero point derivation at a `K`-point, by (M4) applied to a given derivation and to the
one multiplying `A_j` by `j`, so it is finite and reduced over `E` (`et_holds`). With `v(ξ(a)) ≥ 0`, which is
integrality and comes from (M2), this gives `χ([a]; M)(d) ≤ 2^{r+1+r′}·Σ_{ξ∈T} v(ξ(a))`, and the three bounds give the
claim.

*Application* (`model_upper`). Let `l` be an odd prime, `O` finite over `ℤ_l`, `r₀ = (diag(Ψ, 1, Ψ^{−1}), η)` with
values in `GL_3 ⋊ {±1}`, and take a graded model of a universal ring of normalised lifts with conditions, with `a` the
trace of the lift at `σ ∈ ker η` minus `tr r₀(σ)`. The hypotheses of `model_upper` include `LemmaM` itself, finiteness
over `O` of the algebra of weight-zero monomials in the matrix entries (which gives (M2)), rigidity conditions from
which the spanning part of (M1), (M3) and (M4) are derived (the chart of (M1) is part of the model), and, for (M5), that
the trace difference is nonzero at every point neither upper nor lower triangular on `ker η`. It gives a finite set `T`
of points neither upper nor lower triangular on `ker η`, one per class under conjugation by `diag(t, 1, t^{−1})`, with
`ℓ/e ≤ 2·Σ_{f∈T} v_l(tr ρ_f(σ) − tr r₀(σ))`, `e` the ramification index. Smith normal form, norms as products over
embeddings, prime filtrations, and finiteness and reducedness of formally unramified algebras of finite type over a
field come from the Mathlib library. The rest is proved in the source.

## Key steps

- **The squeeze** (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.core_holds`): file `GradedModulesLength/AlgMemCoef.lean`, line 92. Forms the
  Rees algebra and the sections and combines
  `(Σ c_i)·2^{r+r′} ≤ χ(ts; (R/J)/a) ≤ χ([a]; R/(J + (ts))) ≤ 2^{r+1+r′}·Σ v(ξ(a))` at one large degree. The constant 2
  is the ratio of the two powers of 2.
- **Leading forms at the fixed point** (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.low_holds`): file
  `GradedModulesLength/AdmissibleMonoIdeal.lean`, line 191. Passes to `R/𝔮`, where the first `r` sections become
  `π^{2c_i}·y_i²`, and extracts `Σ 2c_i` times a mixed difference of a Hilbert function. The properties of `𝔮` are in
  `lk_holds` (`LkHolds.lean`, line 8).
- **The monomial count** (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.num_holds`): file `GradedModulesLength/FrobeniusInclusionXEqMulQ.lean`,
  line 206. The mixed difference of the number of monomials of given negative and positive order equals `2^{r−1+r′}`
  for large degrees. The identification of the Hilbert function with this number is `hf_holds`
  (`ReesGradedPieceSurjection.lean`, line 199).
- **The order of `a` on graded pieces** (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.th_holds`): file `GradedModulesLength/HerbrandFinLen.lean`,
  line 189. Bounds `length(M/aM) − length(M[a])` by `c·Σ_σ v(σ(a))` when the local lengths at the `K`-points `σ` are at
  most `c`. Inside the proof it is shown equal to `Σ_σ length(M_σ)·v(σ(a))`. Used in `up_holds` (`TwoSigned.lean`,
  line 17).
- **Support of the graded pieces** (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.eventually_mQ_gr_finite_annihilator`): file
  `GradedModulesLength/EventuallyMQGrFiniteAnnihilator.lean`, line 77. In large degrees the pieces are finite over `O`
  and supported at restrictions of two-signed points. This, with `admissibility_holds` (`AdmissibilityHolds.lean`,
  line 30), is where (M3) enters.
- **Reducedness at two-signed points** (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.slice_holds`): file `GradedModulesLength/SliceHolds.lean`,
  line 48. From (M2) and (M4): there are finitely many points on a slice `f = 1`, `g ≠ 0`, and a function vanishing at
  all of them is zero there up to a power of `g` and a nonzero constant. It gives the finiteness of orbits and the local
  length 1.

## Caveats

`UpperTheory.V.Alg.LemmaM` is a definition of a proposition, and `UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.main_holds` is a theorem with a
proof of it, with no hypotheses beyond those of the statement. Later theorems, `model_upper` among them, take `LemmaM`
as an explicit hypothesis, which `main_holds` supplies at the uses we looked at. We searched the 87 source files that
`MainCotSpan.lean` imports directly or indirectly (about 13,000 lines), and the 59 for `ModelUpper.lean` (about 9,200
lines), for `sorry`, `axiom`, `admit`, `native_decide`, `opaque`, `unsafe` and `implemented_by`, and none occurs. We read
the proofs of `main_holds`, `core_holds`, `low_holds`, `up_holds`, `la_holds`, `lk_holds`, `th_holds`, `slice_holds`,
`admissibility_holds`, `rulePos` and `model_upper`. Of the following we read the statements only: the constructions of
points, `ct_holds`, the leading-monomial lemma behind `hf_holds`, `ift_holds`, most of the calculus of `χ`, and, in the
application, the lemmas giving (M1)–(M4), the structure of a model, and the statement of its existence (`exists_model`,
an implication from two named statements, which we did not trace). We compiled nothing and did not examine Mathlib.
