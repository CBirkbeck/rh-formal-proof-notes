## Statement

Let `M/K` be a Galois extension of number fields of prime degree `l`, with `K` totally complex, and let `n ≥ 1`. Call
a non-trivial Hecke character `η` of `K` with `η^l = 1` that is trivial on norms at almost all places a norm character.
Every cuspidal representation `π` of `GL_n(𝔸_K)` either satisfies `π ⊗ η ≅ π` for some norm character `η`, or lifts to
a cuspidal representation `P` of `GL_n(𝔸_M)`. Here a lift means that at almost all places the Satake parameters of `P`
are the `f`-th powers of those of `π`, where `f` is the residue degree, that the infinitesimal characters are the same,
and that `P_w ≅ π_v` at every split place. Every Galois-invariant cuspidal `P` is such a lift, and the other `π` with the
same lift are the twists by norm characters.

This is a special case of Arthur–Clozel's theorem, since only totally complex `K` is allowed, and it is weaker at the
non-split places. There the formal proof gives only the following. Let `τ = π_v` and `τ' = P_w`, let `I` be any
intertwining operator, and let `(φ, f)` be a matching pair with `f` supported where the determinant is a local norm.
Then `tr(τ'(φ)∘I) = c·tr τ(f)`, with a constant `c ≠ 0` that does not depend on `(φ, f)`. The condition on the support
of `f` is our reading of the condition in the Lean source.

Two further results are proved. If `π` is a regular algebraic cuspidal representation of `GL_2` over a totally real
field `F` and `E` is a totally complex quadratic extension of `F`, then either `π ≅ π ⊗ η_{E/F}` or `π` has a cuspidal
base change to `E`. And, under conditions, automorphic induction from `GL_2` to `GL_4` holds for a quadratic extension
of totally complex fields.

## Context

The formal proof uses cyclic base change of prime degree, together with patching, to reach every CM field in its
construction of Galois representations (B01), and descent and the description of the fibres are proved along with it.
Arthur and Clozel compare the full trace formulas, using the fundamental lemma for the spherical Hecke algebra and
local base change for tempered representations. The formal proof compares only the regular elliptic terms, matches the
unit and a restricted class of spherical functions, and proves no local base change map.

## Comparison with the literature

The statement is narrower than Arthur–Clozel's theorem and the route is not theirs. It differs from the published
proofs we could consult on the spectral side. Arthur–Clozel and Labesse compare full trace formulas, and Flicker's
simple proof assumes a supercuspidal component. Here all cuspidal representations are kept, the non-cuspidal spectrum
is removed approximately by multipliers, and exact identities are recovered from exponential growth in the power of an
Iwahori operator. The geometric side and the substitute for the fundamental lemma are a known alternative, found in
Flicker–Kazhdan and in Flicker. Multipliers that kill the continuous spectrum are in print, but we did not find them
used for base change. Treating `GL_2` by theta lifting is also a known alternative, though our evidence for this is
thin. That the route is not Arthur–Clozel's seems clear to us, because they compare full trace formulas and the formal
proof compares only the regular elliptic terms, though we know their book only from the publisher's description and
from later accounts. Whether it is absent from print is much less clear: we know most of the relevant papers only from
their abstracts or from later accounts, and we made a single search for a later proof by this method.

A second, independent check could not tell whether the argument differs from what is in print, so on this point the two
checks disagree. It points out that Labesse (1995) already combines regular functions with multipliers, that Flicker
(1988) proves full base change for `GL_2` by an asymptotic trace formula, that Beuzart-Plessis, Liu, Zhang and Zhu
obtain weak base change for unitary groups, and that Cognet's theorem is local. In its view what may be substantial
here is the uniform control of the errors as the supports and norms grow, and this has not been examined. We would like
to know whether the argument is in Labesse (1995) or in Flicker (1988), and whether its uniform estimates go beyond
routine.

## Sketch of the proof

The test functions on `GL_n(𝔸_M)¹` and `GL_n(𝔸_K)¹` are products. At infinity all places are complex and split, so
matching is convolution, and one takes a smooth compactly supported function and its translates by the centre of the
enveloping algebra. At one auxiliary split finite place `q` one takes the indicator of the Iwahori double coset of
`ϖ^{jν}`, where `ν` is strictly decreasing with sum `0` and no proper sub-sum `0`, and lets `j → ∞`. At finitely many
unramified places one takes spherical functions, and at the bad places a fixed matching pair.

On the geometric side, the product formula shows that for `j` large every rational `δ` contributing to the twisted
kernel has a norm with irreducible characteristic polynomial. The kernel on the diagonal vanishes above height
`e^{C₀j}`, and for matching functions the integrals of the two kernels agree up to the product of the local constants.
There are no weighted orbital integrals, no unipotent terms and no truncation operator.

For the local matching at unramified inert places, the unit element is matched by Kottwitz's lemma, proved by descent of
`σ`-stable lattices and surjectivity of the norm on units. For the other spherical functions the proof does not use the
fundamental lemma for the Hecke algebra. Instead, for each Satake parameter `t` and each degree bound, it produces a
polynomial `γ` with `γ(t) ≠ 0` such that, for every polynomial `p` within the bound, the spherical functions with
transforms `γ·p` upstairs and `b(γ·p)` downstairs match, where `b` is the base change of Satake transforms. This goes
through an explicit matching of Iwahori functions of strictly dominant `a` and `l·a`. At split places products are
matched against convolutions. At the bad non-split places only functions supported near the identity are used, where
the norm is `γ ↦ γ^l`.

The spectral side uses no Eisenstein series and no fine spectral expansion. If `R(f) = A∘M∘R(g)` has kernel vanishing
above height `Z`, the kernel integral differs from the twisted trace on the cuspidal spectrum by at most
`C·Z^N·‖M(1 − P_cusp)‖ + C·Z^{−N'}‖M‖`. The operator `M` combines Hecke operators, the centre of the enveloping algebra
and a positive amplifier `A`. It is built from truncations of an entire series vanishing at the parameters induced from
finitely many cuspidal data of proper Levi subgroups, whose norm on the non-cuspidal part is at most `C^{d+1}/d!` (this
goes through constant terms), from a polynomial separating one eigensystem from finitely many others, and from
Chebyshev polynomials in `A`. On each remaining finite-dimensional space the trace of `U_q^j∘R(g)∘θ`, where `U_q` is the
Iwahori operator at `q` and `θ` the twist, is an exponential polynomial in `j`. So the asymptotic identities become
exact for large `j`: for each non-induced eigensystem over `M`, the twisted traces equal `c` times the sum of the traces
over the eigensystems over `K` that lift to it.

For the lift, descent and fibres, take indicators of open subgroups downstairs. Then every term is a positive multiple
of a power sum, so the `K`-side is non-zero for infinitely many `j`, and a `σ`-stable cuspidal summand exists upstairs.
If the lift is induced, induction on the rank and a count of pole orders of Rankin–Selberg type give `π ≅ π ⊗ η`.
Upstairs the `σ`-stable summand with a given eigensystem is unique with non-zero trace, which gives descent. The fibres
follow from the same pole count, and `P_w ≅ π_v` at split places follows by varying the function there.

The trace identity applies the comparison twice with the same components away from `v`, once with `(φ, f)` and once
with a reference pair, and divides. Base change of `GL_2` from a totally real field to a CM field is a theta lift between
`SL_2` and the orthogonal group of the Hermitian `2 × 2` matrices: if a constant term of the lift vanishes, the lift is
cuspidal, and otherwise `π ≅ π ⊗ η_{E/F}`. The induction to `GL_4` runs the same machinery on the `σ`-stable
non-cuspidal eigensystem of `π ⊞ π^σ`.

## Key steps

- **Elliptic geometric side** (`PositivityTheory.Rg.Tf.Geo.EllipticityFromProductFormula`, a statement, proved as
  `UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.G.g1St`): a high power of a regular Iwahori operator at a split place leaves only
  elliptic regular classes, as in the simple trace formulas of Flicker–Kazhdan and Flicker.
- **Spherical matching without the full fundamental lemma** (`UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.H.locHeckeInert`):
  spherical functions divisible by a polynomial chosen for the Satake parameter are matched through Iwahori functions,
  and the unit is matched separately (`PositivityTheory.Rg.Tf.Loc.bcflUnit`). We read the statement and the top-level
  proof.
- **Approximate removal of the non-cuspidal spectrum** (`UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.Kill`): truncated entire series
  with norm at most `C^{d+1}/d!` on the non-cuspidal part, proved through constant terms as
  `UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.kill`. We read the statement, not the proof.
- **Exact identities from growth in `j`** (`UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.ci`): the asymptotic comparison becomes an
  identity of cuspidal twisted traces because traces of powers of the Iwahori operator are exponential polynomials. We
  read the proof in part.
- **Lift** (`UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.T.posSide`): positivity of the `K`-side for infinitely many `j` produces a
  `σ`-stable cuspidal summand upstairs. The step `T.coreLift` is an implication from the spectral and geometric
  identities, which are themselves proved as `UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.ci` and
  `UpperTheory.ConvolutionTheory.GM.gm`. We read the top-level proofs.
- **Twisted trace identity** (`UpperTheory.Gal.InertialTypesAlt.G.core`): the comparison at a non-split place, divided
  by a reference identity. The fibre `{π ⊗ η}` gives a single trace because `f` lives where the determinant is a local
  norm. We read the top-level proof.

## Caveats

We read the statements and top-level proofs of `cbcc`, `coreLift`, `liftStep`, `gm_of_unit`, `locAll`,
`locHeckeInert`, `deepHecke`, `locUp`, `posSide`, `varTrace`, `G.core`, `quadBC2` and `indCs`, and part of the proof
of `ciOf`. Of `Kill`, `OneSide`, `Kern2`, `CIx`, `coreDesc`, `fibre_holds`, the unit-element lemma and the theta-lift
lemmas we read only the statements or only the names. We compiled nothing. That there is no `axiom` and no `sorry`
rests on a text search, which found `sorry` only in `Challenge.lean`. The source also states results of Arthur–Clozel,
for which we found no proof, and we have not checked that the main theorems do not depend on these statements. We have
not read Arthur–Clozel's book, Langlands's *Base change for GL(2)*, the papers of Clozel and of Labesse on the
fundamental lemma, or Henniart–Herb, and we have not read the full texts of Flicker, Flicker–Kazhdan, Kottwitz (1986)
and Cognet. The web sources we used were read in summary form, and we made only one search for a later proof by this
method.
