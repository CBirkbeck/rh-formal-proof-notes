## Statement

Let `E` be a totally complex number field, and let `V` be a nonzero space of automorphic forms on `GL_N(𝔸_E)` that is
irreducible under `GL_N(𝔸_f)`, `K_∞` and the Lie algebra, invariant under the split centre, and square-integrable modulo
it. Suppose that at one embedding `τ : E → ℂ` the Capelli elements act on `V` through `N` complex numbers that are
pairwise distinct with pairwise integral differences. Then `N = ad`, and there are a unitary cuspidal representation `π`
of `GL_a(𝔸_E)` and an injective equivariant map from `V` into the forms induced from
`π|det|^{−(d−1)/2} ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ π|det|^{(d−1)/2}`, with the exponents ascending, so that the Speh representation appears as a
subrepresentation. The proof also obtains the local components of `V` at every finite place, which are local Speh
representations (`PositivityTheory.Rg.Rs.ascSeed_holds2`), and its infinitesimal character
(`PositivityTheory.Rg.Rs.infAsc_holds`).

This is a special case of the exhaustion half of the theorem of Mœglin and Waldspurger, in the form "cuspidal support
and exponents": the field is totally complex, and the infinitesimal character is regular and integral at one place.
Not proved here are the case without regularity, the existence of the residual representations for `d > 1`,
multiplicity one, and the statement that `V` is a residue of Eisenstein series.

## Context

Mœglin and Waldspurger obtain the discrete spectrum from Langlands' spectral decomposition, with intertwining
operators normalised by `L`- and `ε`-factors of pairs. In the part we read, the formal proof instead finds the
cuspidal support it needs from constant terms and the square-integrability criterion. In that part, its proof of the
Jacquet–Shalika rigidity theorem for isobaric sums contains no non-vanishing statement on `Re s = 1`.

## Comparison with the literature

The argument differs from Mœglin and Waldspurger's at the essential step. The cuspidal support and the exponents come
not from residues of Eisenstein series and intertwining operators but from constant terms along maximal parabolics,
both halves of the square-integrability criterion, an induction through an extremal exponent, unitarity, the
Jacquet–Shalika classification, a local unitarity inequality and the regular integral infinitesimal character. It is
not a proof of the theorem in general. The proof of rigidity is Jacquet and Shalika's, with Landau's theorem in place of
Shahidi's non-vanishing at `s = 1`, and we cannot tell whether that variant is in print. Our comparison rests on the
assembly files, read in full, on part of Mœglin–Waldspurger, on the relevant pages of Jacquet–Shalika and on two
searches. We also have a recollection, which we have not checked, that the regular case may be reachable by other known
means.

A second, independent check could not tell whether the argument is in print, and so disagrees with us: we see a route
different from the published proof we compared, while the second check does not regard it as established that the
route is absent from the literature. In its view the ingredients are standard. The induction through an extremal
exponent could be an idea of its own, but whether it was used before has not been verified, and positivity in place of
non-vanishing is known in spirit, in particular from Brumley. Jacquet's 1983 paper, which we know only from its opening
pages, has both directions of the exponent criterion and partial exhaustion results. We would like to know whether
Jacquet's 1983 paper or the classifications of the unitary dual give this case, and whether the induction through an
extremal exponent is in print.

## Sketch of the proof

The proof is by strong induction on `N`, in nine steps.

(1) Either all constant terms of `V` along the maximal parabolics `P_{r,N−r}` vanish, and then `V` is cuspidal and
`d = 1`, or some constant term has an exponent `c`, that is, an eigencharacter `e^{ct}` of `diag(e^t I_r, I_{N−r})` at
infinity. The sets of exponents are finite.

(2) The necessity half of the square-integrability criterion says that every exponent of a square-integrable form has
`Re c < ρ_P`. It is proved directly, by an `L²` decay estimate for the constant term coming from a lattice-point count.

(3) Choose `(r, c)` minimising `(ρ_P − Re c)/(r(N−r))`, and let `λ > 0` be the minimum. Then for every exponent `c′`
along `P_{m,N−m}` one has `Re c′ − ρ_P ≤ −λ·m(N−m)`, with equality at `(r, c)`.

(4) The `c`-eigenpart of the constant term along `P_{r,s}` is a nonzero, hence injective, equivariant map on `V`. Its
restrictions to `GL_r × GL_s` are automorphic forms whose exponents are exponents of `V`.

(5) By concavity, after central twists `|det|^{−z₁}` and `|det|^{−z₂}` with `r z₁ + s z₂ = 0`, these restrictions have
only negative exponents. Hence they are square-integrable by the sufficiency half of the criterion, which is proved by
bootstrapping growth bounds on Siegel sets.

(6) Projecting to irreducible summands gives discrete `V₁` on `GL_r` and `V₂` on `GL_s`, and an embedding of `V` into
the forms induced from `V₁|det|^{z₁} ⊗ V₂|det|^{z₂}`.

(7) Infinitesimal characters add, so `V₁` and `V₂` are again regular and integral at `τ`, and by induction each embeds
in the forms induced from a segment of some `(π_i, d_i)`.

(8) The string step is where regularity is used. At almost all places the Satake multiset of `V` is the union of the
shifted multisets of `π₁` and `π₂`.
(a) Unitarity makes them stable under `b ↦ b̄^{−1}`. With rigidity for isobaric sums, the bottom exponent of the first
string cannot be matched inside it, so `π₁ ≅ π₂` with real shifts.
(b) The infinitesimal character at `τ` forces the two exponent strings to be disjoint with integral differences.
(c) A local inequality for unitary spherical representations, saying that in the Satake multiset a parameter `z` with
`|z| < q^{−1/2}` has multiplicity at most that of `qz`, together with the Jacquet–Shalika bound
`q^{−1/2} < |β| < q^{1/2}`, closes the exponent set under `u ↦ u − 1` for `u ≥ 1`.
(d) A disjoint union of two unit-step strings, symmetric under negation and closed under that step, with centres
`c₁ < 0 < c₂` and `d₁c₁ + d₂c₂ = 0`, is one string.

(9) Induction in stages gives the embedding for `(π₁, d₁ + d₂)`.

The local inequality is proved directly. The `L²` inner product makes the local component unitary. Vectors attached to
the subspaces of `k^{n+1}`, where `k` is the residue field, together with one translation operator, give a
finite-dimensional space on which an operator is expanding for the invariant form, and a polynomial identity gives the
inequality.

For rigidity for isobaric sums, each cuspidal class is a unitary one twisted by `|det|^t` with `t` real. The inputs are
`|α| ≤ q^{1/2}`, `|det| = 1`, and analyticity of `(s−1)^{δ} L^S(s, x × ȳ)` at real `s > 0`, where `δ = 1` if `x = y`
and `0` otherwise, which comes from Rankin–Selberg integrals. Landau's theorem gives convergence of
`log L^S(σ, x × x̄)` for `σ > 1`, and Cauchy–Schwarz gives the rest. For a family, `Σ_{i,j} log L^S(σ, x_i × x̄_j)` is
unbounded as `σ → 1⁺`. Otherwise Landau's theorem would give convergence for `σ > 0`, and a lower bound from Newton's
identities contradicts `Σ q_w^{−1} = ∞`. Two families with equal power sums of Satake parameters and least exponent `0`
share a member, since otherwise Landau's theorem bounds the exponent-zero block at `σ = 1`, against the unboundedness.
One removes the common member and inducts. No non-vanishing on `Re s = 1` is used.

## Key steps

- **Extremal exponent** (`PositivityTheory.Rg.Rs.PAscAll.exists_extremal`): chooses `(r, c)` minimising
  `(ρ_P − Re c)/(r(N−r))`, so that all exponents lie under a concave curve through it. This choice makes the twisted
  restrictions square-integrable.
- **Both halves of the criterion** (`PositivityTheory.Rg.Rs.expNeg_holds`, `PositivityTheory.Rg.Rs.sqIntCrit_holds`):
  the exponents of a square-integrable form are negative (`Re c < ρ_P`), and a form with only negative exponents is
  square-integrable. These are proved from a lattice-point count and from growth bounds on Siegel sets.
- **String step** (`PositivityTheory.Rg.Rs.stringStep_of`, an implication whose hypotheses are supplied in
  `PositivityTheory.Rg.Rs.ascAll_holds`): the two segments under the embedding join into one. It uses rigidity, the
  Jacquet–Shalika bound, the infinitesimal character at `τ` and the local inequality.
- **Local unitarity inequality** (`PositivityTheory.Rg.Rs.gap_holds`): for unitary spherical representations, a Satake
  parameter `z` with `|z| < q^{−1/2}` has multiplicity at most that of `qz`. It is proved directly. It also follows from
  Tadić's classification.
- **Unboundedness by positivity** (`PositivityTheory.RgJS.dirichlet_series_unbounded`, an implication whose hypotheses
  are supplied in `PositivityTheory.RgJS.absRig_closed`): the double sum of logarithms of partial Rankin–Selberg
  `L`-functions is unbounded as `σ → 1⁺`. It plays the part of non-vanishing at `s = 1`.

## Caveats

We read in full the assembly files, namely the induction, the slice, the chord, the projection, the string step, the
gluing of the local inequality and the two assembly files of the rigidity proof, together with every statement they
combine, and the main proofs of the two positivity lemmas. The supporting proofs we read only as statements. Among
them are the Rankin–Selberg input (about 12,500 lines), the Jacquet–Shalika bound, the operator proof of the local
inequality and the analytic estimates behind the criterion. Many lemma names do not describe their content, so we went
by the statements. We compiled nothing and produced no list of axioms, and a word search for `sorry`, `axiom`, `admit`
and `native_decide` in those directories found nothing. We found no proof of a bundled statement that also includes
uniqueness of `(d, π)`. Apart from the parts listed under References, we did not open Jacquet's paper, the
Mœglin–Waldspurger book (for the criterion), Shahidi's paper or Tadić's classification, and we did not read §§III–IV
and I.4–I.11 of Mœglin–Waldspurger.
