## Statement

Let `w` be a finite place of a number field `E` and `F = E_w`. The formal proof builds a local Artin map `a` and a map
`rc` from the irreducible smooth representations of all the groups `GL_n(F)` to Frobenius-semisimple Weil–Deligne
representations. The map `rc` takes a representation of `GL_n(F)` to one of dimension `n`, satisfies `rc(χ) = χ∘a` on
characters and the segment rule, has irreducible values on supercuspidals, is compatible with twists by `|det|^s` for
real `s`, and satisfies `|det rc(π)| = |ω_π∘a|`. Compatibility with twists by all characters, the identity
`det rc(π) = ω_π∘a`, compatibility with contragredients and injectivity are proved only on "good" representations. These
are the representations whose supercuspidal support consists of characters and, if `F ≅ ℚ_p`, of character twists of
depth-zero supercuspidals. The map stands in for the local Langlands correspondence for `GL_n` and is not that
correspondence: off the good class, every supercuspidal of rank `n ≥ 2` goes to an unramified twist of one fixed
parameter of that rank.

In place of Caraiani's local–global compatibility the formal proof has the following. Let `π` be regular algebraic,
polarized and cuspidal over a CM field, and let `w ∤ ℓ`. Then `WD(r_π|W_w) = rc(π_w ⊗ |det|^{(1−n)/2})` whenever the
right side is trivial on the commutators of the Weil group and has `N = 0`. At certain tame places with `E_w ≅ ℚ_p`, and
provided `n = 1` or `p > 16n²`, it proves that `N = 0` and that the two sides have the same characteristic polynomials on
inertia. In the source, the form without the bound on `p` is obtained from two statements proved separately.

## Context

As we understand the literature, from abstracts and later accounts, the local Langlands correspondence for `GL_n` and
local–global compatibility at every finite place are proved by global methods: Shimura varieties and their bad
reduction, and for the correspondence also automorphic induction. On the part of the formal proof that we read, neither
is used. The proof works with a map that has only the properties it needs, and proves compatibility only at principal
series of characters and at certain depth-zero places over `ℚ_p`.

## Comparison with the literature

The statement is narrower than the published theorems, and the proof is not a new one. The local map is a definition
by cases. Its laws follow from the definition and from standard theory: the Langlands and Zelevinsky classification,
level-zero types, Green's formula at regular elliptic elements, Clifford theory of tame parameters, and class field
theory. Nothing is proved about the local Langlands correspondence itself, not even its formula in depth zero. The
local–global statements are special cases, relative to this map, reached by solvable base change from unramified
places. For principal series of characters this is the base-change technique of Barnet-Lamb, Gee, Geraghty and Taylor,
reduced to the unramified case. At depth zero over `ℚ_p` it is combined with a computation, from a twisted trace
identity, of how depth-zero types move under unramified cyclic base change, that is, Shintani lifting. That conclusion
is in the literature. We found the statement in print, but not this derivation of it, and our search was brief.

We are surer of all this for the local map than for the local–global part. Our view rests on twenty Lean files read in
full, and we have read none of the published proofs in full. Only one check was made on this item: no second,
independent reader looked at it. We would like to know whether this derivation of Shintani lifting is published for
`GL_n`, and whether the depth-zero value of the map agrees with Bushnell–Henniart's formula in the normalisations used
here: geometric Frobenius, `θ` or `θ⁻¹`, and the twist by `|det|^{(1−n)/2}`.

## Sketch of the proof

The local map is built in six steps.

(1) A local Artin map `a : W → F^×`, where `W` is the Weil group at `w`, is obtained, and shown to be unique, from
global Artin reciprocity and a theorem on units.

(2) On supercuspidals a relation is defined. A character `χ` goes to `χ∘a`. If `F ≅ ℚ_p`, a twist `y₀ ⊗ χ` of a
depth-zero supercuspidal `y₀` goes to `V₀ ⊗ χ∘a`, where `V₀` is irreducible of the right dimension and tame, with
determinant the central character and with inertial characteristic polynomials given by the type. The map is a choice
function for this relation, and the choice is shown to be forced.

(3) The parameter `V₀` is constructed as follows. The invariants under the first congruence subgroup `K₁` contain a
cuspidal representation `τ` of `GL_n(𝔽_p)`, unique up to isomorphism. Green's formula gives a regular character `θ` of
`𝔽_{p^n}^×`, unique up to Frobenius, by a Parseval count over the elements of degree `n`. The Artin map of an
unramified extension gives a tame character of inertia with values in `𝔽_{p^n}^×`, and an induced representation has as
inertial characters the Frobenius orbit of `θ` composed with it, and has the prescribed determinant. By Clifford theory
for the tame quotient, an irreducible tame parameter is determined by its inertial type and its determinant.

(4) Every other supercuspidal of `GL_n`, `n ≥ 2`, goes to `J_n ⊗ |·|^e`. Here `J_n` is one fixed irreducible
`n`-dimensional parameter that is not a twist of a tame one, and `e` is chosen so that `|det rc(π)| = |ω_π∘a|`. As far as
we read it, `J_n` is built from a wildly ramified character of the unramified extension of degree `n`. This is where the
map leaves the correspondence.

(5) The map is extended to all irreducibles by the segment rule, using existence and uniqueness of Langlands data, which
are proved elsewhere in the source.

(6) The laws are checked segment by segment. That a tame supercuspidal over `ℚ_p` has depth zero comes from a count of
depth-zero types against irreducible tame parameters with fixed determinant at Frobenius, together with injectivity. It
holds because `J_n` was chosen non-tame.

Local–global compatibility then takes five more steps.

(7) The input, which we did not examine, is the existence of the Galois representation `r` and compatibility where `π_w`
is unramified.

(8) Principal series of characters. For each Weil element `g` of nonzero degree there are a solvable CM extension `M/E`,
reached by cyclic steps of prime degree along which `π` stays cuspidal, and a place `u ∣ w` such that `g` lies in the
Weil group of `M_u` and the base change is unramified at `u` with Satake parameters `ξ_j(a(g))`. Compatibility at `u`
gives the characteristic polynomial of `r(g)`. The elements of degree zero follow, `N = 0` descends, and the two
Frobenius-semisimple classes agree.

(9) Depth zero over `ℚ_p`. By the laws of the map, the hypothesis says that `π_w` has depth zero and that the members of
rank `≥ 2` of its support are pairwise not inertially equivalent. This is our reading of the condition, and we did not
check it against its definition. The form the main theorem uses also needs `n = 1` or `p > 16n²`. Cyclic base changes of
prime degree, all cuspidal, lead to a place `u ∣ w`, unramified of degree `n!`, where the component has supercuspidal
support of tamely ramified characters, pairwise unlinked, reproducing the inertial polynomial of `rc(π_w)`. Step (8)
applies at `u`, and inertia at `w` and at `u` coincide.

(10) The statement about the support is proved by following a combinatorial label of the `K₁`-invariants. At a split
step the label is unchanged. At an inert step it is replaced by its Shintani lift. This goes through a twisted trace
identity, up to a nonzero constant, between `π_v` and its base change, for a class function on `GL_n(𝔽_q)` inflated to
`GL_n(𝒪)` and its Shintani lift. That identity comes from a global twisted trace identity and a matching of inflated
functions.

(11) At `w ∣ ℓ` the same reductions give de Rham and potentially semistable statements, by descent along finite
extensions.

## Key steps

- **Depth-zero values** (`UpperTheory.Zero.LLC.parEx`): builds the tame parameter attached to a depth-zero
  supercuspidal of `GL_n(ℚ_p)` from its `K₁`-type, Green's regular character and the central character. The published
  explicit formula is not used, and we did not check that the result agrees with it in the normalisations of the formal
  proof.
- **The fixed value elsewhere** (`UpperTheory.Zero.LLC.junkEx`, used in `UpperTheory.Zero.LLC.cuspEx`): one irreducible
  non-tame parameter per rank, twisted by an unramified character to fix `|det|`. This is why the map is not the
  correspondence and is unique only on good representations.
- **Segment rule** (`GlobalTheory.LocLanglands.exists_isLExt`): extends a map on supercuspidals to all irreducibles
  through Langlands data, whose existence and uniqueness enter as two statements proved elsewhere in the source
  (`UpperTheory.Zero.LLC.Pr.exh`, `UpperTheory.Zero.LLC.Pr.multi`).
- **Principal series of characters** (`GalTw.GlobalTheory.RoadU.away_eq_of_plainTor`, an implication from two named
  statements, one proved as `UpperTheory.Gal.ToralGalois.inq_holds`, the other obtained from the input of step (7)):
  `N = 0` and equality with the value of the map, from traces of Weil elements of nonzero degree after solvable base
  change to an unramified place.
- **Depth zero over `ℚ_p`** (`UpperTheory.Gal.ToralGalois.R2.toTor`, an implication from seven named statements, whose
  form with all of them supplied is `UpperTheory.Gal.ToralGalois.R2.toTor_holds`): the unramified cyclic tower to degree
  `n!` that turns the depth-zero case into the principal-series case at a place above `w` with the same inertia.
- **Shintani lifting** (`UpperTheory.Gal.InertialTypesAlt.L.finTrace`, an implication from five named statements,
  discharged in `finTrace_holds`, `finTrace_of_matching` and `UpperTheory.Gal.InertialTypesAlt.suppBC_outright`): the
  twisted trace identity for inflated class functions and their Shintani lifts that carries the `K₁`-label up an inert
  step. We did not read its global input.

## Caveats

We read in full twenty Lean files of the local map and of the local–global assembly, and eight more in part. The
construction of the Galois representations, compatibility at unramified places, the global twisted trace identity, the
matching of inflated functions, the Langlands classification and the level-zero type lemmas we read only as statements.
We did not recompute the dependency closure of the main theorem. No `sorry` or `axiom` occurs in the 18,431 files of
`src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic`, but we did not scan lemmas imported from other directories, and we checked that
the main theorem avoids the stated correspondence only along the part of the proof we read. We did not open
Harris–Taylor's book, Henniart (2000; Math. Nachr. 158, 1992), Bushnell–Henniart's "essentially tame" series,
Silberger–Zink, Shin or Chenevier–Harris. Haines (Ann. Sci. Éc. Norm. Supér. 45 (2012), 681–718, arXiv:1012.4968) and
Dhar–Nadimpalli (Math. Ann. 396 (2026), article 69, arXiv:2507.09773) we saw only as a search snippet and an abstract.
We read no published proof in full.
