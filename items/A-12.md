## Statement

Let `p` be any prime, `2` included. Let `C` be an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0 with a real-valued
non-archimedean absolute value such that `|p| < 1`, and let `𝒪` be its valuation ring, assumed `p`-adically complete.
For a real-valued valuation with `|p| < 1` this amounts to `C` being complete. That remark is ours and is not in the
source.

In the source, a `p`-divisible group `G` of height `h` over `𝒪` is a tower of commutative, cocommutative Hopf algebras
`A_n` (the functions on `G[p^n]`), finite projective of rank `p^{nh}`, with surjections `A_{n+1} → A_n` whose kernel is
generated by the image of the augmentation ideal under multiplication by `p^n`. The dual `G^D` has the dual Hopf
algebras `A_n^∨`. The Tate module `T_pG` is the module of systems of points `x_n : A_n → 𝒪` with `p·x_{n+1} = x_n`, and
`ω_{G^D}` is the inverse limit of the cotangent modules `I/I²` of the `A_n^∨`, where `I` is the augmentation ideal. The
Hodge–Tate map `𝒪 ⊗_{ℤ_p} T_pG → ω_{G^D}` sends `1 ⊗ (x_n)` to the classes of `x_n − 1`, with `x_n` read as a
group-like element of `A_n^∨`.

**Theorem.** For every `κ ∈ 𝒪` with `|κ|^{p−1} ≤ |p|`, the module `κ·ω_{G^D}` lies in the image of the Hodge–Tate map,
so `κ` kills its cokernel. At `p = 2`, `κ = 2` qualifies.

**Finite-level form** (`smul_dUnit_mem`, which does not need completeness). Let `A` be a commutative, cocommutative
bialgebra, finite projective over `𝒪`, with finitely many group-like elements and augmentation ideal `I`. Let `q ≠ 0` in
`𝒪`, and let `M ⊆ I/I²` be a submodule containing `q·(I/I²)` and the classes of `u − 1` for `u` group-like. Then
`κ·(I/I²) ⊆ M` for every `κ` as in the theorem.

## Context

The Hodge–Tate map compares two lattices attached to `G`: the span of the Tate module and the invariant differentials
of the dual. A bound for the cokernel says how far apart they are, and in particular the map is onto after inverting
`p`. Integral constructions with the Hodge bundle of abelian varieties with level structure at `p` use such a bound.
The statement covers `p = 2` with the same exponent `1/(p−1)`, and the argument uses only the Hopf algebras of the
levels.

## Comparison with the literature

The statement is known. It is Théorèmes 2 and 3 of Fargues (2011), which make no assumption on `p` and include the
endpoint. Our comparison was made with the statement as proved here, with `C` complete, so it bears on that statement.
At finite level, however, we compared only with finite flat group schemes, and not with the bialgebras, arbitrary `q`
and submodules `M` of the formal proof. What we could not find written out in full is the case `p = 2`. There
Fargues's proof rests on a remark extending Faltings's integral comparison theorem, and Faltings (1999) proves that
comparison for `p ≥ 3` and leaves `p = 2` to a remark. Fargues (2010, Théorème 7) and a 2006 preprint of Fargues,
which we take to be a chapter of Fargues–Genestier–Lafforgue, assume `p ≠ 2`. Pilloni and Stroh (Remarque 1.10) record
the case `p = 2`, with the weaker factor `4`, as not written up. We have looked at later work, though not
systematically, and found nothing that changes this. Boxer and Pilloni (preprint, 2021) still use the factor `4` at
`p = 2`. Howard (2020) describes the Hodge–Tate map integrally only for `p > 2`. Kim (2012) proves part of Fargues's
remark over 2-adic discrete valuation rings, but not the part that Fargues's proof uses.

An independent second check agrees that the statement is known, but differs on the gap at `p = 2`. It points out that
Fargues does supply a proof at every prime, through his remark, which he states for all primes, and that weaker bounds
in families do not show a gap over `𝒪`. So we would not say that no complete proof is in print, only that we did not
locate one. Fargues's theorem is not literally the bialgebra lemma of the formal proof, and the comparison with it
does not settle whether that lemma, or the method of proof, is in print. The formal proof reaches the bound at every
prime by an argument that uses only the Hopf algebras of the finite levels. We would like to know whether the
comparison Fargues uses is written out somewhere at `p = 2`, and whether the bialgebra lemma or its method is in
print.

## Sketch of the proof

*Reduction to linear functionals* (`smul_dUnit_mem`, `mem_of_forall_dvd`). Take `A`, `q`, `M` as in the finite-level
form, with counit `ε`. Let `N ⊆ A` be the `𝒪`-span of the group-like elements, of the products `(a − ε(a))(b − ε(b))`
and of `qA`. It is finitely generated, and this is where the finiteness of the set of group-like elements is used. The
map `a ↦ class of a − ε(a)` sends `N` into `M`, so it suffices to prove `κA ⊆ N`. Being finitely generated and
torsion-free over the valuation ring `𝒪`, `N` is free. Composing multiplication by `q : A → N` with the coordinates of a
basis shows that `x ∈ N` as soon as `q | φ(x)` for every linear `φ : A → 𝒪` with `φ(N) ⊆ q𝒪`. Such a `φ` is divisible
by `q` on group-like elements and satisfies `q | φ(ab) − ε(a)φ(b) − ε(b)φ(a)`, so it is a derivation at the origin
modulo `q`. What remains is `q | κ·φ(a)` for all `a` (`dvd_mul_of_val`, and `dvd_mul_of_norm` once `‖p‖ = 1/p`).

*Two convolution algebras.* `B₁ = Hom_𝒪(A, C)` and `B₂ = Hom_𝒪(A ⊗ A, C)`, with the convolution products, are
`C`-algebras, commutative because `A` is cocommutative. Three algebra maps `B₁ → B₂` are used: `Δ(f) = f∘(product)`,
`i(f) = f ⊗ ε` and `j(f) = ε ⊗ f`. On `B₁`, resp. `B₂`, let `N₁`, resp. `N₂`, be the supremum of `|f|` over `A`, resp.
`A ⊗ A`, which is finite because `A` is finitely generated. These are homogeneous, ultrametric and submultiplicative,
with `N₂(Δf) ≤ N₁(f)` and `N₂(i(f)·j(g)) = N₁(f)·N₁(g)`. Put `y = q⁻¹φ ∈ B₁`. The derivation property says
`N₂(Δy − i(y) − j(y)) ≤ 1`.

*Bounded powers* (`isIntegral_inv_smul_of_closed`, `exists_supN_pow_le`). `φ` lies in the finite `𝒪`-algebra `A^∨`,
whose `𝒪`-points are the evaluations at the group-like elements of `A` (`exists_isGroupLikeElem`, by biduality). So
every point takes on `φ` a value divisible by `q`. Over `C` the minimal polynomial of `φ`, as an element of `B₁`,
splits, and each root `α` is the value at `φ` of a `C`-valued character of the subalgebra generated by `A^∨`. That
character sends `A^∨` to elements integral over `𝒪`, hence into `𝒪`, so it is a point and `q | α`. Thus `y` is a root
of `∏(Y − α/q)`, which is monic over `𝒪`. Its powers lie in a finitely generated `𝒪`-module, and `N₁(y^k) ≤ K` for all
`k`. This uses that `C` is algebraically closed and that `𝒪` is integrally closed in it. Reducedness of `A^∨ ⊗ C` is
not used here.

*The estimate* (`norm_smul_le_one_of_lt`, stated abstractly for `B₁, B₂, N₁, N₂, Δ, i, j, y`). Let
`‖c‖ < p^{−1/(p−1)}`, `z = cy`, `e = Δy − i(y) − j(y)` and `t(m) = N₁(z^m/m!)`. The formula for the `p`-adic valuation
of `m!` gives `‖1/m!‖ ≤ p^{m/(p−1)}` (`norm_factorial_inv_le`). So, with `ρ = p^{1/(p−1)}·‖c‖ < 1`, we have
`t(m) ≤ K·ρ^m → 0`, and `N₂((ce)^l/l!) ≤ ρ^l < 1` for `l ≥ 1`. Suppose `t(1) > 1`, and let `k` be the last index at
which `t` attains its maximum. Since `Δ(z) = i(z) + j(z) + ce`, the binomial formula for divided powers writes
`Δ(z^{2k}/(2k)!)` as the sum of the terms `i(z^a/a!)·j(z^b/b!)·(ce)^l/l!` with `a + b + l = 2k`. The term `(k, k, 0)`
has `N₂` equal to `t(k)²`. Every other term has `N₂ < t(k)²`, because it has a factor `< 1` if `l ≥ 1`, and an index
beyond `k` if `l = 0`. But `N₂(Δ(z^{2k}/(2k)!)) ≤ t(2k) ≤ t(k) < t(k)²`, which contradicts the ultrametric inequality.
So `N₁(cy) ≤ 1`. The case `‖c‖ = p^{−1/(p−1)}` (`norm_smul_le_one`) follows by multiplying `c` by an `n`-th root of `p`
and letting `n` grow. Hence `q | κ·φ(a)`. No step treats `p = 2` separately.

*The `p`-divisible group* (`smul_mem_range_dlogLinear`). For a `p`-divisible group `H`, let `L ⊆ ω_H` be the image of
`𝒪 ⊗ T_p(H^D)`. The finite-level form is applied to `A = A_n(H)`, `q = p^n`, and `M` the image of `L` at level `n`. Its
hypotheses hold: `p^n` kills `I/I²` of `A_n`, and every group-like element of `A_n` is an `𝒪`-point of `H^D[p^n]`.
These points are finite in number and all come from `T_p(H^D)` (`natCard_pts_eq`, `pt_surjective`). The count,
`p^{nh}`, uses that a finite Hopf algebra over a field of characteristic 0 is étale, a classical theorem that the source
proves as `etale_of_hopfAlgebra`. So `κ`, and also `p`, multiply `I/I²` at every level into the image of `L`.

*Passage to the limit* (`smul_mem_of_levels`). `L` is finitely generated, since `T_p` is finite over `ℤ_p`. For
`w ∈ ω_H` the proof builds coefficient vectors `c_n ∈ 𝒪^m` that express `κw` through generators of `L` at level `n + 1`,
with `c_{n+1} − c_n ∈ p^n𝒪^m`. Indeed, a solution at level `n + 2` minus `c_n` gives, at level `n + 2`, a vector
restricting to zero at level `n + 1`, hence `p^{n+1}` times a cotangent vector (`exists_of_cotMap_res_eq_zero`), and `p`
times that vector comes from `L`. The `p`-adic limit of the `c_n` expresses `κw` at every level. This is the only use of
completeness. The main theorem takes `H = G^D` and transports along `G ≅ G^{DD}`. A second proof for `H`,
`coker_dlog_killed` (`DvdMulOfGroupLikeVal.lean`, under `src/Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/AlgebraProofs/`,
line 416), assumes that `ω_H` is finite free and passes to the limit by elementary divisors over `𝒪`.

## Key steps

- **The divided-power estimate** (`GlobalTheory.NearlyPrimitive.norm_smul_le_one_of_lt`): bounded powers of `y` and
  `N₂(Δy − i(y) − j(y)) ≤ 1` give `N₁(cy) ≤ 1` for `‖c‖ < p^{−1/(p−1)}`, by isolating the diagonal term of
  `Δ(z^{2k}/(2k)!)`. The exponent `1/(p−1)` comes from here. (`NormSmulLeOneOfPrimitiveBoundedPowers.lean`, line 121.)
- **Integrality from the values at points** (`GlobalTheory.IntegralOfPointValues.isIntegral_inv_smul_of_closed`): if
  `x` in an integral `𝒪`-algebra has value divisible by `q` at every `𝒪`-point, its image in any `C`-algebra, divided
  by `q`, is integral over `𝒪`. It bounds the powers of `y`. (`IsIntegralOfDivisibleCharacters.lean`, line 24.)
- **Derivations modulo `q`** (`PositivityTheory.HtPt.Farsharp.dvd_mul_of_norm`): sets up `B₁`, `B₂`, the three maps
  and the norms for a bialgebra, checks the hypotheses of the estimate, and concludes `q | c·φ(a)`. The bialgebra
  axioms and cocommutativity enter here. (`FarsharpA.lean`, line 132.)
- **Reduction of the cotangent statement** (`PositivityTheory.HtPt.Farsharp.smul_dUnit_mem`): turns
  `κ·(I/I²) ⊆ M` into the divisibility `q | κ·φ(a)` for functionals `φ` with `φ(N) ⊆ q𝒪`, using that `N` is free over
  the valuation ring (`mem_of_forall_dvd`). (`Farsharp.lean`, line 18.)
- **From the levels to the limit** (`PositivityTheory.HtPt.Farsharp.smul_mem_of_levels`): if `κ` and `p` multiply
  every level `I/I²` into the image of a finitely generated `L ⊆ ω`, then `κ·ω ⊆ L`, when `𝒪` is `p`-adically
  complete. The definition of the tower (kernel of `A_{n+1} → A_n`) is used here. (`FarsharpB.lean`, line 118.)

## Caveats

The main declaration `hodgeTateCokerSharp` is a theorem with a proof. Its type is the proposition
`HodgeTateCokerSharp`, a definition, which this theorem proves and which is not assumed anywhere along this proof. We
read in full the three `Farsharp` files, the files of the divided-power estimate and of the integrality lemma, and the
second proof, and we read the tower, `T_p`, `ω` and the Hodge–Tate map as definitions. We took at the level of
statements only the dual tower and biduality, the count of points and the étaleness theorem, the surjectivity of
`T_p → H[p^n](𝒪)`, the finiteness of `T_p`, the conormal sequence, the elementary-divisor lemma, and everything from
Mathlib. We searched for the words `sorry` and `axiom` in the 324 project files in the import closure of
`Farsharp.lean`, and in the 342 for the second proof, and found neither. We did not examine Mathlib. Nothing was
compiled, and no axiom list was produced.
