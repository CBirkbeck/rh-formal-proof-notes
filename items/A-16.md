## Statement

Let `p` be a prime, `n ≤ r` natural numbers, `ν_0 ≥ ⋯ ≥ ν_{n−1} ≥ 0` integers, and `O` a local integral domain in
which `p` is not a unit (not necessarily a discrete valuation ring, nor of characteristic 0). In `O[x]`, `x` an
`r × n` matrix of variables, let `∇_O(ν)` (Lean: `dualWeyl`) be the module of polynomials homogeneous of degree `ν_t`
in the column `t` and unchanged by every substitution adding `T` times a column to a later column, `T` a new variable.
For rows `a, b`, possibly equal, let `E_{ab}^{(k)}` (Lean: `rowDiv`) send `f` to the coefficient of `T^k` in `f` after
`T` times the row `a` is added to the row `b`. Let `v_ν = ∏_{s<n} D_{s+1}^{ν_s − ν_{s+1}}`, where `D_k` is the leading
`k × k` minor and `ν_n := 0`, and let `Δ_O(ν)` (Lean: `weyl`) be the `O`-span of all words in the `E_{ab}^{(k)}`
applied to `v_ν`. Let `l ≥ 0` with `ν_0 ≤ l`, or with `n = r` and `ν_0 − ν_{n−1} ≤ l`. Then, writing `⌊log_p m⌋` for
the largest `k` such that `p^k ≤ m`,

    p^e · ∇_O(ν) ⊂ Δ_O(ν) ⊂ ∇_O(ν),      e = (r(r−1)/2) · ⌊log_p(l + r − 1)⌋      (⌊log_p 0⌋ := 0).

For the weight `λ = (ν_0, …, ν_{n−1}, 0, …, 0)` of `GL_r` the least admissible `l` is `λ_0 − λ_{r−1}`. The same file
proves the statement over every integral domain in which each integer prime to `p` is a unit (`sandwichDomain`), and
over `ℤ` in the form `c·p^e·f ∈ Δ_ℤ(ν)` for `f ∈ ∇_ℤ(ν)`, with an integer `c` prime to `p` depending on `f`
(`sandwichZ`).

## Context

Over a field of characteristic 0 the two modules coincide and are the irreducible representation `V_λ` of `GL_r` of
highest weight `λ`. Over `ℤ_(p)` or `ℤ_p` they are two integral forms of it. The inclusion says that `p^e` annihilates
`∇_O(ν)/Δ_O(ν)`, with `e` logarithmic in the weight, and it gives `∇ = Δ` when `p > λ_0 − λ_{r−1} + r − 1`. The proof
bounds the `p`-adic denominators of step operators of extremal-projector kind on lattices stable under divided powers.

## Comparison with the literature

The statement is weaker than what one deduces from Andersen (1983). We made the comparison with a differently phrased
form: the Weyl lattice and its dual for the contravariant form over a discrete valuation ring of characteristic 0, for
every dominant weight of `GL_N`, with two refinements that are not Lean statements. The statement here has the
polynomial model, non-negative weights and any local domain in which `p` is not a unit, but it reaches that ring by
base change from `ℤ`, so the comparison applies to it. From Andersen's Proposition 4.1 and Lemma 4.4, for the maps, and
Proposition 4.6 (ii) and Proposition 4.16, for the filtration, one deduces `p^m·∇ ⊂ Δ` over `ℤ_(p)` for every prime,
with `m = Σ_{β>0}(⌊log_p⟨β^∨, λ+ρ⟩⌋ − v_p⟨β^∨, λ+ρ⟩)`. This holds for every split reductive group in which `ρ` is a
character of the torus, which for `GL_r` one arranges by restricting to `SL_r`. For `GL_r` we compute
`m ≤ e − v_p(dim V_λ)`, `V_λ` the irreducible representation of highest weight `λ`. For `GL_3`, `λ = (2,1,0)`, `p = 3`
this gives `1` where `e = 3`. The polynomial model is the standard induced module, and base change of bideterminants,
together with the divided operators, carries Andersen's bound to the rings of the statement. No flatness is needed for
this, and flatness should not be invoked in characteristic `p`. In characteristic `p` the inclusion `p^e·∇ ⊂ Δ` is in
any case automatic once `e > 0`. An independent second check agrees, and it checked Andersen's maps.

Gruber (2022, Remark 5.13, attributed there to Andersen) gives a bound by a sum of `p`-adic valuations of factorials,
which grows linearly in the weight. The exponent `e` is better than this factorial bound only asymptotically, not
uniformly: for `GL_2`, `λ = (1,0)`, `p = 2` it is `1` against `0`. What remains is the method: the formal proof uses
truncated extremal projectors and no sheaf cohomology. The rank-one projector and the extremal projectors of higher
rank are in Löwdin, Shapiro and Asherova–Smirnov–Tolstoy, which we know only through Tolstoy's review. As far as that
review shows, these works are antecedents of the method, not of the integral estimate or of this proof. We would like
to know whether this projector proof, or one like it, is in print, and whether the exact exponent is known for `GL_r`.

## Sketch of the proof

`Δ_R(ν) ⊂ ∇_R(ν)` holds over every commutative ring (`weylLeDual`). The other inclusion is proved first over `ℤ_p`,
then over `ℤ`, then over `O`. The representation theory is all in the first of these, and it is done for abstract
operators.

*Step operators.* Let `E_{ab}` be endomorphisms of a vector space `V` over a field of characteristic 0 with
`[E_{ab}, E_{cd}] = δ_{bc}E_{ad} − δ_{da}E_{cb}`. For an index `z` and a finite set `S` of other indices put `Ω_0 = 1`,
`Ω_{q+1} = Σ_{i∈S} E_{zi}·Ω_q·E_{iz}`, `P_{z,S} = Σ_{q=0}^{l} (−1)^q·|S|!/((q+|S|)!·q!)·Ω_q` (Lean: `Om`, `Pstep`) and
`Π = P_{0,{1,…,r−1}} ∘ P_{1,{2,…,r−1}} ∘ ⋯ ∘ P_{r−1,∅}` (`InvOp`). For `|S| = 1` this is the `sl_2` formula
`Σ_q (−1)^q E_{zi}^q·E_{iz}^q/(q!·(q+1)!)` for the extremal projector on vectors of weight zero, cut off at `q = l`.

*`Π` produces invariants* (`invariant_inverse_holds`). Let `N ⊂ V` be stable under all `E_{ab}` and contain no nonzero
eigenvector of any `E_{zz}` with an integer eigenvalue of absolute value `> l`. If `u ∈ N` and `E_{aa}u = 0` for all
`a`, then `E_{ab}Πu = 0` for all `a, b`, and `Πu − u ∈ Σ_{a≠b} E_{ab}(V)`. For one step, suppose `E_{zz}u = 0` and
`E_{ab}u = 0` for `a, b ∈ S`. The relations give `E_{jz}Ω_{q+1}u = Ω_{q+1}E_{jz}u + (q+1)(q+1+|S|)·Ω_qE_{jz}u` for
`j ∈ S` (`Om_E`, `E_Om_apply`). The coefficients of `P_{z,S}` make the sum telescope to a multiple of `Ω_lE_{jz}u`
(`E_Pstep_apply`), and this is `0` because its innermost factors `E_{i_1z}⋯E_{i_lz}E_{jz}u` are eigenvectors of
`E_{zz}` of eigenvalue `−(l+1)`. That `E_{zj}P_{z,S}u = 0` follows from `sl_2` (`lower_kill`). Complete reducibility is
not used.

*Denominators* (`invariant_integral_holds`). Over `ℚ_p` let `M ⊂ V` be an additive subgroup closed under `ℤ_p` and
under `E_{ab}^k/k!` for `a ≠ b`. Then `p^{(r(r−1)/2)·⌊log_p(l+r−1)⌋}·Π(M) ⊂ M`. The maps `X ↦ E_{zi}XE_{iz}` for
distinct `i` commute, so `Ω_q` for `S ∪ {i}` is `Σ_k C(q,k)·E_{zi}^k·Ω_{q−k}·E_{iz}^k`, with `Ω_{q−k}` taken for `S`
(`Om_insert`). For `Ω^{[c]}_q := (c!/((q+c)!·q!))·Ω_q` this reads
`Ω^{[c]}_q(S ∪ {i}) = Σ_k C(c+k,k)^{−1}·(E_{zi}^k/k!)·Ω^{[c+k]}_{q−k}(S)·(E_{iz}^k/k!)`, and `v_p(C(m,k)) ≤ ⌊log_p m⌋`
(Mathlib, `Nat.factorization_choose_le_log`). Induction on `|S|` (`Om_int`, `Pstep_int`) gives
`p^{|S|·⌊log_p(l+|S|)⌋}·P_{z,S}(M) ⊂ M`, and the sizes `|S| = 0, 1, …, r−1` add up to `r(r−1)/2`.

*The comparison* (`weylInt_of`). Let `V` be a `gl_r`-module over `ℚ_p` with a weight decomposition, `W ⊂ V` a
finite-dimensional submodule generated by a highest-weight vector `v` of weight `λ`, any two weights of `W` differing by
at most `l` in each coordinate, and `M ⊂ V` a subgroup closed under `ℤ_p`, divided powers and weight projections. The
Lean statement allows several generators of weight `λ`, and it assumes in addition that `M` has a lattice basis in
every subspace of `W`, which the proof does not use. Let `Δ` be the smallest subgroup closed under `ℤ_p` and divided
powers containing the highest-weight vectors of weight `λ` in `W ∩ M`. Let `gl_r` act on `Hom(W, V)` by
`φ ↦ E_{ab}φ − φE_{ab}`. Then `𝔐 = {φ : φ(W ∩ M) ⊂ Δ}` is closed under the divided powers of this action, because
`ad(E)^k/k!·φ = Σ_{i+j=k} (−1)^j·(E^i/i!)·φ·(E^j/j!)` (`hom_divided_holds`). The projection `π_λ` of `W` onto its
`λ`-weight space lies in `𝔐` and has weight zero, so `p^e·Π(π_λ) ∈ 𝔐`. The eigenvalue hypothesis holds in `Hom(W, W)`
by the bound on the weights of `W`, so `Π(π_λ)` commutes with all `E_{ab}`. It sends `v` to some `c·v`, hence equals
`c` on `W`. Since `Π(π_λ) − π_λ` is a sum of commutators, `c·dim W = tr π_λ = 1` (`schurTrace_of_homInv`). Thus
`p^e·x/dim W ∈ Δ` for `x ∈ W ∩ M`. The proof then multiplies by the integer `dim W`, and the stated exponent does not
keep this factor.

*The polynomial model* (`coreQp_of`). Take `V = ℚ_p[x]`, `W = ∇_{ℚ_p}(ν)`, `M = ℤ_p[x]` and
`E_{ab} = Σ_s x_{a,s}·∂/∂x_{b,s}`, so that for `a ≠ b` the operator `E_{ab}^k/k!` is the divided row operator. By the
standard bideterminant basis the row degrees of the monomials occurring in `W` lie in `[0, ν_0]`, and in
`[ν_{n−1}, ν_0]` if `n = r` (`spread_top_holds`, `spread_square_holds`). This is where `l` enters. A vector of `W`
killed by all `E_{ab}` with `a < b` is a multiple of `v_ν` (`highest_weight_line`). So `W` is generated by `v_ν`, and
an integral such vector is `c·v_ν`, with `c ∈ ℤ_p` its value at the matrix with entries `1` at `(s, s)` and `0`
elsewhere. Hence `Δ ⊂ Δ_{ℤ_p}(ν)`, and `p^e·∇_{ℤ_p}(ν) ⊂ Δ_{ℤ_p}(ν)`.

*From `ℤ_p` to `O`.* `∇_ℤ(ν)` has a finite basis of standard bideterminants and `∇_ℚ(ν) = Δ_ℚ(ν)`, so
`N·∇_ℤ(ν) ⊂ Δ_ℤ(ν)` for some integer `N = p^k·c` with `p ∤ c`. For `f ∈ ∇_ℤ(ν)`, approximate `p^e·f ∈ Δ_{ℤ_p}(ν)` by
some `d ∈ Δ_ℤ(ν)` modulo `p^k`. Then `g = (p^e·f − d)/p^k` lies in `∇_ℤ(ν)`, which is saturated, and
`c·p^e·f = c·d + N·g ∈ Δ_ℤ(ν)` (`descent_over_integers`). Over a domain `O`, `∇_O(ν)` is spanned by bideterminants,
which come from `ℤ`, and words in the row operators commute with base change. Finally `c` is a unit of `O` because `O`
is local and `p` is not a unit (`ascent_domain_holds`). The inputs on `∇` are proved in
`src/Formalism/GradedWeylDivisibility`.

## Caveats

`sandwichLocal` is a theorem with a proof, of the proposition `SandwichLocal` (a definition of a `Prop`), with no
hypotheses beyond those of the statement above. We read the proofs in full in `FinalW.lean`, in the six files of
`SandwichModelProofs`, and in `InvInv.lean`, `InvInt.lean`, `HomRelationHolds.lean`, `SchurTraceOfHomInv.lean` and
`WeylInt.lean`. We searched the 151 files of the repository that `FinalW.lean` imports, directly or indirectly (about
19,850 lines), for `sorry`, `admit`, `axiom`, `native_decide`, `opaque`, `implemented_by` and `extern`, and none
occurs. We compiled nothing and did not examine Mathlib. We took at the level of statements only the standard
bideterminant basis, the saturation and base change of `∇`, `∇_ℚ = Δ_ℚ`, the uniqueness of the highest-weight vector
and the `gl_r`-module structure on polynomials. Of the theory of highest-weight modules (the Casimir operator) we read
only the definitions and the outline. Of the papers of Kulkarni and of Fang, Lim and Tan we have seen only the
abstracts, which do not tell us what else the papers contain.
