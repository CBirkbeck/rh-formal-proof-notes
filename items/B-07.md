## Statement

Let `k ≥ 1`, `r ≥ 0` and `n = 2r + k + 1`. Let `V = ℂⁿ` be the Hermitian space made of `r` hyperbolic planes and a
block `W` of signature `(k, 1)`, let `G = U(V) ≅ U(r + k, r + 1)`, and fix `z ∈ W` of negative norm. The Bessel subgroup
is `S = N ⋊ U_z`, where `N` is the unipotent radical of the stabiliser of an isotropic flag of length `r` and
`U_z ≅ U(k)` is the compact pointwise stabiliser of the hyperbolic planes and of `z`. Let `ψ` be any generic character of
`N` fixed by `U_z`. Let `σ` be a discrete series of `G` whose Harish-Chandra parameter has entries of different sign at
distance at least `h ≥ 120·n³`, and let `π` be an irreducible representation of `U_z`. A Bessel map is a linear map `B`
from the smooth vectors of `σ` to the space of `π`, continuous for the smooth topology, with
`B(σ(hm)v) = ψ(m)·π(h)·B(v)` for `h ∈ U_z` and `m ∈ N`.

The formal proof shows that any two Bessel maps are proportional. No interlacing condition between the parameters of
`σ` and `π` is needed. This is a special case, for discrete series with the gap condition, of Theorem A of
Jiang–Sun–Zhu on the uniqueness of Bessel models for real unitary groups, in the case `p = r + k`, `q = r + 1` with small
group `U(k)`. The formal proof does not use that theorem.

## Context

The published uniqueness theorem for real unitary groups rests on the analysis of invariant generalized functions on
singular sets. For discrete series far from the non-compact walls, paired with representations of the compact group
`U(k)`, the formal proof obtains uniqueness by a softer argument. That argument also gives a convergent Bessel form of
rank one, which does not follow from uniqueness alone. It does not extend to general representations.

## Comparison with the literature

This is a narrow special case of the published theorem, reached by an argument that differs at its essential step from
the published proofs, those of Jiang–Sun–Zhu together with Sun–Zhu. Here the rank-one property of the Bessel form
comes from the symmetry, under an anti-involution of transpose type, of an absolutely convergent pairing of
coefficients on the open double coset of the Bessel subgroup. The published proofs instead reduce to corank one and
use the vanishing of invariant generalized functions on singular sets. The argument here needs coefficients decaying
like `‖x‖^(−4n³)` and a compact small group, so it cannot give the published theorem. That it differs from the
published proofs is clear to us from the statements and the structure of the proofs in those two papers, which we read
only in summarised form. Whether the argument is in the literature is much less clear. A brief search found no
instance, but the ingredients, namely Gelfand's trick, van Dijk's criterion, Schur orthogonality and folding, are
classical, and a specialist may regard the combination as known in spirit.

A second, independent check regards the argument as known in spirit. It takes Hakim's criterion, in which the symmetry
need hold only on a set of full measure, as a direct precedent. It did not find this real, vector-valued Bessel version
either, and it makes no claim that the argument is absent from print, nor that it is correct. So we classify the result
differently, and neither check has shown that the argument is absent from the literature. We would like to know whether
the open-cell argument for integrable discrete series is in the literature on generalized Gelfand pairs, and how far
Hakim's criterion anticipates it.

## Sketch of the proof

The first step is decay. For a discrete series with gap `h ≥ 120n³`, matrix coefficients of smooth vectors satisfy
`|⟨v, σ(x)w⟩| ≤ C·‖x‖^(−4n³)`. This rests on an explicit formula for the coefficients of the lowest `K`-type, proved
elsewhere in the formal proof, which we did not re-examine. The coefficients are therefore integrable on `G`, and along
`S` with room to spare.

Next comes the Bessel form `be(u, u′) = ∫ ⟨u′, σ(mh)u⟩·conj ψ(m)·π(h)^(−1) dh dm` over `U_z × N`, an endomorphism of
the space of `π`. It converges absolutely, is equivariant in each variable, Hermitian and nonnegative, and satisfies a
reproducing formula coming from Schur orthogonality on `G`.

On the open cell one defines a pairing. For functions `F, F′` on `G` with five decaying derivatives, the two-sided
Bessel integral `𝒥F` over `S × S` is defined at almost every point of the big cell of `S\G/S`, which has coordinates
`(c, 𝔷) ∈ ℂ^r × ℂ`, and `β(F, F′) = ∫ wt·⟨𝒥F, 𝒥F′⟩` is finite. For coefficient functions, unfolding and Schur
orthogonality express `β` as a constant times the Hilbert–Schmidt pairing of `be(b₁, b₂)^*·be(a₁, a₂)` with a test
operator.

The anti-involution is `θ(g) = (ḡ)^(−1)`, for the real structure of a frame adapted to `z`. It is an anti-automorphism
of order two, of transpose type. It preserves `N`, `U_z`, the Haar measures and `ψ`, fixes a representative of each
big-cell double coset, and sends each `y ∈ U_z` to a conjugate of `y` by an element of the stabiliser in `U_z` of one
more vector. Hence `𝒥(f∘θ) = 𝒥(f)` almost everywhere on the big cell, and `β(f∘θ, F′) = β(f, F′)`. The
lower-dimensional double cosets form a null set and are discarded.

Then there is a dichotomy. Either the coefficients of `σ∘θ` are orthogonal to those of `σ`, and then `be = 0`, or an
antiunitary `C` intertwines `σ∘θ` with `σ`, and likewise for `π` on `U_z`. In the second case the previous steps give
the crossing identity `be(b₁, b₂)^*·π(h)·be(a₁, a₂) = be(Ca₁, b₂)^*·π(θh)·be(Cb₁, a₂)`. Schur orthogonality on the
compact group `U_z` turns it into a scalar identity `κ(A, B)·conj κ(E, F) = α(A, F)·γ(B, E)` for the matrix entries `κ`
of `be`. So all `2×2` minors of `κ` vanish, and a nonnegative Hermitian form with vanishing minors is `l ⊗ conj l`. Thus
`be(u, u′)` is the operator `ξ ↦ ⟨l(u′), ξ⟩·l(u)` for an `S`-equivariant linear map `l`, possibly `0`, defined on the
vectors whose coefficient against a fixed unit vector `w` decays like `‖x‖^(−4n³)`.

An a priori bound comes next: any Bessel map `B` of any unitary representation is bounded on the orbit `G·w`. Write
`g = h·m·(Γ·diag(d)·R)` by the Iwasawa, Levi and Cartan decompositions. If a ratio `M` of consecutive torus entries
exceeds `1`, a bounded root vector `Y` gives `B(σ(g·exp(τY))u) = exp(iMτ)·B(σ(g)u)`, and differentiating in `τ` trades
powers of `M` for derivatives of `u`.

Finally one folds. Apply a bounded `B` to the reproducing formula `w = d·∫ conj⟨w, σ(y)w⟩·σ(y)w dy`, passing `B` under
the integral by truncation, and fold the integral over `G` along `S` with a cut-off function. The inner integral is
`be = l ⊗ conj l`, so `B(σ(x)w) = c·l(σ(x)w)`. A general generic character is conjugated to the standard one by a torus
element commuting with `U_z`. Since `w` is cyclic for the smooth topology and `B` is continuous, two Bessel maps that are
proportional on `G·w` are proportional.

## Key steps

- **Decay from the gap** (`CompanionTheory.FamilyDerivativeExpansion.DecS.decayM`): coefficients of smooth vectors
  decay like `‖x‖^(−m)` when `thr*(ε) + m < h`, with `thr*(ε) ≤ 40n³`, and it is used with `m = 4n³`. This is where
  the gap hypothesis enters.
- **Symmetry on the open cell** (`ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.DS.Mult1.stSymBeta`, with `stSym`): the pairing
  `β` is invariant under the anti-involution `θ`, as an almost-everywhere identity over the big cell. This is the step
  in which the argument departs from the published proofs.
- **Vanishing minors** (`ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.DS.Mult1.TopH.sep_minors`, with `minors_DD`,
  `minorsOn_of_DD`, `multOne_of_minors`): the crossing identity and Schur orthogonality on `U(k)` force all `2×2` minors
  of the Bessel form to vanish. The rank-one statement `ThmM` is assembled in `thmM`.
- **A priori bound** (`CompanionTheory.Bmu.bounded_of`): a Bessel map of any unitary representation is bounded on the
  orbit of a smooth vector, by differentiating along root vectors.
- **Folding** (`ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.DS.Mult1.stMFix`, `CompanionTheory.Bmu.orbit_mult`): the reproducing
  formula folded along `S` shows that a bounded Bessel map is a multiple of `l` on `G·w`.
- **Conclusion** (`CompanionTheory.Bmu.uniq_of`): conjugation of the character to the standard one, cyclicity of `w`
  and continuity give proportionality of any two Bessel maps.

## Caveats

We read in full the statement and the top-level proofs: `besselMapUniqGap`, `uniq_of`, `orbit_mult`, `bounded_of`,
`thmM` through `theoremM_of`, `sep_minors`, `rankOneMinors`, `stMFix`, `stSymBeta` and `decayM`. About 15,000 lines of
supporting lemmas, and the decay theorem behind the first step, we took at their statements. We compiled nothing. A text
search found no `axiom`, and `sorry` only in `src/Challenge.lean`. The published theorem occurs in the source only as an
unproved proposition, and we did not trace the import graph from the final statements. We compared the formal notions
of discrete series and of the smooth topology with the Casselman–Wallach setting only through their definitions. We did
not open Gan–Gross–Prasad Theorem 15.1, Beuzart-Plessis's Proposition 3.2.1, the archimedean trace formula beyond its
abstract, van Dijk, Hakim or Sakellaridis–Venkatesh, nor the full texts of the papers on explicit Bessel and Whittaker
functions on small unitary groups, of which we saw only an abstract page and search excerpts. The second check reports
reading Proposition 3.2.1, §§7.2–7.5 of the archimedean trace formula and Theorem 6.4.1 of Sakellaridis–Venkatesh, and
it read Hakim's statement in a later account.
