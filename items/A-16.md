## Statement

Let `p` be a prime, `n ≤ r` natural numbers, `ν_0 ≥ ⋯ ≥ ν_{n−1} ≥ 0` integers, and `O` a local integral domain in
which `p` is not a unit (not necessarily a discrete valuation ring, nor of characteristic 0). In `O[x]`, `x` an
`r × n` matrix of variables, let `∇_O(ν)` (Lean: `dualWeyl`) be the module of polynomials homogeneous of degree `ν_t`
in the column `t` and unchanged by every substitution adding `T` times a column to a later column (`T` a new variable).
For rows `a, b` (possibly equal) let `E_{ab}^{(k)}` (Lean: `rowDiv`) send `f` to the coefficient of `T^k` in `f` after
`T` times the row `a` is added to the row `b`. Let `v_ν = ∏_{s<n} D_{s+1}^{ν_s − ν_{s+1}}` (`D_k` the leading `k × k`
minor, `ν_n := 0`) and `Δ_O(ν)` (Lean: `weyl`) the `O`-span of all words in the `E_{ab}^{(k)}` applied to `v_ν`. Let
`l ≥ 0` with `ν_0 ≤ l`, or `n = r` and `ν_0 − ν_{n−1} ≤ l`. Then, with `⌊log_p m⌋` the largest `k` such that `p^k ≤ m`,

    p^e · ∇_O(ν) ⊂ Δ_O(ν) ⊂ ∇_O(ν),      e = (r(r−1)/2) · ⌊log_p(l + r − 1)⌋      (⌊log_p 0⌋ := 0).

For the weight `λ = (ν_0, …, ν_{n−1}, 0, …, 0)` of `GL_r` the least admissible `l` is `λ_0 − λ_{r−1}`. The same file
proves this over every integral domain in which each integer prime to `p` is a unit (`sandwichDomain`), and over `ℤ`
in the form `c·p^e·f ∈ Δ_ℤ(ν)` for `f ∈ ∇_ℤ(ν)`, with an integer `c` prime to `p` depending on `f` (`sandwichZ`).

## Why it might matter

Over a field of characteristic 0 the two modules coincide and are the irreducible representation `V_λ` of `GL_r` of
highest weight `λ`; over `ℤ_(p)` or `ℤ_p` they are two integral forms of it. The inclusion says that `p^e` annihilates
`∇_O(ν)/Δ_O(ν)`, with `e` logarithmic in the weight, and gives `∇ = Δ` when `p > λ_0 − λ_{r−1} + r − 1`. The formal
proof bounds `p`-adic denominators of step operators, of extremal-projector kind, on lattices stable under divided
powers.

## What the checks found

The first reading compared a differently phrased statement: the Weyl lattice and its dual for the contravariant form
over a discrete valuation ring of characteristic 0, for every dominant weight of `GL_N`, with two refinements that are
not Lean statements. This page has the polynomial model, non-negative weights, and any local domain in which `p` is not
a unit; the wider ring comes by base change from `ℤ`, so the comparison applies. Its verdict: weaker (high confidence).
From Andersen (1983: Proposition 4.1 and Lemma 4.4 for the maps, Proposition 4.6 (ii) and Proposition 4.16 for the
filtration) both readings deduce `p^m·∇ ⊂ Δ` over `ℤ_(p)` with `m = Σ_{β>0}(⌊log_p⟨β^∨, λ+ρ⟩⌋ − v_p⟨β^∨, λ+ρ⟩)`, for
every prime; the first reading adds that this holds for every split reductive group in which `ρ` is a character of the
torus (arranged for `GL_r` by restricting to `SL_r`), and computes for `GL_r` that `m ≤ e − v_p(dim V_λ)`, `V_λ` the
irreducible representation of highest weight `λ`; for `GL_3`, `λ = (2,1,0)`, `p = 3` it gives `1` where `e = 3`. What
remains, for the first reading, is the method of proof: truncated extremal projectors, no sheaf cohomology. The second
opinion agrees: weaker than a published result (high confidence), having checked Andersen's maps; the polynomial model
is the standard induced module, and base change of bideterminants with the divided operators carries Andersen's bound to
the stated rings. Its corrections: flatness is not needed, and should not be invoked in characteristic `p`; the bound
here is better than Gruber's factorial bound only asymptotically (for `GL_2`, `λ = (1,0)`, `p = 2`: `1` against `0`);
abstract-only checks of peripheral papers do not establish what those papers contain; in characteristic `p` the
inclusion `p^e·∇ ⊂ Δ` is automatic once `e > 0`. Open: is this projector proof, or one like it, in print? Is the exact
exponent known for `GL_r`?

## How the formal proof goes

`Δ_R(ν) ⊂ ∇_R(ν)` holds over every commutative ring (`weylLeDual`). The other inclusion is proved over `ℤ_p`, then
over `ℤ`, then over `O`; the representation theory is in the first of these, and is done for abstract operators.

*Step operators.* Let `E_{ab}` be endomorphisms of a vector space `V` over a field of characteristic 0 with
`[E_{ab}, E_{cd}] = δ_{bc}E_{ad} − δ_{da}E_{cb}`. For an index `z` and a finite set `S` of other indices put `Ω_0 = 1`,
`Ω_{q+1} = Σ_{i∈S} E_{zi}·Ω_q·E_{iz}`, `P_{z,S} = Σ_{q=0}^{l} (−1)^q·|S|!/((q+|S|)!·q!)·Ω_q` (Lean: `Om`, `Pstep`) and
`Π = P_{0,{1,…,r−1}} ∘ P_{1,{2,…,r−1}} ∘ ⋯ ∘ P_{r−1,∅}` (`InvOp`). For `|S| = 1` this is the `sl_2` formula
`Σ_q (−1)^q E_{zi}^q·E_{iz}^q/(q!·(q+1)!)` for the extremal projector on vectors of weight zero, cut off at `q = l`.

*`Π` produces invariants* (`invariant_inverse_holds`). Let `N ⊂ V` be stable under all `E_{ab}` and contain no nonzero
eigenvector of any `E_{zz}` with an integer eigenvalue of absolute value `> l`. If `u ∈ N` and `E_{aa}u = 0` for all
`a`, then `E_{ab}Πu = 0` for all `a, b`, and `Πu − u ∈ Σ_{a≠b} E_{ab}(V)`. In one step, if `E_{zz}u = 0` and
`E_{ab}u = 0` for `a, b ∈ S`, the relations give `E_{jz}Ω_{q+1}u = Ω_{q+1}E_{jz}u + (q+1)(q+1+|S|)·Ω_qE_{jz}u` for
`j ∈ S` (`Om_E`, `E_Om_apply`); the coefficients of `P_{z,S}` make the sum telescope to a multiple of `Ω_lE_{jz}u`
(`E_Pstep_apply`), which is `0` because its innermost factors `E_{i_1z}⋯E_{i_lz}E_{jz}u` are eigenvectors of `E_{zz}`
of eigenvalue `−(l+1)`. `E_{zj}P_{z,S}u = 0` follows by `sl_2` (`lower_kill`). Complete reducibility is not used.

*Denominators* (`invariant_integral_holds`). Over `ℚ_p` let `M ⊂ V` be an additive subgroup closed under `ℤ_p` and
under `E_{ab}^k/k!` for `a ≠ b`. Then `p^{(r(r−1)/2)·⌊log_p(l+r−1)⌋}·Π(M) ⊂ M`. The maps `X ↦ E_{zi}XE_{iz}` for
distinct `i` commute, so `Ω_q` for `S ∪ {i}` is `Σ_k C(q,k)·E_{zi}^k·Ω_{q−k}·E_{iz}^k` with `Ω_{q−k}` for `S`
(`Om_insert`). For `Ω^{[c]}_q := (c!/((q+c)!·q!))·Ω_q` this reads
`Ω^{[c]}_q(S ∪ {i}) = Σ_k C(c+k,k)^{−1}·(E_{zi}^k/k!)·Ω^{[c+k]}_{q−k}(S)·(E_{iz}^k/k!)`, and `v_p(C(m,k)) ≤ ⌊log_p m⌋`
(Mathlib, `Nat.factorization_choose_le_log`). Induction on `|S|` (`Om_int`, `Pstep_int`) gives
`p^{|S|·⌊log_p(l+|S|)⌋}·P_{z,S}(M) ⊂ M`, and the sizes `|S| = 0, 1, …, r−1` add up to `r(r−1)/2`.

*The comparison* (`weylInt_of`). Let `V` be a `gl_r`-module over `ℚ_p` with a weight decomposition, `W ⊂ V` a
finite-dimensional submodule generated by a highest-weight vector `v` of weight `λ`, any two weights of `W` differing by
at most `l` in each coordinate, and `M ⊂ V` a subgroup closed under `ℤ_p`, divided powers and weight projections (the
Lean statement allows several generators of weight `λ`, and assumes in addition that `M` has a lattice basis in every
subspace of `W`, which the proof does not use). Let `Δ` be the smallest subgroup closed under `ℤ_p` and divided powers
containing the highest-weight vectors of weight `λ` in `W ∩ M`. `gl_r` acts on `Hom(W, V)` by `φ ↦ E_{ab}φ − φE_{ab}`,
and `𝔐 = {φ : φ(W ∩ M) ⊂ Δ}` is closed under the divided powers of this action, as
`ad(E)^k/k!·φ = Σ_{i+j=k} (−1)^j·(E^i/i!)·φ·(E^j/j!)` (`hom_divided_holds`). The projection `π_λ` of `W` onto its
`λ`-weight space lies in `𝔐` and has weight zero, so `p^e·Π(π_λ) ∈ 𝔐`. The eigenvalue hypothesis holds in `Hom(W, W)` by
the bound on the weights of `W`, so `Π(π_λ)` commutes with all `E_{ab}`; it sends `v` to some `c·v`, hence equals `c` on
`W`; and `Π(π_λ) − π_λ` is a sum of commutators, so `c·dim W = tr π_λ = 1` (`schurTrace_of_homInv`). Thus
`p^e·x/dim W ∈ Δ` for `x ∈ W ∩ M`; the proof then multiplies by the integer `dim W`, and the stated exponent does not
keep this factor.

*The polynomial model* (`coreQp_of`). Take `V = ℚ_p[x]`, `E_{ab} = Σ_s x_{a,s}·∂/∂x_{b,s}` (then, for `a ≠ b`,
`E_{ab}^k/k!` is the divided row operator), `W = ∇_{ℚ_p}(ν)`, `M = ℤ_p[x]`. By the standard bideterminant basis the row
degrees of the monomials occurring in `W` lie in `[0, ν_0]`, and in `[ν_{n−1}, ν_0]` if `n = r` (`spread_top_holds`,
`spread_square_holds`); this is where `l` enters. A vector of `W` killed by all `E_{ab}`, `a < b`, is a multiple of
`v_ν` (`highest_weight_line`), so `W` is generated by `v_ν`, and an integral one is `c·v_ν` with `c ∈ ℤ_p` its value at
the matrix with entries `1` at `(s, s)` and `0` elsewhere. So `Δ ⊂ Δ_{ℤ_p}(ν)`, and `p^e·∇_{ℤ_p}(ν) ⊂ Δ_{ℤ_p}(ν)`.

*From `ℤ_p` to `O`.* `∇_ℤ(ν)` has a finite basis of standard bideterminants and `∇_ℚ(ν) = Δ_ℚ(ν)`, so
`N·∇_ℤ(ν) ⊂ Δ_ℤ(ν)` for some integer `N = p^k·c` with `p ∤ c`. For `f ∈ ∇_ℤ(ν)`, approximate `p^e·f ∈ Δ_{ℤ_p}(ν)` by
`d ∈ Δ_ℤ(ν)` modulo `p^k`; then `g = (p^e·f − d)/p^k` lies in `∇_ℤ(ν)`, which is saturated, and
`c·p^e·f = c·d + N·g ∈ Δ_ℤ(ν)` (`descent_over_integers`). Over a domain `O`, `∇_O(ν)` is spanned by bideterminants,
which come from `ℤ`, and words in the row operators commute with base change; `c` is a unit of `O` because `O` is
local and `p` not a unit (`ascent_domain_holds`). The inputs on `∇` are proved in `src/Formalism/GradedWeylDivisibility`.
