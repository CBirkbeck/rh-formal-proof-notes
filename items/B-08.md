## Statement

Let `L` be a totally complex number field with `r₂(L)` complex places, `j` an odd integer with `j < r₂(L)`, `ℓ ≥ 1` an
integer and `n ≥ 2j + 4`. With `ψ^ℓ : GL_m → GL_{ℓm}` the block sum of `ℓ` copies and `stab` the stabilisation, put
`Z = ∏_{t=0}^{j−2} (ψ^ℓ_* − ℓ^(t+2)·stab_*) : H_j(GL_n(𝒪_L); ℤ) → H_j(GL_{ℓ^(j−1)·n}(𝒪_L); ℤ)` on integral group
homology. The formal proof shows that the image of `Z` generates a subgroup of rank at most `r₂(L)`. This is an upper
bound only, for the homology of the discrete groups `GL_n(𝒪_L)`; no K-group, plus construction or Hurewicz map occurs.
It follows from Borel's computation of the stable real cohomology together with homological stability: `Z` kills the
decomposable part of the stable rational homology in degree `j` and, for `ℓ ≥ 2`, maps onto a multiple of the primitive
part, of rank exactly `r₂(L)` for odd `j ≥ 3`. The formal statement is the upper half of this, for totally complex
fields and degrees below `r₂(L)` only. It stands in for Borel's theorem, and for Chern classes and the Hurewicz map, in
a proof, for a prime `ℓ`, `L` containing the `ℓ`-th roots of unity (the fourth roots if `ℓ = 2`) and an integer `E ≥ 1`,
of the finiteness of `H²(𝒪_L[1/ℓ], ℤ_ℓ(E+1))`, with a bound of explicit shape in the degree and discriminant of `L` and
in `E`, in which one absolute constant is not specified.

## Why it might matter

In the published proof of Soulé's finiteness theorem, Borel's theorem enters through K-groups and Chern classes or the
Dwyer–Friedlander map. Here a rank bound and explicit cycles in the homology of `GL_N` give a bound, explicit up to one
unspecified absolute constant, for `H²(𝒪_L[1/ℓ], ℤ_ℓ(E+1))` at every prime, given the duality theorems; and the rank
bound gets invariant-form representatives of stable real classes from a Hecke operator at a finite prime, not from
Matsushima's argument.

## What the checks found

The first reading finds a statement narrower than Borel's theorem, reached by an argument that differs from Borel's at
the decisive step. It stays in Borel's framework (invariant forms on the symmetric space, reduction theory, `L²`
estimates on `Γ\G`), but proves that stable real classes below degree `r₂(L)` come from invariant forms with a Hecke
operator at a principal prime, which contracts mean-zero square-integrable functions and moves stable cycles very
little, in place of Matsushima's argument as extended by Garland. In the work it read, trivial Hecke action on stable
classes in characteristic zero is deduced from Borel (Calegari–Emerton); here it is proved directly. Confidence
medium: Borel's papers were not read, and the absence of the argument from the literature rests on one extended search.
The second opinion cannot tell, with medium confidence: a difference from Borel's proof is shown, not novelty; the
ingredients are standard, and what may be substantive is the near-identity formula on stable cycles combined with the
contraction. It disagrees on Calegari–Emerton, reading them as giving Hecke triviality from stability independently of
Borel, and notes that the rank equality needs `ℓ ≥ 2` (for `ℓ = 1`, `j ≥ 3`, `Z = 0`). Open questions: how the
stable-cycle formula compares with Ash and Calegari–Emerton, and whether this route to Borel-type surjectivity is known.

## How the formal proof goes

The rank bound. If a real cycle in the span of the classes `Z x` is killed by `r₂(L)` functionals `τ_w`, one for each
complex place, it is a boundary; the rank bound follows. To see this, the cycle is pushed to a large `N` by homological
stability, a real cocycle detecting it is chosen and replaced by an invariant form, and the form is decomposed by
weights; `Z` kills the weights `2, …, j`.

Invariant forms. For `j < r₂(L)` and arbitrarily large `N`, every real `j`-cocycle of `Γ = GL_N(𝒪_L)` agrees on cycles
with the integral, over straight simplices in the cone of positive Hermitian forms, of a form invariant under
`∏_w GL_N(ℂ)`. A Čech–de Rham construction with a `Γ`-partition of unity turns the cocycle into a closed `Γ`-invariant
form bounded by a power of a height function. By reduction theory on Siegel sets every fixed power of the height is
integrable on `Γ\G` once `N` is large, so the form is square-integrable; it is averaged over the centre. Its mean over
`Γ\G` is an invariant form `ω̄`, and `η = ω − ω̄` has mean zero. The normalised Hecke operator `T` of
`diag(π, 1, …, 1)`, for a prime element `π` of norm at least `25`, has norm at most `3/5` on square-integrable functions
of mean zero when `N ≥ 3`; the value of a closed square-integrable form on a fixed cycle is bounded by its `L²` norm, so
the values of `T^r η` on any cycle are `O((3/5)^r)`. On cycles stabilised from `GL_n`, `T_N = (1 − a)·id + a·T_n` with
`a` a ratio of indices, so for large `N` the operator moves the finitely many stable classes by at most `1/5`. Values
that decay like `(3/5)^r` while shrinking by at most `4/5` per step are zero: `η` vanishes on cycles.

The Hecke operator on stable cycles rests on a "centre kills" argument: for `j < r₂(L)` the block-triangular group
`GL_n(𝒪_L) ⋉ M_{n×r}(𝒪_L)` has the degree-`j` real homology of `GL_n(𝒪_L)`, because some unit `v` has
`∏_w |v|_w^(a_w) ≠ 1` whenever `0 < Σ a_w < r₂(L)`, so multiplication by `v` has no eigenvalue `1` on the cohomology
of the abelian normal subgroup in degrees `1, …, j`. This is where the restriction `j < r₂(L)` comes from.

The spectral gap. The groups `K_r = SL_N(L) ∩ d_r⁻¹·SL_N(𝒪_L)·d_r`, `d_r = diag(π, …, π, 1, …, 1)` with `r = 0, 1, 2`
entries `π`, stabilise three adjacent vertices of the building at `π`. A two-transitivity count in finite quotients
bounds the cosine between their spaces of invariant functions by `1/5`, a Hilbert-space lemma on three subspaces gives
`2/5`, and functions invariant under the dense subgroup generated by `K_0` and `K_1` are constant.

Weights. At the base point an invariant form is a `∏ U(N)`-invariant alternating form on tuples of Hermitian matrices.
By the first fundamental theorem for the unitary group, proved through a double-commutant statement, these are spanned
by alternated products of traces, and `ψ^d` multiplies such a product by `d` to the number of its trace factors. In odd
degree the single traces give one functional for each place, `r₂(L)` in all; `Z` kills the rest.

Application. Through a Bockstein and the rank bound, a compatible family `Φ` of `ℤ/ℓ^ν`-valued functionals on
`j`-chains of `GL_n(𝒪_L)` with `Φ∘ψ^d = d·Φ` has `ℓ^(j−1)·Φ` taking at most `(ℓ^ν)^(r₂(L))·t_j·t_(j−1)` values on
cycles, `t_i` the order of the torsion of `H_i(GL_{2j+4}(𝒪_L); ℤ)`. After Poitou–Tate duality, classes in
`H²(𝒪_L[1/ℓ], ℤ_ℓ(E+1))` become eigen-homomorphisms on class groups of a cyclotomic layer, counted through twisted
norms told apart by residues at primes of degree one; the residues are values of such functionals on explicit cycles.
The factor `(ℓ^ν)^(r₂)` cancels against the units, and what remains, torsion in the homology of `GL_{4E+6}`, does not
depend on `ν`. The bound is used in degree `2E + 1`, after enlarging the field so that `2E + 1 < r₂`.

## Key steps

- **Reduction** (`PositivityTheory.KB.sBStarOdd_of`): stability, detection by a cocycle, invariant forms and weights
  give the rank bound through `rank_span_le`.
- **Invariant forms** (`PositivityTheory.KB.JoinReconstruction.key`): partition of unity, square-integrability, mean,
  Hecke contraction and near-identity on stable cycles. This is the step that differs from Borel's proof.
- **Spectral gap** (`PositivityTheory.KB.sGap_of`): norm at most `3/5` on square-integrable functions of mean zero, from
  three vertex stabilisers in the building.
- **Hecke operator on stable cycles** (`PositivityTheory.KB.AffineStabilizerVanishing.assemble`): `T_N` is
  `(1 − a)·id + a·T_n` on stabilised cycles; it uses the affine subgroup and the unit of
  `PositivityTheory.KB.SplitAffineSpectral.unit`.
- **Weights** (`PositivityTheory.KB.sWtOdd_of`): invariant forms are alternated trace products, of weight the number of
  traces; in odd degree there are `r₂(L)` of weight one.
- **Application** (`UpperTheory.V.ZK.lemmaK`, through `UpperTheory.V.ZK.sUpperOf`): the bound for
  `H²(𝒪_L[1/ℓ], ℤ_ℓ(E+1))`, with Poitou–Tate duality, local finiteness and local Tate duality as hypotheses.

## What was checked, and what was not

`bOdd` is a closed theorem without hypotheses. A text search found no `sorry` and no `axiom` in the directories of the
argument (286 files for the K-theory part, 74 for the counting, and five further directories); homological stability and
finite generation of the homology of `GL_N(𝒪_L)` are proved in the source, but those proofs were not read. Read in full:
the statements along the chain and the proofs of `sBStarOdd_of`, `JoinReconstruction.key`,
`AffineStabilizerVanishing.assemble`, `SplitAffineSpectral.unit` and `eigen`, `sUpperOf`; the other steps as statements,
the gap bound skimmed, most of the roughly 28,000 lines beneath not read; nothing was compiled. The discharge of the
duality hypotheses of `lemmaK`, the torsion bounds and the explicit cycles were not traced. Not opened by the first
reading: Borel's papers of 1974 and 1977, Soulé (1979, 2003), a survey by Weibel, Arlettaz (1991),
Blasius–Franke–Grunewald, Borel–Yang, Garland, Ash; the second opinion reports checking parts of Borel (1974).
