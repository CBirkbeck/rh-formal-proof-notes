## Statement

Let `K/ℚ_p` be finite unramified of degree `n`, `G = Gal(ℚ̄_p/K)`, `E ⊂ ℚ̄_p` finite over `ℚ_p` containing the
conjugates of `K(ζ_p)`, with integers `O`, uniformizer `λ`, residue field `k`, and embeddings `τ_i : K → E` (`i ∈ ℤ/n`),
`τ_{i+1}∘Frob = τ_i`. Position data: `t ≥ 1` and, for each `i`, `h_i ≥ 2`, `η_i ≥ 1`, `w_i`, a type (low or high), a
multiset `U_i` of `r` integers; `B_i = {w_i, …, w_i+t−1}`; window `[1, η_i]` (low) or `[h_i−η_i, h_i−1]` (high);
`σ_i = ⌊Σ_{j≥0} h_{i+j+1}/p^{j+1}⌋`. Assume: `U_i ∪ B_i ⊂ [1, h_i−1]`; `B_i` in the window; `η_i + 4σ_i + 6 < p`;
`|u − β| > σ_i + 2` for `u ∈ U_i` in the window, `β ∈ B_i`; for `u ∈ U_i` outside the window and integers
`−2σ_i−2 ≤ y ≤ η_i+2σ_i+3`: `u ≢ y` (low), `h_i − u ≢ y` (high) modulo `p`. Let `ρ` be a crystalline `E`-representation
of `G` of dimension `r` with Hodge–Tate weights `U_i` at `τ_i` (the cyclotomic character `ε` has weight `−1`, which the
source proves for its own period rings), `T₀ ⊂ ρ` a stable `O`-lattice, `χ₁ : G → O^×` crystalline of weight `w_i` at
`τ_i`, `χ_c = χ₁ε^{1−c}`. A crystalline `W` is *full* if its weights are `U_i ⊔ B_i` and, for every stable lattice
`L ⊂ W`, `L/λL` has the Jordan–Hölder factors of `T₀/λ ⊕ k(χ̄₁) ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ k(χ̄_t)`; a finite `O[G]`-module is *admissible*
if it is `L₁/L₂` for stable lattices in a finite sum of full `W`. Then `k`-independent
`z_1, …, z_s ∈ H¹(G, (T₀/λ)(χ̄₁^{−1}))` whose extensions of `k(χ̄₁)` by `T₀/λ` are admissible have
`s ≤ d = Σ_i #{u ∈ U_i : u < w_i}`. In Lean: for abstract period rings, under three named hypotheses (see below).

## Why it might matter

The statement concerns which extension classes modulo `p` occur in reductions of lattices in crystalline representations
with prescribed Hodge–Tate weights. The hypotheses on the weights allow weights above `p`, where Fontaine–Laffaille
theory does not apply; they also allow data inside the Fontaine–Laffaille range. `d` is the value that the Bloch–Kato
formula gives for `dim H¹_f(G, ρ ⊗ χ₁^{−1}) − dim H⁰`. The source uses the bound to identify, for every finite
coefficient module, the admissible classes with the Bloch–Kato finite ones (`stmtAt_of_real`, a theorem under named
hypotheses).

## What the checks found

The first reading compared a stronger statement: the bound without hypotheses, for the usual period rings; the Lean one
assumes three propositions, proved elsewhere and combined only inside larger theorems, over an abstract package of
period rings, so the verdict reaches it only once these are discharged. Its verdict: improves, in the range of
Hodge–Tate weights only (medium confidence). For the first reading, published bounds are for the Fontaine–Laffaille
range (any rank; the theory gives the exact dimension of the space of its extensions, which the first reading computes
as `d + dim Hom`, with no position hypotheses, and it calls this bound known there and weaker, as it needs `p ≥ 11` and
the position hypotheses) or for weights at most `p`: Chang–Diamond (rank two, same method), Gee–Liu–Savitt (rank two),
Gao (upper triangular reductions), the last two incomparable with it. None beyond weight `p` was found, in a search cut
short after about ten queries. The forward search could make no web search; it found a preprint of Bhatt–Gee–Kisin (any
weights, semisimplified reduction only), and its own computation (`11 ≤ p ≤ 29`, Serre weights recalled) found the case
`K = ℚ_p`, `r = t = 1` implied by the Breuil–Mézard conjecture for `GL_2(ℚ_p)` as quoted there. The second opinion
cannot tell (medium confidence): no checked result gives the full assertion, which does not establish priority. It
corrects: Chang–Diamond bound abstract bounded classes, not only lifted ones; Fontaine–Laffaille gives `d + dim Hom`,
and dropping `Hom` needs a reason; Abhinandan's rational theorem has no one-sign restriction; computed cases cannot give
the Breuil–Mézard implication for every prime. They disagree on whether finding no bound beyond weight `p` supports
"improves". Open: does work above weight `p` bound such classes? Does Fontaine–Laffaille give `d` or only `d + dim Hom`?

## How the formal proof goes

Two layers. The first is semilinear algebra over `k((X))`, with no `p`-adic Hodge theory: `AdmBound.main` proves the
bound for an abstract "model theory". The second shows that reductions of Wach lattices form one (`real_of_presented`);
in the statement of `resBound_dsum` it is a hypothesis (`TopSit.Real`), supplied inside
`twistedLatticeCohomology_theoremTOP`, itself an implication from a bundle of named statements.

Setting. `k((X))` carries commuting `φ`, `γ` with `φ(X) = unit·X^p`, `γ(X) = X(1 − θX)`, `θ(0) = 1`; it is the ring of
invariants of the `(p−1)`-torsion of `ℤ_p^×` in `k((π))`. An exact functor sends finite `k[G]`-modules (a class closed
under submodules, quotients and sums) to families `(D_i)_{i∈ℤ/n}` of étale `(φ,γ)`-modules, `φ_i : D_{i−1} → D_i`;
vectors fixed by `φ` and `γ` come from `G`-maps from `k`. For lattices `N_i ⊂ D_i`: the height condition is
`φ_i^*N_{i−1} ⊂ X^{−w_i}N_i` and `X^{h_i−w_i}N_i ⊂ φ_i^*N_{i−1}`; the `Γ` condition is `(γ_i−1)N_i ⊂ XN_i`;
`sen(N_i)(x)` is the multiplicity of `x` as a generalised eigenvalue of `∇ = X^{−1}(γ−1)` on `N_i/XN_i`. A model theory
is a class of base families with both conditions, closed under sums, with `sen` equal to `μ` times the residues of
`(U_i ⊔ B_i) − w_i` for some `μ`, the module having the Jordan–Hölder factors of `μ` copies of `T̄ ⊕ ω⁰ ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ ω^{1−t}`
(`T̄ = (T₀/λ)(χ̄₁^{−1})`, `ω = ε̄`), and with `0` a semisimple eigenvalue of `∇` modulo `X^{h_i−w_i+1}` at high type. A
model of `A` is the image of a base family of `B` intersected with `D(A₁)`, for `A₁ ⊂ B` and `A₁ ↠ A`; standard models
`N^{std}` of `T̄` (`sen`: residues of `U_i − w_i`) and of each `ω^{−e}` are given.

From a class to a Herr pair (`exists_model_repr_inZZ`). Let `A` be an extension of `k` by `T̄` with a model `N_A`. Its
image in `D(k)` is a model of the trivial character, hence `k[[X]]·1` by the uniqueness below. Lift the generator to
`ξ_i ∈ N_{A,i}`: then `b_i = φ_iξ_{i−1} − ξ_i`, `g_i = (γ_i−1)ξ_i` is a 1-cocycle of the Herr complex of `D(T̄)`, with
`g_i ∈ XN_i` and `b_i ∈ X^{−w_i}N_i` (low), resp. `b_i ∈ X^{−v_i}φ_i^*N_{i−1}` and `g_i − (γ_i−1)x_i ∈ X^{v_i+2}N_i` for
some `x_i ∈ N_i` (high, `v_i = h_i − w_i`; semisimplicity is used), where `N = N_A ∩ D(T̄)` is a model of `T̄`. If the
pair is a coboundary `d(x)`, then `ξ − x` is fixed by `φ` and `γ`, so comes from a `G`-invariant vector splitting `A`:
independent classes give independent pairs.

Uniqueness for block characters (`model_chr_eq`). Two lattice families with the same height condition satisfy
`X^{σ_i}N_i ⊂ N′_i` (`CloseTw.main`): the least `m_i` with `X^{m_i}N_i ⊂ N′_i` obey `p·m_{i−1} ≤ m_i + h_i`, and going round
the cycle of embeddings gives `m_i ≤ σ_i`. So a model of `ω^{−e}`, `0 ≤ e < t`, is `X^{κ_i}` times the standard one, with
`|κ_i| ≤ σ_i` and Sen weight `e − κ_i`, and the sign of `κ_{i−1}` is fixed by the type of `i`. In a composition series of the
base module through the given line, `sen` adds up; by the position hypotheses the Sen weights in `[−σ_i, t+σ_i]` come
from character pieces only and are `μ` copies of `0, …, t−1`. So `Σ κ = 0` at each `i` and, the `κ` having one sign, all vanish.

The largest model (`ModelCount.count`). Sums of models are models, and models of `T̄` lie in `X^{−σ_i}N^{std}_i`: there is a
largest model `N^max ⊃ N^{std}`. With `Q = N^max/N^{std}`: `sen(N^max)(y) + mult_Q(y+1) = sen(N^{std})(y) + mult_Q(y)`, and
`mult_Q(y) ≠ 0` only if `y − j` is a Sen weight of `N^{std}` for some `1 ≤ j ≤ σ_i`. Sum over `y ∈ [−w_i, −1]` (low) or
`[0, v_i+σ_i+1]` (high); the position hypotheses kill the boundary terms. Result: `N^max_i` has no Sen weight in
`{0, …, σ_i+1}`; with `d_i = #{u ∈ U_i : u < w_i}`, `Σ_{0≤j<w_i} sen(N^max_i)(j − w_i) = d_i` (low), `d_i + Σ_{0≤y≤v_i} sen(N^max_i)(y) = r` (high).

The count (`PolarBound.main`), applied to `N = N^max`. As `N_i` has no Sen weight in `{0, …, σ_i+1}`, `∇` is bijective
on `N_i/X^{σ_i+2}N_i`. Gauge lemma (`Gauge.main`): a pair can be changed by `d(y)`, `y ∈ ∏N_i`, to get
`g_i ∈ X^{σ_i+3}N_i`; a pair with `b ∈ ∏N_i`, `g ∈ ∏X^{σ_i+3}N_i` is a coboundary, as `φ` maps `∏X^{σ_i+2}N_i` into `X`
times itself and `1 − φ` is bijective there. The corrected first components span, modulo `N_i`, a space of dimension at
most `d_i`. Low type: they lie in the kernel of `∇` on `X^{−w_i}N_i/N_i`. High type (`PolarCount.main`): with
`F = X^{−v}φ_i^*N_{i−1}`, `L = X^{v+1}N_i`, every `x ∈ F` is `X^{−1}φ(ψ(Xx))` plus an element of `∇F`, so the cokernel
of `∇` on `F/L`, hence its kernel, has dimension at most `r`; the kernel contains the classes in question and the
generalised `0`-eigenspace of `N_i/L`, of dimension `r − d_i`. This uses `1 ≤ v ≤ p − 7` and the operator `ψ` modulo `p`
(`lemmaPsi`, proved in the source): `ψ(X^aφ^*M) = M` for `2−p ≤ a ≤ 1`, and `γ − 1` maps the `ψ`-zero part of `X^aφ^*M`
onto that of `X^{a+1}φ^*M` for `2−p ≤ a ≤ 0`.

Realisation. `X` is the reduction of the trace of `π` under the torsion of `Gal(K(μ_{p^∞})/K)`; base families are the
reductions modulo `λ` of the torsion-invariants of Wach lattices of `L ⊗ χ₁^{−1}`, `L` a lattice in a sum of full
representations. Used (`WachUse.QN`): an invariant basis, heights of `φ` bounded by the weights, the rank-one case,
saturation, additivity, and the characteristic polynomial `∏_{u∈U_i}(Y − c(−u))`, `c(a) = (1−(1+p)^a)/p`, of
`π₀^{−1}(γ−1)` modulo `π₀ + p`. In the source a Wach lattice is part of a package of data with listed properties; the
package exists (`exists_wach`) for the tied period rings, given two named statements: one has its own proof in the
source, the other follows by two further implications from a statement on prismatic `F`-crystals, also proved there
(`bhattScholzeIntegral`).

## Key steps

- **Uniqueness of the model of a block character** (`GlobalTheory.Models.CharModel.model_chr_eq`): in position, the only
  model of `ω^{−e}`, `0 ≤ e < t`, is the standard one. It puts the lift `ξ` in a known lattice, and it needs a count
  over a whole composition series, not over one line. (`TwistedPhiGammaModelCharacterEqStandard.lean`, line 107.)
- **Comparison of lattice families** (`GlobalTheory.PhiGam.CloseTw.main`): equal height conditions give
  `X^{σ_i}N_i ⊂ N′_i`. For `σ_i = 0` this is uniqueness of the lattice family; the `σ_i` in the position hypotheses come
  from it. (`PhiGammaLatticeFamiliesShiftInclusion.lean`, line 12.)
- **Sen multiplicities of the largest model** (`GlobalTheory.ModelCount.count`): the telescoping identity and the three
  counts for `N^max`; this is where `d_i` enters. (`TwistedPhiGammaModelSenWeightCount.lean`, line 85.)
- **Polar count at high type** (`GlobalTheory.PhiGam.PolarCount.main`): `dim Z/(Z ∩ N) + Σ_{0≤y≤v} sen(N)(y) ≤ r` for
  `Z ⊂ X^{−v}φ^*N₀` with `(γ−1)Z ⊂ X^{v+2}N`. The only use of `ψ`. (`PolarPartFinrankAddSenWeightsLeRank.lean`, line 200.)
- **Count of polar Herr pairs** (`GlobalTheory.Models.PolarBound.main`): pairs independent modulo coboundaries number at
  most `Σ d_i`, by the gauge lemma (`GlobalTheory.PhiGam.Gauge.main`, `CoeSmulMem.lean`, line 57) and the two polar bounds.
  (`CardCocyclesIndependentModCoboundariesLe.lean`, line 117.)
- **Realisation by reduced Wach lattices** (`GlobalTheory.BasePairs.real_of_presented`): the hypothesis `TopSit.Real`
  holds given the Wach package, its properties `WachUse.QN` and two further named statements (`RealD`, `CrysCalc`),
  which have their own proofs in the source (`realD`; `crysCalc`, for the tied period rings); this is where Wach
  lattices enter. (`ExistsModThOfPres.lean`, line 72.)

## What was checked, and what was not

`resBound_dsum` is a theorem with a proof. It assumes three propositions, which are definitions: `LemmaPsi`,
`TopSit.Real`, `TopSit.Lem0`. Each is derived in the source from further statements: `LemmaPsi` by `lemmaPsi`, with no
hypotheses; `TopSit.Real` by `real_of_presented`, for a Wach package, from three named statements (`RealD`,
`WachUse.QN`, `CrysCalc`); `TopSit.Lem0` by `admissibleLocalSituation_admissible_classes`, from two named statements
(`Closed`, `StdAdm`). The pieces are combined in `stmtAt_of_real` and `twistedLatticeCohomology_theoremTOP`; the second
is itself an implication from a bundle of named statements (properties of the Wach package, local Tate duality, the
local Euler characteristic formula and others), and takes `WachUse.QN` from that bundle. No declaration states the bound
without hypotheses. "Crystalline" refers to an abstract package of period rings; `CrysCalc`, on which the derivations of
`Real` and `Lem0` rest, is proved (`crysCalc`) for a package tied to rings `B_cris`, `B_dR` defined in the source.
Proofs read: the bodies of the linked theorems, the gauge lemma, the Herr pair, the largest model; statements only:
their auxiliary lemmas, `lemmaPsi`, everything behind `TopSit.Real` and `Lem0`. A text search found no `sorry`, `axiom`,
`admit` in the files of the source imported by the files of `resBound_dsum` (318), of the combining theorem (1,489), of
`bhattScholzeIntegral` (1,362). Nothing was compiled; Mathlib was not examined.
