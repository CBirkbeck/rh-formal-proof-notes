## Statement

For every real `C` there is an `n₀` such that the following holds. The statement does not give `n₀`, and the proof
takes about `exp(1424 + 352·C)`.

Let `E/F` be a CM extension unramified at every finite place, `f = [F:ℚ]` and `rd_E = |d_E|^{1/(2f)}`. Let `J` be a
nondegenerate Hermitian matrix over `E` of size `n ≥ n₀`, with an `𝒪_E`-lattice `L ⊂ Eⁿ` equal to its dual, and of one
signature `(p, q)` at all real places, `q` being the number of negative directions. Assume `q² ≤ n`, `log rd_E ≤ eⁿ`
and `log f ≤ √n`, and put `G = U(J)`. Let `b ≥ 2` with `log b ≤ exp(n·f·log n/16)`. At each real place `v` let `σ_v` be
a discrete series of `G(F_v)` with Harish-Chandra parameter `λ_v` (encoded in the Lean through its minimal `K`-type
`λ_v + ρ_n − ρ_c`), with `|λ_{v,a}| ≤ b²`, and with `|λ_{v,a} − λ_{v,a′}| ≥ 8·10¹⁴·(C+4)·n¹⁹·f⁵·log(b·rd_E)` whenever
the directions `a`, `a′` have different signs for the form at `v`, one among the `p` positive and one among the `q`
negative. Let `e` be a locally constant function on `G(𝔸_f)` with `e ∗ e = e` and `e(x⁻¹) = conj e(x)`, of positive
type, supported in the stabiliser of the adelic completion `L̂` of `L`, bi-invariant under the principal congruence
subgroup of this stabiliser of some level `g` with `1 ≤ g ≤ b^C`, and supported on the `x` with `(x − 1)ⁿL̂ ⊂ g₀L̂`,
where `g₀ > 4n` is a rational prime split completely in `E`.

Let `m` be the dimension of the space of maps `R(e)∘T`, with `T` a continuous `G(F_∞)`-intertwiner from `⊗_v σ_v` to
`L²(G(F)\G(𝔸))`. Then `m` is finite and `|m − D*| ≤ exp(−n·f·log n/16)·D*`, where
`D* = ∏_v d_v·vol(G(F)\G(𝔸))·e(1)` and `d_v` is the formal degree of `σ_v`, all for the same Haar measures.

In the Lean source the statement is a definition of a proposition, not a theorem. The theorem
`countingTheorem_of_volumeStep` proves it from one hypothesis, the volume step, which `volumeStep_of_siegelWeil`
derives from the Siegel–Weil formula for unitary groups in the convergent range, itself the theorem
`siegelWeilUnitaryConvergent`. We did not find a declaration that states the result without hypothesis.

## Context

Limit multiplicity compares the multiplicity of a discrete series in `L²` of an arithmetic quotient with formal degree
times volume. Here the comparison is one inequality with an explicit relative error, valid for all ranks above a
threshold and at the same time for all CM extensions unramified at every finite place and all levels and weights in
the stated ranges, on quotients that need not be compact. For a fixed group the error is a fixed number: it does not
tend to zero as the level or the weight grows.

## Comparison with the literature

We made the comparison with a wider statement: any CM extension, `n₀` not given, and the cuspidal multiplicity. The
statement here needs `E/F` unramified at every finite place, has `n₀` about `exp(1424 + 352C)` in the proof, counts in
all of `L²` (cuspidality is proved inside the argument), and is reached only by composing three proved theorems. The
comparisons with results for a fixed group are unaffected, but the uniformity holds here only over CM extensions
unramified at every finite place, and only above that threshold. With that understood, we regard the
statement as not comparable with published results. Shin–Templier, Dalal, Dalal–Gerbelli-Gauthier and
Kim–Wakatsuki–Yamauchi fix the group and obtain errors tending to `0` with the level or the weight, which the fixed error
here does not do. Savin gives no rate. We found no bound uniform in the rank, and uniformity in the field only for
groups of type `A₁` (Matz, Frączyk). We read six of the papers only through summaries and some classical ones not at
all, and we have not searched the later literature.

An independent second check agrees that the statement is not comparable with published results, on the ground that
the closest theorems leave the dependence on the rank uncontrolled. In its view the statement is not a restatement of
a known result, but is sharply limited by the threshold, the signatures, the self-duality and the auxiliary support condition. Dalal's
weight measures the distance from all root walls, while the statement here only separates entries of opposite sign.
For a discrete series the extra parity tests of Dalal–Gerbelli-Gauthier disappear. Abstracts do not certify every rate
quoted below. The second check alone regards the case `q = 0`, where the proof gives `m = D*` exactly, as classical in
substance. We would like to know whether any published bound controls the dependence on the rank, and where the case
`q = 0` is in print.

  <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01394147>. For an almost simple group over `ℚ` and a decreasing sequence
  of congruence subgroups with trivial intersection, the cuspidal multiplicity of every irreducible unitary `π` divided

## Sketch of the proof

The statement is a definition of a proposition. The source proves it from one hypothesis, the volume step
(`countingTheorem_of_volumeStep`), proves the volume step from the Siegel–Weil formula for unitary groups in the
convergent range, and proves that formula separately. The estimate is assembled in
`countingTheorem_of_finite_of_bounds`.

The argument starts from a projector and its trace. Put `φ = (∏_v d_v·conj⟨w_v, σ_v(·)w_v⟩) ⊗ e`, with `w_v` a unit
vector in the lowest `K`-type. Under a gap `h` the coefficient is at most `exp(−(h/8)·min(t², t/√(2q)))`, where `t` is
the Hilbert–Schmidt size of the Cartan component (proved in the source), so `φ` is integrable. By `globalProj_holds`,
which rests on Schur orthogonality, `R(φ)` is an orthogonal projector whose range corresponds to the space counted by
`m` through `T ↦ T(⊗w_v)`. `kernelB` gives an orthonormal basis `F_1, …, F_m` of the range by continuous automorphic
functions, with `K(x, y) = Σ_γ φ(x⁻¹γy) = Σ_i F_i(x)·conj F_i(y)` (`γ ∈ G(F)`) and `∫ K(x, x) dx = m` over a
fundamental domain. The term `γ = 1` integrates to `D*`.

Next come the near terms. For `x ∈ G(𝔸)` let `Λ_x` be the lattice `x_f L` with the metrics given by `x_∞`, and let
`least(x)` be the least normalised covolume of a nonzero sublattice. The moderate part is where
`least(x) ≥ τ* = (n·f·rd_E·g·b)^{−11q}`, and the deep part is the rest. Call `γ` near if `x⁻¹γx` is within Cartan
distance `ε = 10⁻⁶·n⁻⁸·f⁻²` of the maximal compact subgroup at every real place. By `near_unipotent`, for a near `γ`
with `φ(x⁻¹γx) ≠ 0` the traces `tr γ^j`, `j ≤ n`, are within `3n` of `n` in every complex embedding and congruent to `n`
modulo `g₀𝒪_E`, a radical ideal. As `g₀ > 3n` they equal `n`, so `γ` is unipotent and `x⁻¹γx` is within `δ = 3√(n·ε)`
of `1`. If `least(x) ≥ 1/2` this forces `γ = 1`. Otherwise `x` is sorted by the type of its canonical flag, namely the
steps of the canonical filtration of `Λ_x` of size below `1/2`, which are isotropic, of ranks `j_1, …, j_a`
(`J = Σ j_i ≤ q`) and sizes `τ_1 < … < τ_a`. `near_count` bounds the number of near elements by a product of factors
`1 + 12δ/λ` over ratios `λ` of the sizes `τ_i, 1, 1/τ_i` of the self-dual chain. `flagMeasureBound` bounds the measure
of the points of one type with sizes in `B` by `vol·κ·∫_B (density)`. It unfolds along the parabolic subgroup of the
flag (`unfoldBound`), evaluates volumes for `GL_j` over `E` by the residue and values of `ζ_E`, and uses the ratio of
the volumes for `U(p, q)` and `U(p − J, q − J)` given by the volume step. `constantBound` gives
`κ ≤ exp(−J·n·f·log n/2)` for `log n ≥ 15`. Summed over fewer than `2^q` types, the near terms contribute at most
`exp(−n·f·log n/4)·D*` (`nearModerate`). A power of `log(1/τ*)` is absorbed here, and this is where `n₀` comes to depend
on `C`.

For the far terms, `farBound_of_ptBound` sums the decay of the coefficient over dyadic shells against the lattice-point
count `#{γ : distance ≤ r} ≤ (3√n/τ₁(x)²)^{2n²f}·exp(2n²·Σ_v r_v)`, where `τ₁ = min(1/2, least)`. On the moderate part
the size of the gap outweighs the count, and the far terms contribute at most `exp(−n²f)·D*`
(`farModerate_of_farBound`).

On the deep part `K(x, x) = Σ_i |F_i(x)|²` is bounded directly, the `F_i` being values of isometric intertwiners with
cuspidal images. By `unipotent_orbit_cover` there is a step `W` of the canonical flag such that every point of
`N_W(F_∞)x` is, up to `G(F)` on the left and the principal congruence subgroup on the right, a point
`x·exp(A)·exp(Z)` of that orbit with `‖A‖ + ‖Z‖ ≤ ρ = n²·√f·g·rd_E·(2·least(x))^{1/q}/4`. This is a covering-radius
estimate proved in the source, which by its imports goes back to a transference bound, also proved there. As the
constant term along `N_W` vanishes, `step_bound` bounds the values near `x` by `ρ` times a bound for derivatives along
`Lie N_W`, one step further from the lowest `K`-type. This is iterated `8q·n²f` times and closed by a Sobolev bound.
With `vol ≤ (n·f·rd_E·g·b)^{n²f}` in the Hilbert–Schmidt normalisation, from the volume step, it gives
`∫_deep K(x, x) dx ≤ exp(−n²f)·m` (`deepBound`).

To assemble, `nonzeroFlagBound` shows that `least(x) < 1/2` only on a set of measure at most
`exp(−n·f·log n/3)·vol`, which bounds the identity term missing on the deep part. Together,
`|m − D*| ≤ (2·exp(−n·f·log n/4) + exp(−n²f))·D* + exp(−n²f)·m`, which gives the stated bound for `n ≥ e¹⁵`. For
`q = 0` every contributing `γ` is unipotent, hence trivial, and `m = D*` exactly. Finiteness of `m` is proved first,
from pointwise bounds of the same kind and the finiteness of the volume (`rows_finiteDimensional`).

It remains to describe the volume step. For `n ≥ 3` the volumes, in the Hilbert–Schmidt normalisation, of the
quotients for `J` and for the orthogonal complement of a vector of norm `±1` have the ratio
`rd_E^{f(n−1/2)}·L(n, ηⁿ)`, where `η` is the character of `E/F`. `volumeStep_of_siegelMean` gets this by integrating a
sum of a test function over the lattice vectors of that norm in two ways: by Siegel's mean value formula (the
one-vector case of the Siegel–Weil formula) and by unfolding to the stabiliser of a vector.

## Caveats

The main declaration is a definition of a proposition, not a theorem. `countingTheorem_of_volumeStep` is a proved
theorem with one hypothesis, which `volumeStep_of_siegelWeil` derives from the Siegel–Weil formula, itself the theorem
`siegelWeilUnitaryConvergent`. We found no declaration stating the result without hypothesis. We read the proofs of the
assembly, `near_unipotent`, the near and far terms, the deep bounds, `flagMeasureBound`, `nonzeroFlagBound`,
`volumeStep_of_siegelMean`, the case `q = 0` and `globalProj_holds`. Of the coefficient decay, `kernelB`, cuspidality,
`unfoldBound`, the numerics of `constantBound`, the canonical filtration, the covering radius and transference,
`step_bound`, the Sobolev bound, the finiteness of `m`, and the Siegel–Weil and mean value formulas we read only the
statements. A text search of all Lean files found no `axiom`, and `sorry` only in `src/Challenge.lean`, which lists the
final statements. We compiled nothing and produced no axiom report, and we did not check that the hypotheses can be
satisfied.
