## Statement

`K` is a totally complex number field with `r` infinite places, `I` a nonempty finite set. A *lattice* is a
finite-dimensional `K`-vector space with a finitely generated `O_K`-submodule spanning it and a Hermitian norm at each
infinite place (in the source: a `K`-basis and, at each place, an invertible complex matrix whose columns are
orthonormal). Let `N_i ⊂ V_i` (`i ∈ I`, `d_i = dim V_i`, `R = Σ d_i > 0`) and `C ⊂ W` (`n = dim W`) be lattices and
`φ_i : V_i → W` `K`-linear maps with `φ_i(N_i) ⊂ C`. Write `μ`, `μ_max`, `μ_min` for twice the normalised Arakelov
slope, maximal slope and minimal slope, and `h(φ) = (1/r)·Σ_w log ‖φ‖_w²` (operator norms); let `h_i` be reals with
`h(φ_i) ≤ h_i` when `φ_i ≠ 0`. At one infinite place `v`, let `Y_i`, `Y′` be invertible complex matrices, `D ≥ n` an
integer, `A_i` complex `D × d_i` matrices whose first `n` rows are `Y′⁻¹·[φ_i]_v·Y_i` (`[φ_i]_v`: the matrix of `φ_i`
under the embedding of `v`), `γ_i > 0`, and `0 ≤ δ < 1`, `0 ≤ e ≤ 1/4`, `η ≥ 0` with `(1 − δ)·D ≤ n`,
`Σ_i ‖γ_i A_i^*A_i − 1‖²_HS ≤ e·R` and `tr(Q²) ≤ (1 + η)·(tr Q)²/D`, where `Q = Σ_i γ_i A_i A_i^*`. Let `s` be real and
`u ≥ 0`, `H ≥ 0` satisfy `R·s ≤ Σ_i d_i·(μ(N_i) − h_i)`, `μ_max(N_i) − h_i ≤ s + u` for all `i`, and `s − μ_min(C) ≤ H`.
Then `μ(C) ≥ s − (1 − δ)⁻¹·(4u·log(1 + H/u) + (η + 4√e)·H)`, where the term in `u` is `0` if `u = 0`.

## Why it might matter

The inequality bounds the mean slope of a Hermitian lattice from below by the mean slope of several lattices mapped
into it, less the heights of the maps and explicit error terms. No map need be injective or surjective; instead a
spanning condition is imposed at a single archimedean place, with defects `e` and `η` that need not be small against
the ranks. The bound is not always better than the trivial inequality `μ(C) ≥ μ_min(C)`. Two later files of the
source use it through `joint_a_ramified_holds`, a form with `log|I|` subtracted.

## What the checks found

The first reading compared a wider form (any number field, arbitrary linear maps, heights at all places, `e < 1`, the
constant `2/(1 − √e)`, which is 2 for exact frames, where the Lean has 4, a codimension bound), so its finding that
nothing published implies it concerned a stronger statement. Its verdict, incomparable with medium confidence: no
published result takes an approximate tight frame at one place as hypothesis; where both apply (exact frames) Chen
(2009, Theorem 4.2) and the filtration route (error-free, by its own argument) give more; the frame step is a variant of
Gurvits's lemma (Theorem 2.12 of Garg, Gurvits, Oliveira, Wigderson). By its hand check, with the constant 4 the bound
beats `μ(C) ≥ μ_min(C)` only when `H/u > 9.35`. The forward search, without web search, found in citation lists the
exact case of the frame lemma with constant 1 (Bennett–Carbery–Christ–Tao; Chindris–Derksen) but no slope inequality
under a frame hypothesis; verdict unchanged. The second opinion, judging this statement: incomparable, medium
confidence; exact-case results are stronger on their domain, approximate hypotheses reach beyond it, and whether this
precise combination is in print is unsettled. Its corrections: this statement's restrictions create no published
implication; calling the combination new exceeds the evidence; the first reading's Bost–Chen degree inequality is
misnormalised and omits rank-two ambient restrictions, the slope inequality being the right comparison. They disagree
only on that novelty. Open: can the coefficient of `ε` in the frame lemma tend to 1 as the defects vanish? Is a slope
inequality under approximate frames in print?

## How the formal proof goes

`frame_theorem_sharp` is `theoremFSharp_of` applied to the frame lemma `frame_linear_algebra_first`. The statement
is homogeneous in `(μ, h, s, u, H)`, so the factor 2 plays no role. A lattice is made Euclidean by taking real and
imaginary parts of the coordinates of its vectors at all infinite places; `place_lattice_structure` shows that a
finitely generated `O_K`-submodule becomes discrete, of `ℤ`-rank `[K:ℚ]` times its `K`-rank `rk`. Degrees are
`−log covol`. The step `L_{≥θ}` (`hnStep`) is the largest `O_K`-submodule of minimal slope `≥ θ`; `hnStep_of` shows,
from supermodularity of the degree, that it is saturated and contains every nonzero submodule of minimal slope
`≥ θ`. The source also proves that the Harder–Narasimhan slopes, listed with multiplicity, sum to the degree, that
the number of them `≥ θ` is the rank of `L_{≥θ}`, and that they lie between `μ_min` and `μ_max`.

Step 1, a count (`thmF_share`). Fix `τ ≥ 0` and let `N_i(τ)` be the step of `N_i` at level `s − τ + h_i`. The
numbers `μ_j(N_i) − h_i`, over all `i` and all slopes `j`, have mean `≥ s` and are `≤ s + u`, so at most the
proportion `u/(u + τ)` of them is `< s − τ`: `Σ_i (d_i − rk N_i(τ)) ≤ R·u/(u + τ)`.

Step 2, images of steps (`slope_inequality_mean`). For a `K`-linear `φ` and a lattice `A` with `φ(A) ≠ 0`,
`μ_min(A) − h(φ) ≤ μ_min(φ(A))`. The source proves this first for a map of Euclidean lattices that multiplies norms
by at most a constant (`slope_inequality_general`: saturated sublattices of the image are pulled back and the
covolumes of the quotients are compared through Gram determinants), then makes the operator norms at all places
equal by rescaling the norms of the target by factors of product one, which changes no slope (`twist_of`). With
`h(φ_i) ≤ h_i` and `φ_i(N_i) ⊂ C`, each `φ_i(N_i(τ))` has minimal slope `≥ s − τ`, hence lies in `C_{≥ s−τ}`.

Step 3, rank at one place (`thmF_place`). If `O_K`-submodules `N′_i ⊂ V_i` have `Σ_i (d_i − rk N′_i) ≤ ε·R`, then
`n − rk Σ_i φ_i(N′_i) ≤ (η + 4ε + 4√e)·D`. The `ℂ`-span `U_i` of the vectors `Y_i⁻¹·x_v`, `x ∈ N′_i` (`x_v`: the
coordinates of `x` under the embedding of `v`), has dimension `rk N′_i` (`finrank_span_complex`); `Y′⁻¹[φ_i]_v Y_i`
maps it into the like span for `Σ_i φ_i(N′_i)`; and this matrix is `A_i` followed by the coordinate projection
`P : ℂ^D → ℂ^n`. With `S = Σ_i A_i(U_i)`, `dim P(S) ≥ dim S − (D − n) = n − dim S^⊥`; the frame lemma bounds `dim S^⊥`.

The frame lemma. Let `B_i : V_i → ℋ` be linear maps of finite-dimensional complex inner-product spaces,
`D = dim ℋ`, satisfying the two conditions of the statement (with `B_i` for `A_i`), and `W_i ⊂ V_i` subspaces of
total codimension `≤ ε·R`. Then `m ≤ (η + 4ε + 4√e)·D`, where `m = dim 𝒲` and `𝒲 = (Σ_i B_i(W_i))^⊥`. Put `t = tr Q`
and `T = Σ_l ⟨g_l, Q g_l⟩` for an orthonormal basis `(g_l)` of `𝒲`. From below, `T ≥ (t/D)·m − √m·‖Q − t/D‖_HS`, and
the trace condition says `‖Q − t/D‖²_HS ≤ η·t²/D`. From above, `B_i^* g_l ⊥ W_i`, so `T` is at most the sum of the
traces of `γ_i B_i^*B_i` on the spaces `W_i^⊥`, which is `≤ R·(ε + √(ε·e))` by the first condition and
Cauchy–Schwarz; that condition also gives `t ≥ R·(1 − √e) ≥ R/2`. `final_real1` combines these, absorbing the
Hilbert–Schmidt term by `√(m·ηD) ≤ (m + ηD)/2`.

Step 4, integration. By Steps 1–3 with `ε = u/(u + τ)` and `D ≤ n/(1 − δ)`, the proportion of slopes of `C` below
`s − τ` is at most `c(τ) = (1 − δ)⁻¹·(η + 4u/(u + τ) + 4√e)`; all of them are `≥ μ_min(C) ≥ s − H`.
`layer_cake_formula` gives `μ(C) ≥ s − ∫_0^H min(1, c(τ)) dτ`, and `∫_0^H 4u/(u + τ) dτ = 4u·log(1 + H/u)`.

Companions. `compressTraceSquareBound` and `compressDiagDefectBound` derive the two conditions from bounds on pairs:
if `‖√(γ_iγ_j)·B_i^*B_j‖²_HS ≤ (1 + η₀)·d_i d_j/D*` for `i ≠ j`, `‖γ_i B_i^*B_i − 1‖²_HS ≤ e₀·d_i`, `D ≤ (1 + η₁)·D*`
and `R ≥ D*/ϖ`, with `D* > 0`, `0 < ϖ ≤ 1/20`, `−1 ≤ η₀ ≤ ϖ/2`, `η₁ ≤ ϖ/2`, `√e₀ ≤ ϖ/2`, then the restrictions of
the `B_i` to subspaces of total codimension `≤ ϑ·R`, `0 ≤ ϑ ≤ 1/4`, satisfy them with `η = 5ϖ + 3√ϑ` and `e = 2e₀`;
`tr Q²` is the sum of the squared norms of the blocks `√(γ_iγ_j)·B_i^*B_j`, which do not grow under restriction.
`ramification_condition_holds` inserts this into the main theorem.

## What was checked, and what was not
