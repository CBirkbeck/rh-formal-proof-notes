## Statement

`E/F` is a CM extension, `E` Galois over `ℚ`, `E/F` unramified at finite places, and each inert place of `F` has odd
residue characteristic and residue cardinality `> 3`, and the additive character `ψ` of the adeles of `F` (trivial on
`F`, `exp(−2πi·Tr x)` at infinity) has conductor `𝒪_v` there. `V`, `V′` are Hermitian spaces of dimensions `a > a′`,
`a ≡ a′ (mod 2)`, with signatures `(a − p, p)`, `(q, a′ − q)` at each real place; `W` is skew-Hermitian of dimension
`N = a + a′` and signature `(N − p − q, p + q)`; `2(p + q) + 1 < min((a − a′)/2, a′)`; `V`, `V′` have unimodular
lattices, `W` a lattice self-dual for the trace form. `α`, `β` are unitary Hecke characters of `E` equal to
`η_{E/F}^{a′}`, `η_{E/F}^a` on the ideles of `F`, with infinity types `m_α − m_β > N` at the real places, unramified
outside a finite set `T` of split places at which `ψ` has conductor `𝒪_v`, where `ν = α/β` is ramified. `I_V(Φ)`,
`I_{V′}(Φ′)` are the integrals over `[U(V)]`, `[U(V′)]` of the theta functions with splitting characters `(β, β/α)`,
`(α, α/β)`. Then `I_V(Φ)` converges for all Schwartz `Φ`, and `I_{V′}(Φ′)` for regular `Φ′` (of rank `a′` at a fixed
place `v₁ ∉ T` over a prime of `ℚ` split completely in `E`); some regular `I_{V′}(Φ′)` is nonzero; matching data exist
(at each place an irreducible `U(W)(F_v)`-module `Π_v`, quotient of both local Weil representations), and each has a
unique `c`, nonzero, with `I_V(Φ) = c · I_{V′}(Φ′)` for matched pairs, `Φ′` regular. Two more clauses give its change on
removing a plane.

## Why it might matter

Under these hypotheses the Witt index of `W` is at most `p + q < N/2`, so `W` is not split and `U(W)` has no Siegel
parabolic subgroup: the two theta lifts of the trivial representation cannot be compared through a Siegel Eisenstein
series on `U(W)`. The statement bears on whether theta lifts from two different Hermitian spaces to a unitary group that
is not quasi-split give the same automorphic forms, and on the constant relating them.

## What the checks found

The first reading compared a differently phrased, partly stronger statement: an isometry `δ⁻¹W ≅ V ⊕ V′(−1)` where the
Lean prescribes the dimension, signature and lattice of `W`, plus square-integrability, a unique invariant extension of
`I_{V′}` and definite signs in the recursion, which the Lean lacks. Its key point, that `W` is not split, holds here, so
its comparison with the published first term identities carries over to this statement. Its verdict, incomparable with
medium confidence: the first term identities (Kudla–Rallis, Ichino, Yamana, Gan–Qiu–Takeda, Lu) need a completely
polarised `W`, excluded here; bare proportionality for `p + q ≤ 1` would follow from Chen–Zou, conditionally on the
endoscopic classification for quasi-split unitary groups and on the packets involved being multiplicity free.
Kudla–Rallis and Ichino were read through restatements. A forward search found no such identity without a complete
polarisation and no multiplicity formula for these parameters beyond `F`-rank one, and an example (Yasuda, an inner form
of `Sp(2)`) where theta lifts from two spaces are not proportional; verdict unchanged. The second opinion agrees,
incomparable with medium confidence for the direct analogues, without excluding an indirect deduction; it sees a
specialised theorem of limited reach; unspecified normalisations (the signs in the recursion among them) weaken any
claim of an explicit constant. Corrections: proportionality is stated for regular second vectors only, with no invariant
extension; square-integrability is internal; lacking a Siegel parabolic does not exclude published doubling methods; the
rank-one deduction is qualified. Open: do doubling or multiplicity arguments already give the identity for regular
second vectors? Is the packet multiplicity free, so that Chen–Zou settles `p + q ≤ 1`?

## Nearest published work

- S. S. Kudla, S. Rallis, *A regularized Siegel-Weil formula: the first term identity*, Ann. of Math. 140 (1994), 1–80.
  For `Sp(n)` and `O(V)`, with `V₀` the complementary space in the Witt tower of `V` and the symplectic group split:
  the residue of the Siegel Eisenstein series attached to `V` is a nonzero constant times the regularised theta
  integral of `V₀`, and the leading term of the regularised theta integral of `V` factors through the representation
  attached to `V₀`; the proof rests on a uniqueness theorem for automorphic realisations. The same type of statement
  for another dual pair; neither implies the other. Read by neither in the original: the first reading used Kudla's
  survey *Some extensions of the Siegel-Weil formula* (version of 2002), the second opinion the restatement by Gan, Qiu
  and Takeda.
- A. Ichino, *A regularized Siegel-Weil formula for unitary groups*, Math. Z. 247 (2004), 241–277,
  <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00209-003-0580-5>. For the quasi-split `U(n, n)`: a residue of a Siegel
  Eisenstein series is a constant times the theta integral of the complementary space in the same Witt tower,
  regularised as its unique invariant extension. The unitary first term identity, with `W` split. Abstract and Wu's
  restatement for both readings (the first reading saw Wu's paper in summary only); the first reading also used the
  restatement by Gan, Qiu and Takeda.
- W. T. Gan, Y. Qiu, S. Takeda, *The regularized Siegel-Weil formula (the second term identity) and the Rallis inner
  product formula*, Invent. Math. 198 (2014), 739–831, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.4709>. Theorem 7.3(i) (a recall,
  credited among others to Kudla–Rallis, Ichino and Yamana) with Theorem 8.1(i): the leading term of the regularised
  theta integral of the larger space is a constant, a ratio of measures, times the theta integral of the complementary
  one, for the classical dual pairs including unitary ones, with `W` split by construction. The split counterpart of
  the identity here; the two ranges are disjoint. Read by the first reading (arXiv version 3); checked by the second
  opinion (§§2, 7–8, 11), which notes the constant-term induction and doubling as precedents for the method.
- S. Yamana, *The Siegel–Weil formula for unitary groups*, Pacific J. Math. 264 (2013), 235–256,
  <https://msp.org/pjm/2013/264-1/p11.xhtml>. Theorem 2: for a skew-Hermitian space with a complete polarisation over a
  division algebra with an involution of the second kind, the Siegel Eisenstein series equals `2` or `1` times the
  (regularised) theta integral when `m ≤ n` or `m − r > n`. Groups that are not quasi-split, but with a parabolic of
  Siegel type; a skew-Hermitian space over `E` of Witt index below half its dimension is not covered. Read by the first
  reading; checked by the second opinion.
- H. Lu, *The Siegel-Weil formula for unitary groups: the second term range*, preprint, arXiv:1908.11222 (2019),
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.11222>. In Yamana's setting, with a complete polarisation, the first and second term
  identities for `n + 1 ≤ m ≤ n + r` (Theorem 1.2). Its setting is excluded by the hypotheses here, for the same reason.
  Read by the first reading through a summary only.
- R. Chen, J. Zou, *Arthur's multiplicity formula for even orthogonal and unitary groups*, J. Eur. Math. Soc. 27 (2025),
  4769–4843, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07956>. Theorem 7.7: for a unitary group of `F`-rank at most one, any pure
  inner form, and every elliptic `A`-parameter, the discrete spectrum is described by global theta packets, given the
  endoscopic classification for quasi-split groups. The first reading's own deduction, not a statement of the paper: for
  `p + q ≤ 1` the bare proportionality would follow, if the packets involved are multiplicity free; not the convergence,
  the nonvanishing or the constant. The second opinion: a qualified deduction, not a verified identification. The
  forward search found no extension to these parameters beyond `F`-rank one. Read by the first reading; checked by the
  second opinion.
- J.-S. Li, *Automorphic forms with degenerate Fourier coefficients*, Amer. J. Math. 119 (1997), 523–578. Theorem A, as
  quoted by Chen and Zou: in the stable range the discrete multiplicity of a representation is at most that of its
  theta lift, with no quasi-split hypothesis. The second opinion: an indirect route to proportionality once
  multiplicity one is known upstairs, not the rest of the statement. Both readings: through Chen and Zou's quotation.
- C. Wu, *Irreducibility of theta lifting for unitary groups*, J. Number Theory 133 (2013), 3296–3318, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0770>. First occurrence, irreducibility and tower properties of theta lifts, for groups
  that need not be quasi-split; the regularised Siegel–Weil formula enters only on a doubled, split group. The second
  opinion: doubling and constant-term arguments overlap with the proof here, and the identity does not follow directly.
  Read by the first reading through a summary; checked by the second opinion.
- C. Wu, *Poles of Eisenstein series and theta lifts for unitary groups*, Pacific J. Math. 317 (2022), 207–237,
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11954>. Theorem 0.1: for a cuspidal representation of `U(X)`, not assumed quasi-split, the
  maximal pole of an Eisenstein series for a non-Siegel maximal parabolic gives the lowest occurrence of its theta
  lifts. Cuspidal representations only; the theta integral of the trivial representation on a non-split `W` is not
  treated. Read by the forward search (introduction); abstract only for the first reading.
- T. Yasuda, *CAP automorphic representations of inner forms of Sp(2)*, RIMS Kôkyûroku 1617 (2008), 42–50. For the
  non-quasi-split inner form of `Sp(2)` over a quaternion division algebra, theta lifts of the trivial representations
  from two spaces that are everywhere locally isometric but not globally isometric have the same local components and
  different spaces of automorphic forms. In the forward search's reading: proportionality of theta lifts from two
  spaces to a non-quasi-split group is not automatic; the mechanism, a failure of the Hasse principle over the
  quaternion algebra, is absent over a field `E`, so there is no contradiction. Read by the forward search.

## How the formal proof goes

`PositivityTheory.Sph.Tr.closeSph` assembles the proof from about 150 propositions. Two are hypotheses of
`theoremK_head2`, proved elsewhere in the source: the Siegel–Weil formula for Hermitian spaces definite at the real
places, in every rank (`SiegelWeilStd`), and the continuation with moderate growth of Siegel Eisenstein series on the
doubled group (`EisBL`). Other inputs, all theorems of the source: Siegel sets, Hecke L-functions, a filtration of
geometric-lemma type at split places, duality for lattice vectors at inert places. No isometry `W ≅ V ⊕ V′(−1)` is
assumed: the three spaces are constrained only by their dimensions, signatures and lattices. Infinity type `m`:
`z ↦ (z/|z|)^m`; the additive character is `exp(−2πi·Tr x)` at infinity.

Convergence (`PositivityTheory.ThKEst.kDecay`): `|θ|²` is bounded by a sum over the rational points of `V ⊗ W` (of full
rank, for regular vectors) of a Schwartz majorant moved by both groups, estimated on Siegel sets. This gives the
convergence statements (`I_V` is in Weil's range, `N + 2p < 2a`) and square-integrability on `[U(W)]` of both integrals,
for the vectors used (`GlobalTheory.ThK.SqInt`; here `2(p + q) + 1 < a′` is used). The identity is proved for polynomial
Fock vectors at the real places and extended by continuity, which is why matched pairs are defined as limits.
Factorisation (`PositivityTheory.ThKF.factor_of`): the `L²` pairing is an invariant positive semidefinite form on the
local coinvariants and vanishes on the kernel of the map to `Π_v`, so both integrals factor through `⊗ Π_v`.

Induction on `p + q` (`PositivityTheory.ThKG.Leaves.const_of`), with nonvanishing of both integrals. Base case
`p = q = 0` (`polZero_of`), all groups compact at the real places: on the irreducible module of matched pairs every
invariant Hermitian pairing of combinations of `I_V`, `I_{V′}` is a multiple of `⟨I_{V′}, I_{V′}⟩` (`hermLine_of`). This
gives `c` such that `D = I_V(Φ) − c·I_{V′}(Φ′)` is orthogonal to all `I_{V′}(Ψ)`, and `ϰ` with
`⟨D, D⟩ = ϰ·⟨I_{V′}(Φ′), I_{V′}(Φ′)⟩`. The lift of `D` back to `U(V′)` against `θ′(·, ·; Ψ)` is invariant under
`U(V′)(F_v)` at the places outside `T` above rational primes split completely in `E` (local uniqueness, using
`ν_v(ϖ_v) ≠ 1`, which follows from `E` Galois over `ℚ` and the infinity type), hence constant (`constOfGood`, using the
nonvanishing of Hecke L-functions at 1), hence equal to `⟨D, I_{V′}(Ψ)⟩/vol = 0`. The doubling integral of the Siegel
Eisenstein series against `φ ⊗ conj φ` unfolds, for large real part, into matrix coefficients of `φ`; at the point
`(a′ − N)/2` it is, by the Siegel–Weil formula for `V′`, a constant times the squared norm of the back-lift of `φ`
(`coefLine_of`). Comparing `φ = D` with `φ = I_{V′}(Φ′)` gives `0 = ϰ·(nonzero)`, so `D = 0`. Nonvanishing at the bottom
(`nonZeroBottom_of_doubling_integral`): if `I(Φ) = 0`, a doubling zeta integral of the matrix coefficient `⟨ω(g)Φ, Φ⟩`
vanishes at its Siegel–Weil point; for suitable `Φ` it is a product of Hecke L-values of `ν^{±1}` at integers, nonzero
because `m_α − m_β > N`, times nonzero local factors.

Induction step. A hyperbolic plane adapted to the lattices is removed from `V′` and `W` if `q ≥ 1`, from `V` and `W` if
`q = 0 < p`, giving a triple `G₁`. The constant term of either integral along the isotropic line of `W` has two parts,
on which the torus acts by `α|t|^{a′/2}` and by `β|t|^{a/2}` (`PositivityTheory.ThKCT.ct_all`). For `q ≥ 1`, up to
nonzero constants, the `β`-part of `I_V(Φ)` is the theta integral for `(V, W₁)` of a restriction of `Φ`, and that of
`I_{V′}(Φ′)` is the theta integral for `(V′₁, W₁)` of a "cone" transform of `Φ′`, an integral of Tate type over the
ideles of `E` against `ν|·|^{ε₀+1}`, `ε₀ = (a − a′)/2`. Descent of matching (`locB_of`): if `(Φ, Φ′)` is matched, then a
multiple of the restriction of `Φ` and the cone transform of `Φ′` are matched for `G₁`. Theta integrals of `G₁` are
injective on `⊗ Π_v` (induction hypothesis), so the two constant-term maps induce maps `Π_v → Π_{1,v}` equivariant for
the Levi subgroup; local multiplicity-one statements make them proportional place by place, and test vectors give the
scalars (Euler factors outside `T`, Gauss sums at `T`). This fixes `c` so that the `β`-parts of `I_V(Φ)` and
`c·I_{V′}(Φ′)` agree (`keyB_of`). Then `f = I_V(Φ) − c·I_{V′}(Φ′)` has constant term of the single law `α|t|^{a′/2}`,
and such differences vanish by induction on `p` (`endB_of`, `sumB`): for `p = 0` the `α`-part vanishes too; a difference
with zero constant term along one line is cuspidal (`cuspOfLine_of_keys`); a cuspidal difference is orthogonal to every
`I_V(Ψ)`, `I_{V′}(Ψ′)`, again through constant back-lifts (`orthIso_fin`, `orthAn_of`); being itself a difference of two
such square-integrable functions, it is zero (`perpZero_of`); for `p ≥ 1` the `α`-part restricts to a difference for a
smaller triple. The case `q = 0 < p` is symmetric, with the `α`-parts and `L^T(ε₀, ν)` (`locA_of`, `keyA_of`,
`endA_of`); the recursion clauses record these constants.

## Key steps

- **The compact case** (`GlobalTheory.ThK.Dbl.polZero_of`):

  ones being that the pairings form a line, that back-lifts are constant and an inner product formula for back-lifts, it
  derives `ϰ = 0` and the identity when all three groups are compact at the real places. The induction ends here.
- **Constancy of back-lifts** (`GlobalTheory.ThK.Dbl.thConst_of`): `src/Development/WeilBound/ThetaLifts/LatticeBasics/ThConst.lean`, line 134.
  The lift to `U(V)` or `U(V′)` of a rapidly decreasing combination of the two integrals does not depend on `g`. This
  takes the place of a multiplicity-one statement for `U(W)`; `E` Galois over `ℚ` and the L-value at 1 are used here.
- **Inner product formula for back-lifts** (`GlobalTheory.ThK.Dbl.coefLine_of`): `src/Development/WeilBound/ThetaLifts/LatticeBasics/CoefLine.lean`, line 13.
  Analytic continuation of a doubling integral from the range where it unfolds to the point `(a′ − N)/2`, where the
  Siegel–Weil formula for the definite space `V′` applies. The Eisenstein series is on the doubled group, not on `U(W)`.
- **The constant from one part of the constant term** (`PositivityTheory.ThKG.keyB_of`): `src/Development/WeilBound/ThetaLifts/ConeHypotheses/KeyAOf.lean`, line 37.
  Combines the two constant-term formulas, the descent of matching and the induction hypothesis; the constant of the

- **Descent of matching** (`PositivityTheory.ThKG.Loc.locB_of`): `src/Development/WeilBound/ThetaLifts/LocalTheory/LocBOf.lean`, line 17.
  Matched pairs go to matched pairs under restriction and cone transform, up to the factor
  `C · ν(𝔟)^{±1} ν(𝔠)^{±1} · ∏_{v∈T} ν_v(±1) Nv^{f_v ε₀} ν_v(ϖ_v)^{f_v} G(ψ_v, ν_v) · L^T(ε₀ + 1, ν)` (ideals `𝔟`, `𝔠`

  theta integrals, then vanishing by square-integrability. This closes each induction step.

## What was checked, and what was not

`ThetaIntegralsAgree` is a definition of a proposition. The theorem `theoremK_head2` proves it, with `LocalMatching`,
from two hypotheses, themselves proved without hypotheses (`PositivityTheory.SiegelWeilFormula.Fin.siegelWeilStd`,
`PositivityTheory.RgEis.Hd.eisBL`). The one declaration found applying it to them has a further argument (three other
statements), passed on but never used. Proofs were read for the assembly, the induction, the compact case, the
comparison of constant terms, the descent of matching and the end of the step; statements only for the convergence
estimates, the constant-term formulas, the local statements and the cuspidality and orthogonality lemmas; of the
existence of matching data only the top statement `LocalMatching` was read; the proofs of the inputs were not read. A
text search found no `axiom` declaration, and `sorry` only in `src/Challenge.lean`. Nothing was compiled; satisfiability
of the hypotheses was not checked.
