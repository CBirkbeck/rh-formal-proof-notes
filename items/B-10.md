## Statement

Let `ℓ` be an odd prime, `e ≥ 1`, and `W : G_ℚ → GL₂(ℚ̄_ℓ)` a continuous representation such that:

- `det W` is an odd quadratic character, unramified at `ℓ`;
- `W` is irreducible on `ker χ_ℓ ∩ ker det W`;
- at every prime `p ≠ ℓ`, inertia acts diagonally through characters of finite order;
- at `ℓ`, `W` is upper triangular with inertial characters `χ_ℓ^{e}χ` and `χ_ℓ^{−e}χ` (`χ` of conductor dividing `ℓ`),
  and the sub-character is never `χ_ℓ`;
- twists of `W` by `χ_ℓ^{−e}` times characters of finite order are attached to newforms of weight `2e + 1`, one of
  them of level prime to `ℓ` with `a_ℓ` an `ℓ`-adic unit, and the quotient character of that twist at `ℓ` is
  unramified.

Then, given a bundle of standard facts of Galois cohomology, the Bloch–Kato Selmer group `H¹_f(ℚ, W)` is zero.

In published terms `W` is `V_λ(f)(e)` twisted by a character of finite order, with `f` of odd weight `k = 2e + 1`, and
the statement is the case `K = ℚ`, `r = e` of Kato's Theorem 14.2, read rationally, under extra hypotheses. The
irreducibility hypothesis is not the one under which the Euler-system method is published: no element of the Galois
group with a one-dimensional quotient is assumed. In the Lean source the theorem proving the statement takes a bundle
of standard facts of Galois cohomology as a hypothesis (see the sketch below).

## Context

In the published treatments we compared, the Euler-system bound needs an element of the Galois group acting with a
one-dimensional quotient. For elliptic curves in Rubin's book this element comes from Serre's open image theorem, and
for CM curves there is none. Here the image hypothesis is only irreducibility on `ker χ_ℓ ∩ ker det W`, and Kato's
Euler system is constructed in the formal proof, not taken as an input.

## Comparison with the literature

The statement is a special case of Kato's theorem, and most of the proof is the published method specialised: Kato's
zeta elements and norm relations, a reciprocity law proved only on the ordinary quotient at `ℓ`, and Rubin's direct
passage over `ℚ`. We have not determined how that reciprocity law relates to Kato's proof. One step differs from the
published proofs we compared. It uses auxiliary primes whose Frobenius has no eigenvalue 1, a derivative that is a
cochain corrected by `u` with `δu = y ∪ z`, and non-degeneracy obtained from commutators. At the published auxiliary
primes its key congruence is empty, so it is not the published step restricted. We read the descent only at its top
levels and the Euler system only through its statements, and we did not open Kato's paper. One search found no
published argument that does without a one-dimensional quotient, but that does not exclude one.

A second, independent check could not decide whether the step is in print. In its view the correction by a cochain is
standard algebra of height pairings, and the point that might be substantive, non-degeneracy through commutators
without the rank-one hypothesis, has been neither located in print nor excluded by either comparison. So the second
check does not share our conclusion, and neither check has shown that the step is absent from the literature. It also
points out that Kato's Theorem 14.2 is unconditional only for `1 ≤ r ≤ k − 1`. We would like to know whether this
auxiliary-prime step is in print, for instance in Kato, Perrin-Riou, Nekovář or Chapter IX of Rubin, and whether the
reciprocity law is Kato's local argument restricted to the ordinary quotient.

## Sketch of the proof

The theorem is a deduction (`vanishOrdL_of_zetaUr`) from two inputs. The first is a bundle of standard facts of Galois
cohomology (Poitou–Tate, local Tate duality, finiteness, Euler characteristics, the Greenberg–Wiles formula, the
Bloch–Kato local conditions), taken as a hypothesis. Elsewhere the source builds a term of this bundle, but we did not
audit that construction. The second input is Kato's Euler system for `W`, proved in the source with no hypothesis
(`zetaUr`). It is used for `W` and for `W ⊗ det W`.

The Euler system. A field of modular functions of all levels, with `q`-expansions and actions of `GL₂⁺(ℚ)` and of
Galois, models the étale `H¹` of the open modular curve with coefficients `Sym^w` through its Kummer theory, with
explicit Hecke and Galois operators. Zeta classes are cup products of the Kummer classes of two Siegel units of level
`Nℓ^{r+n}`, carried to weight `k` along the `ℓ`-power tower, traced down and projected to `W`. These are Kato's
elements `_{c,d}z`. Norm relations along `ℚ(μ_{ℓ^r})` and at tame primes come from Hecke operators and an
Eichler–Shimura relation. The output is an integral class `y` over `ℚ`, unramified outside `ℓ`, and, for each prime
`w ≡ 1 mod ℓ^n` outside a finite set, a class over the cyclic field of degree `ℓ^n` and conductor `w` with norm `P·y`,
where `P ≡ unit·det(W(Frob_w) − 1) mod ℓ^n`.

The reciprocity law is proved only on the unramified quotient line of `W` at `ℓ`. There the tower classes are Kummer
classes of units `P_i(ζ_{ℓ^m})`, and character sums of `ζP_i′(ζ)/P_i(ζ)` equal a Hecke eigenfunctional on the
`q`-expansion of a product of two Eisenstein series. The local work is over the completion of `ℤ_ℓ((q))` with
`ζ_{ℓ^n}` and `q^{1/ℓ^n}` adjoined. By Rankin's method this value is a non-zero multiple of
`L(g ⊗ α, e + 1)·L(g ⊗ β, 2e)`, for a newform `g` of weight `2e + 1` and two Dirichlet characters `α`, `β`. Both
values are non-zero, by positivity from Rankin's mean-square bound. Hence `y` is not ordinary at `ℓ`.

The descent. First, the strict Selmer group of `W^*(1) = W ⊗ χ_ℓ(det W)^{−1}` is zero. Let `z` be a strict class that
is not a coboundary. Since `y ∪ z` is locally trivial, `y ∪ z = δu mod ℓ^n` for a cochain `u`, where `δ` is the
coboundary. For `w ≡ 1 mod ℓ^n`, the Kolyvagin derivative `d` of the class at `w` is not a cocycle: `δd = −ψ ∪ P·y`,
with `ψ` the character of the cyclic field. So `ξ = d ∪ z − P·ψ·u` is a 2-cocycle with values in `μ_{ℓ^n}`, trivial at
every place but `w`, hence also at `w`. A local computation gives `P·h(Frob_w) ≡ 0`, where
`h(s) = det(W(s) − 1)·u(s) − ω(adj(W(s) − 1)·y(s), z(s))` and `ω` is the determinant pairing
`ω(x, x′) = x₁x′₂ − x₂x′₁`. By Chebotarev, `det(W(σ) − 1)·h(σ) ≡ 0` on the commutator subgroup of
`ker χ_ℓ ∩ ker det W`. For `φ` there and `a, b` in `ker χ_ℓ ∩ ker W`,
`h(φ[a,b]) − h(φ) ≡ det(W(φ) − 1)·(ω(y(b), z(a)) − ω(y(a), z(b)))`, and irreducibility gives `φ, a, b` with both
factors non-zero. Letting `n → ∞` gives a contradiction. Second, the ordinary Selmer group of `W^*(1)` is zero, by
pairing a class with `y`, which is not ordinary. Third, the ordinary Selmer group of `W` is zero by the
Greenberg–Wiles formula. A Bloch–Kato class is unramified outside `ℓ` and ordinary at `ℓ`, so `H¹_f(ℚ, W) = 0`.

## Key steps

- **Zeta elements and norm relations** (`UpperTheory.Kd.K4.kato_eulerSystem_integrality`): assembles the integral class
  `y` over `ℚ` and the classes over the cyclic fields of conductor `w` with norm `P·y`, from Siegel units in a Kummer
  model of the cohomology of the modular curve. We read it as a statement only.
- **Reciprocity on the ordinary quotient** (`UpperTheory.V.Z.Kato.K5.smoothedZetaClass_hodgeTateLaw`): the image of
  the tower classes on the unramified quotient at `ℓ` is a Hecke eigenfunctional on a product of two Eisenstein series.
  We read it as a statement only, and did not determine its relation to Kato's proof.
- **Non-vanishing of L-values** (`GlobalTheory.EisM.twisted_lvalue_ne_zero_edge`): twisted L-values are non-zero at
  real part `≥ (k + 1)/2`, `k` odd, by positivity. This makes `y` non-ordinary at `ℓ`.
- **Auxiliary-prime step** (`GlobalTheory.Ord2.ht_vanish_commutator`): from the classes at primes `w ≡ 1 mod ℓ^n` and
  the correcting cochain `u`, `det(W(σ) − 1)·h(σ)` vanishes mod `ℓ^n` on commutators. This is the step that differs
  from the published proofs we compared.
- **Strict Selmer group** (`GlobalTheory.Ord2.StrictKill.main`): the commutator identity and irreducibility on
  `ker χ_ℓ ∩ ker det W` give the vanishing of the strict Selmer group of `W^*(1)`.
- **From strict to Bloch–Kato** (`GlobalTheory.Ord2.Upgrade.upgrade`, then
  `GlobalTheory.Ord2.grZero_of_grZero_dual1`): pairing with the non-ordinary class `y`, then the Greenberg–Wiles
  formula.

## Caveats

We read the Lean deduction in full at its top two levels (`vanishOrdL_of_zetaUr`, `es_vanish`, `StrictKill.main`,
`ht_vanish_commutator`) and as statements below that. The Euler system we read through definitions and statements
only. We did not read the modular-function tower, the projection to `W`, the norm and Eichler–Shimura relations, the
reciprocity law or the positivity proof, nor the proof of the Galois-cohomology bundle. A text search of every `.lean`
file found `sorry` only in a template (`src/Challenge.lean`) and no `axiom`. Nothing was compiled. The source also
states Kato's theorem in general (`UpperTheory.V.U3.KatoUse`), and we did not look for a proof of it. We did not open
Kato's paper, Kolyvagin, Perrin-Riou, or three further works on Euler systems that we know only by name. A separate
Kolyvagin argument in the source, with an Euler system of Jacobi sums bounding minus parts of class groups, plays no
part here, and we only spot-checked it.
