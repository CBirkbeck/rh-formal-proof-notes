## Statement

Let `L` be a totally complex number field of degree `n`, with ring of integers `𝒪_L` and discriminant `d_L`, and let
`N`, `m` be natural numbers. The torsion subgroup of `H_m(GL_N(𝒪_L); ℤ)` (group homology, trivial coefficients) is
finite, of order at most `(m+2)^A · s^(m·A·s^m)`, where `s = 3^(nN²)` and `A` is given below.

Here `A = I^(1+nN²) · ((2R+1)^(nN) + 1)^(nN²(m+1))`, with `I = |d_L|^N · (nN)^(nN)` and `R = N^N · I²`, and
`A ≤ (2|d_L|·nN)^(12(m+1)(nN)³N)` for `N ≥ 1` (`orbnum`). No prime is set aside, and nothing is assumed about rational
homology.

Two logarithmic forms are stated. For `N = 4e+4`, `m = 2e` and `e ≥ 1`,
`log #H_m(GL_N(𝒪_L); ℤ)_tors ≤ exp(49·m·(nN)³·N·log(2|d_L|·nN))` (`torsBound`). For some real `C` (24 in the proof),
`log #H_m(GL_N(𝒪_L); ℤ)_tors ≤ exp((2en)^C·log(2|d_L|))` for `e ≥ 1`, `1 ≤ N ≤ 4e+6` and `m ≤ 2e+1` (`torsAll`). No
declaration states `log log # ≤ c·(m+1)(nN)³N·log(2|d_L|·nN)` for all `N`, `m` at once. The proof of `torsAll` has, as
an intermediate step for all `N ≥ 1` and `m`, `log # ≤ exp(12(m+1)(nN)³N·log(2|d_L|·nN) + 2(m+1)nN² + 2m)`.

Finite generation of these groups is proved separately (`hglFG_of`, `fgShift`). Fields with a real place are not
covered. The source has no bound for the torsion of `K_m(𝒪_L)`. That an integer depending only on `m` kills the kernel
of the Hurewicz map is there only a proposition, which the source does not prove.

## Context

The question is how large the torsion of `H_m(GL_N(𝒪_L); ℤ)` can be in terms of the degree and discriminant of `L`. In
the literature such bounds are passed, through the Hurewicz map and stability, to torsion in K-groups of rings of
integers and in étale cohomology groups `H²(𝒪_L[1/ℓ], ℤ_ℓ(i))`, but the formal proof states no bound for K-groups, and
its Hurewicz step is a named hypothesis for which we found no proof. Here the whole torsion subgroup of the homology
is bounded, including the primes dividing the orders of finite subgroups of `GL_N(𝒪_L)`. In the source `torsAll` feeds
an argument, which we did not read, about a Galois cohomology group `H²` with coefficients `ℤ_ℓ(e+1)`, defined by
continuous cochains on the Galois group of the maximal extension of `L` unramified at the finite places not above `ℓ`.
The source does not identify this group with étale cohomology.

## Comparison with the literature

We compared the literature with a stronger statement: one bound of this shape for every number field and all `N`, `m`,
together with a corollary for K-groups. The Lean proves neither. It covers totally complex `L` only, with a product
bound that gives the shape, and what we say here bears on those fields only. For them the bound improves on what we
found in print in the range of primes it covers, not in its size. The whole torsion subgroup is bounded, explicitly
and with no hypothesis on rational homology, whereas the published explicit bounds set aside the primes up to about
`nN + 1`. Away from those primes, Bayer-Fluckiger–Emery–Houriet (Theorem 1.1) have a bound of the same shape, and de
la Torre Durán (a 2026 preprint) a smaller one for totally real and CM fields. Both are stated for the K-groups `K_m`
and obtained through the homology of `GL_{2m+1}`, where the Lean has the homology only. Emery (2014) covers all
primes, but only when rational homology vanishes, and Emery's 2024 bound for all number fields is for `PGL_N`, not
`GL_N`. The notion of small torsion in Bayer-Fluckiger–Emery–Houriet, a quotient by the elements of small order, is
not the removal of whole primary parts. The step that reaches the small primes is elementary, and some would say it is
known in substance.

We looked at later papers, among them papers citing these, and found no bound at all primes that is uniform in the
field, though the search was not systematic. An independent second check agrees, but only provisionally, that the
bound improves in its explicit coverage of the whole torsion for totally complex fields, and not in sharpness. We
weigh the evidence differently from the second check. Our search for later work inclines us to think that no such
bound is in print, while the second check points out that remarks in the literature that related problems are open do
not show that this one has not been done. We would like to know whether a bound at all primes for `GL_N(𝒪_L)` is in
print, and whether the published cell counts, combined with the bound for finite groups, already give this shape.

## Sketch of the proof

*A general bound* (directory `TorsionBoundsChains`). Let a group `G` act on sets `X_p`, `p ≥ 0`, with equivariant face
maps, such that the augmented complex `⋯ → ℤ[X_1] → ℤ[X_0] → ℤ → 0` is exact in degrees `≤ m`, with at most `A` orbits
on `X_p` and stabilizers of order at most `s` for `p ≤ m`. Then `H_m(G; ℤ)_tors` is finite of order at most
`(m+2)^A · s^(m·A·s^m)` (`mainFace`). The proof (`shift`) is dimension shifting, by induction on `m`, for a module `M`
with a partial resolution by the permutation modules `ℤ[X_p]`. In degree zero (`base`), `H_0(G; M)` is the quotient of
`ℤ[X_0/G]` by the images of the boundaries of the generators of `ℤ[X_1]`. There `cokerTors` bounds the torsion of
`ℤ^ι/⟨Λ⟩` by `b^(#ι)` when the vectors of `Λ` have `ℓ¹`-norm at most `b`. A maximal independent subfamily is completed
by unit vectors, the index of its span is a determinant, at most the product of the `ℓ¹`-norms of the rows, and the
torsion injects into the quotient by that span. In the inductive step let `K` be the image of `ℤ[X_1]` in `ℤ[X_0]` and
`k = m+1`. The long exact sequence gives an exact piece `H_k(G; ℤ[X_0]) → H_k(G; M) → H_m(G; K)`. The first group has
order at most `s^(A·s^(m+1))` (`permCard`), `K` is resolved by the shifted complex, and the orders multiply. For
`permCard` the set is split into orbits. For one orbit Shapiro's lemma reduces to the stabilizer `S`, and `finGroup`
gives `#H_j(S; ℤ) ≤ #S^(#S^j)` for `j ≥ 1`, because the group is killed by `#S` (`kill`) and is a quotient of a
subgroup of the free abelian group on `S^j`. So the primes dividing the orders of the stabilizers are kept. No
spectral sequence is used and nothing is inverted. The factor `(m+2)^A` comes from the base case, where `m+2` bounds
the `ℓ¹`-norm of a boundary.

*The complex* (`HermiteNormalForms`, `ConvexNerveComplexes`). Let `E` be the real vector space of tuples `h = (h_w)`
of `N × N` complex Hermitian matrices, one for each infinite place `w` of `L`, and let `q_h(y) = Σ_w y_w^* h_w y_w`
for `y ∈ 𝒪_L^N`. Let `P = {h : q_h(y) ≥ 1 for all y ≠ 0}`, on which `GL_N(𝒪_L)` acts. A vertex is an `h ∈ P`
determined by its minimal vectors (the `y` with `q_h(y) = 1`), that is, a perfect form of minimum one. `X_p` is the
set of `(p+1)`-tuples of vertices, ordered and with repetitions allowed, for which some linear functional on `E`,
positive on the nonzero positive semidefinite tuples, takes its minimum over `P` at each of them. This is the ordered
nerve of the cover of the open dual cone by the normal cones of the vertices. `good` shows that `P` is nonempty, lies
in the positive definite cone (`rypos`) and is locally finite (`locfin`). Exactness in all degrees (`sx_exact`) is a
nerve lemma for a cover of a convex set by closed convex sets, finitely many of which meet the convex hull of any
finite subset (`nerve_convex`). It is reduced to compact convex sets and proved there by induction on the number of
faces of the nerve (`nerve_compact`). If some point lies in every member that meets the set, the complex is a cone.
Otherwise a maximal face and a member disjoint from its intersection are separated by a hyperplane (Hahn–Banach, from
Mathlib), the set is cut into two halves and a slice, each with fewer faces, and a Mayer–Vietoris argument on chains
concludes. The cover property is `exists_vert`: every functional of the dual cone is minimal over `P` at a vertex.

*Orbits and stabilizers.* The common minimal vectors of the vertices of a simplex span `L^N` (`simp_span`), so some of
them are the columns of a nonsingular matrix `T` over `𝒪_L`. With `Nm` the norm to `ℚ`, `|Nm(det T)| ≤ I` (`nrmle`).
Indeed, for `h ∈ P`, `∏_w det h_w ≥ 1/I` (`detlow`, from Minkowski's convex body theorem of Mathlib for the Gram
matrix of `q_h` on `ℤ^(nN)` and the identity `2^(nN)·det(Gram) = |d_L|^N·(∏_w det h_w)²`), while each `T^* h_w T` is
positive semidefinite with diagonal at most `1`, so of determinant at most `1`. Nonsingular matrices with
`|Nm(det)| ≤ I` fall into at most `I^(1+nN²)` classes under left multiplication by `GL_N(𝒪_L)` (`hnf`). For a fixed
`T`, at most `((2R+1)^(nN)+1)^(nN²)` vertices have the columns of `T` among their minimal vectors (`nearvx`), because
a vertex is determined by at most `nN²` minimal vectors (`vxdet`), and for such a vector `y` Cramer's rule bounds by
`R` all conjugates of the coordinates of `|Nm(det T)|·T⁻¹y` (`nearcoord`). This gives `A` (`orb`). The stabilizer of a
simplex fixes its first vertex, so it permutes a finite set of minimal vectors containing a basis and is finite
(`stabfin`). A finite subgroup of the units of a ring free of rank `r` over `ℤ` has order at most `3^r` (`mink3`),
since an element of finite order congruent to `1` modulo `3` is `1`. Here `r = nN²`.

*Assembly.* `tors_le` is `mainFace` for this action, and `torsBound` and `torsAll` follow by arithmetic from `orbnum`
and `TorsLe.log_le_exp`. That `L` is totally complex is built into `E` and used in `rypos`, `detlow`, `nrmle` and the
dimension count behind `vxdet`. No published theorem is assumed as a hypothesis. Shapiro's lemma, the long exact
sequence, Hahn–Banach separation and the convex body theorem come from Mathlib, and the rest is proved in the source.

## Caveats

`UpperTheory.V.KT.tors_le` is a theorem with a proof, and its only hypotheses are that `L` is a number field and is
totally complex (Mathlib's `IsTotallyComplex`). `torsBound` and `torsAll` are proved theorems. We read the proofs of
the declarations named above down to their calls to Mathlib, except for about thirty auxiliary lemmas that we took as
statements, among them the identification of `H_0` with a quotient of `ℤ[X_0/G]`, `indPerm`, `permSplit`, `subquot`,
`cntconj`, `scinv`, and part of the Gram determinant identity. A text search of the 92 files of the three directories,
of the files defining the statements and of three files imported from elsewhere found no `sorry`, no `axiom` and no
bundle of assumptions. We compiled nothing, and we did not ask Lean which axioms the proofs use. The Hurewicz
statement `UpperTheory.V.Published.KHurewicz` is the definition of a proposition, not a theorem. Apart from its
definition it occurs only as a field of a bundle of named statements, and we found no declaration that uses it.
