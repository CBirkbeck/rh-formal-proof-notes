## Statement

Let `L` be a totally complex number field with `r₂(L)` complex places, let `j` be an odd integer with `j < r₂(L)`, let
`ℓ ≥ 1` be an integer and let `n ≥ 2j + 4`. Write `ψ^ℓ : GL_m → GL_{ℓm}` for the block sum of `ℓ` copies and `stab`
for the stabilisation, and put `Z = ∏_{t=0}^{j−2} (ψ^ℓ_* − ℓ^(t+2)·stab_*) : H_j(GL_n(𝒪_L); ℤ) →
H_j(GL_{ℓ^(j−1)·n}(𝒪_L); ℤ)` on integral group homology. Then the image of `Z` generates a subgroup of rank at most
`r₂(L)`.

This is an upper bound only, and it is about the homology of the discrete groups `GL_n(𝒪_L)`: no K-group, plus
construction or Hurewicz map occurs. It follows from Borel's computation of the stable real cohomology together with
homological stability. Indeed `Z` kills the decomposable part of the stable rational homology in degree `j` and, for
`ℓ ≥ 2`, maps onto a multiple of the primitive part, of rank exactly `r₂(L)` for odd `j ≥ 3`. For `ℓ = 1` and `j ≥ 3`
the map `Z` is zero, so the equality needs `ℓ ≥ 2`. The formal statement is the upper half of this, and only for
totally complex fields and degrees below `r₂(L)`.

In the formal proof this bound stands in for Borel's theorem, and for Chern classes and the Hurewicz map, in a proof of
the finiteness of `H²(𝒪_L[1/ℓ], ℤ_ℓ(E+1))` for a prime `ℓ`, a field `L` containing the `ℓ`-th roots of unity (the
fourth roots if `ℓ = 2`) and an integer `E ≥ 1`. That proof gives a bound of explicit shape in the degree and
discriminant of `L` and in `E`, in which one absolute constant is not specified. In the Lean source the rank bound
`bOdd` is a theorem without hypotheses. The application `lemmaK` is an implication: it takes the rank bound and three
duality statements (Poitou–Tate duality, local finiteness and local Tate duality) as hypotheses.

## Context

In the published proof of Soulé's finiteness theorem, Borel's theorem enters through K-groups and Chern classes or the
Dwyer–Friedlander map. Here a rank bound and explicit cycles in the homology of `GL_N` give a bound for
`H²(𝒪_L[1/ℓ], ℤ_ℓ(E+1))` at every prime, explicit up to one unspecified absolute constant, once the duality theorems
are granted. The rank bound itself gets invariant-form representatives of stable real classes from a Hecke operator at
a finite prime, and not from Matsushima's argument.

## Comparison with the literature

The statement is narrower than Borel's theorem, and the argument differs from Borel's at the decisive step. It stays
within Borel's framework of invariant forms on the symmetric space, reduction theory and `L²` estimates on `Γ\G`. But
where Borel uses Matsushima's argument as extended by Garland, the formal proof shows that stable real classes below
degree `r₂(L)` come from invariant forms by means of a Hecke operator at a principal prime, which contracts
square-integrable functions of mean zero and moves stable cycles very little. In the work we read, the triviality of
the Hecke action on stable classes in characteristic zero is deduced from Borel's theorem (Calegari–Emerton). Here it
is proved directly. We have not read Borel's papers ourselves and know his argument from later accounts and partly
from memory, although an independent second check reports reading parts of Borel (1974) and reports that they support
the comparison with Matsushima and Garland. That we did not find the argument in the literature rests on a single
extended search, so we cannot rule out that it is in print.

The second check could not decide whether the argument is known. It agrees that the argument differs from Borel's
proof, but points out that this is all that has been shown: the ingredients are standard, and what may be substantive
is the formula for the Hecke operator on stable cycles, which keeps it close to the identity, combined with the
contraction. It also reads Calegari–Emerton differently from us, as giving the triviality of the Hecke action from
stability, independently of Borel. We would like to know how the formula for the Hecke operator on stable cycles
compares with the results of Ash and of Calegari–Emerton, and whether this route to Borel-type surjectivity is known.

## Sketch of the proof

The rank bound. Suppose a real cycle in the span of the classes `Z x` is killed by the `r₂(L)` functionals `τ_w`, one
for each complex place. Then it is a boundary, and the rank bound follows. To see this, push the cycle to a large `N` by
homological stability, choose a real cocycle detecting it, replace the cocycle by an invariant form and decompose the
form by weights. The map `Z` kills the weights `2, …, j`.

Invariant forms. For `j < r₂(L)` and arbitrarily large `N`, every real `j`-cocycle of `Γ = GL_N(𝒪_L)` agrees on cycles
with the integral, over straight simplices in the cone of positive Hermitian forms, of a form invariant under
`∏_w GL_N(ℂ)`. A Čech–de Rham construction with a `Γ`-partition of unity turns the cocycle into a closed `Γ`-invariant
form bounded by a power of a height function. By reduction theory on Siegel sets every fixed power of the height is
integrable on `Γ\G` once `N` is large, so the form is square-integrable. It is averaged over the centre. Its mean over
`Γ\G` is an invariant form `ω̄`, and `η = ω − ω̄` has mean zero. Take a prime element `π` of norm at least `25`. When
`N ≥ 3`, the normalised Hecke operator `T` of `diag(π, 1, …, 1)` has norm at most `3/5` on square-integrable functions
of mean zero. The value of a closed square-integrable form on a fixed cycle is bounded by its `L²` norm, so the values of
`T^r η` on any cycle are `O((3/5)^r)`. On cycles stabilised from `GL_n` one has `T_N = (1 − a)·id + a·T_n`, with `a` a
ratio of indices, so for large `N` the operator moves the finitely many stable classes by at most `1/5`. Values that
decay like `(3/5)^r` while shrinking by at most `4/5` per step are zero, and so `η` vanishes on cycles.

The formula for the Hecke operator on stable cycles rests on a "centre kills" argument. For `j < r₂(L)` the
block-triangular group `GL_n(𝒪_L) ⋉ M_{n×r}(𝒪_L)` has the degree-`j` real homology of `GL_n(𝒪_L)`. The reason is that
some unit `v` has `∏_w |v|_w^(a_w) ≠ 1` whenever `0 < Σ a_w < r₂(L)`, so multiplication by `v` has no eigenvalue `1` on
the cohomology of the abelian normal subgroup in degrees `1, …, j`. This is where the restriction `j < r₂(L)` comes
from.

The spectral gap. The groups `K_r = SL_N(L) ∩ d_r⁻¹·SL_N(𝒪_L)·d_r`, where `d_r = diag(π, …, π, 1, …, 1)` has `r = 0, 1,
2` entries `π`, stabilise three adjacent vertices of the building at `π`. A two-transitivity count in finite quotients
bounds the cosine between their spaces of invariant functions by `1/5`, and a Hilbert-space lemma on three subspaces
gives `2/5`. Functions invariant under the dense subgroup generated by `K_0` and `K_1` are constant.

Weights. At the base point an invariant form is a `∏ U(N)`-invariant alternating form on tuples of Hermitian matrices.
By the first fundamental theorem for the unitary group, proved through a double-commutant statement, these forms are
spanned by alternated products of traces, and `ψ^d` multiplies such a product by `d` raised to the number of its trace
factors. In odd degree the single traces give one functional for each place, `r₂(L)` in all, and `Z` kills the rest.

Application. Through a Bockstein and the rank bound, a compatible family `Φ` of `ℤ/ℓ^ν`-valued functionals on
`j`-chains of `GL_n(𝒪_L)` with `Φ∘ψ^d = d·Φ` has the property that `ℓ^(j−1)·Φ` takes at most
`(ℓ^ν)^(r₂(L))·t_j·t_(j−1)` values on cycles, where `t_i` is the order of the torsion of `H_i(GL_{2j+4}(𝒪_L); ℤ)`.
After Poitou–Tate duality, classes in `H²(𝒪_L[1/ℓ], ℤ_ℓ(E+1))` become eigen-homomorphisms on class groups of a
cyclotomic layer. These are counted through twisted norms, which are told apart by residues at primes of degree one,
and the residues are values of such functionals on explicit cycles. The factor `(ℓ^ν)^(r₂)` cancels against the units,
and what remains, torsion in the homology of `GL_{4E+6}`, does not depend on `ν`. The bound is used in degree `2E + 1`,
after enlarging the field so that `2E + 1 < r₂`.

## Key steps

- **Reduction** (`PositivityTheory.KB.sBStarOdd_of`): stability, detection by a cocycle, invariant forms and weights
  give the rank bound through `rank_span_le`.
- **Invariant forms** (`PositivityTheory.KB.JoinReconstruction.key`): a theorem from eleven named statements, which are
  supplied in `bStarOdd`. It combines the partition of unity, square-integrability, the mean, the Hecke contraction and
  the near-identity on stable cycles. This is the step that differs from Borel's proof.
- **Spectral gap** (`PositivityTheory.KB.sGap_of`): norm at most `3/5` on square-integrable functions of mean zero,
  from three vertex stabilisers in the building.
- **Hecke operator on stable cycles** (`PositivityTheory.KB.AffineStabilizerVanishing.assemble`): a theorem from ten
  named statements, whose hypothesis-free form is `PositivityTheory.KB.sHecke`. On stabilised cycles `T_N` is
  `(1 − a)·id + a·T_n`. It uses the affine subgroup and the unit of `PositivityTheory.KB.SplitAffineSpectral.unit`.
- **Weights** (`PositivityTheory.KB.sWtOdd_of`): invariant forms are alternated trace products, of weight the number of
  traces, and in odd degree there are `r₂(L)` of weight one.
- **Application** (`UpperTheory.V.ZK.lemmaK`, through `UpperTheory.V.ZK.sUpperOf`): the bound for
  `H²(𝒪_L[1/ℓ], ℤ_ℓ(E+1))`, with Poitou–Tate duality, local finiteness and local Tate duality as hypotheses.

## Caveats

`bOdd` is a closed theorem without hypotheses. We searched the text of the directories of the argument (286 files for
the K-theory part, 74 for the counting, and five further directories) for `sorry` and `axiom` and found neither.
Homological stability and finite generation of the homology of `GL_N(𝒪_L)` are proved in the source, but we did not
read those proofs. We read in full the statements along the chain and the proofs of `sBStarOdd_of`,
`JoinReconstruction.key`, `AffineStabilizerVanishing.assemble`, `SplitAffineSpectral.unit` and `eigen`, and
`sUpperOf`. The other steps we read as statements only, the gap bound we skimmed, and most of the roughly 28,000 lines
beneath we did not read. Nothing was compiled. We did not trace how the duality hypotheses of `lemmaK` are discharged,
nor the torsion bounds or the explicit cycles. We did not open Borel's papers of 1974 and 1977, Soulé (1979, 2003), a
survey by Weibel, Arlettaz (1991), Blasius–Franke–Grunewald, Borel–Yang, Garland or Ash. The second check reports
reading parts of Borel (1974).
