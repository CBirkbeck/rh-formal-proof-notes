## Statement

Assuming the absolute Siegel lemma (below), there is `C ≥ 0` with `μ̂_max(Lie Ḡ) ≤ C·log(2 + g + max(h, 0))` for every
semi-abelian scheme `G` over the integers `O_L` of a number field with an action of the integers `O_E` of a CM field `E`
and a line bundle `ℒ` as follows. The hypothesis, with constant `½·log n`: a Hermitian lattice of rank `n` over the
integers of a number field has, over a finite extension, a line of normalised degree at least `μ̂ − ½·log n − ε`, for
every `ε > 0`. The data: `O_E` is unramified over the integers of the maximal real subfield of `E` and contains a square
root `r` of `−d`, `d` a prime with `d ≡ 7 (mod 16)`; `w₀ = (1 + r)/2`; `ℒ` is a symmetric rigidified line bundle on `G`
with a cubical structure such that, for `Λ(ℒ) = m^*ℒ ⊗ p₁^*ℒ⁻¹ ⊗ p₂^*ℒ⁻¹ ⊗ 0^*ℒ` on `G × G`,
`(a × 1)^*Λ(ℒ) ≅ (1 × ā)^*Λ(ℒ)` for every `a ∈ O_E` with complex conjugate `ā`, and `ℒ ≅ (1, w₀)^*Λ(ℒ)`; the generic
fibre `A` is proper, `ℒ` is ample on `A` and `K(ℒ)` is trivial on `A` (a principal polarisation). `Lie Ḡ` is the
`O_L`-module of tangent vectors of `G` along the unit section with the inner product `π·H_σ` at each `σ : L → ℂ`, `H_σ`
the hermitian Riemann form of `ℒ` on `A_σ(ℂ)`; `g` is its rank, `h = −deĝ(Lie Ḡ)/[L:ℚ]`, `μ̂_max` the supremum of
`deĝ(F̄)/([L:ℚ]·rk F)` over non-zero submodules `F`. The source proves no form without the action and these conditions.

## Why it might matter

The maximal slope of the tangent lattice of a semi-abelian model enters slope arguments for abelian varieties over
number fields (periods, isogenies, heights of points of moduli spaces); here, assuming the Siegel lemma of the
statement, it is bounded by the logarithm of the height and of the dimension, with one constant, in the class above. By
an identification that is not in the source (whose statement does not name the Faltings height), `h` should be, for this
metric, the stable Faltings height of `A` for the metric `‖α‖² = (2π)^(−g)·|∫ α ∧ ᾱ|` on `det ω_A`.

## What the checks found

The first reading compared a stronger statement: an unconditional bound, with one absolute constant, for every
principally polarised abelian variety. The Lean statement is an implication from an absolute Siegel lemma, for the class
above with an action of `O_E`; so that verdict does not bear on it, and the first reading made no comparison for this
class under the Siegel hypothesis. For the general form the first reading found an improvement (medium confidence; no
citation database for a search of citing works) on Bost (1996, Prop. 5.3 (ii)) and Gaudron (2019, Prop. 4.2, Cor. 4.5),
whose bounds are linear in the Faltings height, hold for any polarisation, so for this class too, and are not superseded
numerically for given `(g, h)`, `C` not being explicit. The second opinion judges the statement on this page
incomparable with the literature (medium confidence): better growth in height and dimension on a restricted class, while
the published bounds cover substantially more varieties; no exact precedent, but its coverage of work on special
endomorphisms is incomplete. Its corrections: the unrestricted verdict needs qualification; higher-order filtered jets
are established machinery; later use of linear cotangent bounds does not show that no logarithmic bound exists; "no
bound for the tangent space" was too strong, as cotangent bounds give linear tangent bounds by duality. It adds that the
Siegel hypothesis is proved in print. The two verdicts disagree, but they are not about the same statement. Open: is
this slope bounded in work on abelian varieties with a CM-field action? Which abelian varieties carry the class's data?

## How the formal proof goes

Write `h⁺ = max(h, 0)`, `‖x‖_σ` for the length of a tangent vector at `σ`, `mean_σ` for the mean over the `[L:ℚ]`
embeddings. The proof combines `TopSlope.slopeBound`, `line_of_key`, `keyDeg_of_keyBad` and `key_bad_reduction_holds`.

*Reduction to one vector.* For a non-zero submodule `F` of `Lie(G)`, the Siegel hypothesis with `ε = 1` gives a finite
extension `L′` and a non-zero `x` in `Lie(G) ⊗ O_L′` with `μ̂(F) − ½·log g − 1 ≤ −mean_σ log‖x‖_σ`. The data extend to
`O_L′` with the same `g` and `h` (`ext_baseChange`). So it suffices to prove `−mean_σ log‖x‖_σ ≤ C·log(2 + g + h⁺)` for
all data and every non-zero integral tangent vector `x` (`LineSt`); the constant of the theorem is then `C + 2`.

*A theta section of bounded L²-norm.* After a finite extension `Lie(G)` has a basis `e` and `ℒ` has a section `θ` over
`G` vanishing identically on no fibre. The source proves a key formula (`keyFormula_holds`): a rule giving, for a
principally polarised abelian variety in characteristic 0 with rigidified bundle, a non-zero section `θ` and a basis
`v` of the Lie algebra, a non-zero scalar `κ(θ, v)` with `a⁸·κ(aθ, Mv) = det(M)⁴·κ(θ, v)`, compatible with base change,
a unit under good reduction, and with `|κ|·‖θ‖₂⁸·‖v₁ ∧ … ∧ v_g‖⁻⁴ = (2π)^(2g)` over `ℂ` (`‖θ‖₂` the L²-norm for the
Haar probability measure). In the class of the statement `κ(θ, e)` is integral at places of bad reduction too
(`Kftwo.kp`): the first use of the action and the conditions on `ℒ`, through `G × G` with the integers of a second CM
field acting by 2 × 2 matrices over `O_E`. By the product formula, `mean_σ log‖θ‖₂ ≤ (g/4)·log 2π + h/2 ≤ h⁺/2 + g`.

*From the L²-norm to the sup norm.* At each `σ` sections of `ℒ^⊗n` are theta functions on `ℂ^g` (`frames_main`). An
archimedean lemma (`nform`) gives `ρ_σ` in `(0, √(2g)]` with `sup‖θ‖_σ ≤ ‖θ‖₂·exp(E_σ)`,
`E_σ = g·log(1 + 1/ρ_σ) + C₀·g·log 2g`, such that for large `m` the mean of `−log(‖θ‖_σ/‖θ‖₂)` over the `m`-torsion
points where `θ ≠ 0` is at least `π/(96ρ_σ²) − E_σ`. The value of `θ` at an `N`-torsion point `y`, raised to the power
`N²` and read in the rigidification through `[N]^*ℒ ≅ ℒ^⊗N²`, is an algebraic integer `T` with
`|σ(T)| = ‖θ‖_σ(y)^(N²)` (`integral_torsVal`). This is the second use of the action: the proof uniformises `G` over
complete discrete valuation rings (`uDvrSt_holds`), which the source does given endomorphisms `w`, `w′` of sum 1 with
`ℒ ≅ (1, w)^*Λ(ℒ)` and `(w × 1)^*Λ(ℒ) ≅ (1 × w′)^*Λ(ℒ)`; here `w = w₀`. The product formula for the `T` bounds
`mean_σ ρ_σ⁻²` by a constant times `Φ + g²·log 2g`, `Φ = h⁺/2 + g`, and gives numbers `M_σ ≥ sup‖θ‖_σ` with
`mean_σ log M_σ ≤ Φ + C·g·log(2 + g + Φ)` (`mean_log_sup`).

*Integral sections of a power.* Put `K = ⌈2 + g² + h⁺⌉`, `m = 3K`, `n = m²`, `n′ = n/3`. After a further extension
there are `r = m^(2g)` sections `s_j` of `ℒ^⊗n` over `G`, one for each `m`-torsion point `r_j`, with
`‖s_j‖(w) = ‖θ‖(r_j + m·w)`. They are integral, again through the integrality of torsion values; their sup norms are at
most `M_σ`; and at each `σ` they span the theta functions of level `n` (`Basis.main`).

*Jets along the vector.* `JetMain.jets` defines `J_{i,j}` in `L` as the value at the origin of the `i`-th iterate of
the derivation of `x` applied to `s_j`, divided by the `n`-th power of the rigidification and not by `i!`. If `c` has
coordinates in `O_L` and `Σ_j c_j·J_{k,j} = 0` for all `k < i`, then `Σ_j c_j·J_{i,j}` is an algebraic integer; at each
`σ`, under the same vanishing, it is the `i`-th derivative at `0` of `τ ↦ Σ_j c_j·f_j(τ·v)`, `f_j` the theta function of
`s_j`, `v` the coordinates of `x`. For each `i ≤ n′` some combination vanishes to order exactly `i`: `θ` gives theta
functions `s₀`, `s₁` of level 3 (from products of three translates) with `s₀(0) ≠ 0`, `s₁(0) = 0` and non-zero
derivative of `s₁` along `v`, and `s₀^(n′−i)·s₁^i` has level `n` (`lefschetz_direction`). The Cauchy estimate on the
circle of radius `√(i/n)/‖x‖_σ` bounds that derivative by `A_{i,σ}·|c|`, `A_{i,σ} = (e·n·n′)^(i/2)·‖x‖_σ^i·√r·M_σ`.

*Product formula.* `flag_slope` takes a non-zero maximal minor of the matrix `(J_{i,j})`, `i ≤ n′`. It is integral at
every finite place, by an elimination that uses only the filtered integrality above; at each `σ` it is at most
`∏_i A_{i,σ}`, by Gram–Schmidt. Hence `0 ≤ Σ_{i ≤ n′} Σ_σ log A_{i,σ}`, that is
`−mean_σ log‖x‖_σ ≤ ½·log(e·n·n′) + (2/n′)·(g·log m + mean_σ log M_σ)`. Since `n′ = 3K²` and `K ≥ 2 + g² + h⁺`, the
last term is at most a constant times `log(2 + g + h⁺)`, and so is the first (`SlopeParameterChoice.full`). The height
enters through the one section `θ` and is divided by the number `n′` of vanishing orders.

*Inputs.* The Siegel lemma is the only hypothesis. The source derives that proposition from four named statements (an
arithmetic intersection system, two statements of Hilbert–Samuel type, resolution of singularities;
`lineOfLargeDegree_of_use`); for each of the four it has a declaration without hypotheses, or a derivation from such,
seen by its signature and not read. The bound is used in theorems that take the Siegel lemma, or these four, as
hypotheses. The key formula, the two uniformisations and the facts on theta functions are theorems of the source without
hypotheses.

## Key steps

- **Reduction to one tangent vector** (`GlobalTheory.T.slope_bound_of_line_large_degree`): the Siegel hypothesis turns
  a sub-lattice of large slope into a short integral vector over a finite extension; carried out in
  `TopSlope.slopeBound` (`src/Development/…/GeometryProofs/SlopeBoundOfLargeDegreeLine.lean`, line 94).
- **Theta section of bounded L²-norm** (`GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.Sl.keyDeg_of_keyBad`): the key formula and the
  integrality of its scalar give `mean_σ log‖θ‖₂ ≤ h⁺/2 + g`; integrality at bad reduction, which needs the action, is
  `PositivityTheory.HtPt.Kftwo.kp` (`src/Development/WeilBound/…/HodgeTatePoints/LieMultComp.lean`, line 159).
- **Sup norm from torsion values** (`GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.Sl.Sup.mean_log_sup`): values of `θ` at torsion
  points are integral, which also needs the action (`IntTors.integral_torsVal`,
  `src/Development/…/GeometryProofs/TorsionValueIntegral.lean`, line 727); this bounds `mean_σ ρ_σ⁻²` inside the proof.
- **Every vanishing order up to n/3** (`GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.Sl.Jump.lefschetz_direction`): a theta function
  of level `n` vanishing to order exactly `i` along a given direction, for each `i ≤ n/3`; this makes the rows of the
  jet matrix independent.
- **Integral jets and the filtered product formula** (`GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.FlagSlope.flag_slope`): only the
  first non-vanishing undivided derivative is integral (`JetMain.jets`,
  `src/Development/…/GeometryProofs/ExistsIntegralJetBasis.lean`, line 239); the criterion needs no more.
- **Choice of the power and assembly** (`GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.Sl.LineProof.line_of_key`): the power is
  `n = 9·⌈2 + g² + h⁺⌉²`; the logarithm comes from paying the norm of `θ` once for `n/3 + 1` vanishing orders.

## What was checked, and what was not

The main declaration is a theorem with a proof, of the implication above; the bound itself (`SlopeBoundSt`) is a
definition of a proposition, and no declaration proving it without the Siegel hypothesis was found. Read in full: the
reduction, `line_of_key`, `keyDeg_of_keyBad`, `mean_log_sup`, `flag_slope`, `lefschetz_direction`, the Cauchy estimate;
in their main parts: `jets`, `Basis.main`, `integral_torsVal`. Statements only: the key formula and its integrality at
bad reduction, both uniformisations, `nform`, the base-change lemmas, the derivation of the Siegel lemma. A text search
of the whole source found `sorry` only in `src/Challenge.lean` and no `axiom` declaration; nothing was compiled. The
form for every principally polarised abelian variety is not proved: `GlobalTheory.LieHt.LieSlope`, with a constant
`C(g)`, is a proposition derived from three statements taken as hypotheses. Non-emptiness of the class was not checked.
