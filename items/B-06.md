## Statement

Let `E/F` be a CM extension and `G = U(n, n)`. A Hermitian space of dimension `m` gives a Siegel–Weil section at
`s₀ = (m − n)/2`. The formal statements of B and of the first two cases of A are only for the CM fields of the standing
data of the proof: `E` is Galois over `ℚ` and embedded in a cyclotomic field, `F` is its maximal real subfield, and there
are further arithmetic conditions. The definite case of A, and C, are for any CM extension satisfying the stated
conditions on the base.

Statement A is the Siegel–Weil formula where the theta integral converges, in three cases. The first is `m > 2n`, with
all Schwartz data. The second is `n < m ≤ 2n` under Weil's convergence criterion, isotropic spaces included, with Fock
data at the real places. The third is for spaces definite at the real places, all `m, n ≥ 1` and `K`-finite data, under
conditions on the base, with the constant `2` when `m ≤ n`.

Statement B. Let `W` be a Hermitian space of rank `r ≥ 1`, let `t > r` and `n = r + t`. Assume that the Siegel
Eisenstein series of a Siegel–Weil section of `W ⊥ ℍ^t` has a continuation to a neighbourhood of `t/2`. This
continuation is a hypothesis of the statement, and it is proved separately. Then that series has at most a simple pole
at `t/2`, and its residue is annihilated by every finite measure carried by a relatively compact part of `G(𝔸)` that
annihilates all theta functions of `W`.

Statement C. Let `V` and `V′` be Hermitian spaces of dimensions `a > a′` of equal parity, and let `W ≅ V ⊕ V′(−1)` be a
non-split skew-Hermitian space. Under conditions on signatures, characters and the base, the theta integral of `V` is a
unique nonzero constant times that of `V′` on matched test vectors. In the Lean source C is obtained from two inputs that
are proved elsewhere.

A gives cases of theorems of Weil, Kudla–Rallis, Ichino and Yamana. B is weaker than the first term identity of the
regularised formula, and C is not a case of it.

## Context

In the published proofs, as we know them from later accounts, the regularised formula and its first term identity rest
on regularised theta integrals, and the simple pole rests on intertwining operators and degenerate principal series.
There is also a published proof of simple poles by Fourier–Jacobi descent, Proposition 7.2 of Ikeda (1996). B is what
the formal proof uses where the published route would quote the first term identity: together with a doubling zeta
integral that has a pole at `t/2`, it shows that some theta lift to `U(W)` of a form on a definite unitary group is
nonzero. C concerns a unitary group with no Siegel parabolic.

## Comparison with the literature

Our comparison rests more on statements and assemblies than on leaf proofs, and on later accounts of the original papers
rather than on the papers, so what follows is tentative for all three statements. A is a standard proof, specialised. In
Weil's range it goes by fibre measures and their uniqueness, with the constant fixed fibre by fibre by a dilation
argument in rank one, which as we recall is the shape of Siegel's proof. For `n < m ≤ 2n` it is the Kudla–Rallis
comparison of Fourier coefficients, with nonsingularity handled at a real place. For definite spaces with `m ≤ n` it is
an induction on `n`, which is not Yamana's route. B is narrower, and it is not a new proof of the first term identity. It
uses Fourier–Jacobi coefficients as in Ikeda and Wu, shortened by the assumption `t > r`, with a rank cut and uniqueness
of measures. In what we read we did not find a proof of the simple pole without intertwining operators, and we could not
settle the point without Ikeda (1996), which we did not read. C is not a case of a published identity, and an earlier
search found no such statement. Its proof combines published devices with a base case and a closing step unlike anything
we found in what we read.

A second, independent check regards A and B as known in spirit. For B it is surer of the overall architecture than of
the exact lemmas, and it points out that Ikeda's Proposition 7.2 proves simple poles by Fourier–Jacobi descent, though
not by this unitary cut. On C, and overall, it could not decide: that the group has no Siegel parabolic does not exclude
a derivation of C by doubling, seesaws or injectivity of theta lifting. So we and the second check disagree on the pole
in B and on C. We would like to know whether Ikeda's descent gives the pole in B, and whether C can be derived by
doubling, seesaws or injectivity of theta lifting.

## Sketch of the proof

For A in Weil's range, both sides are cut by rank, and Poisson summation over the Hermitian matrices turns the formula
into Siegel's formula for each Gram matrix `β`. For nondegenerate `β` the theta side is a `U(V)(𝔸)`-invariant locally
finite measure on the fibre `{Gram = β}`, hence a constant times the product of the local fibre measures. The constant is
`1` by induction on the rank, splitting off a rank-one block. In rank one, with `m ≥ 3`, for the lattices `a⁻¹L` the
theta measure of the fibre differs from the product of local densities by at most `C·a^{[F:ℚ]m}`, while that product is
at least `κ·a^{[F:ℚ](2m − 2)}` for infinitely many `a`. Degenerate `β` reduce to smaller rank, and we did not read this
part further.

For A with `n < m ≤ 2n`, the nondegenerate Fourier coefficients of the continued Eisenstein series at `s₀` are computed
by unfolding and equal those of the theta integral. The singular ones are removed at a real place. For the vacuum vector
of a suitable `K`-type, a compactly supported `ψ` on Hermitian matrices whose Fourier transform vanishes at singular
matrices, together with two group elements, gives an operator that multiplies the vacuum section by a nonzero constant.
So a function of that type with only singular coefficients vanishes. The first fundamental theorem for the unitary group
passes from the vacuum vector to all polynomial Fock vectors. For definite spaces, the case `n < m` is as above, the case
`m = n` comes from the nondegenerate coefficients with the factor `2`, and the case `m < n` is an induction on `n`. No
functional equation and no regularised formula are used.

B goes in six steps.
(1) The Fourier–Jacobi coefficient for a nondegenerate `b₀` of size `ρ`, paired with a theta function, is a Siegel
Eisenstein series on `U(n − ρ, n − ρ)`.
(2) For `ρ > r` it converges at `t/2`, so the polar coefficients have Fourier coefficients of rank `≤ r` only. For
`ρ = r` the group is `U(t, t)` at its edge of convergence, where a direct estimate bounds the spherical series by a
constant times `(σ − t/2)⁻¹`.
(3) At a split place a finite combination `C` of unipotent translations has Fourier transform vanishing in rank `< r`,
and, averaged over `K_v`, acts on `K_v`-invariant sections by an entire scalar `c(z)` with `c(t/2) ≠ 0`. This is a
positivity computation.
(4) The operator `C` kills functions whose coefficients all have rank `< r`, so descending induction removes all orders
`≥ 2`.
(5) The rank-`r` coefficients of the residue give a positive invariant measure on an orbit whose singular points are
null and on whose remaining points the group acts transitively, so it is unique up to a scalar. The theta side gives a
measure of the same kind, and the coefficient is `κ` times an orbital integral over `U(W)`, where `κ` is not shown to be
nonzero.
(6) Comparison of Fourier coefficients and weak approximation make the residue a multiple of a `K_v`-average of theta
integrals of `W`, and Fubini gives the annihilation.

For C we can describe the proof only as far as the names and dependencies of its assembly show it. We did not read the
leaf proofs, and we did not check the following steps against them. The two theta quotients of the trivial
representation are matched locally at every place, matched pairs sharing an irreducible quotient of the two local Weil
representations. Convergence comes from test vectors of full rank at one split place, and in the form used later this is
replaced by an element of the group algebra at an auxiliary place, with no auxiliary Eisenstein series. The `L²` pairing
factors through an irreducible quotient. Nonvanishing comes from the Rallis inner product formula, which uses A for
definite spaces, and is carried up the tower by constant terms. There is a base case with all groups compact at
infinity, where the back-lift of a difference is constant, hence zero, and an induction removing hyperbolic planes. No
multiplicity one for automorphic representations of `U(W)` is used.

## Key steps

- **Rank-one constant** (`CompanionTheory.PlacePermOperatorBicommutantSpan.sw_one`): in Weil's range, the constant `1`
  for a rank-one Gram matrix and `m ≥ 3`, by the dilation asymptotic. The induction on the rank starts here.
- **Vacuum test at a real place** (statement `PositivityTheory.Rg.Sw.LV`, proved as `PositivityTheory.Rg.Sw.lv`):
  smearing the vacuum section by `ψ` along the unipotent radical and projecting to its `K`-type gives a nonzero multiple
  of it. For `n < m ≤ 2n` this takes the place of the published use of an irreducible local module.
- **Polar part** (statement `PositivityTheory.Rg.Rsw.Polar`, proved as `PositivityTheory.Rg.Rsw.polar`): bounds, through
  Fourier–Jacobi coefficients, the rank of the Fourier coefficients of each polar coefficient at `t/2`, and describes
  the rank-`r` part of the residue. This is the source of the pole bound in B.
- **The rank cut** (statement `PositivityTheory.Rg.Rsw.L.JPos`, proved by
  `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Jp.jPos_of_idemEx`): the split-place operator with Fourier transform vanishing in low
  rank and nonzero scalar at `t/2`. It gives the simple pole without intertwining operators.
- **Uniqueness on the orbit** (statement `PositivityTheory.Rg.Rsw.L.Uniq`, proved by
  `GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.OrbUniq.uniq_of_trans`): an invariant measure of the right homogeneity on the
  nonsingular orbit is unique up to a scalar. It matches the rank-`r` part of the residue with theta integrals of `W`.
- **Assembly of C** (`PositivityTheory.Sph.Tr.closeSph`, with `PositivityTheory.ThKG.Leaves`): combines matching,
  convergence, nonvanishing, the base case and the tower steps. We read it only at this level.

## Caveats

We read A at the level of statements and assemblies, and read in full the rank-one dilation lemma, the vacuum test and
the abstract theorem behind it. For B we read the statement and all its leaf statements, and their proofs in outline.
C we read only at the level of its assembly. We compiled nothing. No `sorry` or `axiom` occurs in the directories we
read, over 2,600 files. The first term identity appears in the source only as unproved statements and as hypotheses of
conditional lemmas, and we did not verify whether the main theorems depend on any of these declarations. A statement
for every anisotropic space exists in the source only as a definition and as a hypothesis, namely as a field of
hypothesis bundles and as an argument of conditional theorems, and we found no proof of it. We did not open Weil
(1965), Kudla–Rallis (1988 I, II; 1994), Ichino (2004, 2007), Ikeda (1994, 1996), Yamana (2014) or Tan's paper on the
poles for `U(n, n)`, so our comparison for A and B rests on later papers' descriptions of them. The second,
independent reader checked Ikeda (1996).
