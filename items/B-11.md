## Statement

Let `X` be an integral closed subscheme of a product of projective spaces `∏ ℙ^{n_k}_ℤ`, flat over `ℤ`, of absolute
dimension `d ≥ 1`, not assumed regular or normal, and consider the bundles `O(a)` with metrics smooth on the ambient
product. There is a unique real function `I(X; L̄_1, …, L̄_d)`, additive in the last factor, with the following
property: for every bundle `Ā` of multidegree `a ≥ 0` with a smooth metric and every form `s` of multidegree `a` not
vanishing on `X`, `I(X; L̄_1, …, L̄_{d−1}, Ā)` equals `Σ_{W horizontal} m_W·I(W; L̄_1, …, L̄_{d−1})` plus
`Σ_{W vertical} m_W·deg_W(L_1, …, L_{d−1})·log p_W` minus `∫_{X(ℂ)} log‖s‖·c_1(L̄_1)⋯c_1(L̄_{d−1})`. This function is
symmetric and multilinear.

This is the height of Faltings and Bost–Gillet–Soulé, with several bundles, for these subschemes. It is a published
theorem (for one bundle, Theorem 1 of Soulé's 2017 notes), narrower than Gillet–Soulé's arithmetic Chow rings, whose
role it plays here. In the Lean source the four parts of this statement are not brought together by a declaration
without hypotheses (see Caveats).

The source also states an arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem of its own. Let `X ⊂ ℙ^N_ℤ` have absolute dimension
`D + 1 ≥ 2`, with coordinates linearly independent over `ℤ`, and give `O(1)` a smooth semipositive metric invariant
under complex conjugation. Then `−log covol` of the lattice of restrictions to `X` of forms of degree `n`, in the `L²`
norm on a resolution, is `Ō(1)^{D+1}·n^{D+1}/(D+1)! + o(n^{D+1})`. In this form it is a proposition with a derivation
from thirteen further named statements, and no declaration proves it without hypotheses. We found by search that each
of the thirteen is obtained elsewhere in the source, inside the proof of the corresponding statement for a product of
projective spaces, and the source derives that statement from two named statements which it proves separately.
One-sided counts of small sections are closed theorems.

## Context

The formal proof uses these numbers in place of arithmetic Chow rings, and their construction needs no regularity of
the model. As far as its statements and their assembly show, the source's Hilbert–Samuel argument uses neither
arithmetic Riemann–Roch nor analytic torsion, and its derivation of Moriwaki's inequality does not use the continuity of
the volume. The Hilbert–Samuel theorem and Moriwaki's inequality serve two one-sided implications of Zhang's theorem on
successive minima.

## Comparison with the literature

For the intersection numbers the statement is narrower than Gillet–Soulé's, and it is proved by the standard elementary
argument, specialised. The proof follows the steps of Soulé's notes: uniqueness by induction, dimension one by the
product formula for an order, an auxiliary section, commutativity of two divisors through lengths, and archimedean
symmetry by Poincaré–Lelong and Stokes on a resolution. Where the notes argue for the analytic step "at least
formally", the formal proof justifies it by the method of Chambert-Loir–Thuillier. We read Soulé's notes and the main
Lean proofs in full.

For the Hilbert–Samuel theorem the route differs from Gillet–Soulé's, and the change of metric is Berman–Boucksom's
argument, a known alternative. For the arithmetic half (projection to `ℙ^D` with traces, the index of a change of
lattice, doubling by the second Veronese embedding) we could not determine how it relates to published proofs. We read
that part of the Lean only as statements, and we did not read Abbes–Bouche or Zhang, so this is not a finding that the
argument is absent from print. The argument for Zhang's upper implication looks standard to us, with the same
limitation. For the lower implication and for Moriwaki's inequality we could not determine how the arguments relate to
the published ones. Only one check was made: no second, independent reader has looked at this statement or at the
comparison. We would like to know whether this arithmetic half of the Hilbert–Samuel argument is in Abbes–Bouche, in
Zhang or elsewhere, and whether a proof of Moriwaki's inequality by perturbation and counting, without the continuity of
the volume, is in print.

## Sketch of the proof

Objects. `X` is given by a multihomogeneous prime `P` of `ℤ[x_{k,i}]` with `P ∩ ℤ = 0`. A bundle is a multidegree `a`
with a positive function `h` homogeneous of degree `a`, and `‖s‖ = |s|/h`. The archimedean term is defined through a
linear projection onto `ℙ^{d−1}` that is finite on `X(ℂ)`: one integrates over the chart the sum over the fibre of the
integrand (in the recursion, `log‖s‖`) times the mixed Monge–Ampère density of the `log h_j` along local sheets.
Independence of the projection comes from a resolution of singularities. The components of `div s` are the minimal
primes of `P + (s)`, with lengths as multiplicities. A vertical one over `p` enters with its geometric degree in the
fibre, defined by its own recursion, times `log p`.

Construction and proof.

1. `I(X; L̄, Ā)` is defined by recursion on the number of factors, as the right side of the recursion for `Ā ⊗ H̄^m`
   along a form `t` minus that for `H̄^m` along a form `c`, with `H̄ = O(1, …, 1)` and Fubini–Study metrics.
2. In each dimension three facts are proved together: the right side does not depend on the section, the recursion
   holds for every section, and `I` is additive in the last factor.
3. In dimension one `X` is the spectrum of an order in a number field, and the claim is the product formula
   `log #(O/fO) = Σ_σ log|σ(f)|`.
4. In higher dimension, prime avoidance gives auxiliary forms meeting `div s` and `div t` properly, and independence
   follows from step 5.
5. For sections `s`, `s′` whose divisors share no component, the recursion along `s` with `Ā′` among the other factors
   equals that along `s′` with `Ā`. Algebraically, `div(s)·div(s′) = div(s′)·div(s)`: in a two-dimensional noetherian
   local domain both sides are `ℓ(A/(a, b))` minus the length of the `b`-torsion of `A/a`, which is symmetric.
   Analytically, the archimedean expression is symmetric.
6. For the analytic symmetry, the other factors are made semipositive by additivity and a twist, and `log‖s′‖` is
   replaced by `½·log(‖s′‖² + ε)`. Then Poincaré–Lelong and Stokes for smooth functions apply. The curvature of the
   regularisation is bounded below uniformly in `ε`, so the integrals are monotone, and `ε → 0` by dominated
   convergence. This gives `≤`, and exchanging the sections gives equality.
7. Symmetry follows from step 5 with sections in general position, multilinearity from symmetry and additivity, and
   uniqueness by induction with coordinate monomials.

Hilbert–Samuel, which we read as statements and their assembly.

1. A finite linear projection `X → ℙ^D` with trace maps compares the lattices of `X` with those of `ℙ^D`, and the
   intersection numbers by the projection formula.
2. A change of coordinates over `ℚ` changes the lattice by an index growing like a difference of intersection numbers.
3. A change of metric changes `log covol` by the Monge–Ampère energy: derivative against the Bergman function, upper
   bound by the sub-mean inequality on polydiscs of radius `R/√n`, total mass the rank, `L¹` convergence.
4. For `ℙ^D`, steps 1 to 3 applied to the second Veronese embedding give `δ(2n) − 2^D·δ(n) = o(n^{D+1})` for the defect
   `δ`, and, with bounds on the increments, `δ(n) = o(n^{D+1})`.

Moriwaki's inequality comes from this count for an ample perturbation and a fixed small section, without the continuity
of the volume. Zhang's two implications use it, the lower one through an induction on the dimension of Kleiman's type.

## Key steps

- **Construction** (`GroundTheory.Heights₂.Heights.Inter.ThmIExists.fam`): the numbers are defined by recursion on the
  number of factors, as a difference of two right-hand sides along arbitrary forms of nonnegative multidegree. All the
  work is in showing that this is independent of the choices.
- **Independence of the section** (`GroundTheory.Heights₂.Heights.Inter.WellDefined.proof`): prime avoidance for
  multihomogeneous forms and the exchange of two sections, applied twice. We read it in full.
- **Exchange of two sections** (`GroundTheory.Heights₂.Heights.Inter.Swap.of_cycles`): an implication. It expands both
  sides once more and reduces the exchange to three named statements: the cut formula for the degrees of vertical
  components, the commutativity of divisors (`GroundTheory.Heights₂.Heights.Inter.SwapCycles.full`, which rests on a
  length identity in a two-dimensional local domain that we read as a statement only) and the archimedean symmetry.
- **Archimedean inequality** (`GroundTheory.Heights₂.Heights.Inter.DeLeSemiPos.proof`): an implication from seven
  named statements, among them the Poincaré–Lelong formula, Stokes' formula for smooth functions, the uniform curvature
  bound and the limit `ε → 0`. Its own argument is the regularisation of `log‖s′‖` and the passage to the limit, and
  equality then follows by antisymmetry. We read it in full.
- **Change of metric** (`ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.G.Geo.metricChange`): tensor with the trivial bundle and
  apply the recursion to the section `1`.
- **Projective space by doubling**
  (`ReciprocityTheory.GeometryOfNumbersTheory.hilbertSamuelDefect_projectiveSpace_isLittleO`): an implication. If the
  Hilbert–Samuel defect `δ` of `ℙ^D`, formed with any real number in the place of the height of `ℙ^D`, satisfies
  `δ(2n) − 2^D·δ(n) = o(n^{D+1})`, then `δ(n) = o(n^{D+1})`. The doubling relation is a hypothesis here. In the
  assembly it comes from the comparison with the second Veronese embedding, and the height of `ℙ^D` is not computed. We
  read it as a statement only.

## Caveats

We read in full all definitions and statements of the intersection numbers, and the proofs of the construction, the
independence of the section, the exchange of two sections, the archimedean inequality and `thmIExists`. The length
lemma, dimension one, symmetry in the bundles and the projection formula we read as statements, with their assembly. We
did not check the published inputs that are proved inside the source: resolution of singularities, local
Poincaré–Lelong and Stokes, and independence of the projection. A search of the three folders of the construction found
no `sorry` or `axiom`. Nothing was compiled.

The four parts of the main statement are not brought together by a declaration without hypotheses. Uniqueness has one
(`GroundTheory.Heights₂.Heights.Inter.ThmIUnique.full`). Existence is `thmIExists`, an implication from a
Poincaré–Lelong statement and a resolution statement, each of which has a declaration without hypotheses elsewhere in
the source (`PositivityTheory.Tls2.Pl.poincareLelong`, and `PositivityTheory.ResPlug.FromRes.resK` with
`GlobalTheory.T.resolution_exists_of_resolution_statement`). The function it produces is symmetric and additive in the
last factor by the definition of an intersection family. For the symmetry and the multilinearity of an arbitrary
function satisfying the recursion, and for the conjunction of the four parts, a search found only implications from
further named statements: `GroundTheory.Heights₂.Heights.Inter.ThmISymm.proof`,
`GroundTheory.Heights₂.Heights.Inter.ThmIMultilin.proof`, and `GroundTheory.Heights₂.Heights.Inter.TheoremI.of_remaining`,
the last from seven.

We read the Hilbert–Samuel chain (about 800 files), Moriwaki's inequality and the Kleiman-type step only as
statements. For the `L²` Hilbert–Samuel statement a search found a derivation from thirteen named statements
(`GroundTheory.Heights₂.Heights.HilbertSamuel.TheoremHSM.of_open`), applied nowhere in the source, and no declaration
proving the statement without hypotheses. Each of the thirteen is obtained inside the proof of
`PositivityTheory.LocalParametersCuspidal.MB.HSM.theoremHSMBlock`, which derives the corresponding statement for a
product of projective spaces (`GroundTheory.Heights₂.Heights.HilbertSamuel.TheoremHSMBlock.Statement`) from two named
statements, the system of intersection numbers and a resolution statement. Ten of the thirteen come from declarations
whose signatures have no hypotheses. The other three (the projection formula for coordinate projections and two
change-of-model statements) come from those two statements, which are supplied without hypotheses where the lower bound
for the arithmetic volume is proved. We established this from the signatures, by search, and did not read the proofs
below them. The lower bound for the arithmetic volume and the upper bound for the count of small sections (when the
monomials of the degree of the bundle have norm at most `1`) are closed theorems with no hypotheses. A two-sided count
for nef bundles is stated, but we did not find it proved outright. We did not examine heights of Chow forms.

We did not open the papers of Faltings (1991), Zhang (1995), Gillet–Soulé (1990, 1992), Deligne (1987), Gubler (2003)
and Elkik on heights and arithmetic intersection theory, and we read Bost–Gillet–Soulé and Abbes–Bouche only in
abstract.
