## Statement

Let `E` be a totally complex number field and `V` a nonzero space of automorphic forms on `GL_N(𝔸_E)`, irreducible under
`GL_N(𝔸_f)`, `K_∞` and the Lie algebra, invariant under the split centre and square-integrable modulo it. Suppose that
at one embedding `τ : E → ℂ` the Capelli elements act on `V` through `N` complex numbers that are pairwise distinct with
pairwise integral differences. Then `N = ad` and there are a unitary cuspidal representation `π` of `GL_a(𝔸_E)` and an
injective equivariant map from `V` into the forms induced from `π|det|^{−(d−1)/2} ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ π|det|^{(d−1)/2}`, exponents
ascending, so that the Speh representation appears as a subrepresentation. The proof also obtains the local components
of `V` at every finite place (local Speh representations; `PositivityTheory.Rg.Rs.ascSeed_holds2`) and its infinitesimal
character (`PositivityTheory.Rg.Rs.infAsc_holds`). This is a special case (totally complex fields, regular integral
infinitesimal character at one place) of the exhaustion half of the theorem of Mœglin and Waldspurger, in the form
"cuspidal support and exponents". Not proved: the case without regularity, the existence of the residual representations
for `d > 1`, multiplicity one, and that `V` is a residue of Eisenstein series.

## Why it might matter

Mœglin and Waldspurger obtain the discrete spectrum from Langlands' spectral decomposition, with intertwining operators
normalised by `L`- and `ε`-factors of pairs. In the part that was read, the formal proof finds the cuspidal support it
needs from constant terms and the square-integrability criterion instead, and its proof of the Jacquet–Shalika rigidity
for isobaric sums contains no non-vanishing statement on `Re s = 1`.

## What the checks found

The first reading (medium confidence) finds that the argument differs from Mœglin and Waldspurger's in the essential
step: cuspidal support and exponents come not from residues of Eisenstein series and intertwining operators but from
constant terms along maximal parabolics, both halves of the square-integrability criterion, an induction through an
extremal exponent, unitarity, the Jacquet–Shalika classification, a local unitarity inequality and the regular integral
infinitesimal character; it is not a proof of the theorem in general. Rigidity: the Jacquet–Shalika proof with Landau's
theorem in place of Shahidi's non-vanishing at `s = 1`; whether that variant is in print it could not tell. This rests
on the assembly files read in full, part of Mœglin–Waldspurger, the relevant Jacquet–Shalika pages and two searches; a
proof of the regular case by other known means may exist (recollection, unchecked). The second opinion cannot tell
(medium confidence): the ingredients are standard, the extremal-exponent induction could be an idea of its own but
priority is unverified, and positivity in place of non-vanishing is known in spirit, notably from Brumley. It finds the
first reading's account of Jacquet's paper incomplete: that paper has both directions of the exponent criterion and
partial exhaustion results. So they disagree: the first reports a route different from the published proof compared, the
second finds novelty not established. Open: do Jacquet's 1983 paper or the unitary-dual classifications give this case;
is the extremal-exponent induction in print?

## How the formal proof goes

Strong induction on `N`. (1) Either all constant terms of `V` along the maximal parabolics `P_{r,N−r}` vanish and `V` is
cuspidal (`d = 1`), or some constant term has an exponent `c`, an eigencharacter `e^{ct}` of `diag(e^t I_r, I_{N−r})` at
infinity; the exponent sets are finite. (2) Necessity half of the square-integrability criterion: for a
square-integrable form every exponent has `Re c < ρ_P`, proved directly by an `L²` decay estimate for the constant term
from a lattice-point count. (3) Choose `(r, c)` minimising `(ρ_P − Re c)/(r(N−r))`, and let `λ > 0` be the minimum: for
every exponent `c′` along `P_{m,N−m}` one has `Re c′ − ρ_P ≤ −λ·m(N−m)`, with equality at `(r, c)`. (4) The
`c`-eigenpart of the constant term along `P_{r,s}` is a nonzero, hence injective, equivariant map on `V`; its
restrictions to `GL_r × GL_s` are automorphic forms whose exponents are exponents of `V`. (5) By concavity, after
central twists `|det|^{−z₁}`, `|det|^{−z₂}` with `r z₁ + s z₂ = 0`, these restrictions have only negative exponents,
hence are square-integrable by the sufficiency half of the criterion (proved by bootstrapping growth bounds on Siegel
sets). (6) Projecting to irreducible summands gives discrete `V₁` on `GL_r` and `V₂` on `GL_s`, and an embedding of `V`
into the forms induced from `V₁|det|^{z₁} ⊗ V₂|det|^{z₂}`. (7) Infinitesimal characters add, so `V₁`, `V₂` are again
regular and integral at `τ`, and by induction each embeds in the forms induced from a segment of some `(π_i, d_i)`. (8)
The string step, where regularity is used: at almost all places the Satake multiset of `V` is the union of the shifted
multisets of `π₁` and `π₂`. (a) Unitarity makes them stable under `b ↦ b̄^{−1}`; with rigidity for isobaric sums the
bottom exponent of the first string cannot be matched inside it, so `π₁ ≅ π₂` with real shifts. (b) The infinitesimal
character at `τ` forces the two exponent strings to be disjoint with integral differences. (c) A local inequality for
unitary spherical representations (in the Satake multiset a parameter `z` with `|z| < q^{−1/2}` has multiplicity at most
that of `qz`), with the Jacquet–Shalika bound `q^{−1/2} < |β| < q^{1/2}`, closes the exponent set under `u ↦ u − 1` for
`u ≥ 1`. (d) A disjoint union of two unit-step strings, symmetric under negation and closed under that step, with
centres `c₁ < 0 < c₂` and `d₁c₁ + d₂c₂ = 0`, is one string. (9) Induction in stages gives the embedding for
`(π₁, d₁ + d₂)`.

The local inequality is proved directly: the `L²` inner product makes the local component unitary, and vectors attached
to the subspaces of `k^{n+1}` (`k` the residue field) with one translation operator give a finite-dimensional space on
which an operator is expanding for the invariant form; a polynomial identity gives the inequality.

Rigidity for isobaric sums. Each cuspidal class is a unitary one twisted by `|det|^t`, `t` real; the inputs are
`|α| ≤ q^{1/2}`, `|det| = 1` and analyticity of `(s−1)^{δ} L^S(s, x × ȳ)` (`δ = 1` if `x = y`, `0` otherwise) at real
`s > 0`, from Rankin–Selberg integrals. Landau's theorem gives convergence of `log L^S(σ, x × x̄)` for `σ > 1`,
Cauchy–Schwarz the rest. For a family, `Σ_{i,j} log L^S(σ, x_i × x̄_j)` is unbounded as `σ → 1⁺`: otherwise Landau gives
convergence for `σ > 0`, and a lower bound from Newton's identities contradicts `Σ q_w^{−1} = ∞`. Two families with
equal power sums of Satake parameters and least exponent `0` share a member, since otherwise Landau's theorem bounds the
exponent-zero block at `σ = 1`, against the unboundedness; remove it and induct. No non-vanishing on `Re s = 1` is used.

## Key steps

- **Extremal exponent** (`PositivityTheory.Rg.Rs.PAscAll.exists_extremal`): chooses `(r, c)` minimising
  `(ρ_P − Re c)/(r(N−r))`, so that all exponents lie under a concave curve through it; this choice makes the twisted
  restrictions square-integrable.
- **Both halves of the criterion** (`PositivityTheory.Rg.Rs.expNeg_holds`, `PositivityTheory.Rg.Rs.sqIntCrit_holds`):
  exponents of a square-integrable form are negative (`Re c < ρ_P`), and a form with only negative exponents is
  square-integrable; proved from a lattice-point count and from growth bounds on Siegel sets.
- **String step** (`PositivityTheory.Rg.Rs.stringStep_of`, an implication whose hypotheses are supplied in
  `PositivityTheory.Rg.Rs.ascAll_holds`): the two segments under the embedding join into one; uses rigidity, the
  Jacquet–Shalika bound, the infinitesimal character at `τ` and the local inequality.
- **Local unitarity inequality** (`PositivityTheory.Rg.Rs.gap_holds`): for unitary spherical representations, a Satake
  parameter `z` with `|z| < q^{−1/2}` has multiplicity at most that of `qz`; proved directly. The second opinion notes
  that it follows from Tadić's classification.
- **Unboundedness by positivity** (`PositivityTheory.RgJS.dirichlet_series_unbounded`, an implication whose hypotheses
  are supplied in `PositivityTheory.RgJS.absRig_closed`): the double sum of logarithms of partial Rankin–Selberg
  `L`-functions is unbounded as `σ → 1⁺`; it plays the part of non-vanishing at `s = 1`.

## What was checked, and what was not

Read in full: the assembly files (the induction, slice, chord, projection, string step, the gluing of the local
inequality, the two assembly files of the rigidity proof) and every statement they combine; of the two positivity
lemmas, the main proofs. Read only as statements: the supporting proofs, among them the Rankin–Selberg input (about
12,500 lines), the Jacquet–Shalika bound, the operator proof of the local inequality and the analytic estimates behind
the criterion; many lemma names do not describe their content, so the reading went by statements. Nothing was compiled
and no axiom list was produced; a word search for `sorry`, `axiom`, `admit` and `native_decide` in those directories
found nothing. No proof was found of a bundled statement that also lists uniqueness of `(d, π)`. Not opened by the first
reading: Jacquet's paper, the Mœglin–Waldspurger book (for the criterion), Shahidi's paper, Tadić's classification; not
read, §§III–IV and I.4–I.11 of Mœglin–Waldspurger.
