## Statement

The data are those of the proof: a CM field `E` (a special abelian one), a prime `l` (`2` allowed), a DVR `O₀` finite
over `ℤ_l`, an odd `r ≥ 100`, an odd `t ≥ r + 5`, and finitely many `T_s = ρ_s ⊗ χ_s⁻¹` with `ρ_s : G_E → GL_r(O₀)`
continuous. The statement gives one character `ψ` whose twists of all the `T_s` have short Selmer groups.

For each `s` we assume the following. The image `ρ̄_s(G_E)` spans the matrix algebra, and `T_s^†∘c ≅ T_s ⊗ χ_cyc^t`,
where `†` is the Tate dual and `c` complex conjugation. `ρ_s` is unramified outside `l` and two primes `g_i`, and at
`g_i ≠ l` wild inertia is trivial on `ρ̄_s` and an inertia element has no eigenvalue `1` on `T̄_s`. Above `l`, `T_s`
is de Rham, potentially crystalline, without Hodge–Tate jump in `[−1, t]`, and `dim D_dR/Fil⁰` at `τ` and at `τ∘c` add
up to `r`. No decomposition group fixes a nonzero vector of `T_s[1/l]` or of its Tate dual. Finally `χ_s = δ^{±1}` on
inertia above a third prime, with `δ` of order `15` there, so that an inertia element `z` acts on each `T̄_s` by a
scalar `ϑ_s` with `ϑ_s² ≠ 1`.

Then for every `ε′ > 0` and every finite set `A` of places there are a finite set `Q` of degree-one places, split over
`E⁺`, outside `A`, and a character `ψ` of `G_E` of `l`-power order, anticyclotomic, trivial on the decomposition
groups above `l` and ramified exactly at `Q`, such that `length Sel_Q(T_s ⊗ ψ^a ⊗ E′/O′) ≤ (ν + ε_s)·length(O′/l)`
over a DVR `O′ ⊇ O₀` with fraction field `E′`, for all `s` and all `a` prime to `l`. Here `Σ_s ε_s ≤ ε′`, and `ν` is
maximal with `χ_cyc^{tr} ≡ 1 (mod l^ν)`. The Selmer group `Sel_Q` has the Bloch–Kato condition above `l`, no condition
at `Q`, at the `g_i` and at the third prime, and the unramified condition elsewhere. The Lean statement has further
hypotheses and clauses, described at the start of the sketch below.

In the Lean source this is an implication from four named propositions, for the special CM field and lattices of the
formal proof. We did not find a declaration that gives the conclusion without hypotheses.

## Context

The statement bears on how short the Selmer groups of twists of a conjugate self-dual lattice can be made by one
tamely ramified character of `l`-power order. It is derived, from four named propositions, for the special CM field
and lattices of the formal proof, not for an arbitrary CM field. The bound comes from Galois cohomology and class
field theory, without L-values or Euler systems, and it covers `l = 2`, several lattices at once and all prime-to-`l`
powers of the character. In the formal proof the same character must also satisfy conditions on automorphic objects.

## Comparison with the literature

We compared the literature with a wider statement: any CM field, abstract lattices with a local condition above `l`
and a scalar element, and at most `4R` ramified places. The statement here concerns the proof's own data, namely a
special abelian CM field, odd `r ≥ 100`, odd `t ≥ r + 5` and three auxiliary primes. It comes from four named
propositions and does not bound the number of ramified places. It lies inside the range we compared, but our searches
aimed at the wider statement, and descriptions of it such as "any rank", "any CM field" or "only irreducibility plus a
scalar" do not fit the statement here. We found nothing in print that implies the wider statement or follows from it.
Mazur and Rubin obtain vanishing twisted Selmer groups for abelian varieties, but at `t = 0`, which is excluded here,
and with an element of one-dimensional coinvariants. Their vanishing is for the strict condition at the ramified
primes and does not bound the larger relaxed Selmer group used here, so their result is not simply stronger, and
conversely a small normalised length, as here, does not imply vanishing. A preprint of October 2026 uses the same
device in rank `2` at `t = 0`. The nearest later work we found, by Smith, by Assim and Movahhedi, and by Kriz and
Nordentoft, does not change this.

An independent second check agrees, and regards the statement as a technical lemma limited by its fields, ranks,
weights, ramification and named hypotheses. That check did not verify the preprint. We cannot rule out that something
closer is in print, because some of the searches we planned were not run and some of the comparisons rest on abstracts
only. We would like to know whether such a twist is published for conjugate self-dual lattices of higher rank with
`t ≠ 0`, and whether Smith's conditions can hold for them.

## Sketch of the proof

The proposition concluded is `admissibleIndexBoundAllPowers`. The main theorem derives it from four hypotheses that
are named propositions. The first is the Galois-side statement below. The second, `AdmACD`, contains the properties of
the `T_s`, Bloch–Kato duality and dimension statements for the period rings, and statements on automorphic objects.
The other two, `DelUTie` and `PropABCD`, link characters and polynomials to the automorphic side. The `s` are those
marked 'good' there. The theorem also assumes `g_i ≥ 2r` and an index condition, and, for the automorphic clauses, two
conditions on group-ring elements and a condition (`ABHyp`) on a family of lattices of forms. Above `l`, `ρ_s`
satisfies the same conditions as `T_s`, and both are crystalline if `l ≠ g_i`. The conclusion also has
`t·Σ_{P∈Q} v_l(ψ(σ_P) − 1) ≤ ε′`, where `σ_P` is an inertia element at `P` with `ψ(σ_P) ≠ 1` of least valuation, and
two clauses on automorphic objects.

*Galois side* (`galois_twisted_selmer_auxprimes_of_matrix_and_bounds`). For finitely many `T_s` there are `R`, `D` and
`M₀` such that at every level `M ≥ M₀` and for every finite `A` there are `R` first-half and `R` second-half places
avoiding `A`, and for each `s` a polynomial `F` over `O₀` in `2R` variables with
`length Sel_Q(T ⊗ ψ_u⁻¹ ⊗ E′/O′)/length(O′/l) = v_l(F(u − 1))` for all `u ∈ μ_{l^M}^{2R}`, where `ψ_u = ∏ u_q^{d_q}`,
and with a coefficient of valuation `≤ v(l^ν)` in degree `≤ D`. The `d_q : G_E → ℤ/l^M` are anticyclotomic, trivial
above `l`, and ramified exactly at `q` and `cq` (class field theory). `F` lifts `det Φ`, where `Φ` is a square matrix
over `Λ = O₀[(ℤ/l^M)^{2R}]` with `Sel_Q(T ⊗ κ⁻¹ ⊗ B) ≅ ker(Φ | B^a)` naturally in torsion `B`, and `κ = ∏[d_q]`. Such
a `Φ` exists because this functor is left exact with bounded defect of exactness (Poitou–Tate, Euler characteristics,
the `Fil⁰` condition). The places have norm `≡ 1 (mod 2l^M)`. First-half Frobenius elements are conjugate to `z`
modulo an open normal subgroup `L`, which is stable under `c` and trivial on `T̄_s` and modulo which `z` is central,
so local cohomology with torsion coefficients vanishes there. Second-half decomposition groups act trivially on
`T̄_s`, and `d_b(Frob_a) = δ_ab` across the halves.

*Frobenius classes* (`chooseH`). A cocycle vanishing on `L` is a coboundary, since `z` is a scalar `≠ 1` that is
central modulo `L`. So classes in `S = Sel_∅(T̄_s)` and `S′ = Sel_∅(T̄_s^†)` are equivariant homomorphisms on `L`. For
`h ∈ L` put `B_h(x, y) = ⟨x(h), y(h)⟩ − ⟨x(chc⁻¹), y(chc⁻¹)⟩`. If `T̄_s ≇ T̄_s^†∘c`, the `B_h` span all bilinear forms
on `S × S′` (`span_abstract`, which uses polarisation, the eigenvalues `ϑ ≠ ϑ⁻¹` of `z`, Burnside's theorem and inner
automorphisms of matrix algebras). As `dim S = dim S′` (an order formula), some combination has zero left kernel. If
`T̄_s ≅ T̄_s^†∘c`, then `ν ≥ 1` by determinants, vanishing combinations are symmetric (`r` odd), and an alternating
form on the sum of `S` and the transported `S′` has left kernel of dimension `≤ 1`. Chebotarev makes these `h`
second-half Frobenius classes.

*Residual bound* (`resBound`). Suppose that `Σ_a θ_a B_{φ_a}`, over the second-half Frobenius elements, has left
kernel of dimension `≤ e ∈ {0, 1}`. A lift of `Φ` is restricted to a line `X_q = b_q Y` over a field `K` containing
the residue field `k` of `O₀`, with a formal parameter `σ` in `b`, and read over the dual numbers. A first-order
kernel vector is then a Selmer class `ω` for the character `1 + εμ`, `μ = −Σ b_q d_q`. The tame relation
`μ(γ)ω₀(φ) = μ(φ)ω₀(γ)` for `ω₀ = ω mod ε` at second-half places, expanded in `σ`, shows that the leading part is
unramified at `Q`, so lies in `S`, and reciprocity (the sum of local invariants and the tame pairing) puts it in the
left kernel. So at most `e` independent residual classes lift to first order, and `dim Sel_Q(T̄_s) ≤ 2rR + e`. Linear
algebra over `K[Y]` then shows that `det` (`e = 0`) or an adjugate entry (`e = 1`) is not divisible by `Y^{4rR+1}`,
and as `l^M > 4rR + 2` a lift has a unit coefficient in degree `≤ 4rR`.

This is the claim if `ν = 0`. If `ν ≥ 1`, which is always the case when `l = 2`, `thmC_of_parts` inducts over the
lattices. All first-half places are chosen at once, one unpaired per lattice, and `step` adds, for each lattice in
turn, a second-half place paired with that one. Suppose that no low coefficient of the determinant lies outside
`𝔞 = λ l^ν O₀`, where `λ` is a uniformiser of `O₀`. Then the presentation is evaluated in a finite local
`O₀/𝔞`-algebra `A` with a nilpotent `τ`. The determinant vanishes, an adjugate column is a Selmer class `x`, and a
count gives a dual class `y` not killed by `l^ν τ^m`. So `B_h(x, y) ≠ 0` for some `h` in a small subgroup, Chebotarev
gives a place with Frobenius in the class of `h`, and over an extension of `A` by two nilpotent variables the new
determinant is nonzero (`det_ne_zero`). `compare` keeps the earlier bounds.

*Specialisation* (`order_eval`). Suppose that for `M ≥ M₀` we are given finitely many polynomials with a coefficient
of valuation `≤ μ` in degree `≤ D`, and some of a second kind, from the automorphic side. Then there are `M`, `k ≤ M`
and `e_q < k` with `l^{e_q}/φ(l^k) ≤ ε` such that all values at `(ζ^{l^{e_q}} − 1)_q` are nonzero, of normalised
valuation at most the level plus `ε`, for every primitive `l^k`-th root of unity `ζ`. The proof substitutes
`X_q ↦ (1 + Y)^{l^{e_q}} − 1` with widely spaced `e_q`, and then all but boundedly many `k` work. The main theorem
takes `ε` proportional to `ε′` and `ψ = ∏ ζ^{l^{e_q} d_q}`. Here `ζ^a` is again primitive, and `#Q ≤ 4R`. The inputs,
all named hypotheses, are Chebotarev, Poitou–Tate, cohomological dimension 2, tame quotients, local duality,
unramified orthogonality, finiteness, Euler characteristics, ray class characters and Bloch–Kato.

## Key steps

- **Choice of Frobenius classes** (`GlobalTheory.ChooseForms.chooseH`): elements `h_a ∈ L` whose classes, taken as
  second-half Frobenius classes, give a combination of the `B_h` with left kernel of dimension `0` (if `ν = 0`) or
  `≤ 1`. Irreducibility and the scalar `z` enter in `span_abstract` (`CocycleFormsSpanOddResidueChar.lean`, line 96).
- **Residual determinant bound** (`GlobalTheory.ResOrder.resBound`): a unit coefficient in degree `≤ 4rR` of a lift of
  the determinant, or of an adjugate entry. The argument is in `core` (same file, line 118), by first-order
  deformation along a line with a formal parameter, the tame relation and reciprocity.
- **One more place** (`GlobalTheory.NewPrime.step`): for `ν ≥ 1`, it passes from a unit coefficient of an adjugate
  entry to a coefficient of the determinant outside `λ l^ν O₀`, after adding one second-half place. The enlarged
  determinant is shown to be nonzero in `det_ne_zero` (line 85 of the same file), and the factor `l^ν` enters through
  `exists_bform_ne_zero`.
- **Persistence** (`GlobalTheory.Growth.compare`): when a second-half place is added, `u^{2r}·det Φ` lies in the ideal
  of `det Φ′` restricted to the old variables, modulo `λ l^ν`, for a polynomial `u` without constant term and with a
  unit coefficient on the paired variable, and likewise for adjugate entries. The induction over the lattices needs
  this.
- **Specialisation** (`GlobalTheory.OrderEval.order_eval`): one level `M`, one order `l^k` and exponents `e_q` at
  which finitely many polynomials of the two kinds take nonzero values of valuation at most their level plus `ε`, for
  every primitive `l^k`-th root of unity. It turns coefficient bounds into one character and gives all prime-to-`l`
  powers.

## Caveats

The main declaration is a proved theorem. The statement it proves is introduced by a definition, and its hypotheses
are four named propositions. The Galois-side one is proved by theorems of the same form whose only hypotheses are the
inputs listed above. We did not trace the other three. `AdmACD` contains the properties of the `T_s` given in the
Statement, which are assumptions on the data, together with statements about period rings and automorphic objects.
Where the main theorem is applied (once), `AdmACD` and `DelUTie` are again hypotheses, and `PropABCD` is derived from
further named propositions. No declaration gives the conclusion without hypotheses. For most of these hypotheses the
source has, by name and statement, a theorem proving them or deriving them from another (for example `cheb_holds`),
but we did not read those proofs, and we did not trace the path from the final theorem of the source to this one. We
read in full the main theorem, `thmC_of_parts`, `chooseH`, `span_abstract`, `core`, `step`, `det_ne_zero` and the end
of `order_eval`. We read only the statements of the presentation and the length formula, first-half vanishing,
reciprocity, the dimension equality, `compare`, the dual-class count and the automorphic side. A text search of the
whole source finds no `axiom`, and `sorry` only in `src/Challenge.lean`, where the final theorems are restated. We
compiled nothing.
