## Statement

Let `ℓ` be an odd prime, `e ≥ 1`, and `W : G_ℚ → GL₂(ℚ̄_ℓ)` continuous, with: `det W` an odd quadratic character,
unramified at `ℓ`; `W` irreducible on `ker χ_ℓ ∩ ker det W`; at every prime `p ≠ ℓ`, inertia acting diagonally through
characters of finite order; at `ℓ`, `W` upper triangular with inertial characters `χ_ℓ^{e}χ` and `χ_ℓ^{−e}χ` (`χ` of
conductor dividing `ℓ`), the sub-character never `χ_ℓ`; twists of `W` by `χ_ℓ^{−e}` times characters of finite order
attached to newforms of weight `2e + 1`, one of them of level prime to `ℓ` with `a_ℓ` an `ℓ`-adic unit, the quotient
character of that twist at `ℓ` being unramified. Then the Bloch–Kato Selmer group `H¹_f(ℚ, W)` is zero (given a bundle
of standard facts of Galois cohomology, which the proving theorem takes as a hypothesis; see below). In published terms
`W` is `V_λ(f)(e)` twisted by a character of finite order, `f` of odd weight `k = 2e + 1`, and the statement is the case
`K = ℚ`, `r = e` of Kato's Theorem 14.2, read rationally, under extra hypotheses. The irreducibility hypothesis is not
the one under which the Euler-system method is published: no element of the Galois group with a one-dimensional quotient
is assumed.

## Why it might matter

In the published treatments compared, the Euler-system bound needs an element of the Galois group acting with a
one-dimensional quotient (for elliptic curves in Rubin's book it comes from Serre's open image theorem; none exists for
CM curves). Here the image hypothesis is only irreducibility on `ker χ_ℓ ∩ ker det W`, and Kato's Euler system is
constructed in the formal proof, not taken as an input.

## What the checks found

The first reading finds a special case of Kato's theorem, reached mostly by the published method specialised: Kato's
zeta elements and norm relations; a reciprocity law only on the ordinary quotient at `ℓ`, its relation to Kato's proof
not determined; Rubin's direct passage over `ℚ`. One step differs from the published proofs it compared: auxiliary
primes whose Frobenius has no eigenvalue 1, a derivative that is a cochain corrected by `u` with `δu = y ∪ z`, and
non-degeneracy from commutators; at the published auxiliary primes its key congruence is empty, so it is not the
published step restricted. Confidence medium: descent read at its top levels, Euler system by statements, Kato's paper
not opened; one search found no published argument without a one-dimensional quotient, which does not exclude one. The
second opinion cannot tell (medium confidence): the cochain correction is standard height-pairing algebra, and the
potentially substantive point, non-degeneracy through commutators without the rank-one hypothesis, is neither located
nor excluded in print by either comparison; it notes that Theorem 14.2 is unconditional only for `1 ≤ r ≤ k − 1`. The
two disagree in verdict; neither establishes novelty. Open: is this auxiliary-prime step in print (Kato, Perrin-Riou,
Nekovář, Rubin Ch. IX)? Is the reciprocity law Kato's local argument restricted to the ordinary quotient?

## How the formal proof goes

The theorem is a deduction (`vanishOrdL_of_zetaUr`) from two inputs: a bundle of standard facts of Galois cohomology
(Poitou–Tate, local Tate duality, finiteness, Euler characteristics, the Greenberg–Wiles formula, the Bloch–Kato local
conditions), taken as a hypothesis (elsewhere the source builds a term of this bundle; that construction was not
audited); and Kato's Euler system for `W`, proved in the source with no hypothesis (`zetaUr`). It is used for `W` and
for `W ⊗ det W`.

The Euler system. A field of modular functions of all levels, with `q`-expansions and actions of `GL₂⁺(ℚ)` and Galois,
models the étale `H¹` of the open modular curve with coefficients `Sym^w` through its Kummer theory, with explicit Hecke
and Galois operators. Zeta classes are cup products of the Kummer classes of two Siegel units of level `Nℓ^{r+n}`,
carried to weight `k` along the `ℓ`-power tower, traced down and projected to `W`: Kato's elements `_{c,d}z`. Norm
relations along `ℚ(μ_{ℓ^r})` and at tame primes come from Hecke operators and an Eichler–Shimura relation. The output is
an integral class `y` over `ℚ`, unramified outside `ℓ`, and for each prime `w ≡ 1 mod ℓ^n` outside a finite set a class
over the cyclic field of degree `ℓ^n` and conductor `w` with norm `P·y`, where `P ≡ unit·det(W(Frob_w) − 1) mod ℓ^n`.

The reciprocity law is proved only on the unramified quotient line of `W` at `ℓ`. There the tower classes are Kummer
classes of units `P_i(ζ_{ℓ^m})`, and character sums of `ζP_i′(ζ)/P_i(ζ)` equal a Hecke eigenfunctional on the
`q`-expansion of a product of two Eisenstein series; the local work is over the completion of `ℤ_ℓ((q))` with `ζ_{ℓ^n}`
and `q^{1/ℓ^n}` adjoined. By Rankin's method this value is a non-zero multiple of `L(g ⊗ α, e + 1)·L(g ⊗ β, 2e)`, for a
newform `g` of weight `2e + 1` and two Dirichlet characters `α`, `β`; both are non-zero, by positivity from Rankin's
mean-square bound. Hence `y` is not ordinary at `ℓ`.

The descent. First, the strict Selmer group of `W^*(1) = W ⊗ χ_ℓ(det W)^{−1}` is zero. Let `z` be a strict class that is
not a coboundary. `y ∪ z` is locally trivial, so `y ∪ z = δu mod ℓ^n` for a cochain `u` (`δ` the coboundary). For
`w ≡ 1 mod ℓ^n`, the Kolyvagin derivative `d` of the class at `w` is not a cocycle: `δd = −ψ ∪ P·y`, `ψ` the character
of the cyclic field. So `ξ = d ∪ z − P·ψ·u` is a 2-cocycle with values in `μ_{ℓ^n}`, trivial at every place but `w`,
hence also at `w`; a local computation gives `P·h(Frob_w) ≡ 0`, where
`h(s) = det(W(s) − 1)·u(s) − ω(adj(W(s) − 1)·y(s), z(s))`, `ω` the determinant pairing `ω(x, x′) = x₁x′₂ − x₂x′₁`. By
Chebotarev, `det(W(σ) − 1)·h(σ) ≡ 0` on the commutator subgroup of `ker χ_ℓ ∩ ker det W`. For `φ` there and `a, b` in
`ker χ_ℓ ∩ ker W`, `h(φ[a,b]) − h(φ) ≡ det(W(φ) − 1)·(ω(y(b), z(a)) − ω(y(a), z(b)))`, and irreducibility gives
`φ, a, b` with both factors non-zero; letting `n → ∞` gives a contradiction. Second, the ordinary Selmer group of
`W^*(1)` is zero, by pairing a class with `y`, which is not ordinary. Third, the ordinary Selmer group of `W` is zero by
the Greenberg–Wiles formula; a Bloch–Kato class is unramified outside `ℓ` and ordinary at `ℓ`, so `H¹_f(ℚ, W) = 0`.

## Key steps

- **Zeta elements and norm relations** (`UpperTheory.Kd.K4.kato_eulerSystem_integrality`): assembles the integral class
  `y` over `ℚ` and the classes over the cyclic fields of conductor `w` with norm `P·y`, from Siegel units in a Kummer
  model of the cohomology of the modular curve. Read by statement.
- **Reciprocity on the ordinary quotient** (`UpperTheory.V.Z.Kato.K5.smoothedZetaClass_hodgeTateLaw`): the image of the
  tower classes on the unramified quotient at `ℓ` is a Hecke eigenfunctional on a product of two Eisenstein series.
  Read by statement; its relation to Kato's proof was not determined.
- **Non-vanishing of L-values** (`GlobalTheory.EisM.twisted_lvalue_ne_zero_edge`): twisted L-values are non-zero at
  real part `≥ (k + 1)/2`, `k` odd, by positivity; this makes `y` non-ordinary at `ℓ`.
- **Auxiliary-prime step** (`GlobalTheory.Ord2.ht_vanish_commutator`): from the classes at primes `w ≡ 1 mod ℓ^n` and the
  correcting cochain `u`, `det(W(σ) − 1)·h(σ)` vanishes mod `ℓ^n` on commutators. This is the step that differs from
  the published proofs compared.
- **Strict Selmer group** (`GlobalTheory.Ord2.StrictKill.main`): the commutator identity and irreducibility on
  `ker χ_ℓ ∩ ker det W` give the vanishing of the strict Selmer group of `W^*(1)`.
- **From strict to Bloch–Kato** (`GlobalTheory.Ord2.Upgrade.upgrade`, then `GlobalTheory.Ord2.grZero_of_grZero_dual1`):
  pairing with the non-ordinary class `y`, then the Greenberg–Wiles formula.

## What was checked, and what was not

The first reading read the Lean deduction in full at its top two levels (`vanishOrdL_of_zetaUr`, `es_vanish`,
`StrictKill.main`, `ht_vanish_commutator`) and by statement below; the Euler system by definitions and statements only:
the modular-function tower, the projection to `W`, the norm and Eichler–Shimura relations, the reciprocity law and the
positivity proof were not read, nor the proof of the Galois-cohomology bundle. A text search of every `.lean` file found
`sorry` only in a template (`src/Challenge.lean`) and no `axiom`; nothing was compiled. The source also states Kato's
theorem in general (`UpperTheory.V.U3.KatoUse`); no proof of it was looked for. Kato's paper, three further works on
Euler systems that it only names, Kolyvagin and Perrin-Riou were not opened by the first reading. A separate Kolyvagin
argument in the source, with an Euler system of Jacobi sums bounding minus parts of class groups, plays no part here and
was only spot-checked.
